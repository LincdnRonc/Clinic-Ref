BCC

Epidemiology · DDx unpigmented skin lesion · Management · EBRT

Clinic page: Skin: BCC, SCC, melanoma and Merkel

Epidemiology

I: most common skin cancer

A: 60yo

G: M

R: Caucasian, fair skin

RF:

E: UV exposure (intense intermittent exposure), Fitzpatrick I & II

I:

G: Gorlin’s syndrome (PTCH1, PTCH2, SUFU - no RT!), Xeroderma pigmentosum, albinism

I: HIV, solid organ transplant, haematological malignancy

TNM

T1: <2cm

T2: 2-4cm

T3: >4cm, large nerve PNI, DOI >6mm

T4: into adjacent bone

N1: n+

M1: mets

DDx unpigmented skin lesion

Benign: actinic keratoses, keratoacanthoma, cutaneous horn, scar, keloid, dermatofibroma, inclusion cyst, dermatitis

Malignant: BCC, SCC, desmoplastic melanoma, merkel cell, mycosis fungoides, AFX/PDS

DDx pigmented skin lesion:

Benign: naevus, café au lait spot, lentigo maligna, harmatoma, angiofibroma

Malignant: BCC, melanoma, kaposi’s sarcoma, merkel cell carcinoma, angiosarcoma

Path:

  • Favourable: nodular, superficial
  • Unfavourable: micropapillary, morpheic, infiltrative
  • PNI: large nerve ≥0.1mm
  • IHC: Ber-EP4

Management

Topical therapy:

  • Imiquimod, cryotherapy, 5FU
  • Used for pre-invasive disease

1. Surgical excision – WLE, Moh’s

  • R0 excision
  • If R+ then re-excision
  • If high risk (T4, extensive or large nerve PNI, large, recurrent, aggressive histology, immunosuppressed) or unable to re-excise: adjuvant RT

2. Definitive radiotherapy

  • If cosmetically sensitive area, unfit for surgery
  • Caution:
  • lower limb or other poorly perfused areas esp if T2DM
  • Costochondritis / infection
  • Curved or uneven surface

Aiming EQD2 60Gy:

  • 50Gy/25# - if skin graft, especially BTM or split skin
  • 40.05Gy/15#
  • 36Gy/9# alternate days

3. Metastatic/locally adv disease:

  • Sonic hedgehog pathway inhibitors for advanced disease – vismodegib
    • Tox: muscle cramps and spasms are dose limiting, poorly tolerated
  • Historical expected 4 yr LC from upfront surgery 99% vs RT 93% (Avril et al)
    • Caveat is that RT was unconventional: 55% had brachytherapy, 30% had 50kV ‘contact therapy’ 20Gy/2#. Modern RT likely better than this
  • Modern case series demonstrate 10 yr LC 95% for lesions <2cm, 90% for 2-5cm, 50% for >5cm (Hezewijk et al)
  • Meta-analysis (Drucker et al) demonstrated equivalent 2-5 yr LC for RT and surgery (excision or Moh’s) of 95%

EBRT

Dose: 40.05Gy/15#, orthovoltage 125kV, daily treatment

  • 125kV approximate PDD: 95% at 1cm, 90% at 2cm, 50% at 5cm

Mark-up:

  • reclined supine, target surface flat, stretch skin folds out using tape
  • pink stuff at free margins for backscatter
  • lead shielding around target volume
  • additional shield for behind ear, internal or external eye, internal nasal septum
  • 5-10mm margin around scar or tumour bed

Notes:

  • Aiming 95% at 5mm depth
  • Refer to departmental PDD
  • 250kV applicators and above are open ended for stand in
  • TLD day 1, read out should be within 10% of expected dose

Conchal bowl BCC options:

SXRT:

  • Need to flatten ear as much as possible whilst maintaining comfort
    Accept stand off
  • Shield behind ear

Electrons:

  • Bolus is tricky – pack conchal bowl with wet gauze, then place superflab on top to create flat surface OR 3D print bolus
  • Sufficient energy required for increased depth
  • Constriction of high isodose lines at depth, so margin to field edge needs to be larger and minimum 4cm

cSCC

Epidemiology · Pathology · Management · EBRT – PORT in high risk H&N cSCC · CTVLR

Clinic page: Skin: BCC, SCC, melanoma and Merkel

Epidemiology

I: 2nd most common skin ca

A: 60

G: M

R: Caucasian, fair skin

RF:

E: chronic UV exposure, Fitzpatrick type I & II, Marjolin’s ulcer, prv RT

I:

G: Xeroderma pigmentosum

I: immunosuppression – HIV, solid organ transplant, haematologic malignancy

T1: ≤2cm

T2: 2-4cm

T3: >4cm, large nerve PNI, DOI >6mm

T4: adjacent bone

N1: single node, <3cm

N2a: single node, 3-6cm

N2b: multiple ipsilateral nodes <6cm

N2c: contralateral node, <6cm

N3: single node >6cm

M1: DM

WPOI: appearance of invasive front [see oral cavity]

Pathology

  • Pre-invasive: bowen’s disease, actinic keratosis, keratoacanthoma
  • Grade 1-3
  • Unfavourable subtypes: spindle cell, basosquamous, sarcomatoid, desmoplastic, carcinosarcoma
  • IHC: p63, p40, CK 5/6

Absolute PORT indications:

  • T3, T4
  • R1
  • Recurrent disease
  • Clinical or radiological PNI

Relative PORT indications 2 or more:

  • T2
  • DOI >6mm
  • Margins <2mm
  • PNI >0.1mm or multifocal PNI
  • G3
  • LVSI
  • Immunosuppression
  • Infiltrative margin
  • Aggressive subtype
  • High risk site: H zone (embryological clefts)

PORT nodal irradiation indications:

  • N2 disease, ENE
  • Any parotid node +
  • Tumour spill intra-op

Elective nodal irradiation:

  • High risk lesion – esp in H&N
  • High risk site where 1st echelon nodal treatment en block is feasible - pre-auricular, temple

Management

1. Surgical excision

  • WLE +/- flap or graft
  • Moh’s surgery
  • Often preferred as 1st line to optimise local control – no high level evidence
  • Lymph node sampling or dissection should be completed for high risk lesions
  • Adjuvant radiotherapy:
    • 66Gy(SM+), 60Gy (+ve node), 56Gy (-ve node), 50Gy ENI
  • Adjuvant concurrent carboplatin had no benefit (POST trial)

2. Definitive radiotherapy

  • Inoperable disease
  • Pre-op IO to convert to operable disease to be used cautiously with MDT agreeance given response rates of ~50% and risk of progression on IO
  • 60Gy/30#, 55Gy/20#, 45Gy/15#
  • No high level evidence to support concurrent chemo (POST trial negative)

3. Immunotherapy

  • Adjuvant cemiplimab after resection and PORT for 48 weeks for high risk or locally advanced cSCC improves DFS 87% vs 64% – C-POST trial
  • Expect response rate of ~ 50%
  • Indications:
    • ENE
    • ≥ 3 nodes
    • T4
    • PNI
    • In transit met
    • Recurrent dx with one above RF

4. Metastatic SCC:

  • Cemiplimab → no high level evidence

EBRT – PORT in high risk H&N cSCC

Dose: 66Gy/60Gy/54Gy/30#, VMAT, daily treatment with BD compensation

Pre-sim: Ensure the surgical scars/graft have healed, discuss in MDT, aim to commence 4-6 weeks post-op

Sim:

Supine, arms down, TPS head and neck shell

  • Frog leg for groin

IV contrast

Fuse pre-op PET and MRI

Volumes:

CTVHR: 1cm margin around the SM+

PTV6600: +5mm

CTVIR: entire surgical bed, resected nerve pathway, nodal level of N+ (include entire nodal dissection bed if heavy N+)

PTV6000: +5mm

CTVLR

elective nodal levels (next echelon), proximal nerve pathway to BoS if named nerve involvement

PTV5400: +5mm

Daily CBCT with a bony match and soft tissue check

OARs:

Globe Dmax <50Gy, mean <35Gy

Lens D0.03cc <6Gy

Optic nerve, chiasm, brainstem Dmax <54Gy

Parotid mean <26Gy

Larynx mean <35Gy

Pharyngeal constrictors mean <50Gy

Cochlea mean <45Gy

Spinal cord Dmax <45Gy

Merkel

Epidemiology · Pathology · Management · EBRT – PORT for R1 · In-transit mets mgmt

Clinic page: Skin: BCC, SCC, melanoma and Merkel

Epidemiology

I: Rare however AU has highest rate

A: 70

G: M

R: Caucasian

RF

E: UV exposure, fair skin

I: Merkel polyomavirus

G:

I: HIV, solid organ transplant, haematologic malignancy

TNM Staging

T1: <2cm

T2: 2-5cm

T3: >5cm

T4: adjacent organs – bone, cartilage

N1: N+

N2: in-transit met

N3: N+ and in-transit met

M1: DM

Work-up:

  • Full skin and nodal basin assessment
  • SLN mapping
  • CTCAP and FDG PET in all
  • MRIB if metastatic or neuro sx

Surveillance:

  • Post treatment PET at 3mo, 12mo +/- 24mo
  • Clinical f/u q3m for 2 years, then q6mo till 5 years

Pathology

  • Red, violaceous, pearly
  • Neuroendocrine carcinoma – small round blue cells, salt and pepper chromatin, crush artifact, rosettes
  • IHC: CD56+, high Ki67, synaptophysin, chromogranin A, perinuclear dot like CK20

Natural Hx:

  • Fast growing
  • Primary can spontaneously regress – good prognostic factor
  • Very early lymphatic and haematogenous spread ~ 40% at diagnosis
  • High occult nodal +ve
  • Lower limb primary has worse locoregional control

Outcomes

Median OS

  • N- 5 yr OS 50%
  • N+ 5 yr OS 30%
  • M+ 12mo, 5 yr OS 10% - likely better with IO

Radiosensitive disease with excellent LC post RT >95%

Poor prognostic factors:

  • UV assoc (viral negative)
  • No spontaneous regression of primary
  • Size > 1cm
  • H&N
  • LVSI

Figure in the original document (not reproduced here).

Management

1. WLE + nodal management if not assoc with significant morbidity

  • Aim 2cm margins from primary
  • If not feasible due to location then prefer definitive RT
  • cN0 should all have SLNBx, 40% of T2 tumours will have occult N+
  • cN+: RT or LND
  • Prospective data shows similar outcomes for LND and RT - 5 yr OS 70%
  • RT preferred if pt likely to require post-op nodal RT, high risk LE, comorbid

2. Adjuvant radiotherapy: 60Gy for gross disease/R1, 50Gy tumour bed/R0

Adj RT has been shown to improve LRC and OS in all subsites regardless of margin

Primary site adjuvant RT can be excluded if all met:

  • SM >2cm
  • Tumour <1cm
  • No LVSI
  • No H&N primary
  • Not Immunosuppressed

Adjuvant nodal basin RT: 60Gy for gross disease/ENE/R1, 50Gy tumour bed, 45Gy elective

  • ENE (60Gy – treat as R1)
  • Multiple nodes positive
  • Bulky node
    • NB: adjuvant nodal RT has not been shown to provide benefit but is commonly given

3. Definitive RT:

  • Primary site: 60Gy/30#
  • if cN+: 60Gy node +ve, 50Gy elective nodes
  • if cN-: 50Gy if ALL of the following criteria are not met:
  • Successful negative SLNB performed
  • Low false-negative concern, e.g. reliable drainage/mapping, not complex H&N drainage
  • No major nodal-risk features:
    • chronic immunosuppression
    • LVSI
    • large primary
    • head & neck site where SLN mapping is less reliable
  • Adj nodal RT can be omitted after a negative LND

Chemotherapy:

  • No concurrent chemo used routinely
  • TROG 9706 showed good outcomes with carbo/etop but high G3-4 tox. A further study (Poulsen et al) found no survival advantage for these patients when compared to a historical control
  • Given most pts are elderly they are often unfit for chemo, can be considered in young fit patient

Locally advanced:

  • Adj Avelumab for up to 2 yrs shown to improve RFS by approx. 50% on the ADAM ph III trial (28% vs 45% at 5 yrs)
  • Previously available on I-MAT trial in AU (now closed, results pending)

Metastatic:

  • Immunotherapy: Avelumab – 30-40% ORR (Javelin), pembro – 60% ORR, nivo
  • Chemotherapy 2nd line or IO ineligible

EBRT – PORT for R1

Dose: 60Gy/50Gy EQD2/30#, VMAT, daily treatment

Pre-sim: scars healed, aim to commenced 4-6 weeks post op, discuss at MDT

Sim: reclined, surface flattened, wire 3-5cm around surgical scar to delineate primary.

If ITM then wire entire cutaneous strip between primary and in transit-met site

1cm bolus over this entire region

Spare skin strip if treating limb to preserve dermal lymphatics

Volumes:

CTV6000: wired cutaneous region + subcutaneous tissue 15mm deep to wired surface, cropped to anatomical boundaries

PTV6000: +5mm

CTV5000: next echelon elective nodes

PTV5000: 5mm expansion

Note – for a positive node a 5-10mm margin is sufficient

Daily CBCT verification with soft tissue match

TLD day 1

In-transit mets mgmt

  • If ITM + then cover skin between primary and ITM, even if ITM has been resected
  • If ITM + then should have ENI (even with negative SLNB) due to high risk of nodal relapse or occult nodal disease
  • If N+ but no ITM then do not need to cover intervening skin unless primary and nodes are very close to each other (e.g. temple & pre-auricular)
  • Intervening skin to be covered to elective dose – 50Gy
  • Up to 20cm field to treat intervening lymphatics is okay as per TROG trial

Kaposi

Epidemiology · Types · Management

Clinic page: Sarcoma

Epidemiology

I: uncommon

A: 60

G: M

R: Mediterranean, Eastern European, African

RF:

E: Mediterranean

I: HIV, HHV-8

G:

I: Immunosuppression

Staging

Patch → plaque → nodule

Patch:

  • flat, red or violaceous
  • Spindle cell proliferation through dermis, complex vascular channels, extravasated red blood cells

Plaque:

  • Thickened nodule
  • spindle cell proliferation into subcutis
  • Hyaline globules

Nodule:

  • More mitoses and pleomorphism
  • Lumens become large blood filled spaces

Types

  1. Classic
  2. Mediterranean & Eastern European
  3. Older males
  4. Indolent
  5. Affects lower limb

2. Endemic:

  • African
  • Children and adults
  • Aggressive in children
  • Lower limb but earlier nodal and distant spread, esp in kids

3. Epidemic: HIV assoc

  • Aggressive biology
  • Presents with disseminated disease
  • Responsive to antiretroviral Rx

4. Immunosuppressed

  • Aggressive biology
  • Presents with disseminated disease

Pathology:

  • IHC: HHV8 (LANA-1), CD34, CD31, vimentin, D240

Management

Localised disease

  • ‘Curable’ but high chance of recurrence elsewhere
  • Resection if amenable site
  • Definitive radiotherapy: highly radiosensitive: 30Gy/15#, 20Gy/5#
  • Intralesional chemotherapy injection
  • Topical imiquimod, retinoid cream
  • Cryotherapy

Widespread

  • Palliative radiotherapy to lesion: 4-8Gy/1#
  • TSET if significant skin involvement: 12Gy/6#
  • Systemic therapy: reversal of immunosuppression may cause spontaneous regression, ART optimisation for HIV related, doxorubicin chemotherapy, etoposide, paclitaxel

RT notes:

  • Orthovoltage vs MV depends on thickness, if >2cm preference for MV with 1cm bolus
  • Use generous radial margins 2-3cm
  • >90% LC rates
  • 50% of tumours will respond but remain pigmented
  • Distant recurrence frequent in localised disease

Outcomes:

  • OS 5+ years with DM if treated
  • Limited disease, classic type: survival near normal LE
  • Visceral involvement with irreversible immunosuppression, untreated HIV: 1-2 years

AFX & PDS

Epidemiology · Pathology · Mgmt: AFX

Epidemiology

I: Rare

A: 60

G: M

R: Caucasian

RF:

E: UV exposure, Fitzpatrick type I-II skin, prv RT

I: nil

G: nil

I: HIV, solid organ transplant, haematological malignancy

Pathology

Arise over sun exposed/damaged skin

Diagnosis of exclusion

AFX:

  • Pleomorphic spindle/epitheliod cell proliferation confined to dermis

PDS:

  • Histology of AFX + invasion into subcutis
  • Also can have: Necrosis, PNI, LVI

IHC: CD10+

Must exclude other skin primaries: CK5/6-ve, Ber-EP4, S100, CD56, CD34

Mgmt: AFX

  • Surgical resection – often Moh’s surgery or WLE, aim for 1cm margins
  • No radiation
  • Excellent prognosis 90% LC

Mgmt: PDS

  • Surgical resection – aim for 1-2cm margin
  • SLNBx not routinely indicated if no clinical suspicion
  • Adjuvant RT: 66-60Gy/33-30# if:
    • SM <2mm
    • LVI/PNI
    • Broad subcutis extension
    • Recurrent disease
  • Definitive RT for inoperable: 60Gy/30#
  • Use radial margin of 2-3cm for RT

Melanoma

Epidemiology · Mgmt- pre-invasive · RT for R1 resection + ITM · Volumes

Clinic page: Skin: BCC, SCC, melanoma and Merkel

Epidemiology

I: Common in AU, 3rd most common cancer after prostate then breast

A: 60

G: M

R: Caucasian, Asian for acral

RF:

E: UV exposure – intense intermittent > chronic, blistering sunburn in youth, multiple naevi, Fitzpatrick type I-II skin, psoralen UV beds

I:

G: FAMMM (CDKN2Am), Xeroderma pigmentosum, atypical naevi syndrome

I: HIV, solid organ transplant

Pathology:

Pre-invasive: confined to epidermis

  • Lentigo maligna/Hutchinson’s freckle – variable coloured patch usually across cheeks, irregular shape
    • <5% risk of invasive transformation
  • Melanoma in situ – flat pigmented lesion, grows radially rather than deep
    • <10% risk of transformation in 15 years

Invasive:

Clark level – for ≤1mm thickness

  • I: in epidermis (pre-invasive)
  • II: in papillary dermis
  • III: reaches junction between reticular and papillary dermis
  • IV: into reticular dermis
  • V: beyond dermis

Breslow thickness – depth from skin surface to deepest point of lesion

  • Most important prognostic factor – relates to nodal risk and survival

Ulceration – also relates to nodal risk and survival

Non-cutaneous – 5%:

  • Ocular – usually uveal ~ 80%
  • Mucosal ~ 20%, KIT mutations – imatinib
  • GIT
  • Adrenal
  • Meninges
  • All have poor prognosis – 5 yr OS 25%
  • Manage with surgical resection if operable, otherwise definitive RT

Mutations:

  • Very high mutation burden
  • UV damage → DNA mutations
  • BRAFm → transduction of cell survival and proliferation signals via MAPK/ERK
  • CDK2NA HD → unchecked progression through cell cycle
  • TERT promoter mutation → activates telomerase → evasion of senescence
  • TP53 → evade apoptosis

BRAF mutations:

  • Present in 50% of all cutaneous melanoma.
  • Classified as BRAF V600 or non V600
    • V600E is 40% of all, 80% of BRAFm positive melanomas
    • V600K is 10% of all, 20% of BRAFm positive melanomas (worse response)

TNM Staging:

T1: 1mm BT

T2: 1-2mm BT

Microsatellite: adj to primary with intervening normal stroma

T3: 2-4mm BT

T4: >4mm BT

  • A= no ulceration, B= ulceration

N1: 1 regional node

N2: 2-3 nodes

N3: ≥4 nodes

  • C for all = in transit met/microsatellites/satellite

M1a: skin, soft tissue, non-regional node

M1b: lung

M1c: other viscera

M1d: CNS

  • 0= normal LDH, 1= ↑LDH

Outcomes: 5 yr OS

Stage I: >90%

Stage II: 80-90%

Stage III: 70-80%

Stage IV: median OS 5 years with ipi/nivo, 3 years nivo alone

RT notes:

  • Treat entire surgical bed with a 2cm margin radially, 15mm deep margin
  • No longer a clear role for RT for nodal sites as these patients require IO

Mgmt- pre-invasive

1. Surgical excision using dermoscopy and confocal microscopy to map lesion

  • Pathological 0.5cm margin required or else consider re-excision or adj RT (40Gy/20# for R0, 50-55Gy/20# for R1)
  • LC >90%

2. Topical therapy: imiquimod - applied for 3-4 months, causes dermatitis

  • LC 80%

3. Definitive radiotherapy: 56Gy/28# - RADICAL or 50-55Gy/20# eVIQ

  • LC 80%

Mgmt – T1-T4, cN0, M0

1. WLE + SLNBx

  • Lentigo maligna melanoma has infiltrative radial borders –confocal microscopy to assist in mapping
  • Intra-op surgical margins depend on T stage:
    • T1: 1cm, T2: 2cm, T3: 3cm, T4: 3cm
  • Adjuvant radiotherapy to primary: 48Gy/20# if:
    • Insufficient pathological margin unable to be re-excised
      • <2mm or <8mm for desmoplastic or SM+ (55Gy/20#)
    • Neurotropism
    • Extensive LVSI
    • Recurrent
    • Desmoplastic: retrospective data shows RT improves LRC
  • Adjuvant DPCP cream (IO cream) if ineligible for RT

2. Nodal management

  • SLNBx if CL III or BT >0.8mm or ulceration
  • Can also use MIA SLN risk calculator, should have Bx if >5% risk
  • SLNBx improved DFS and provides prognostic value - MLST I
    • Alternative is US surveillance
  • If SLNBx +ve → LND – MLST II showed PFS benefit but no OS or DM benefit. LND helps guide adj Rx and prognostication
    • Alternative:
      • definitive RT 55-50Gy/20# (55Gy for bulky node, 50Gy for small node)
      • US surveillance given no survival benefit with treatment
    • Adj RT post LND as per TROG 02.01 trial criteria – 48Gy/20#
    • IO or targeted therapy if BRAFm – Keynote trials, combi-AD

Mgmt – ITM or satellite lesion (=N+)

  • Consider NA IO as below
  • Resection: if isolated and feasible
    • Adjuvant RT → guided by independent ITM site risk factors (assess based on its own merits as separate primary site)
    • Treatment of intervening skin and elective nodal basin is not routine unlike in Merkel cell
    • All should have adjuvant systemic therapy
  • Unresectable:
    • Isolated limb perfusion/infusion– melphalan, actinomycin D, TNF-a
    • Radiotherapy
    • DPCP cream
    • Intralesional injection – T-VEC

Mgmt – Nany, M0 (stage III)

  • All should have neoadjuvant immunotherapy ipi/nivo x2 cycles – NADINA & SWOG
  • WLE + LND
  • Consider adjuvant radiotherapy to nodal basin (48Gy/20#) if:
    • Any parotid node
    • ≥2 cervical/axilla nodes, or node >3cm, or ENE
    • ≥3 inguinal nodes, or node >4cm, or ENE
    • SM+
    • TROG 02.01 trial, pre-dated IO
  • Adjuvant systemic therapy if not MPR (>10% viable tumour)
    • Further nivo
    • Targeted therapy if BRAFm: dabrafenib/trametinib

Mgmt – metastatic

  • Symptomatic, large brain met: excision or SRS. If going onto doublet IO after GTR, cavity SRS not mandated
  • Small asymptomatic brain mets: commence immunotherapy – Checkmate 204 demonstrated better BM response to ipi/nivo if mets are small & asymptomatic
    • 45% intracranial response with ipi/nivo
    • 20% with nivo
    • 60% with dab/tram however not durable
    • Phase 2 data (ABC 2, COMBI-AD)
  • Others: immunotherapy 1st line
  • Doublet ipi/nivo > single agent, nivo > ipi alone
  • BRAFm: Consider dab/tram for visceral crisis or 2nd line
    • faster response (days vs months) but less durability than IO
  • WH TKI if giving SRS, continue IO

RADICAL (A.Hong, MIA)

-RT and topical imiquimod had equiv LC for HF

MLST I

-SLNBx in cN0 pts improves DFS but not OS and provides prognostic value

MLST II

-LND for SLNBx +ve pts improves PFS and reduces nodal recurrence 90% vs 80%

-LND provides prognostic value and guides adjuvant treatment

-Positive non-sentinel node was independent poor prognostic factor

Combi-AD

-Post-operative dab/tram in BRAF V600E or V600K improves DFS 50% vs 35%.

-OS data pending, but trend to benefit

NADINA

P: resectable stage III-IV melanoma. Response adapted.

I: NA ipi/nivo x2 cycles → surgery → if no MPR (>10% viable tumour) then adj nivo or targeted therapy vs surgery → adjuvant nivo

O: 25% EFS benefit, major pathological response prognostic of RFS

SWOG 1801

P: resectable stage III-IV melanoma. Not response adapted.

I: NA pembro → surgery → adj pembro vs surgery → adj pembro

O: NA improved EFS and OS

TROG 02.01

-Adjuvant RT 48Gy/20# to nodal basin reduces nodal relapse from 35% to 20%

-no OS benefit

-Significant grade 3-4 toxicity, mostly skin and LE for LL

-ENE was a prognostic factor

Checkmate 204

-Doublet ipi/nivo has 55%

-Significantly more toxicity with doublet IO

ABC

-Ipi/nivo had 45-50% intracranial response, nivo alone had 20%.

-Higher toxicity with doublet

RT for R1 resection + ITM

55Gy/48Gy/20# delivered as per ICRU 83 using a VMAT technique and 6MV photons

Delivered daily

ITM with SLNB- = N1c → should be referred for adj systemic

Pre-sim: ensure surgical scars have healed

Sim: supine, legs separated with contralateral left leg elevated vertically and supported

Vac bag immobilisation

Wire surgical scar and 2cm radius around scar

Wire 2cm radius around ITM

1cm of bolus wired regions and intervening skin (ensure entire circumference of limb is not covered)

Volumes

CTV5500: wired 2cm radial regions + 15mm subcutaneous tissue deep to this region, cropped to fascia/bone

PTV5500: 5mm expansion

CTV4800: intervening skin tract between CTV 5500 with 2cm radial margin around tract and 5mm margin below skin

PTV4800: 5mm expansion

Daily CBCT with soft tissue match
