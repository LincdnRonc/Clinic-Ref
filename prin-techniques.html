Proton therapy

Why protons are different

  • Protons deposit most of their dose at the end of their range (Bragg peak), with almost no exit dose. A spread-out Bragg peak (SOBP) covers the target depth.
  • Pencil-beam scanning paints the target spot by spot and layer by layer; it allows intensity-modulated proton therapy (IMPT) and needs no patient-specific apertures.
  • Range uncertainty (≈3–3.5% of range + 1–3 mm, from CT number conversion, anatomy change and set-up) is the main weakness: plans are made robust to range and set-up error rather than relying on a PTV alone, and beams are not aimed to stop just short of a critical organ.
  • RBE is taken as 1.1, but rises at the distal end of each beam: a concern for brainstem necrosis in paediatric posterior fossa tumours.
  • The entrance dose is similar to photons, and skin dose can be higher. Integral (low) dose is much lower, which matters most for children and young adults.
  • Moving targets (lung, liver) need interplay-effect mitigation: rescanning, gating or breath-hold.

Where the evidence is strongest Verify

  • Paediatric tumours treated for cure: medulloblastoma (CSI), ependymoma, craniopharyngioma, germ cell tumours, rhabdomyosarcoma, Ewing sarcoma, low-grade glioma, retinoblastoma. Lower expected late effects and second cancers.
  • Skull base and spinal chordoma and chondrosarcoma needing ≥70 Gy next to the brainstem or cord.
  • Uveal melanoma not suitable for plaque.
  • Re-irradiation and selected young adults with lymphoma (mediastinal, to lower heart and breast dose), CNS tumours and sarcoma.
  • Randomised adult data remain limited: oesophagus (phase IIB, lower total toxicity burden, Lin JCO 2020); oropharynx (DAHANCA 35, TORPEdO and the US phase III MDACC trial reporting lower long-term toxicity); prostate (PARTIQoL: no clear quality-of-life advantage); lung (RTOG 1308 ongoing).

Access in Australia Verify

  • The Australian Bragg Centre for Proton Therapy and Research (Adelaide) is the first Australian proton centre; check its current status and referral criteria.
  • The Medical Treatment Overseas Program (MTOP) has funded overseas proton therapy for eligible patients, mainly children and young adults with tumours where protons are clearly preferable. Eligibility needs specialist support and an application.
  • Refer early: planning, travel and approval take weeks.

Other particles

  • Carbon ions: high LET (lower OER, less cell-cycle dependence); used in Japan, Germany, Italy and China for radioresistant tumours (chordoma, sarcoma, mucosal melanoma, pancreas). Randomised data are sparse.
  • FLASH (ultra-high dose rate, >40 Gy/s): spares normal tissue in animal studies; early human feasibility only.
  • Boron neutron capture therapy: investigational, for recurrent H&N and brain tumours.

MR-guided radiotherapy

What MR guidance adds

  • Soft-tissue contrast on the treatment unit, without imaging dose: the target and nearby bowel are visible every day.
  • Daily online adaptation: re-plan to the anatomy of the day (adapt-to-shape) or shift the plan (adapt-to-position). Workflow details: MR-linac adaptive workflow.
  • Real-time cine imaging and gating on the target itself (no fiducials), allowing smaller margins for moving targets.
  • Costs: long sessions (often 30–60 min), staff time at the console, patient tolerance and claustrophobia, MR safety screening, and the electron-return effect at air–tissue boundaries in the magnetic field.
  • Platforms: Elekta Unity (1.5 T) and ViewRay MRIdian (0.35 T, gating with cine).

Key evidence Verify

MIRAGE (2023) · Phase III, prostate SBRT 40/5 · MR-guided (2 mm margins) vs CT-guided (4 mm) · Less acute grade ≥2 GU and GI toxicity with MR guidance (Kishan, JAMA Oncol).
SMART (2023) · Prospective phase II, locally advanced and borderline pancreas · Stereotactic MR-guided on-table adaptive RT 50/5 · Low rate of acute grade ≥3 GI toxicity with encouraging survival (Parikh, IJROBP).
MOMENTUM (2019 onward) · International Unity registry · Prospective outcomes across sites; source of many single-site reports.
  • Ongoing or maturing: LAP-ABLATE (pancreas, randomised), MR-ADAPTOR (adaptive strategies), MR-linac liver, oligometastatic and rectal studies.
  • Most other evidence is single-arm: liver SABR, adrenal and central lung oligometastases, prostate bed, bladder.

Site considerations

  • Pancreas and upper abdomen: the main gain. Daily adaptation keeps the high-dose region off the duodenum and stomach (constraints applied to OARs within about 2 cm of the PTV).
  • Liver: lesions not visible on CBCT can be seen and gated without fiducials.
  • Prostate: smaller margins, urethra visualisation, intra-fraction motion monitoring.
  • Pelvis: bladder and rectal filling variation is seen and adapted to.
  • Contouring and sequence tips for these sites: liver and upper GI, pelvis, tumours and nodes.
