General GI: constraints, toxicity, re-irradiation

Shared upper-abdominal and pelvic limits, and toxicity management that applies across GI sites.

CP-B: keep mean liver <9 Gy (3–5 fx). See the hepatobiliary page for the full risk-adapted algorithm.

Pelvic re-irradiation (SABR Delphi)

  • Cumulative EQD2 (α/β 3), D0.5cc: bladder ≤120 Gy, small bowel ≤98 Gy, colon/rectum ≤110 Gy, sacral nerve roots ≤60 Gy (assuming up to ⅓ recovery).
  • Reconstruct prior dose, discuss at a specialist MDT, and set goals (cure vs symptoms) before planning.

Radiation proctitis and bowel

  1. Optimise bowel habit and stool consistency.
  2. Vitamins A/E/C (A 10,000 IU, E 400 IU TDS, C 500 mg TDS, 90 d) or metronidazole 400 mg TDS for 30 d.
  3. Sucralfate enema 2 g BD for 30 d if bleeding affects QoL (77% improve two grades); consider holding anticoagulation.
  4. APC for persistent bleeding (ulcers in 26%), or formalin or hyperbaric oxygen.
  • Inflammation-predominant: loperamide, fibre/bulking agents, rectal hydrocortisone 90 mg BD.
  • High-dose probiotics (L. plantarum) reduced days with loose stools during pelvic RT (ProRad).
  • Fasting before treatment protected bowel in animal work; NPO 2 h is standard for upper GI plans.

Inguinal node volumes

  • Inguinal nodes sit anterior and anteromedial to the femoral vessels, rarely posterior or lateral.
  • Lower border: ~2 cm below the saphenofemoral junction (rectal/anal atlases).

Neuroendocrine, small bowel, oligometastases

Rarer GI sites, where RT plays a smaller part.

Neuroendocrine tumours

  • Small-intestine NETs appear radiosensitive; colorectal and pancreatic NETs less so (hypothesis-generating).
  • NETTER-1: midgut SSTR+ well-differentiated (Ki-67 ≤20%) progressing on octreotide → 177Lu-DOTATATE ×4 vs high-dose octreotide: 20-mo PFS 11 → 65%, ORR 3 → 18%; OS 36 → 48 mo (NS); G3–4 haematological <10%.
  • COMPETE: 177Lu-edotreotide beat everolimus for PFS and ORR in progressive SSTR+ G1–2 GEP-NETs, with less G3–4 toxicity.
  • Retreatment PRRT after durable response is being tested (NET RETREAT).

Small bowel adenocarcinoma

  • Resection → 5-FU-based chemo. Duodenal cancers are managed like gastric (may need Whipple; adjuvant chemoRT for high risk). Jejunum/ileum like colon.
  • ChemoRT for close/positive margins has no convincing benefit in retrospective series.

GI oligometastases

  • MDT is most established in colorectal liver/lung metastases; for oesophagogastric, pancreatic and biliary cancers it remains largely trial-based.
  • Gastric: MDT to all sites with systemic therapy conditionally recommended after PET/laparoscopy (ASTRO 2025).
  • CRC lung mets: higher BED, smaller size and lower burden predict control.
