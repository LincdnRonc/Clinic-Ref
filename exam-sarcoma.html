Soft tissue

Epidemiology · Differential diagnosis · Management · EBRT · Special cases: RPS

Clinic page: Sarcoma

Epidemiology

I: rare 1% of adult ca, relatively more common in paediatric population

STS 80% of all sarcoma

2/3rd in thigh, 1/3rd elsewhere

A: <20yo and 70yo

G: M
R:

RF:

E: previous RT (osteosarcoma, UPS, angiosarcoma), smoking, ETOH, thorotrast, arsenic, chronic inflammation

I: nil

G: chronic lymphedema (Stewart Treves), NF-1 (MPNST), Gardener’s syndrome (desmoid tumours), Li Fraumeni, Rb (OS, LMS, radiation sarcoma), Gorlin’s syndrome (RMS), Carney’s triad (GIST)

I: HIV for Kaposi’s sarcoma

Staging

T1: <5cm

T2: 5-10cm

T3: 10-15cm

T4: >15cm

N1 - node

M1 – DM

TNM also incorporates grade into final stage

-must be G1 to be stage I

SCARE subtypes more likely to have nodal spread:

-Synovial

-Clear cell

-Angiosarcoma

-RMS

-epitheliod sarcoma

Haematogenous spread more common generally – lung, liver, bone

-↑ risk with size and grade

5yr OS

Localised: 80%

Regional: 50%
DM: 15%

Follow-up:

MRI and CTC q6monthly - yearly

Notes:

-a/b ratio for sarcoma between 3-5 for tumour control

Prognostic factors:

  • Deep location
  • Truncal location
  • Large size
  • Grade
  • Aggressive histology

Differential diagnosis

Benign: fibroma, lipoma, haemangioma, cyst, abscess, adenoma, fibrosis, neurofibroma, schwannoma

Boderline: paraganglioma, desmoid tumour

Malignant: soft tissue sarcoma, metastases, melanoma, extramedullary plasmacytoma, lymphoma

Pathology:

>50 subtypes.

Common:

Undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma -15%

Liposarcoma: well diff, de-diff, myxoid liposarcoma- 15%

Leiomyosarcoma -15% (tendency for DM)

Dermatofibrosarcoma – 10%

Malignant peripheral nerve sheath cell tumour – 10%

Synovial sarcoma – 10%

Angiosarcoma - <5%

Solitary fibrous tumour - <5%

Others: rhabdomyosarcoma, GIST, pleomorphic dermal sarcoma

IHC:

Mesenchymal (all) – vimentin

Rhabdosarcoma – myogenin, MyoD1

Leiomyosarcoma – SMA, desmin, h-caldesmon

Liposarcoma – MDM2, myxoid – FUS-DDIT3

Angiosarcoma – factor VII related antigen, ERG, CD34, CD31

GIST – cKIT, PDGFRA, DOG1

MPNST – SOX10, S100

Synovial – AE1/AE3, EMA, TLE1

Fibrosarcoma – NTRK3 fusion

French grading system

  1. Mitoses: 0-9, 10-19, >20
  2. Necrosis: 0, <50%, >50%
  3. Differentiation: well, de-diff, pleomorphic

-Grade correlates with DM and OS

Management

Diagnosis:

Bx performed by sarcoma surgeon:

  • Longitudinal tract that can be excised
  • mark skin entry site
  • does not cross compartments or transperitoneal
  • limits functional and cosmetic deficit
  • core bx

Discuss at MDT and refer to high volume treatment centre

1. Surgical resection

Amputation

  • if extent of tumour prevents limb preservation
  • involvement of major NVB
  • dysfunctional limb which is unsalvageable
  • Compromised normal tissue – PVD, T2DM
  • Contraindication to RT

WLE

  • Adequate for tumours <5cm, superficial, low grade, margins >1cm (LRR <15%)
  • Others need RT to reduce risk of LRR <15% (Rosenberg trial, NCI by Yang et al, and MSKCC trial using brachytherapy)
  • Restrospective data suggests 10% 3 yr OS benefit for high grade (SEER data)
  • Consider SLN in SCARE subtypes

(O’sullivan trial, RTOG 0630 IMRT and tailored margins can ↓ late tox to <5%)

The wound healing toxicity is less so in the upper limb sarcoma

Pre-op hypofractionation:

  • Hypofractionation 42.75Gy/15# is safe with late toxicity rates <5% and 4 yr LC 93% (HYPORT-STS, ph II)
  • Ultra-hypofrac pre-op RT with 30Gy/5# has shown favourable toxicity with lower late tox between 4-12%, acute wound tox 30%. 5 yr LC 86% (ASTRO 2024 – Nikitas et al, ph II)
  • Ultra hypofractionation may be assoc with higher amputation rates, fracture rate and worse late wound complication

Other RT:

  • Pre-op modified Eilber protocol: 30Gy/20# BD + infusional doxorubicin 72 hrs prior
  • Post-op HDR BT also improves LC 46Gy over 5 days (Pisters et al)

3. Systemic therapy

Chemotherapy: ifosfamide, doxorubicin

  • NCCN and ESMO suggest considering on case by case scenario, beware toxicity preventing definitive mgmt in neoadjuvant setting
  • Conflicting data regarding OS benefit (SMAC MA)
    • Improved recurrence free survival
    • OS outcomes not statistically significant, absolute 4% gain in 10 yrs
    • Later analysis showed ifosfamide regimes were significant for OS improvement
  • Certain subtypes exhibit chemosensitivity: synovial sarcoma, uterine LMS, myxoid liposarcoma
  • Sarculator & MSKCC nomogram to aid decision making

Immunotherapy:

  • Peri-op pembrolizumab in G3 UPS or LPS improved DFS by 15% but increased G3 tox- SARC032

Unresectable:

Chemotherapy to downstage

Isolated limb perfusion chemotherapy

Limb amputation

RT alone: 70Gy

Consider pembro in UPS or dediff LS (SARC028)

Special cases:

Myxoid liposarcoma: NA 36Gy/18# provides 100% LC at 2 years, 97% LC at 5 yrs (Doremy trial, ph II)

Path: bland spindle cells, abundant myxoid stroma, variable round cell component, chicken wire capillaries

RPS:

  • Most commonly liposarcoma
  • LR after GTR is common
  • RT may improve ARFS in well diff and G1-2 de-diff liposarcoma (STREXIT and STRASS pooled analysis)
  • RT should be given in NA setting as 60Gy to abdomen unachievable due to OARs and bowel filling tumour bed
  • STRASS II trial examining pre-op chemo vs surgery alone pending
  • CDK4i reduced mortality and improved PFS in recurrent liposarcoma. No increased G3 tox. (SARC041, ph III)

NCIC RCT

P: Pre-op vs post-op RT for STS

I: 50Gy/25# vs 66Gy/33#

O: Efficacy equivalent 7 yr LC ~92%, DFS and OS similar

Toxicity is where difference lies

SARC032

P: stage III STS G2-3 UPS, DD/pleomorph liposarcoma

I: Pre-op RT vs pembro + pre-op RT 50Gy/25# + adj pembro

O: 15% DFS benefit at 2 yrs

STRASS:

P: RPS, most liposarcoma, ph III

I: pre-op RT 50.4Gy/28# → surgery vs surgery alone

O: ARFS not improved by pre-op RT. However flawed trial as ARFS was included R2 surgery, progressive disease during RT, patient having unresectable disease

Post-hoc analysis of just liposarcoma had benefit in ARFS, however underpowered for this

STREXIT:

Retrospective propensity matched cohort analysis of pre-op RT for RPS. Pooled analysis of STRASS and STREXIT demonstrated ARFS benefit. Benefit more pronounced in WD and G1-2 DD liposarcoma rather than G3 DD liposarcoma

EBRT

Dose:

-Neoadjuvant: 50Gy/25#, daily treatment, VMAT technique

-Adjuvant: 2 dose levels 50Gy/25# + sequential boost to 60Gy for R0, 66Gy for R1

Pre-sim: ensure scars healed, bx completed by sarcoma surgeon, refer to high volume treatment centre

Sim:

-Supine, limb immobilised and isolated, reduce skin folds– vertically raising leg, frog leg

-vac bag or TPS immobilisation

-wire scar

-1cm bolus if skin/surgical scar involvement (spare strip of skin to preserve dermal lymphatics)

-Fuse T1 & T2 MRI and PET

Volumes (as per Hass review – red journal):

Neoadjuvant

  • GTV – on T1 post gad MRI
  • CTV 5000: +3cm longitudinally along muscle fibres/tissue plane, +1.5cm radially clipped to muscle compartment and uninvolved bone
    • Peritumoral oedema should be assessed on T2/STIR MRI and manually included
    • In subcutaneous sarcoma without tissue planes 3cm isometric margin
  • PTV 5000: + 7mm

Adjuvant:

Uses sequential cone down technique 50Gy → 60Gy or 66Gy

  • CTV 6600: R1 area + 1.5cm anatomical expansion clipped to uninvolved anatomical boundaries (bone, muscle compartments)
  • PTV: +7mm

Tumour bed:

  • CTV 6000: tumour bed + 2cm longitudinally, +1.5cm radially, clipped as above
  • PTV 6000: +7mm

CTV 5000: tumour bed + 4cm longitudinally along muscle fibres, +1.5cm radially clipped to bone and uninvolved muscle compartments + surgical clips, operative bed, seroma, drain sites (omitted in wide R0 low risk scenario or where inclusion would cause significant toxicity)

  • PTV 5000: +7mm

Special cases: RPS

  • DTPA, fertility sparing
  • 4DCT or EEBH or bladder/bowel prep
  • NA RT: 50.4Gy/28#
  • RPS: CTV expansion: 15mm, cropped to bone, major vessels, solid organs, expand to include entire retroperitoneal fat compartment, areas difficult to resect and at high risk of local recurrence
  • 5mm PTV expansion

Verification:

  • Daily CBCT with bone match

OARS:

  • Pre-op RT major risk is wound breakdown. This can be mitigated by contracting volume to 3mm below skin and avoiding bolus IF no skin involvement
  • Spare 2cm strip of skin to preserve dermal lymphatics is historical, now use V20Gy<100% of limb circumference
  • Fem head/humeral head: V50Gy<5%, V45Gy<50%
  • Joint: V50Gy <50% to reduce joint contracture
  • Bone: V40Gy <64%, mean <37Gy, max <60Gy.
    • If not met consider prophylactic fixation due to # risk

Toxicities:

  • Acute: dermatitis, limb pain, fatigue
  • Late: fibrosis, lymphedema, joint stiffness, reduced ROM, neuropathic pain, fracture, non-healing wound

Osteosarcoma

Epidemiology · DDx for bone lesion · Management · EBRT · Volumes

Clinic page: Sarcoma

Epidemiology

I: rare, relatively more common in paeds

A: Bimodal: paeds and 60yo

10-15: Ewings

10-20: osteosarcoma

30-40: giant cell tumour

60+: chrondosarcoma, osteosarcoma

G: M

R: Caucasian

RF

E: prv RT, chronic osteomyelitis, bone infarction

I: nil

G: Rb, Li Fraumeni

I: nil

Anatomic location

Epiphysis: giant cell tumour of bone

Metaphysis: osteosarcoma, chondrosarcoma

Diaphysis: mets, Ewing’s, haem

Common presentation in adults:

50% about knee

50% axial skeleton or skull

DDx for bone lesion

Benign: osteoma, chrondroma, paget’s disease of bone, bone island, degenerative change, fibroma, bone infarct

Malignant: osteosarcoma, chrondrosarcoma, ewing’s sarcoma, GCT, plasmacytoma, myeloma, leukemic sarcoma, metastases

Bloods: ALP, LDH

Path:

Anaplastic malignant cells with abnormal osteoid formation, immature bone matrix, irregular architecture

Subtypes:

-Classic (85%): chondroblastic, osteoblastic, fibroblastic, high grade

-Parosteal (5%): Ø periosteal reaction, low grade

-Periosteal (5%): juxtacortical reaction present, intermediate grade

IHC: osteocalcin, osteonectin, ALP

Radiological:

Sunburst appearance

Codmans triangle

Sclerotic

Management

1.Surgical resection in mainstay

  • WLE
  • Amputation

Osteosarcoma is relatively radio and chemoresistant

2.Chemotherapy in high grade/classic subtype

  • NA MAP: MTX, doxorubicin, cisplatin
  • Re-stage to exclude DM pre-op
  • Adj chemo for all high grade or if >10% viable tumour cells

3.Radiotherapy

  • Adjuvant if residual unresectable disease: 66Gy if microscopic, 70Gy if macroscopic
  • Unresectable: 70Gy+

F/u:

CT Chest and primary site q6monthly

5 yr OS:

Localised: 80%

Regional: 60%

DM: 30%

EBRT

Dose: 66-70Gy in 33-35#, VMAT

Pre-sim: ensure scars healed

Sim:

Supine, limb isolated, raise leg vertically, frog leg, reduce skin folds

Vac bag, TPS to immobilise

Wire scar

1cm bolus if skin SM +ve

Fuse MRI/PET

Volumes

CTV 6600: Margin positive + 1.5cm

PTV 6600: +7mm

CTV 6000: pre-op tumour extent + 2cm isotropic expansion + operative bed, clips

PTV 6000: +7mm

Consider smaller LR CTV expansions in non-long bones

Daily CBCT with bone match

I: nil

Pathology

Atypical chondroblasts

Subtypes: conventional, clear cell, myxoid, mesenchymal

Highly radioresistant pathology

Anatomic:

Vertebral body (C spine)

More indolent than osteosarcoma

Management

2.Radiotherapy

  • High grade with SM+

Desmoid

Epidemiology · Pathology · Management

Clinic page: Sarcoma

Epidemiology

I: Rare

A: Pregnant women

G: F

R: nil

RF:

E: estrogen increase during pregnancy, prior abdo surgery

I: nil

G: Gardeners’ syndrome

I: nil

Anatomy:

Extra-abdominal soft tissue – 70%

Abdominal wall – pregnancy female, 30%

Intra-abdominal – 10%

Outcomes:

Stabilisation/regression in 50%

Surgery: 50-70% LC for R1/R0

RT: 80% LC

Pathology

Spindle cell with uniform appearance

CTNNB1 mutation (B-catenin) – 90%

If CTNNB1 WT then assess for FAP (APC mutation → B-catenin upregulation → ↑Wnt signalling)

IHC: vimentin, SMA, B-catenin

Natural hx:

Benign however locally aggressive and tendency for recurrence

3 disease trajectories:

  • Progressive ~ 50%
  • Stabilisation
  • Regression

Can spontaneously regress after childbirth with estrogen reduction

Management

1.Surveillance - imaging q6monthly for 2 years then annual

  • Asymptomatic
  • No impending organ compromise

2.Resection

  • If immediate effect required
  • High risk of LR

3.Medications:

  • Nirogacestat – gamma secretase inhibitor
  • Anti-oestrogen therapy – tamoxifen + NSAID
  • TKI: sorafenib
  • Doxorubicin based chemo if rapid response required

4.Radiotherapy:

  • Recurrence of symptomatic disease which is unresectable, or post-op re-residual disease where progression is morbid, unsuitable for systemic
  • LC 70-80%
  • 56Gy/28# (2cm radial, 3-5cm longitudinal CTV margins, cropped to muscle compartment. If no muscle compartment then 2cm isotropic expansion)
  • Can take several years to regress
