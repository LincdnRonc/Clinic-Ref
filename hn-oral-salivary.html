Oral cavity and lip

Oral cavity cancer is managed with surgery first, and neck dissection for most. Give PORT for adverse features and chemoRT for a positive margin or ENE. Start RT within 6 weeks of surgery.

Decisions

  • Neck (ASCO): T2+ (>2 cm or depth of invasion >5 mm) → ipsilateral neck dissection levels Ia–III (≥18 nodes). T1 → neck dissection, or ultrasound surveillance in highly selected patients. Add level IV if cN+. Dissect the contralateral neck for midline or T3–4 floor of mouth tumours.
  • Neck RT: not needed for pN0 or a single node without ENE after a good dissection, unless there is PNI, LVSI or T3–4. Undissected neck: 50–56 Gy.
  • PORT to primary: pT3–4, LVSI, PNI, close margin (≤5 mm), depth of invasion >3–4 mm (oral tongue). Starting within 6 weeks improves 5 y LC (about 40 → 80% in one series).
  • Margins: goal >5 mm. Margins <5 mm are associated with worse outcomes.
  • Definitive RT can be considered for oral commissure, buccal mucosa or retromolar trigone tumours where surgery would be morbid.

Doses and volumes

  • CTV_63–66: positive margin or ENE + 1 cm. CTV_60: tumour bed + 1–1.5 cm (pre-op tumour + 2 cm for oral tongue and floor of mouth).
  • Dissected involved neck EQD2 60 Gy; undissected at-risk neck 50 Gy; high-risk nodal (ENE) 64–66 Gy. Add 5 mm around the nodal bed (10 mm with ENE).
  • Lip: RT if the commissure is involved, upper lip, ≥2 cm or >50% of the lip. T1 lip 50/20. Commissure involved → cover levels Ib–II to 54 Gy.
  • Brachytherapy (GEC-ESTRO): LDR 60–70 Gy at 0.4–0.6 Gy/h, or HDR 60 Gy (5 Gy BID × 12) as sole treatment for small tumours.
  • Keep the mandible ≤70 Gy.

Salivary gland

Surgery first. PORT for high-grade, positive margins, PNI, nodes, LVSI or T3–4, and for all adenoid cystic carcinomas. Treat named nerves to the skull base for PNI. Concurrent chemo is not routine.

PORT (ASCO)

  • Offer PORT: G3, positive margin, PNI, node-positive, LVSI, T3–4, and all resected adenoid cystic carcinoma.
  • May offer: G2 or close margins.
  • Start within 8 weeks of surgery.
  • Dose: 60–66/30 (RTOG 1008). The trial tests adding weekly cisplatin; it is not standard yet.
  • Neck: ipsilateral I–III dissection for G3 or cN+. Occult nodal rates are about 10% for G1–2 and about 50% for G3. Adenoid cystic: cover the nerve; the neck usually does not need treating.
  • Pleomorphic adenoma: consider 50–60 Gy for multiple recurrences or deep lobe involvement.
  • Particle therapy has no proven advantage over photons.

Notes
Oral cavity overview
-There have been few studies for oral cavity specifically. Refer also to post-op trials
-Most randomized trials come from Tata Memorial. They showed than LND improves OS. They also held a phase II study to show that neoadjuvant chemo can help mandibular preservation
-However the Senti MERORL trial and a Japanese trial showed that SLNB and LND are noninferior.
-Much data in oral cavity centers around management of RT field and lymphatics
-Retrospective studies show that ipsilateral RT can be employed for well-lateralized lesions
-The ACRIN 6685 study supports omission of RT in PET negative dissected neck

Oral cavity

From the exam summary.

Epidemiology

I: Most common H&N ca globally

A: 60

G: M

R: South and central America

RFs

E: smoking, poor oral hygiene, chronic dental infection, betel nut chewing, ETOH, periodontal disease

I: chronic oral candidiasis

G: Fanconi anaemia

I: HIV, solid organ transplant

Staging

T1: <2cm, DOI <5mm

T2: 2-4cm, DOI <10mm

T3: >4cm, DOI >10mm

T4: fixed/invading structures outside oral cavity

N1: single, ipsi, <3cm

N2a: single, ipsi, 3-6cm

N2b: multiple, ipsi, 3-6cm

N2c: contralateral, <6cm

N3: >6cm, ENE

M1: DM

WPOI:
- Graded from 1-5 by borders (pushing vs infiltrative),

  • 4: separated tumour islands and budding
  • 5: dispersed tumour satellites >1mm from invasive front

Subsites & 1st echelon

  • Lip – 1a if central, 1b if lateral
  • Buccal mucosa – pre-auricular, 1b
  • Gingiva / alveolar ridge – 1a/1b
  • RMT- 1b
  • FOM – 1a or 1b
  • Hard palate – RP, 1b, 2
  • Tongue – 1a if tip, 1b if lateral

Path

Pre-invasive: see OPSCC

  • SCC – verrucous, acantholytic, spindle cell, adenosquamous, papillary
  • Salivary gland carcinoma
  • Mucosal melanoma
  • EM plasmacytoma
  • Lymphoma
  • Sarcoma, Kaposi’s sarcoma
  • Cutaneous SCC or BCC

Benign

  • Odontogenic cyst,
  • Odontoma
  • Cyst
  • Abscess

Management

Curable

Surgery

  • WLE + neck node dissection is mainstay (Iyer et al)
  • SLNBx reasonable if DOI <4mm and high volume centre, neck dissection if DOI >4mm (Cruz et al)
  • If within 1cm of midline should do bilateral neck dissection
  • SLNB vs LND reduces fibrosis, shoulder dysfunction and sensory impairment
  • Total glossectomy is accompanied with laryngectomy as risk of aspiration is very high without tongue to propel food bolus
  • If borderline resectable can trial downstaging NA induction chemo (TPF) if MDT agreeance.
  • If clearly unresectable proceed to definitive CRT accepting worse OS
  • Peri-op pembro improves EFS by 10%

Adj treatment

  • Adj RT and chemo follows same principles as OPSCC: T3/T4, 2 or more nodes, LVI, PNI, SM+ve not re-excisable, close SM <2mm, node >3cm, ENE (pooled analysis EORTC and RTOG trials)
  • Adj RT should include the entire surgical bed, including nodal dissection bed even if N-
  • Consider omission of adj chemo unless multiple high risk features: T3/T4 + N2-N3 + ENE (OCAT)
  • Peri-op immunotherapy for locally advanced resectable improves 3 yr EFS by 11% and possibly OS (KEYNOTE 689)

Metastatic

  • cisplatin based chemotherapy, cetuximab, immunotherapy
  • Palliative RT

Iyer et al

P: retrospective, locally advanced oral cavity SCC

O: OS and LRC better with surgery than definitive CRT. 5 yr OS 60% vs 30%

Senti-MERORL

P: T1-2N0 oral cavity SCC

I: SLNBx vs neck node dissection

O: SLNB non-inferior for 2 year RFS. OS similar. Less shoulder dysfunction, fibrosis, sensory deficit.

OCAT

P: Resected locally advanced oral cavity SCC

I: Adj treatment with RT 60Gy/30# vs CRT vs accelerated RT 60Gy/30# 6 fx/week

O: 10 yr LRC 60% for all. 10 yr OS 40% for all. CRT had DFS benefit if T3-4, N2-3 and ENE

KEYNOTE 689

P: All H&N operable stage III or IV (most were oral cavity)

most HPV negative

I: NA pembro x2c → surgery → pembro x12c concurrent/adj with + PORT +/- chemo

PORT +/- chemo if ENE or close SM <1mm

O: 3 yr EFS benefit of 11%, estimated 10% OS benefit (not statistically sig)

Post-op RT

Dose: 66Gy (SM not re-excisable, ENE) / 60Gy/56Gy/30-33#.

10# per week, BD compensation. OTT <49 days

Concurrent IV cisplatin if ENE or SM+ve as per OPSCC

Pre-sim: ensure scar healed, DT/SP, PEG if bilateral neck treatment, dental rv, audiology if cisplatin

Sim

Supine, arms down, neck extended, bite block, tongue depressor, TPS over head and shoulders

1cm bolus if cervical N+

CT scan

Fuse PET/MRI

Volumes

CTV 6600: area of ENE or SM+ve + 10mm expansion, cropped to uninvolved anatomical boundaries

PTV 6600: +3mm

CTV 6000: tumour bed + 5mm + involved nodal level + adjacent high risk surgical bed site + CTV6600

PTV 6000: +3mm

CTV 5600: elective undissected nodes (level 1a/b – IV +/- V if level II node +), entire operative bed + CTV6000

PTV 5600: +3mm

Osteoradionecrosis

  • Non-healing exposed irradiated bone, >3-6 months post RT, without persistent/recurrent tumour
  • Higher risk after marginal mandibulectomy (≥1cm of residual bone) vs segmental mandibulectomy (poorly vascularised bone left vs reconstruction)
  • Mandible has less blood supply than maxilla so at higher risk
  • RT doses >60Gy

Pathophys

  • Radiation induced vascular damage (endarteritis obliterans)
  • chronic inflammation and activation of fibroblasts lead to fibrosis
  • osteocyte death and reduced osteoblast activity → hypovascular, hypoxic and hypocellular tissue → impaired healing → necrosis
  • Dental extraction can precipitate by causing non healing injury

Mgmt

  • Exclude recurrence
  • Treat concurrent infection, oral hygiene (chlorhexidine mouthwash), regular dental reviews, avoid further trauma (dentures)
  • PENTO regime: pentoxifylline + vitamin E to improve vascularity and reduce fibrosis
  • HBO – mixed evidence
  • Debridement or mandibulectomy + vascularised free flap reconstruction

Mgmt

Surgery is mainstay

  • Total parotidectomy with or without facial nerve preservation.
  • Superficial parotidectomy for benign lesions
  • Submandibular resection = sublingual, floor of mouth and ipsilateral submandibular resection
  • Observation appropriate for Warthin’s

PNI

If clinical or radiological PNI → cover to BOS foramen with CTV5400, cover involved segment with CTV6600, consider coverage of communicating nerves

If microscopic large nerve PNI → cover to exiting foramen (stylomastoid foramen for CN VII)

Best visualised on post contrast fat supressed T1 images

Parotid

  • Small nerve PNI → cover to stylomastoid foramen
  • Multiple large nerve PNI, named nerve PNI, clinical/radiological PNI, ACC histo → cover to IAM
  • Clinical or radiological evidence of intracranial neural extension → cover to brainstem
  • Cover auriculotemporal nerve up till V3 at foramen ovale if evidence of involvement or very very high risk

Submandibular

  • Cover to BoS if multiple large nerve PNI, named nerve PNI, clinical/radiological PNI, ACC histo
  • Where there is multiple large nerve PNI but no named nerve:
    • FOM involvement predominant → lingual nerve → cover to V3 foramen ovale
    • Extension of tumour to deep tongue or tongue weakness → hypoglossal nerve at risk → cover to hypoglossal canal
    • Avoid covering both unless it is plausible that cancer extended to both

Skin lesions

  • V1: Meckel cave → cavernous sinus → SOF → skin
    • Upper eyelid, medial canthus, eyebrow
  • V2: meckels cave → foramen rotundum → inferior orbital fissure → infraorbital foramen → skin
    • Nasal ala, upper lip, temple
  • V3: meckels cave → foramen ovale → between ptyergoid m → medial to mandible ramus → under submandibular gland → tongue → mental foramen → chin
    • Lower lip, chin, pre-auricular

VII via the chorda tympani
