1Terminology, imaging and simulation

Terminology, imaging and simulation

TermMeaning (ILROG)
Pre-chemo GTVDisease evident before systemic therapy (generally PET-positive); drawn on the planning CT from fused or carefully transferred staging imaging
Post-chemo GTVRadiologically evident or biopsy-proven disease after systemic therapy; always part of the CTV
CTVRT alone: GTV + adjacent nodes. Combined modality: post-chemo GTV + the tissue that held the initially involved nodes and infiltrated sites, adjusted for shrinkage; plus specific at-risk sites
Equivocal nodesEnlarged (>1 cm) but PET-negative; normal-size with equivocal uptake; or increased number/asymmetric. Include by clinical context

1.1 Imaging and simulation

  • Best: pre-chemotherapy PET-CT in the treatment position with the planned immobilisation; otherwise fuse and correct. Review PET and structural imaging with a PET physician or radiologist (Wirth).
  • Arms: PET is often done arms up, but treating young women arms down (or akimbo) reduces breast in the field — expect shifts in the axilla and supraclavicular region (Wirth Fig. 2; mini-atlas axilla).
  • Deep-inspiration breath hold for mediastinal disease moves the CTV away from heart and breast but can complicate fusion with free-breathing staging scans (Wirth Fig. 3, 11).
  • After bulky disease shrinks, normal structures move; compare landmarks on staging and planning scans and add an individual radial/cranio-caudal expansion where uncertain.
  • Nodes >5 cm apart can be planned as separate CTVs (Specht 2014).

Section 2 · Scenario A

2Early indolent lymphoma treated with RT alone (FL, nodal MZL, NLPHL)

Early indolent lymphoma treated with RT alone (FL, nodal MZL, NLPHL)

  • GTV: PET-positive nodes plus nearby enlarged or equivocal nodes (especially in low-avidity disease).
  • CTV: GTV + adjacent visible nodes (even if not enlarged); may extend to the boundaries of the involved nodal compartment axially and several cm cranio-caudally, depending on imaging uncertainty.
  • Near salivary glands or breast in young patients, a minimal expansion may be used.
  • Historical series used 3–6 cm field margins with excellent control; smaller margins are usual now.
  • Dose: FL 24–30 Gy (24 Gy equivalent to higher doses in a randomised trial); NLPHL 30 Gy (Specht: 30–35 Gy).

Mini-atlas groin case: stage IA grade 2 FL; GTV, CTV, 8 mm PTV; 24 Gy IMRT sparing marrow, genitalia, bladder and femur.

Section 3 · Scenario B

3Early Hodgkin and DLBCL after limited chemotherapy; mediastinum

Early Hodgkin and DLBCL after limited chemotherapy; mediastinum

  • CTV: the volume of all initially involved nodes, including those that normalised; separate distant sites need not be joined. Original cranio-caudal extent; axially adjusted for shrinkage. Include initially infiltrated lung or chest wall. Consider contiguous equivocal nodes; do not add initially normal nodes.
  • Residual PET-positive lesion = post-chemo GTV; if it sits in a larger PET-negative mass, include the whole CT mass and initial sites.
  • GHSG favourable after 2 ABVD: some use a slightly more generous volume (as for RT alone).
  • Mediastinum: well-defined anatomic boundaries allow precise CTV; give a generous supraclavicular CTV for arm-position change (Wirth Fig. 7). If bulky lower mediastinal or lung/chest wall disease cannot be treated safely even with IMRT/VMAT, DIBH or protons, consider full chemotherapy with a "modified ISRT" to initial bulk, slow responders and residual mass.

Section 4 · Scenario C

4Advanced disease, residual masses, relapsed/refractory

Advanced disease, residual masses, relapsed/refractory

  • After a full course (typically 6 cycles): CTV = any residual GTV plus a subset of sites at higher risk (initial bulk, slow responders).
  • Relapsed/refractory with salvage ± transplant: CTV = some or all relapse sites and selected sites of prior disease. Small cardiophrenic nodes may be omitted after chemotherapy when it would add much heart dose (Wirth).
  • Mini-atlas mesenteric case: consolidation to the site of the original bulky mass, 30 Gy; CTV only where the mass was, accounting for response, so it is smaller than the original tumour.
  • Specht 2014: residual mass after chemotherapy may be more refractory — consider 36–40 Gy.

Extranodal lymphomas track along walls, cavities and planes and move with organs, so ITV/PTV need image guidance and motion management (Wirth). 24–30 Gy is standard for MZL; 4 Gy gives durable control in many and is under trial (Wirth).

Section 6

6Pearls, doses, margins and OARs

6.1 Pearls

  • Draw the pre-chemo GTV on the planning CT first; then subtract what was only displaced normal tissue.
  • Keep the full cranio-caudal extent even when the axial width shrinks.
  • Check arm position: PET arms up vs treatment arms down changes axillary and supraclavicular anatomy.
  • Assess the 5 Gy isodose on breast, heart, lungs, parotids and thyroid, not only the prescription coverage (mini-atlas).
  • Pericardial or pleural extent and displaced vs infiltrated lung are common uncertainties after bulky mediastinal disease (Wirth Fig. 1).

PTV and IGRT: institutional; DIBH and IMRT/VMAT for mediastinal disease; protons where available (Wirth).

ROI workflow
  1. Deformable or rigid fusion of staging PET-CT; draw GTV_PreChemo on the planning CT, correcting for position.
  2. CTV = GTV_PreChemo (axially edited to current anatomy, cranio-caudal extent kept) ∪ GTV_PostChemo − uninvolved displaced organs.
  3. ITV from 4D or breath hold for chest/upper abdomen; PTV = ITV/CTV + institutional margin.
