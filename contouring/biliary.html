Cholangiocarcinoma and gallbladder contouring guide

Target volumes for intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (ablative / SBRT, conventional chemoRT and post-op), perihilar and distal extrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (definitive and post-op) and gallbladder cancer, with the regional nodal areas defined by vascular surrogates. OARs, the central hepatobiliary tract and SABR constraints are in the Liver SBRT OAR guide.

Section 1

1Scope, simulation and imaging

Photon EBRT for biliary tract cancer: intrahepatic (iCCA), perihilar (pCCA), distal (dCCA) cholangiocarcinoma and gallbladder cancer (GBC). Brachytherapy boosts, protons and neoadjuvant chemoRT before transplant are summarised only (Section 7). Hepatocellular carcinoma and liver metastases are not covered here.

1.1 Diagnostic work-up that changes the volume

  • Contrast CT and MRI of the liver. iCCA is usually a mass-forming lesion with irregular but well-defined edges; periductal-infiltrating and intraductal types show duct thickening or ectasia with little mass (Bisello 2019). On CT it is typically low-density with irregular peripheral enhancement (Wang 2022).
  • Extrahepatic tumours spread along the submucosa: papillary, nodular and sclerosing types extend microscopically about 10–16 mm, so imaging under-calls longitudinal extent (Bisello 2019, citing Chang and Ebata).
  • After resection: collect the pre-operative CT/MRI, operation note (type of resection, anastomosis), pathology (margins, nodes by station) and any surgical clips (Goodman 2012).

Section 2 · Scenario A

2Intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma

2.1 Indications

  • Unresectable, localised: ablative RT (SBRT, hypofractionated photons or protons) or conventional chemoRT. BED10 >80.5 Gy: 3-year local control 78% vs 45% and 3-year OS 73% vs 38% (Tao, in Thonglert 2023). In the NCDB, ablative RT gave median OS 23.7 vs 12.8 months (De, in Thonglert 2023).
  • Node-positive unresectable disease: systemic therapy is standard; RT is case by case (Thonglert 2023).
  • After resection: consider chemoRT for positive margins or positive nodes; several institutions extrapolate SWOG S0809 (Wang 2022; ARRO/UW case).

2.3 Why 10 mm

Comparing imaging with resected specimens, microscopic extension beyond the visible iCCA edge was 0.4–8.0 mm; a 9.8 mm expansion of the imaging GTV covered all microscopic disease (Bi, cited by Bisello 2019 and Wang 2022). Ebata found a median spread of 10 mm in margin-positive cases (Wang 2022).

2.4 Step by step (conventional iCCA, cranial → caudal)

  1. Fuse diagnostic MRI (hepatobiliary and portal-venous phases) to the planning CT on the liver, not the spine.
  2. Contour the GTV on every slice, including satellite nodules and any duct thickening or dilated duct segment containing tumour; check sagittal and coronal.
  3. Expand 10 mm (CTV-T); trim off stomach, duodenum, bowel, kidney and chest wall unless invaded; leave it in liver.
  4. Draw the vessel surrogates for the nodal regions in Section 5 (portal vein from confluence to upper pancreas, common hepatic artery, left gastric artery, aorta, posterior pancreaticoduodenal artery) and add the margins; add lesser-curve and cardial regions without expansion.
  5. Union CTV-T and CTV-N; add ITV from the 4D-CT; add the PTV.
  6. For SBRT skip steps 3–4: GTV (+ involved node) → ITV → PTV.

Section 3 · Scenario B

3Perihilar and distal extrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma

3.1 Definitive or neoadjuvant chemoRT

VolumeDefinition (Bisello 2019)
GTVVisible tumour on contrast planning CT with MRI/MRCP fused; include thickened duct wall.
CTV-TGTV + 25 mm proximally along the bile duct, 20 mm distally, and 15 mm radially in all other directions.
CTV-NHepatoduodenal ligament (12), left gastric artery (7), common hepatic artery (8), para-aortic (16), posterior (13) and anterior (17) pancreaticoduodenal, pericholedochal (12b2): margins in Section 5.
CTVCTV-T ∪ CTV-N, kept off duodenum, liver, small bowel and stomach.
PTVCTV + 5 mm, 7 mm superiorly and inferiorly (Wang 2022). CHECK local

Why: microscopic spread in 90% of cases was 15.6 mm (papillary), 10.0 mm (nodular) and 15.6 mm (sclerosing) (Chang); a 20 mm surgical margin was negative proximally in 89% and distally in 94% (Ebata) — both cited by Bisello 2019.

3.2 Post-operative chemoRT

  • Who: pT2–4, node-positive or margin-positive resected eCCA or GBC (SWOG S0809 eligibility; ARRO/UW case; Wang 2022). Start after adjuvant gemcitabine–capecitabine where used; about 8 weeks after surgery is reasonable (Wang 2022).
  • Bed: the tumour bed, the liver resection surface and the bilioenteric anastomosis (hepaticojejunostomy or choledochojejunostomy), individualised to the operation; these were the commonest local relapse sites (liver resection margin 16/29, anastomosis 8/29) after resected perihilar tumours (Park 2023).
  • Nodes: see 3.3. Para-aortic nodes are the commonest regional relapse site (13/58 regional relapses) and the area most often under-covered by experienced contourers (Park 2023; Koo 2022).

Microscopic extension in advanced GBC averaged 15.2 mm (range 2–25 mm) (Ogura, in Bisello 2019), hence 25 mm towards the liver. GBC infiltrates early through the thin subserosa. SWOG S0809 included resected GBC (ARRO/UW case).

Section 8

8Organs at risk and constraints

Contour liver, liver minus GTV (or minus PTV for conventional plans), duodenum, stomach, small and large bowel, kidneys, spinal canal, oesophagus and, for central tumours, the central hepatobiliary tract and common bile duct. Definitions on CT and MRI, the cHBT and the UK 2022 SABR limits (1, 3 and 5 #) are in the Liver SBRT OAR guide (Sections 3, 4 and 8).
