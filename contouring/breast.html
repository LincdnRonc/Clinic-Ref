1.1 DIBH (department)

  • Who: all left-sided patients, and right-sided patients having IMC treatment. Not suitable: dentures, or unable to follow simple English commands.
  • Workflow: RO explains and assesses at clinic; patient practises with a snorkel and watches the DIBH video; ABC training at simulation.
  • ABC criteria: tolerates ABC; breath hold at least 20 s; threshold set at 80% of maximum inspiratory capacity. Patients not meeting all criteria are planned free-breathing.
  • Radiotherapy starts ≥3 weeks after chemotherapy (department).

  • Early disease (e.g. T1–2 N1): ESTRO 2015 / v1.1 borders (department Appendix 5). The ESTRO and RTOG atlases cover high-risk areas equally in early disease, so ESTRO is preferred for its lower toxicity (tips & tricks 2021).

3Whole breast after breast-conserving surgery

Before drawing, look at the simulation wire, the glandular tissue on CT (breast window), the seroma and clips, and (for chest wall) the other breast (department Appendix 4).

Superficial border: the department contours to skin and evaluates on NS volumes; ESTRO stops 5 mm under the skin. Both end with the same evaluated region. This page follows the department.

3.1 Walkthrough

  1. Start on a mid-breast slice where the breast edges are obvious, then work cranially and caudally (tips & tricks 2021).
  2. Cranial slices: stop where breast tissue ends, no higher than the inferior edge of the sternoclavicular joint.
  3. Medially, find the perforating branches of the internal thoracic artery and stay lateral to them.
  4. Laterally, find the lateral thoracic artery; stay in front of it, or on the glandular tissue if that is clear.
  5. Posteriorly, stop on the front of pectoralis major; no ribs or muscle.
  6. Caudal slices: stop where breast shape disappears; in obese patients bring the deep border forward off abdominal fat (lowers heart dose on the left).
  7. Check that the tumour bed and its margin sit inside the breast CTV, especially for upper-inner or very lateral tumours.

3.2 Pitfalls

  • No inframammary fold on CT (small breasts): use the wires carefully; wrong marking makes the CTV and abdominal dose large (tips & tricks 2021).
  • Asymmetric arms or rotation on the board makes the other breast a poor guide; check the humeral heads (tips & tricks 2021).
  • High tangents: in Z0011 about half the patients with evaluable records had high tangents (cranial border ≤2 cm from the humeral head), which irradiate level 1 and part of level 2 (Jagsi 2014). Whole-breast-only patients should not get this by accident.

Section 4 · Scenario B

4Tumour bed, boost and SIB

4.1 Indications and doses (department)

  • Strongly consider a boost for patients aged ≤50 and those with positive or close margins. Prefer SIB (48 Gy with 42.4/16; 60 Gy with 50/25); sequential boost if SIB is unsuitable: 10 Gy/4 or 16 Gy/8, photons or electrons.
  • After oncoplastic surgery, reserve a boost for truly high-risk patients, since the bed may have moved; tell the surgeon before surgery so clips go in before tissue is rotated (Kaidar-Person 2023).

4.2 Tumour bed (department Appendix 6)

  • Use visible seroma, mammogram and ultrasound reports for tumour position, pathology (size and margins), surgical clips and the simulation wire.
  • Seroma or clips easy to see: contour seroma and clips as TUMOURBED.
  • Bed hard to see: ESTRO approach — reconstruct the tumour position and size from the mammogram and pathology, then add 1.5 cm for microscopic spread to make the CTV.

The department for SIB (CTV = tumour bed, PTV 5 mm). When the bed is uncertain or margins are close in one direction, use Boersma's margin-based CTV (15 mm minus the free margin) rather than a uniform expansion. A pre-operative CT in treatment position cut the boost CTV from 42 to 36 cc and reduced observer variation (Boersma 2012).

4.4 Seroma, clips and pitfalls

  • Clips placed on the chest wall mark the surgical bed, not always the tumour bed; decide which clips matter (GEC-ESTRO 2015).
  • The skin scar is on average 4 cm (up to 9 cm) from the clips; do not use the scar alone to place the boost (GEC-ESTRO 2015).
  • Large seromas shrink during treatment; check bed position on IGRT for long courses CHECK departmental IGRT policy.

  • 40 Gy/15 (or 26 Gy/5) partial breast: IMPORT LOW volumes — tumour bed + 15 mm CTV, + 10 mm PTV, both 5 mm off skin. 26 Gy/5 to the partial breast extrapolates FAST-Forward to the IMPORT LOW volume CHECK local and eviQ guidance.
  • 30 Gy/5 alternate days (IMRT/VMAT): Florence volumes — clips + 1 cm CTV, + 1 cm PTV.
  • Avoid 38.5 Gy/10 twice daily (RAPID toxicity and cosmesis).
  • All PBI needs clips (≥4, ideally 6) or a clearly visible cavity; without them no PBI CTV can be drawn (GEC-ESTRO 2015; Florence).

5.1 Building the PBI volume (IMPORT LOW)

  1. Contour the tumour bed around the clips or seeds and any change in tissue architecture.
  2. Expand 15 mm → CTV_PBI; crop 5 mm inside skin and at the pectoral fascia.
  3. Expand 10 mm → PTV_PBI; crop 5 mm inside skin; leave posteriorly.
  4. Check the CTV lies within CTVp_breast.
  5. Coverage: partial breast CTV 95% to receive 95% (IMPORT LOW planning pack).

Intra-operative techniques (TARGIT, ELIOT) have no external-beam contouring step and are not covered. Patient selection: ASTRO 2024 PBI guideline (site library).

Section 6 · Scenario D

6Multicatheter brachytherapy APBI (GEC-ESTRO 2015)

For APBI or boost with interstitial multicatheter brachytherapy after closed-cavity or oncoplastic breast-conserving surgery. Requirements: detailed knowledge of the operation (clips: number and site, scar), the full pathology report with margins in all six directions, and the pre-operative imaging. 4–6 clips marking the lumpectomy are a prerequisite.

6.1 Three surgical situations

SituationReference for the tumour bed
Open cavity (cavity visualisation score 4–5)The seroma cavity
Closed cavity (score 1–3)The scar inside the breast, plus clips and skin scar
OncoplasticLarge tissue shifts; clips may be in different quadrants; CTV large and poorly defined

No visible scar and no clips: no tumour bed or CTV can be drawn.

6.2 Step by step after closed-cavity surgery

  1. CT with a marker on the skin scar.
  2. Contour the clips. Clips inside breast tissue belong to the target; decide which chest-wall clips mark the tumour bed.
  3. Whole scar (WS): the whole visible surgical path inside the breast plus all clips, from skin scar to the deepest point on the chest wall.
  4. ImTV (imaging-related target volume): the tumour projected from the pre-operative mammogram/US/MRI (relative distance to skin and chest wall). Less reliable in large fatty breasts.
  5. ETB (estimated tumour bed): the part of WS that matches the tumour position and size (ImTV), with all clips except irrelevant chest-wall clips.
  6. CTV = ETB + (20 mm − surgical margin), at least 10 mm in each direction (e.g. 3 mm margin → 17 mm). Skin (about 5 mm) and chest wall are never part of the CTV.
  7. PTV: brachytherapy with clearly seen bed and clips — PTV = CTV. If position is uncertain (large breasts, bra ≥38 and/or ≥D cup, absent clips, poor scar visibility), add 5–10 mm in the doubtful region.

Section 8 · Scenario F

8Chest wall after implant reconstruction

The CTV is the subcutaneous lymphatic plexus and any residual glandular tissue in front of the implant, not the implant itself. The implant and transplanted tissue are not CTV (ESTRO-ACROP 2019; department Appendix 4).

Adverse factors (ESTRO-ACROP Table 2; department): pT3 treated by mastectomy and implant; locally advanced disease without pCR after primary systemic therapy; invasion of pectoralis major or chest wall.

8.1 Pitfalls

  • Including the whole implant out of habit treats far more tissue than needed and raises heart and lung dose.
  • The skin over the implant is thin; "5 mm under skin" may leave no CTV. ESTRO-ACROP uses 3–5 mm; with tangents the build-up region is mostly covered except medially and laterally.
  • Department 50 Gy/25 is the option for patients with implants (Section 11).
  • Autologous flap reconstruction is not covered by any source in the folder CHECK for an ESTRO-ACROP guideline on autologous reconstruction.

9.2 Locally advanced disease (department, from RTOG and RADCOMP)

  • Level 4: include the internal carotid region and retroclavicular nodes, and extend back beyond the lateral edge of SCM into the "posterior triangle" (trapezius behind, clavicle laterally, paraspinal muscles below).
  • Level 3: higher cranial limit (up to the pectoralis minor insertion on the coracoid) and lower caudal limit (to where the fat between pectoralis major and minor disappears).
  • Level 1: extend back to the front of subscapularis and latissimus dorsi.
  • Involved nodes: contour as GTVn, add margin and the at-risk regions (CTVn; department radical names, Section 13). Fuse diagnostic PET-CT to place them (tips & tricks 2021).
  • Gross SCF disease: contour the brachial plexus (department).

9.5 Walkthrough, cranial to caudal (ESTRO)

  1. Find the subclavian arch on coronal view; start level 4 one slice above it. Medially include the internal jugular vein (no margin); exclude carotid and thyroid; back to pleura; laterally to anterior scalene.
  2. Follow the subclavian vein laterally behind the clavicle: level 3 medial to pectoralis minor, 5 mm around the vein.
  3. Level 2 behind pectoralis minor, 5 mm around the axillary vein; interpectoral nodes in front of it, behind pectoralis major.
  4. Level 1 lateral to pectoralis minor; stop 1 cm short of the humeral head; caudally to rib 4–5; include the SLNB scar.
  5. IMC: from the bottom of level 4 down to the top of the 4th rib, vein and artery + 5 mm, cropped to lung.
  6. View all volumes in 3D to check they join (ESTRO 2015).
