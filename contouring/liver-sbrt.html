Section 1

1Scope, simulation & imaging for OARs

This protocol covers OAR delineation for photon SBRT/SABR to the liver in 1–5 (occasionally 8) fractions: HCC, intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma and liver metastases, planned on CT (C-arm linac) or on MRI with daily online adaptation (MR-linac). The same OAR set applies to both; Section 5 adds the online-adaptive rules.

1.1 Simulation details that change the OARs

  • Motion management sets which anatomy you contour. Contour OARs on the same breathing state that the plan is delivered in: end-exhale breath-hold or gated scan for gated delivery; the average (or MidV) dataset for free-breathing ITV delivery. On the MR-linac, T2 navigator images show the exhale position, and 3D Vane is closer to the time-averaged position (see MR-linac sequences and signal).
  • Stomach filling. Simulate and treat with the same fasting instruction so that stomach and duodenal position is reproducible; the site's liver technique card recommends an empty stomach (Liver and biliary page). Fasting duration is departmental check.
  • On the MR-linac, a glass of water shortly before each fraction makes the stomach and duodenum bright on TrueFISP and T2 images, and an antiperistaltic (e.g. butylscopolamine) reduces motion artefact (MR-guided liver RT review, Front Oncol 2021). In a pilot pancreatic MRgOART study, interobserver agreement (Dice) for luminal OARs was higher when butylscopolamine was given (Chiba group, Front Oncol 2024). Use only within a departmental protocol check.

1.2 CT

  • Contrast-enhanced, multiphase planning CT (arterial, portal venous and delayed) per the site's Quick reference tab. The portal venous phase is the phase to draw the portal vein (cHBT seed, Section 4), IVC and bowel wall from.
  • The RTOG CT atlas (Jabbour 2014) was written for RTOG 1112 (SBRT then sorafenib vs sorafenib in HCC) on a breath-hold, portal venous phase CT with 3 mm slices, IV contrast and 1000 mL of oral water. Agreement (κ) between expert contours: liver 0.94, stomach 0.90, duodenum 0.87, oesophagus 0.62, CBD 0.42, GOJ 0.41. Expect the most variation, and spend the most care, on the GOJ and CBD, and on the caudate and D4, which were often missed.

1.3 MRI (diagnostic fusion and MR-linac)

  • The MRI upper abdominal OAR atlas (Lukovic 2020, Princess Margaret–led international panel) was contoured on two diagnostic sequences — gadoxetic acid T1 in the hepatobiliary phase and axial balanced steady-state free precession (TrueFISP type) — plus two clinical sequences from commercial MR-linac systems.
  • Compared with CT, MRI showed the liver and biliary tree best on hepatobiliary-phase gadoxetic T1, and intrahepatic and upper abdominal vessels more clearly. The bile duct and biliary tree are highlighted as structures better seen on MRI than CT.
  • The full DICOM and DICOM-RT data sets (diagnostic and MR-linac) are free for teaching at econtour.org. Use them to calibrate new contourers and RTTs.
  • Register diagnostic MRI to the planning scan on the liver, not bone, in the matching breathing phase.
Structure-naming convention used throughout

OAR names follow AAPM TG-263 (Section 9): capitalised first letter, no spaces, underscores between categories, at most 16 characters, and a z prefix for planning helpers that are not clinical structures (e.g. z_cHBT15).

Departmental decision

The RTOG panel recommends contouring the entire duodenum, because partial contours make dose–volume limits hard to compare. For example, the Stanford pancreatic SBRT duodenal model contoured only 1 cm beyond the PTV cranio-caudally and 3 cm radially. The extent for small and large bowel and the oesophagus is not fixed by the sources: set a departmental rule so D5cc/D10cc metrics are comparable between patients.

Two lesions, one liver

When two liver GTVs are treated in one course, assess constraints on the summed plan. UK 2022 recommends 5 fractions for multiple liver lesions, meeting the single-lesion constraints, with at least 40 hours between treatments.

3.1 Liver contour rules that change the numbers

  • GTV out. Liver−GTV (or liver minus the sum of GTVs) is the structure for mean dose and D≥700 cc; much of the liver dose–volume and NTCP work used it (HyTEC).
  • Caudate in, gallbladder out. These are the two commonest liver contour errors (Jabbour 2014).
  • Simple vessel rule. Keep intrahepatic vessels inside the liver. Remove the main PV only where the caudate tail separates it from the IVC (Jabbour Fig 1).
  • Same phase as the dose calculation. Draw the liver on the dataset used for dose, not the arterial phase used to define an HCC.
  • Cirrhosis. Small inconsistencies in the liver contour matter more when the spared volume is close to 700 cc (HyTEC).

3.2 Couinaud planes (Jabbour 2014)

  • Right vs left lobe: vertical plane through the gallbladder fossa and middle hepatic vein.
  • Right anterior (V, VIII) vs posterior (VI, VII): vertical plane through the right hepatic vein.
  • Left lateral (II, III) vs medial (IV): left hepatic vein and falciform ligament.
  • Superior (VII, VIII) vs inferior (V, VI): plane of the main right and left portal veins.
  • Segment I (caudate) is part of the left lobe and sits between the PV and IVC.

Order of work
  1. Liver first (auto-contour allowed, then edit), then Liver−GTV by ROI algebra.
  2. Stomach and duodenum as one continuous tube from GOJ to DJ flexure, then split at the pylorus.
  3. Bowel loops in the dose region, tracing each loop slice to slice.
  4. Portal vein seed → cHBT (if indicated) → CBD on fused MRI.
  5. Kidneys, canal, heart, chest wall, vessels as indicated by lesion position (Section 2.2).
  6. Review every luminal OAR in coronal and sagittal before sign-off.
Auto-contours

Auto-contours must be checked manually for every patient (UK 2022). Luminal GI organs and the duodenum–jejunum boundary are where auto-segmentation most often needs editing.

Section 5

5MR-linac: online re-contouring

There is no consensus MR-linac contouring guideline for liver OARs. The MRI atlas (Lukovic 2020) defines the organs; how much to re-contour each day comes from trial protocols and planning studies. ESTRO-ACROP's MR-linac implementation recommendations (Corradini 2021) note that re-contouring time is limited with the patient on the table and that departments should supply contouring guidelines and train RTTs on the online tools.

5.1 How far from the PTV to re-contour — named positions

This page follows: re-contour luminal GI OARs (stomach, duodenum, small and large bowel, oesophagus) and the CBD inside z_Recontour = PTV + 3 cm radial and 2 cm cranio-caudal (single-fraction SMART protocol). Outside the zone, accept the propagated contour. For parallel organs, edit Liver−GTV and kidneys only where the edge has moved near the PTV. Evidence comes from pancreas SMART, so the liver default needs departmental sign-off.

5.2 Practical online rules

  • Start from the reference. Rigid registration on the liver, not bone, then edit the propagated contours.
  • Match the image to the delivery. For gated exhale delivery edit on T2 nav; for free-breathing ITV delivery check position on 3D Vane (site sequences page).
  • Gas and bulk density. Daily gas in stomach and bowel changes dose; follow the departmental bulk-density override rule (site adaptive workflow page).
  • Know what drives adaptation. In the SMART trial, a plan was adapted when >1 cc of OAR fell inside the 33 Gy isodose (5 fx).
  • RTTs can contour online after structured training; in one Unity prostate study, certified MR-linac RTTs trained offline and online for 4–6 weeks.
Physics / clinical review

Confirm the zone size, which OARs are always re-contoured (e.g. duodenum even beyond the zone in hilar lesions) and the bulk-density convention before adopting this section.

Liver-specific conditions (UK 2022)

Liver constraints apply only to Child-Pugh A6 or better. For HCC, treat the 3-fraction optimal mean liver dose as mandatory. Constraints apply to SABR alone (not re-irradiation); recent or concurrent anti-angiogenic, small-molecule or immunotherapy may raise toxicity risk.

9.3 Registration & QA

  • Diagnostic MRI → planning CT: rigid registration on the liver in the matching breathing phase; check at the hilum (portal vein and CBD) and at the lesion.
  • Peer review of OARs (with targets) before planning; auto-contours always edited and approved by a clinician (UK 2022).
  • On the MR-linac, keep the reference plan's OAR names identical to the online template so that propagation and DVH evaluation work.
  • Record in the plan which constraint set was used (UK 2022, eviQ, trial) and whether a cHBT was evaluated.
