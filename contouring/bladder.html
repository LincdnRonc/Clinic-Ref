Bladder contouring protocol

Bladder preservation (chemoradiation or radiotherapy alone) for muscle-invasive bladder cancer: whole-bladder CTV and what to add to it, the high-risk CTV for an integrated boost, involved and elective pelvic nodes, plan of the day and PTV; and adjuvant radiotherapy after radical cystectomy with the international consensus cystectomy bed and nodal borders. Doses, constraints and names.

GTVp / GTVn CTV high risk (boost) CTV whole bladder / nodes PTV (anisotropic)

Section 1

1Scope, preparation, simulation and imaging

1.1 Scope

Muscle-invasive urothelial bladder cancer treated with curative bladder preservation (trimodality therapy: maximal safe TURBT, then chemoradiation or radiotherapy with a radiosensitiser) and adjuvant pelvic radiotherapy after radical cystectomy. Palliative bladder radiotherapy is outside the scope; see the bladder cancer page.

1.2 Before simulation (FROGG Guidelines 1–3)

StepFROGG recommendation
MDT and consultsEvery MIBC case at an MDT; the patient sees a urologist, radiation oncologist and medical oncologist so that bladder preservation and cystectomy (± neoadjuvant chemotherapy) are both offered. In the ANZ survey only 37% said new MIBC is always discussed at MDT, and only 10% were referred fit, operable patients "most of the time".
Cystoscopy and TURBTExamination under anaesthesia and safe maximal TURBT. If biopsy-proven MIBC and macroscopic clearance is not expected, or a re-look TURBT would delay treatment, consider going straight to bladder preservation.
StagingCT chest, abdomen and pelvis and a whole-body bone scan. MRI (T2 and DWI) can be considered for local extent; FDG-PET for regional and distant disease.
Who suits preservationFavourable: no or limited CIS, unifocal tumour, minimal macroscopic tumour after TURBT. Cystectomy preferred with widespread CIS, multifocal tumours or gross residual disease after TURBT, poor baseline bladder function, or relative contraindications to RT or chemotherapy. Preservation preferred with high operative risk or patient preference.
Medical oncologyAll preservation patients seen for concurrent chemotherapy (fluorouracil + mitomycin C, or cisplatin). Neoadjuvant chemotherapy when RT will be delayed or disease is locally advanced. Carbogen and nicotinamide if chemotherapy is contraindicated.

1.3 Simulation (FROGG Guidelines 4 and 5)

  • Position and immobilisation: supine with knee and foot support as for prostate CHECK (not specified in the sources).
  • Bladder: empty or comfortably full, but reproducible every day. A partially or comfortably full bladder from the start can be used with an integrated boost; a second phase with a full bladder is occasionally needed to meet OAR limits. Department choice CHECK.
  • Fluids: restrict oral and IV fluids and diuretics in the 2–4 h before simulation and each fraction; avoid chemotherapy given with fluid loading or diuretics immediately before planning or treatment.
  • Rectum: department bowel preparation CHECK.
  • Contrast: not specified by the sources CHECK.
  • Imaging to fuse: the pre-TURBT CT and MRI (T1, T2, DWI) for the GTV and the TURBT bed; PET for nodes. In the ANZ survey 36% used MRI and 46% PET for target definition.
  • Fiducials: cystoscopic gold seeds or lipiodol injection can help delineation and image guidance (FROGG 5.4). Ask the urologist at TURBT.
  • Cystoscopy map: obtain the urologist's tumour map and the operation note; they decide the high-risk CTV when no tumour is visible.
  • Plan of the day: if used, acquire the extra CT scans for the plan library at simulation (number and timing CHECK).
  • Post-cystectomy: simulate and treat with an empty ostomy bag where possible (Baumann 2016).

Section 3

3Scenario A — whole-bladder CTV (bladder preservation)

3.1 Definitions (FROGG Table 5)

VolumeDefinition
GTVpResidual tumour after TURBT on CT ± MRI, plus any gross extravesical spread.
CTV (whole bladder)The whole bladder, including the GTV, with a 5 mm margin on gross extravesical extension seen on CT (MRI may help if experienced). No margin is added to the rest of the bladder wall.
CTV extensions
  • Bladder neck or trigone involved, CIS or multifocal disease → add the proximal 1–2 cm of urethra.
  • Positive proximal prostatic urethral biopsies → add the whole prostatic urethra.
  • Direct prostate invasion (T4) → add the whole prostate.
  • Ureteric orifice involved → add the distal 1.0 cm of the ureter.
PTVCTV + an institution- and IGRT-specific anisotropic margin (section 8).

3.2 Borders of the whole-bladder CTV

BorderDescription (anatomy of "whole bladder")
CranialOuter wall of the dome on the planning CT; filling changes are handled by the PTV or plan library, not the CTV.
CaudalBladder neck (internal urethral orifice), or lower to include 1–2 cm urethra, the prostatic urethra or prostate by the rules above.
AnteriorOuter anterior wall; the retropubic fat is not CTV unless there is gross extravesical extension (then + 5 mm).
PosteriorOuter posterior wall and trigone; seminal vesicles, rectum, vagina or uterus only if invaded (then they are GTV + 5 mm).
LateralOuter lateral walls; perivesical fat only around gross extravesical extension.

FROGG defines the CTV by organ, not by a border table; this table spells out the outer-wall rule.

3.3 Slice by slice (cranial to caudal)

  1. Above the dome: confirm the top of the bladder on sagittal and coronal views; bowel loops lying on the dome are the main OAR (section 9).
  2. Dome and body: outline the outer wall. Where the GTV breaches the wall, draw the extravesical tumour as GTV and add 5 mm into the perivesical fat only there.
  3. Ureteric orifices and trigone: if the tumour involves an orifice, follow the ureter 1 cm proximally (it runs posterolaterally and upward from the trigone).
  4. Bladder neck: stop at the internal urethral orifice unless the neck/trigone is involved or there is CIS or multifocal disease (then add 1–2 cm of urethra); in men the prostatic urethra or prostate by biopsy and staging.

Section 4

4Scenario B — high-risk CTV, integrated boost and dose escalation

FROGG recommends a single phase with IMRT/VMAT, either uniform dose or a simultaneous integrated boost. Dose escalation is investigational (prospective data collection or a trial, e.g. the ICR/TROG RAIDER trial of adaptive standard-dose or escalated RT), and is reserved for patients without widespread field change or multifocal tumours.

Scenario B — high-risk CTV, integrated boost and dose escalation

GTVpResidual tumour on CT and MRI (T1, T2, DWI help) plus gross extravesical disease.
CTV high riskGTV + 5 mm, edited to anatomical boundaries. If there is no visible tumour after complete TURBT: the resected tumour bed, as defined by the pre-TURBT CT, MRI or PET (fiducials or lipiodol help).
CTV intermediate riskThe whole bladder CTV of section 3.
PTVsHigh-risk and whole-bladder PTVs with institution-specific margins CHECK (the high-risk PTV margin is not given in the sources).

So in an escalated plan the whole bladder drops to 50 Gy/20 (60 Gy/32) and the standard prescription moves to the high-risk PTV.

4.2 The small-bowel question decides whether escalation is possible

FROGG set small-bowel limits by how close bowel lies to the escalated volume (expert opinion at the 2019 meeting, aimed at about a 5% risk of grade ≥3 toxicity at 5 years). If anatomy is unfavourable and small-bowel dose is high, rediscuss cystectomy with the patient.

FROGG Tables 13 and 14. The text calls Table 14 "64 Gy in 34 fractions" while the table title says 32 fractions CHECK; this page uses 32, matching the standard schedule.

4.3 Pitfalls

  • Drawing the high-risk CTV on the post-TURBT CT alone when the tumour has been resected: the bed must come from pre-TURBT imaging and the cystoscopy map.
  • Escalating with multifocal disease or CIS: FROGG limits escalation to unifocal disease without field change.
  • Letting the boost PTV run into a bowel loop at the dome: apply the overlap table and accept lower coverage, or do not escalate.

Section 5

5Scenario C — pelvic nodes (involved and elective)

5.1 When to treat nodes (FROGG Guideline 10)

  • Node-positive, confined to the pelvis: bladder preservation can be offered to fit patients (5-year survival about 29% historically). Treat involved nodes to the GTV dose where tolerances allow, plus elective bilateral pelvic nodes with concurrent radiosensitising therapy; neoadjuvant chemotherapy can be considered.
  • Clinically node-negative: elective nodal irradiation adds toxicity with no proven benefit (a 230-patient randomised trial showed no effect on disease-free or overall survival or bladder preservation). It remains optional.
  • If nodes are treated: presacral nodes and bilateral distal common iliac, external iliac, internal iliac and obturator nodes, contoured as in the post-cystectomy consensus (Baumann 2016), which mirrors the RTOG pelvic nodal atlas for high-risk prostate cancer (Lawton 2009).

5.2 Nodal CTV build (Baumann 2016)

RegionDefinition and borders
Iliac vessels (contour first)Common, external and internal iliac vessels from L5–S1. External iliac down to the top of the femoral heads. Internal iliac down until no longer visible on CT or until it leaves the true pelvis through the greater sciatic notch.
Iliac nodal CTVVessels + 7 mm anteriorly, posteriorly and laterally; no superior or inferior expansion.
PresacralL5–S1 to the top of S3; 1–1.5 cm of tissue in front of the sacrum, between the vessel contours.
ObturatorA 1 cm wide strip medial to obturator internus, from the anterior to the posterior border of the ilium; from the inferior end of the iliac vessel contours down to the top of the pubic symphysis.
Combined CTVnUnion of iliac, presacral and obturator volumes. Trim out of muscle and bone and keep it inside the true pelvis. Do not crop it out of bowel: the pelvic side wall is the main site of failure.
Involved node (GTVn)Visible node on CT/PET; treated to the GTV dose (FROGG 10.3). Margin from GTVn to CTV not given CHECK.

For bladder preservation FROGG names the distal common iliac nodes; the Baumann vessel contours start at L5–S1, which in practice is the distal common iliac segment. FROGG does not give a separate cranial border for preservation CHECK.

Section 6

6Scenario D — adjuvant RT after radical cystectomy

The international consensus (Baumann 2016; 15 institutions in 5 countries; contours adopted for NRG-GU001) chose the targets from a prior patterns-of-failure analysis (Baumann 2013): failures after cystectomy cluster in the pelvic side-wall nodes and, when margins are positive, the cystectomy bed. The language was validated externally by a third group of six radiation oncologists.

Post-cystectomy nodes, organs at risk and reproducibility

6.2 Nodal CTV

Exactly as section 5.2: iliac vessels from L5–S1 + 7 mm (not superior or inferior), presacral L5–S1 to the top of S3 (1–1.5 cm in front of the sacrum), obturator strip 1 cm wide down to the top of the symphysis. Trim to the true pelvis, out of muscle and bone; not out of bowel.

Section 7

7Pearls and common errors

Bladder preservation

  • Same bladder every day: the aim is little day-to-day variation so margins can be tight. Fluid and diuretic restriction for 2–4 h is part of the prescription; tell the patient why.
  • Get the pre-TURBT imaging: without it, and without a cystoscopy map or fiducials, the TURBT bed (high-risk CTV) cannot be drawn reliably.
  • The 5 mm margin is only for extravesical disease: do not add it around the whole bladder.
  • Urethra and ureter extensions follow rules: neck/trigone, CIS or multifocal → 1–2 cm urethra; positive prostatic urethral biopsy → prostatic urethra; T4 prostate → prostate; ureteric orifice → 1 cm ureter.
  • Bowel on the dome is what usually limits dose, not the rectum. Look before choosing escalation, and rediscuss cystectomy if the small-bowel limits cannot be met.
  • Daily CBCT: match so the bladder sits inside the PTV, or match to the highest-risk part of the bladder; both are feasible and safe (FROGG 8.1, Whalley 2015).
  • Keep treatment short: start 20-fraction courses on a Monday so they finish on a Friday; split courses worsen local control.

After cystectomy

  • Margin status chooses the volume: negative → nodes; positive → nodes + presacral + bed.
  • Never crop the nodal CTV out of bowel; the side wall is where it fails.
  • Keep the nodes inside the true pelvis and out of muscle and bone.
  • Iliac expansion is 7 mm in-plane only: no superior or inferior growth, so the vessel contours themselves set the cranial and caudal limits.
  • Obturator strip starts where the iliac vessel contours end and stops at the top of the symphysis.
  • Contour the diversion and the stoma bag: avoid beams through the bag; an empty bag at simulation and treatment.
  • Bed inferior border differs by sex: penile bulb (men) versus obturator foramen + 1 cm (women).

8.3 IGRT and adaptive RT (FROGG Guideline 8)

  • Daily CBCT with a soft-tissue match before every fraction.
  • Plan of the day: a library of plans made in advance; the best fit is chosen on each day's CBCT. Online or offline adaptive RT is endorsed. TROG 10.01 BOLART showed online adaptive RT is feasible across centres.
  • VMAT to shorten beam-on time and limit filling during delivery.
  • Fiducials (gold seeds, lipiodol) can be matched to the high-risk area.

9.3 Follow-up after preservation (FROGG Guideline 11)

  • CT chest, abdomen and pelvis at 3, 6, 12 and 24 months.
  • Fit for salvage cystectomy: cystoscopy every 3 months for 2 years, every 6 months for the next 2 years, then yearly.
  • Not fit for salvage cystectomy: still follow up; superficial isolated recurrence can be managed cystoscopically. FROGG endorses the NICE follow-up guideline.

10.1 ROI algebra

Bladder preservation
  1. Bladder = outer wall (manual, on the planning CT)
  2. zEVE+5 = gross extravesical GTV + 5 mm − bone
  3. CTV_5500 = Bladder ∪ GTVp ∪ zEVE+5 ∪ (urethra 1–2 cm / prostatic urethra / prostate / distal ureter 1 cm, as indicated)
  4. CTV_5700 (escalation) = GTVp + 5 mm, edited to anatomy (or TURBT bed)
  5. PTV_5500 = CTV + anisotropic margin (e.g. sup/ant 15 mm, others 10 mm) CHECK
Nodes and post-cystectomy
  1. zIliacVessels = CIA (from L5–S1), EIA (to femoral head tops), IIA (to sciatic notch)
  2. CTVn_Iliac = zIliacVessels + 7 mm (ant, post, left, right; 0 sup/inf) − muscle − bone, kept in the true pelvis
  3. CTVn_Pelvis = CTVn_Iliac ∪ CTVn_PreSacral ∪ CTVn_Obt (no bowel subtraction)
  4. CTV_Total = CTVn_Pelvis ∪ CTV_CystBed (positive margins)
  5. Bowel bag from 3 cm above CTVn_Pelvis
  • Registration: pre-TURBT MRI and PET rigid to the planning CT on pelvic bone, then check the bladder outline and fiducials; bladder shape differs between scans, so draw the CTV on the planning CT.
  • Plan of the day: label each library plan with its filling state and record the selection rule for the therapists CHECK.
