Radiation Oncology · Lower Gastrointestinal

Rectal long-course atlas

A step-by-step CTV / GTV delineation protocol for definitive and neoadjuvant long-course rectal radiotherapy, with explicit high-dose (50–54 Gy) versus elective (45 Gy) volume rules, chemotherapy-response adaptation, and schematic atlas figures.

This atlas covers conventionally fractionated long-course treatment. Two dose levels are described throughout: an 45 Gy elective nodal volume and a 50–54 Gy high-dose volume covering gross disease, delivered either by sequential boost or simultaneous integrated boost (SIB). Dose escalation above 54 Gy (organ-preservation / watch-and-wait) is summarised separately in Step 7.

Synthesises ESTRO/ESGAR consensus (Valentini 2016 · Chiloiro 2026), RAPIDO Appendix F, the Dutch/SAR nodal criteria and Lateral Node Consortium, and the rectal dose-escalation / organ-preservation trial literature. Clinical reference document — verify against your local guide, planning system and MDT before use.

Overview

================= STEP 0 =================

0Before you contour — imaging & fusion

Required source information

  • Pelvic MRI is mandatory for defining gross disease and is the gold standard for primary staging and restaging. The minimum protocol is 2D T2-weighted sequences in three planes, with a high-resolution oblique-axial T2 angled perpendicular to the tumour axis (slice ≤3 mm), plus an axial high-b-value DWI (b≥800) read against the ADC map.
  • For distal tumours, add a coronal sequence aligned to the anal canal to assess the tumour–sphincter relationship.
  • Do not use endorectal filling / gel for the diagnostic MRI — luminal distension distorts the tumour–mesorectal-fascia (MRF) distance and gel causes T2 shine-through on DWI. (A separate planning consideration: matching rectal/bladder filling between diagnostic MRI and CT-sim improves transfer of the GTV onto CT.)
  • CT simulation: supine or prone (belly board optional), comfortably full bladder, empty rectum, with a reproducible immobilisation. IV contrast helps define the iliac vessels that anchor the nodal CTV.
  • FDG-PET/CT is not routine — reserve it as a problem-solver for large/equivocal nodes; it does not define a biological target volume here.
Fusion is the foundation
Co-register the diagnostic MRI to the planning CT before contouring gross disease — a non-angulated T2 sequence registers most reliably. The GTV is read off MRI and transferred to CT; the elective CTV is then built on CT anatomy. Account for organ-motion mismatch between the two scans when judging the registration.

Stage, locate, measure

From MRI + endoscopy record: distance of the lower tumour edge from the anal verge and anorectal junction; craniocaudal length; cT and cN; MRF status (involved if tumour ≤1 mm from fascia); EMVI; relation to the peritoneal reflection; and every suspicious node by compartment, short-axis size and morphology (criteria in Step 3).

================= STEP 1 =================

1Compartment map — the seven elective subsites

The elective nodal CTV is assembled from a defined set of pelvic subsites. Whether each is included depends on tumour height, depth and MRF/nodal status. The mesorectum, presacral space and posterior lateral nodes are always included; the rest are conditional.

================= STEP 2 =================

2Cranial & caudal borders of the elective CTV

Cranial & caudal borders of the elective CTV

Figure 2 · Sagittal craniocaudal ladder
Cranial border is nodal-status driven. The default elective top sits around S1–S2 / the superior rectal artery (SRA) bifurcation; it is raised to ≥1 cm above the most cranial pathological presacral or internal-iliac node. Caudal extent depends on tumour height and canal involvement.

Practical border rules

  • Cranial (default): bifurcation of the superior rectal artery for cT3N0 / MRF-negative; otherwise the common-/internal-iliac bifurcation level (typically S1–S2, occasionally above L5–S1). Local recurrence above S1–S2 is unusual, so do not routinely extend higher unless nodal disease drives it.
  • Cranial (nodal-driven): if a pathological presacral or internal-iliac node sits high, carry the relevant compartment to ≥1 cm above the most cranial involved node (≥5 mm specifically quoted for abdominal presacral nodes). This is the key modification for a high tumour with high mesorectal/presacral nodes.
  • Caudal: include node-bearing mesorectum to ~4 cm below gross disease. Whole mesorectum to the pelvic floor for low tumours; spare the most distal mesorectum/sphincters for high tumours.
  • Lateral: internal-iliac chain to just below the internal/external iliac bifurcation; obturator added for sub-peritoneal-reflection / ≤8–10 cm tumours.
  • Posterior: abut — do not carve into — the anterior sacral cortex; QA programmes repeatedly flag erroneous editing of the elective CTV away from sacrum/OARs.
================= STEP 3 =================

3Gross tumour volume — GTVp and GTVn

GTV is read primarily from MRI. It comprises the primary tumour (GTVp) and, where relevant, pathological nodes (GTVn) and tumour deposits. Structure naming follows ICRU / AAPM TG-263.

GTVp

  • All macroscopic primary tumour on T2 MRI (high T2 signal), correlated with DWI and endoscopy/EUA. Transfer to CT via fusion.
  • Mucinous tumours: persistent/expanding pure-mucin pools are not necessarily viable tumour — judge on serial imaging.

GTVn — which nodes count (Dutch / ESGAR criteria)

Node groupTreat as positive (GTVn) when…
Mesorectal / superior rectal / IMAShort axis ≥9 mm; or 5–8 mm with ≥2 suspicious features; or <5 mm with ≥3 features; or any size if mucinous. Features = irregular border, heterogeneous signal, round shape.
Lateral pelvic (internal iliac / obturator)Lateral Node Consortium emphasises size: short axis ≥7 mm in cT3/4 tumours <8 cm from the verge carries increased post-CRT lateral recurrence risk. Report all visible lateral nodes with location + short axis.
DWI / DCE and PET for nodes
Do not rely on DWI or DCE-MRI to call nodes positive — DWI highlights benign and malignant nodes alike and quantitative ADC has limited discrimination. FDG-PET/CT is a problem-solver for large/equivocal nodes only.
================= STEP 4 =================

4From GTV to high-dose CTV (50–54 Gy)

The high-dose volume covers gross disease plus a margin for microscopic spread. There is no single universally-agreed GTV→CTV margin in the dose-escalation literature (reported 0–2 cm); the operational consensus is a directional, compartment-respecting expansion rather than a uniform ball.

From GTV to high-dose CTV (50–54 Gy)

CTV high-dose

If the tumour is entirely within the mesorectum with no threatened MRF, the 2 cm need not cross the fascia. If the tumour invades sacrum/bladder, the 2 cm applies within that organ.

Nodal high-dose (CTVn): a margin of ~0.5 cm from GTVn may be used (ESTRO 2026).

Sequential vs SIB
Both are acceptable; there is no strict guidance. SIB is preferred for dose escalation and spares most OARs in dosimetric comparison, while sequential boost better facilitates mid-treatment adaptive replanning. A typical SIB delivers 45 Gy/25 to the elective PTV with a simultaneous 50–54 Gy to the boost PTV; a typical sequential schedule gives 45 Gy/25 then a 5.4–9 Gy boost (RAPIDO permits 1.8–2 Gy × 2–4).
Figure 3 · Nested target concept (axial)
Each CTV is grown to its own PTV by the institutional setup/internal-motion margin (commonly 5–10 mm; tighter with daily IGRT/online correction). The 2 cm boost margin is predominantly cranio-caudal and stays within the involved compartment.
================= STEP 5 =================

5PTV, OARs & verification

PTV, OARs & verification

PTV

  • CTV → PTV by local setup + internal-motion margin, typically 5–10 mm.
  • Generate PTV45 and PTV50–54 separately (SIB) or sequentially.
  • Rectal/bladder filling is the dominant internal-motion source — standardise it.

Organs at risk

  • Small & large bowel / bowel bag, bladder, femoral heads, anal sphincter/canal, external genitalia, cauda equina, pelvic bone marrow.
  • For escalation, anal canal and uninvolved rectal wall become priority OARs.

Verification

  • Daily IGRT (CBCT). RAPIDO specifies imaging ≥2× in week 1 then weekly for long-course; online correction strongly recommended where available.
  • Acceptable residual error must fit your chosen CTV→PTV margin.
================= STEP 6 =================

6Modifying for chemotherapy response

Tumour shrinks fast and early under (chemo)radiation — ~26%/week initially, slowing later; residual volume ~46% at end of CRT and ~32% at surgery. This kinetics has two distinct implications depending on whether chemo is concurrent or given as induction before RT.

Modifying for chemotherapy response

ScenarioHow to define gross volumes
Concurrent chemo-RT
(standard long-course)		 Plan on the pre-treatment (baseline) extent. Do not shrink the GTV/boost to interim appearance — regression occurs during the same course and microscopic disease persists in the regressed bed. Elective compartments are anatomical and unchanged by response.
Induction (neoadjuvant) chemo, then RT
(TNT induction arm)		 Contour the boost on the baseline pre-chemotherapy gross extent and location, fused with the planning CT — analogous to head-and-neck induction practice (pre-induction GTV defines the target; the post-induction volume only re-shapes the contour if a dramatic response leaves the original volume in air/outside the body). Elective CTV stays defined by the baseline stage/nodal map: nodes that were high or borderline at diagnosis still drive the cranial border even if they have regressed.
Consolidation chemo after CRT Does not change RT volumes (delivered after RT). Relevant only to restaging/response assessment for organ preservation.
Adaptive / response-guided escalation Interim MRI (≈10th fraction, or BEDα/β=5≈25 Gy / end of week 2) can stratify responders and trigger an adaptive boost in poor responders. If you adapt, escalate onto residual disease while keeping elective coverage at baseline extent.

Post-chemo MRI — how the picture changes

Always read the restaging MRI side-by-side with baseline, on the same sequences. The central task is separating residual viable tumour from treatment fibrosis.

StructureBaselineAfter CRT — classical change
Primary tumour Intermediate T2, restricted diffusion (DWI bright / ADC dark). Fibrosis = low T2 signal (dark, like muscle), no diffusion restriction. Residual tumour = persistent intermediate T2 signal within/through the fibrosis, often with focal DWI restriction. ymrT is scored on the residual intermediate-signal component.
mrTRG pattern — Graded like pathological TRG: TRG1 = near-complete low-signal fibrosis / normalised wall (best); through to TRG5 = no signal change (worst). Predominantly dark, layered wall = good response.
Mucinous tumour Very high T2 (fluid-like). Caveat: persistent or even enlarging pure-mucin pools (acellular mucin) are not necessarily viable tumour — do not over-call progression. Judge on serial change and any solid/restricting nodule within the mucin.
Nodes Sized + morphology criteria (Step 3). Re-measure short axis. Mesorectal nodes that shrink and lose malignant features down-stage; nodes with persistent malignant morphology and short axis ≥5 mm are still considered involved. Lateral nodes regress less reliably than mesorectal — persistent lateral nodes are clinically important.
cCR / near-CR — No visible tumour on T2 with restored normal wall layers, or only thin residual fibrosis confined to the wall, and no suspicious DWI restriction (plus normalised mucosa on endoscopy). Drives watch-and-wait decisions.
DWI pitfalls after CRT
Post-treatment inflammation and the anal canal/normal wall can show bright DWI — always correlate DWI with the T2 morphology and the ADC map, and against baseline. Use DWI to find residual disease, not to define its border in isolation.
Figure 4 · Tumour vs fibrosis after CRT (axial T2)
Good response = the intermediate-signal tumour is replaced by dark, low-T2 fibrosis. Any nodule of persistent intermediate signal with diffusion restriction within that fibrosis is treated as residual tumour for ymrT scoring and (if escalating/adapting) for the residual-disease target. Schematic only.
Core principle
Chemotherapy response re-shapes the boost/GTV conversation, never the elective compartments. The 45 Gy elective CTV is anatomy- and baseline-stage-driven and is not down-sized because a tumour or node regressed. When in doubt, anchor gross volumes to the maximal pre-treatment extent.
================= STEP 7 =================

7Dose escalation >54 Gy (organ preservation)

Distinct from the 50–54 Gy boost above. ESTRO 2026 defines escalation as any dose >54 Gy, indicated chiefly for mid–low rectal tumours near the verge where surgery means a stoma, and for comorbid/unfit patients pursuing watch-and-wait.

Dose escalation >54 Gy (organ preservation)

  • Dose–response: ~50–60+ Gy (EQD2) needed for meaningful complete-response rates; reported cCR up to ~78% with high-dose schedules.
  • For tumours within 6 cm of the verge, consider 60–62 Gy via endoluminal contact X-ray brachytherapy (CXB) or HDR endorectal brachytherapy, or MR-guided adaptive RT — expertise/equipment dependent.
  • ESMO 2025 recognises endorectal brachytherapy after standard CRT as a validated escalation strategy in selected early cT2–cT3 tumours <5 cm pursuing non-operative management.
  • Nodal dose escalation has no consensus and no prospective support — boost the primary, not the nodes, outside a trial.
