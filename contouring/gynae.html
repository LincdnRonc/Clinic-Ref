Section 1

1Scope, preparation, simulation and imaging

1.1 Before simulation (department)

  • Stage with FIGO 2018 and TNM 8; record the date and type of surgery. A clinical drawing of the EUA findings, by the gynae-oncologist or RO, is scanned into MOSAIQ (repeat it at every brachytherapy insertion).
  • Discuss at the gynae MDT; consider a trial; check chemotherapy suitability (pre-chemo bloods include FBC, EUC, CMP, LFT and hepatitis B serology ± HIV).
  • Radical cervix chemoRT + brachytherapy is booked as category A: 5 days a week, treat on machine down days, no twice-daily catch-up.
  • All gynae patients follow the bladder and bowel protocol (Benefiber started 3 days before CT; low-gas diet; empty bladder and bowel, then drink 500 mL within 10 minutes, 1 hour before CT and each treatment).

1.3 Contouring workflow (EMBRACE II figure 9.10)

  1. On the MRI (full bladder, then empty bladder): GTV-T, normal cervix, uterus, vagina, parametria, ± mesorectum; then CTV-T HR and CTV-T LR for each scan.
  2. On the planning CT (full bladder): copy GTV-N to CTV-N and adjust to the CT node; draw CTV-E; build ITV-T LR from both CTV-T LRs plus margin.
  3. Join ITV-T LR and CTV-E as ITV45; grow PTV45 (5 mm) and PTV-N (5 mm).
  4. OARs on CT (radiation therapists, auto-contours edited), plus the whole vagina for PIBS points.
  5. Registrar contours are prefixed REG_; replan structures RP_.

2.2 What counts as a pathological node (EMBRACE II Table 9.2)

  • FDG-PET positive; and/or short axis ≥1 cm on CT or MRI; and/or short axis 0.5–1.0 cm on MRI with an irregular border, high signal or round shape.

3.3 ITV-T LR and PTV (EMBRACE II; department)

  1. CTV-T LR = CTV-T LR_FB ∪ CTV-T LR_EB.
  2. ITV-T LR = CTV-T LR + individualised margin; the department example is 10 mm anterior, 10 mm posterior, 10 mm superior, 5 mm lateral, 0 mm inferior (EMBRACE II standard margins: 10 mm AP, 10 mm SI, 5 mm lateral, none along the vagina inferiorly).
  3. Edit off pelvic muscle and bone (especially the lateral parametria). Add 5 mm in all directions around the uterine body if the upper, mobile uterus is involved.
  4. Minimum margin 5 mm AP and SI. Reducing one direction usually means adding to the opposite side; judge whether bladder and rectum are in their average positions.
  5. Check that ITV-T LR covers the CT-defined uterus and cervix and the PET GTV, and that its inferior slices match the lower edge of CTV-T LR.
  6. ITV45 = ITV-T LR ∪ CTV-E (which already contains every CTV-N). PTV45 = ITV45 + 5 mm. No ITV is made for CTV-E.
  7. Helper: CTV-T HR_FB ∪ CTV-T HR_EB + 10 mm; keep dose below 103% of 45 Gy inside it (EMBRACE II 9.6).

3.4 Walkthrough (MRI, cranial to caudal)

  1. Fundus: whole uterus; stop the parametria once bowel loops sit beside the uterus (top of broad ligament / uterine arteries).
  2. Corpus and isthmus: parametria from the uterus out to the internal iliac and obturator vessels; anterior edge at the bladder wall; posterior edge at the uterosacral ligaments and mesorectal fascia.
  3. Cervix: GTV-T (bright on T2) and the black stromal ring of uninvolved cervix → CTV-T HR. Look for parametrial spiculation and uterosacral ligament thickening (add mesorectum if involved).
  4. Vagina: follow the canal down 2 cm below the lowest CTV-T HR slice, measured along the vaginal axis on the sagittal; include paravaginal tissue in the parametria down to the levator ani.
  5. Repeat on the empty-bladder MRI; the uterus usually tilts back and drops.

3.5 Pitfalls

  • Measuring the 2 cm vaginal margin from the fornix rather than from the lowest tumour point under-covers tumours that extend into the vagina (EMBRACE II).
  • Couch shifts on IGRT must use bone, not the uterus; matching to the uterus can move nodes and CTV-E out of the field (EMBRACE II 9.8).
  • Average bladder volume falls during treatment, so the full-bladder position at simulation may not be the average (EMBRACE II 9.3.6).

4.1 Nodal boost (EMBRACE II 9.3.3, 9.7; department)

  • GTV-N: outer edge of each node plus visible extracapsular spread, drawn on MRI where it is in the field of view, otherwise on PET-CT and planning CT.
  • CTV-N: copy of GTV-N adjusted to cover the node on both CT and MRI; typical extra margin 0–3 mm, off bone and muscle.
  • PTV-N = CTV-N + 5 mm. If PTV-N is not inside PTV45, use a larger margin (e.g. 10 mm) from CTV-E in that region.
  • Dose (SIB, 25 fractions): PTV55 (2.2 Gy) for external/internal iliac and obturator nodes; PTV57.5 (2.3 Gy) for common iliac, para-aortic and inguinal nodes. Brachytherapy adds about 3–4 Gy EQD2 inside the true pelvis and almost nothing outside it; aim for 55–65 Gy EQD2 total (EMBRACE II).
  • Plan with coverage probability: centre >100%, edge cooled to 90%; large nodes may have GTV-N D50 >102%, max 107%.
  • Poorly responding nodes may need ≥58 Gy; one series had 100% control at ≥58 Gy versus 44% below it (Brower 2023 review).

4.2 Pitfalls

  • A contrast-distended IVC makes the para-aortic CTV too wide on the right and raises kidney dose (Small 2021).
  • Presacral width differs within the department document (10 mm vs 15 mm); Small 2021 recommends 1–1.5 cm. This page uses 10–15 mm, judged on the sagittal CHECK.
  • The external iliac CTV should stop where the vessels leave the pelvis; going to the acetabular roof every time can over-treat the groin (department uses top of acetabulum; Small 2021 the circumflex origin).
  • Number nodes the same way on GTV-N, CTV-N and PTV-N so dose reporting matches.

Section 5 · Scenario B

5Cervix image-guided adaptive brachytherapy

5.1 Department workflow

  • All definitive cervix patients have brachytherapy unless contraindicated; MR-based planning unless contraindicated. Four consecutive Wednesdays from week 4 or 5 of EBRT; 7.75 Gy × 4 (31 Gy) after 45 Gy, aiming for CTVHR D90 ≈ 85–95 Gy EQD2. Patients who cannot have brachytherapy may have an IMRT boost (25.2 Gy/14), which is unlikely to be curative.
  • EUA drawing at each insertion; IDC, bladder emptied then filled with 200 mL saline and clamped for MRI and treatment; empty rectum (consider a Microlax enema before later fractions if the rectum was full).
  • Interstitial needles: prophylactic cefazolin 2 g IV + metronidazole within 60 min (clindamycin 600 mg if penicillin-allergic). Uterine perforation and post-removal bleeding pathways are in the department document.
  • CTVHR contoured by the RO or fellow and reviewed with a radiologist; OARs and vaginal points by radiation therapists. Dose and DVH entered in MOSAIQ.

5.2 Targets (GEC-ESTRO / ICRU 88 via EMBRACE II 3.2, 10.5)

The NRG GYN protocol template (2018) defines CTVIR as CTVHR + 5–15 mm at brachytherapy. The direction-by-direction rules are in GEC-ESTRO I (Haie-Meder 2005) and ICRU 88, which are not in the folder CHECK.

5.3 How to contour

  1. Have at the station: the diagnostic MRI, the EBRT MRI, the brachytherapy MRI with applicator, and the drawings from diagnosis and today's EUA.
  2. Contour on the para-axial T2 (perpendicular to the tandem); check on para-sagittal and para-coronal.
  3. GTVres first, then the whole cervix and grey zones as CTVHR, then CTVIR using the diagnostic MRI so it covers the original tumour extent.
  4. OARs from at least 2 cm below CTVIR to 2 cm above the uterus: bladder (outer wall incl. neck), rectum (sphincter to sigmoid), sigmoid (to ≥2 cm above parametria and uterus), bowel loops within 3–4 cm of uterus and applicator. Urethra if there is significant vaginal involvement.
  5. If the recto-sigmoid junction sits next to the applicator, move the transition up or down so neither organ's D2cc is falsely low.

5.5 Pearls

  • No PTV around CTVHR orthogonal to the tandem (it escalates dose everywhere); a 1 cm cranial margin above CTVHR along the tandem is advised where OAR doses allow (EMBRACE II 10.6.6).
  • Vaginal dose de-escalation: recto-vaginal point <65 Gy (primary aim); vaginal TRAK <30–40% of total; lateral vaginal points at 5 mm <85 Gy; keep the 140% isodose inside the applicator where possible (EMBRACE II Table 10.2).
  • Large or poorly responding tumours: plan for intracavitary + interstitial; EMBRACE II expects at least 20% of patients to need needles.
  • Australia: GOROC (RANZCR 2017) regards MRI-based IGBT as the gold standard; CT-based planning is acceptable where MRI is unavailable, using the pre-brachytherapy MRI and examination to limit CTVHR width, since CT overestimates it. Formal CT-based HR-CTV rules (Viswanathan 2014) are not in the folder CHECK.

Section 6 · Scenario C

6Post-operative cervix and endometrium

6.1 Indications (department)

  • Cervix after radical hysterectomy + PLND: any of positive nodes, parametrial invasion or positive margins (with concurrent cisplatin); node-negative with ≥2 Sedlis factors (size >4 cm, deep stromal invasion >⅓, LVSI) or GOG score ≥120. Add vaginal brachytherapy (10 Gy/2 at 5 mm) for deep stromal invasion, LVSI, close margins or simple hysterectomy.
  • Endometrium: pelvic RT 45 Gy/25; add VBT for any cervical stromal invasion, LVSI or high-risk histology (clear cell, serous, carcinosarcoma). VBT alone 21 Gy/3 for high-intermediate risk (≥2 of age >50, grade 2–3, LVSI, outer-half invasion; PORTEC-2).

6.4 Walkthrough

  1. Find the vault on the sagittal using clips and the vaginal marker; mark the apex and measure 3.5–4 cm down (or half the vaginal length).
  2. Axially, draw vagina + paravaginal tissue from bladder to anterior mesorectum, out to obturator internus; include the cuff "ears" on the slices above the apex.
  3. Draw nodal CTVs (section 4). Endometrium without cervical involvement: no presacral.

6.5 Pitfalls

  • A distended rectum at CT pushes the vagina forward; on treatment days with an empty rectum the vagina falls back out of the PTV. Rescan or include the anterior rectal wall (Small 2021).
  • Stopping at the vaginal apex misses the lateral cuff tissue that rises above it.
  • Para-aortic RT for endometrium: top 1–1.5 cm above the left renal vessels, higher than for cervix (Small 2021).

9.4 Walkthrough and editing

  1. Read the operative note and pathology (margins, nodes, ECE); fuse the pre-op MRI/PET to reconstruct where the tumour was.
  2. Tumour bed (CTV_TB) from scar wire, clips and pre-op imaging; then vulval CTV.
  3. Edit to about 5 mm under the skin where there is no skin involvement and no bolus; to the surface where bolus is used or skin is involved (aide).
  4. Off bone and muscle except inside a tumour-bed expansion; 5 mm of bone may be included where the bed lies on it (aide).
  5. Groin and pelvic nodal CTVs (sections 4 and 8); join the mons and groins so there is no skin gap.

9.5 Pitfalls

  • Leg and vulval oedema change shape during treatment; consider adaptive replanning or soft-tissue IGRT (Gaffney 2016).
  • Asymmetric nodal fields are not recommended outside a trial (Gaffney 2016).
  • Frog-leg and legs-together positions move the groin vessels: draw radial margins on the actual vessel angle.

Section 10

10Pearls and common errors

Cervix (EBRT and brachytherapy)

  • Uterus: include it all; isolated uterine recurrence is rare but myometrial invasion is hard to judge on imaging (Lim 2011).
  • Parametrial top: stop when bowel loops sit beside the uterus; with a very anteverted uterus, stop where the cervix appears (Lim 2011).
  • Lim 2011 kappas: GTV 0.68, but uterus, vagina and parametria only 0.42–0.57 — the parametrium is where observers disagree most; use the coronal.
  • Uterosacral ligament involvement → mesorectum in CTV-T LR; posterior spread is easy to miss on axial slices alone.
  • IGRT: daily bony match; soft-tissue (uterus) review only to decide on replanning (EMBRACE II).
  • Non-involved uterus may get 10% less than prescribed (≈40 Gy EBRT); brachytherapy usually adds >5–10 Gy (EMBRACE II 9.8).
  • Brachytherapy: contour GTVres on today's MRI but CTVIR from the diagnostic tumour extent.

Post-op, vagina and vulva

  • Obturator and presacral regions had the lowest agreement in the NRG update (kappa 0.36 and 0.21) — check them on coronal and sagittal (Small 2021).
  • Common iliac region had the best agreement (kappa 0.64) (Small 2021).
  • The circumflex iliac node is often enlarged but rarely malignant; do not chase it (Small 2021).
  • Vulva: look at coronal and sagittal to find the fold between vulva and thigh/buttock; leave thigh tissue out unless treated deliberately (Gaffney 2016).
  • Bolus: scan with and without, or use virtual bolus, so the plan still works if bolus is stopped (Gaffney 2016).
  • Groin: the femoral vessels move with leg position; draw margins on the planning position.

13.1 ROI algebra

Definitive cervix (EMBRACE II)
  1. CTVp_HR_x = GTVp_x ∪ Cervix_x (x = FB, EB)
  2. CTVp_LR_x = CTVp_HR_x ∪ Uterus_x ∪ Vagina_x ∪ Parametrium_x (∪ Mesorectum_x, ovaries)
  3. CTVp_LR = CTVp_LR_FB ∪ CTVp_LR_EB
  4. ITVp_LR = CTVp_LR + asymmetric margin (A 10, P 10, S 10, I 0, R/L 5) − bone − muscle; edit by hand
  5. CTVn_Elective = ∪ nodal CTVs ∪ CTVn#
  6. ITV_4500 = ITVp_LR ∪ CTVn_Elective → PTV_4500 = ITV_4500 + 5 mm
  7. PTVn# = CTVn# + 5 mm; zCTVp_HR+10 = (CTVp_HR_FB ∪ CTVp_HR_EB) + 10 mm
Post-op cervix / endometrium (department)
  1. ITVp = Vagina_EB ∪ Parametrium_EB ∪ Vagina_FB ∪ Parametrium_FB (+5 mm posterior if rectum full)
  2. CTVn = ∪ nodal CTVs ∪ (Seroma + 7 mm) − bone − muscle
  3. PTVp = ITVp + 10 mm; PTVn = CTVn + 10 mm
  4. PTV_4500 = PTVp ∪ PTVn
Vulva definitive (department)
  1. CTVp = (GTVp + 5–10 mm) ∪ (GTVn + 10 mm) ∪ SkinBridge
  2. PTVp = CTVp + 10 mm; PTVn = CTVn + 10 mm
  3. Skin-cropped evaluation PTVs if no bolus (NRG: PTV_4500-03)

13.2 Registration and QA

  • MRI (FB and EB) and PET rigidly registered to CT on pelvic bone; check the match around the cervix and the sacrum.
  • Peer review before planning; brachytherapy CTVHR reviewed with a radiologist (department).
  • EBRT reporting (EMBRACE II 9.10): volumes of GTV-T, CTV-T HR, CTV-T LR; ITV45 and PTV45 D98; CTV-N D98; bowel V15–V50 (cc); bladder, rectum and sigmoid V30–V50 (%); body V43 and V50; PIBS points.
