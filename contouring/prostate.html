Section 1

1Scope, simulation & imaging

This protocol covers microscopic (elective) and macroscopic (gross) target delineation for external-beam photon radiotherapy of prostate adenocarcinoma. Brachytherapy, proton and metastasis-directed therapy are out of scope. Organ-at-risk delineation follows the ESTRO ACROP primary-prostate OAR guidance and the FROGG constraint tables reproduced in Section 6.

1.1  Simulation & patient preparation

  • Position: supine, arms on chest, reproducible immobilisation (knee-fix + ankle-fix / flat couch).
  • Bladder: comfortably full per a written departmental bladder-filling protocol (e.g. void then 300–500 mL water ~30 min pre-scan), reproduced daily.
  • Rectum: empty; laxative / micro-enema protocol. Rectal and bladder filling reproducibility has a greater impact in the post-prostatectomy setting than for the intact gland.
  • CT: 2–3 mm slices, superior border of femoral heads to below ischial tuberosities. For the intact gland, IV contrast is recommended (unless contraindicated) to separate prostatic base from bladder lumen and to depict median-lobe protrusion.
  • Fiducials: useful for intact soft-tissue / clip matching; not recommended routinely in the post-prostatectomy bed. Rectal balloons and hydrogel spacers are not recommended in the postoperative setting.

1.2  MRI fusion

  • T2-weighted anatomical MRI is the reference modality for soft-tissue delineation. For planning purposes multiparametric acquisition is not mandatory unless a focal DIL boost is intended (then PI-RADS-compliant mpMRI).
  • Registration workflow: rigid CT–MRI fusion initialised on bony pelvis (pubic symphysis closure) then locally refined at the level of the external urethral sphincter / peri-prostatic fat (or implanted fiducials, which are superior to bone-only alignment). Mitigate bladder/rectal filling differences between modalities; reproduce treatment conditions.
  • Accuracy matters: the randomised MIRAGE trial showed that reducing the planning margin from 4 mm (CT-guided) to 2 mm (MRI-guided) significantly reduced GU and GI toxicity — i.e. contour / set-up errors as small as 2 mm are clinically meaningful.
  • The greatest MRI segmentation uncertainty is at the apex (3 mm partial-volume) and the prostatic base; the dorsal venous plexus and neurovascular bundle are the commonest sources of postero-lateral over-contouring.

1.3  Post-prostatectomy MRI protocol

When available, postoperative ± preoperative MRI improves bed delineation and excludes macroscopic recurrence. Acquisition should include T2W in three orthogonal planes, high-b-value DWI and dynamic contrast-enhanced sequences, covering the vesico-urethral anastomosis (VUA), prostatic bed, bladder base, levator ani, rectum and residual seminal vesicles — the common sites of relapse. The MRI-defined VUA lies a median ~5 mm inferior to the CT-defined VUA (Lim Joon), which conditions the inferior-border margin in Section 3.

1.4  Molecular imaging (PSMA-PET)

  • PSMA-PET/CT is the preferred modality for detecting nodal / distant recurrence, particularly when pre-salvage PSA ≥ 0.2 ng/mL; detection rises with PSA (≈33% at 0–0.19, 45% at 0.2–0.49, up to 95% at ≥2 ng/mL).
  • Sensitivity for local bed recurrence is limited at low PSA (urinary tracer excretion / incontinence). A negative PSMA-PET therefore excludes metastatic disease rather than local recurrence — offer salvage RT regardless if otherwise appropriate.
  • Furosemide 20 mg IV shortly before/after tracer improves surgical-bed sensitivity (European guideline option).
  • Any macroscopic disease on clinical exam / imaging is contoured as GTV; PSMA and MRI fusion may be used to define it.
Structure-naming convention used throughout

Target volumes follow AAPM TG-263 conventions (Section 7). Dose-encoded suffixes are in cGy (e.g. PTV_7800, PTVn_4500). Colour pills in this document map to dose tiers, not to fixed prescriptions, which are region- and fractionation-dependent.

Section 2 · Setting A (cont.)

2CT vs MRI: apex and base pearls

Source: McLaughlin PW, Evans C, Feng M, Narayana V. Radiographic and anatomic basis for prostate contouring errors and methods to improve prostate contouring accuracy. Int J Radiat Oncol Biol Phys 2010;76(2):369–378 (doi:10.1016/j.ijrobp.2009.02.019). Summary in our words; see the paper for the image sets.

Earlier work cited by the authors found physician CT contours were on average about 30% larger than the high-resolution standard yet included only about 84% of it: CT adds volume in the wrong places and misses the true gland. Errors cluster at the apex, the anterior mid-gland and the base.

2.x Apex: work up from the penile bulb (genitourinary diaphragm levels)

  • Do not set the apex with a bony or bulb rule (e.g. a fixed distance above the bulb): the distance varies.
  • On CT, the lowest slice with a clear circle or slit is below the apex; start the prostate one level up and check on MRI if available.

2.x Mid-gland and anterior surface

  • The anterior fibromuscular stroma, dorsal venous plexus and endopelvic fascia look like gland on CT and cause anterior over-contouring. Narrow the window/level to separate them.
  • Posteriorly, follow the rectal wall; do not leave a concave notch unless the seminal vesicles create it.

2.x Base: the commonest site of under-contouring

  • The superior base extends higher than expected beside the bladder neck; follow it cranially until the bladder neck and seminal vesicle origins are clearly separate.
  • An anterior-superior “beak” is usually bladder wall or fascia, not prostate.
  • A posterior “reverse beak” or concave posterior surface at the base is the seminal vesicle junction.

2.x Check the shape in the sagittal view

  • The gland should look globular. Common artefacts: a stepped “pedestal” from repeated small contours at the apex; an inferior-anterior projection into the GU diaphragm; an inferior-posterior projection along the rectum.
  • Axial contours should taper smoothly from the largest slice; sudden jumps in size between slices usually mean a contour error.
Practical

When MRI is available, register it on the prostate (fiducials or soft tissue) and contour on MRI, using CT for the posterior rectal interface and bladder. Without MRI, apply these level rules deliberately at the apex and base.

Section 3 · Setting B (cont.)

3Macroscopic recurrence, positive margins & IGRT

Primary sources: ESTRO ACROP prostate-bed guideline (Dal Pra 2023); FROGG 2024 update (Warrender-Sparkes); comparison with GFRU (Robin 2021) and PERYTON (Staal 2024).

3.1  Definitions & timing (FROGG)

TermDefinition
Adjuvant RT (ART)RT within 6 months of prostatectomy with PSA <0.10 ng/mL and not rising.
Salvage RT (SRT)RT delivered with PSA ≥0.10 ng/mL, or a rising PSA of any magnitude.

Early SRT is preferred to ART for most pN0/Nx patients (RAVES, RADICALS-RT, GETUG-AFU 17, ARTISTIC meta-analysis: equivalent event-free survival, less toxicity). Refer before PSA exceeds 0.2 ng/mL. In pN1 disease, particularly with multiple positive nodes or other high-risk features, ART remains reasonable (Tilki).

Uniform CTV, independent of indication

Both ESTRO ACROP and FROGG apply a single prostate-bed CTV recommendation irrespective of ART vs SRT timing, tailored to case-specific pathology (EPE site, margin, SV involvement). The contours below assume no macroscopic recurrence; visible disease is handled as GTV (§3.4).

3.2  Prostate-bed CTV borders — reconciled bed CTV

Where ESTRO ACROP and FROGG diverge, both are shown as named positions with a departmental default. The two guidelines agree on the lateral border (medial levator ani / obturator internus) and on the primacy of covering the VUA and the postero-lateral rectal recesses (antero-lateral angles of the rectum) — a common recurrence site (26% of local recurrences in the Horsley PSMA series).

Inferior border — Position 1 (ESTRO ACROP): 8–12 mm below the VUA. VUA = the slice below the last slice on which urine is seen; no routine contrast; if VUA not visible, start at the slice immediately above the penile bulb.

Inferior border — Position 2 (FROGG): 5–10 mm below the VUA — the larger margin when MRI or IV contrast is not used (MRI-defined VUA sits ~5 mm inferior to the CT-defined VUA). Extend lower to include any inferior surgical clips.

Departmental default: with MRI-defined VUA use 5 mm below; with CT/contrast-only use up to 10 mm below; always extend to enclose inferior clips. Up to 9% of recurrences fall >5 mm below the VUA, but observed local-failure rates remain very low with adequate coverage.

Anterior border — ESTRO: cranially cover 1–2 cm of posterior bladder wall (or up to the posterior bladder-wall margin if reproducible daily IGRT, sparing bladder); caudally stop at the posterior symphysis, up to half–two-thirds of its height; do not extend to the top of the symphysis.

Anterior border — FROGG: from the lower CTV border to 2–3 cm superior, the anterior border is the posterior symphysis, incorporating ≥1.5 cm of bladder neck; more superiorly the border wraps around the lateral aspects of the posterior bladder wall to extend 1.5 cm anteriorly.

Rationale (both): anterior recurrences above the bladder neck / behind the pubic symphysis are rare; lowering this antero-superior border spares bladder and reduces the >50% acute grade-2 GU toxicity seen historically (RAVES, RTOG 0534).

Posterior border — both: anterior rectal wall; include the antero-lateral rectal angles / postero-lateral recesses (FROGG: 5 mm coverage each side of the anterior rectal wall in the mid-CTV only). At/below the anorectal junction the CTV need not wrap posteriorly. Superiorly the posterior border is the anterior mesorectal fascia. Include existing surgical clips.

Superior border — Position 1 (ESTRO ACROP): include a 3–5 mm “bridge” between the SV beds. No SV invasion (pT2–3a): include the base (lower third) of the SV bed, to the level of the cut end of the vas deferens. SV invasion: include the entire SV bed (+ cranial clips).

Superior border — Position 2 (FROGG): encompass the SV bed (non-vascular clips) to the cut end of the vas. If SV not involved and prostatic base not involved, do not extend >4.5 cm above the VUA (avoids a bony surrogate; VUA position is variable relative to the symphysis). If SV involved, include residual SV.

Superior border — Position 3 (GFRU / PERYTON): 1 cm above the pubic symphysis; in pT3b, 3 cm above the top of the symphysis (GFRU: up to 4 cm if distal-third SV involved; + posterior third of bladder wall).

Departmental default: anchor to the cut end of the vas / SV bed and cap at ≤4.5 cm above the VUA when SV and prostatic base are uninvolved (FROGG), reserving fuller SV-bed coverage for pathological SV involvement. Only ~6% of recurrences occur >45 mm above the VUA, predicted by prostatic-base involvement.

Anchor points

The VUA is the caudal spatial reference for the whole bed; the cut end of the vas / SV bed anchors the cranial extent. Preferring these soft-tissue anchors over bony surrogates (symphysis) accounts for the variable VUA–symphysis relationship.

3.3  Expansion at a positive surgical margin

Older EORTC guidance recommended a supplementary 5 mm CTV expansion toward a microscopically involved margin (except the rectal wall). ESTRO ACROP reached no consensus on this — judicious expansion is left to the treating radiation oncologist. Direct such expansion by the pathology report (site of EPE / R1) and preoperative MRI where available.

3.4  Macroscopic bed recurrence — GTV boost

  • Any macroscopic recurrence on clinical exam / MRI / PSMA-PET is contoured as GTVp; MRI fusion best delineates PSMA-avid bed disease.
  • The elective bed CTV is treated to the standard salvage dose 64–66 Gy EQD2; gross recurrence is boosted to ≥70–74 Gy EQD2 respecting OAR constraints (SPIDER-supported PFS benefit at ≥72 Gy).
  • Principle: the boost is anchored to the demonstrable gross-disease extent; the surrounding elective bed envelope is unchanged.

3.5  IGRT & motion (bed)

The dedicated ESTRO ACROP IGRT guideline (Ghadjar 2019) covers the intact gland only; the bed rules below are from Dal Pra 2023 — see §5.4 for the intact IGRT margin framework.

  • Daily (or ≥3×/week) CBCT with soft-tissue and/or surgical-clip matching; bone-only match or no IGRT is not recommended. Train RTTs in online soft-tissue matching.
  • The postero-superior bed moves most; a larger antero-posterior PTV margin is appropriate in the upper bed. Divide upper vs lower bed at the level where the CTV leaves the pubic symphysis.
  • Maintain an empty rectum and comfortably full bladder; no routine fiducials, rectal balloon or spacer in the bed.
Adherence matters

Central review of a salvage phase-3 trial (SAKK 09/10) showed that limited protocol adherence — notably CTV overlap with the rectal wall — was associated with higher toxicity and a trend to worse biochemical control. MRI-aided CT contouring reduces inter-observer variability, shifts the inferior border caudally and sharpens the anterior rectal-wall interface.

Physics / clinical review
  • Positive-margin CTV expansion (0 vs 5 mm directional) is a governance decision — agree a departmental default and document per case.
  • Bed boost dose / EQD2 assumptions (α/β for the bed) and hotspot control near the VUA require physics validation (Section 6).

Section 4 · Setting C (cont.)

4Node-positivity criteria, ENI indications & dose

Primary source: NRG Oncology updated international consensus atlas (Hall 2021). Used for elective nodal irradiation in intact and post-prostatectomy settings (FROGG 7.4 defers to NRG for nodal volumes).

Node-positivity criteria, ENI indications & dose

Prophylactic steps — clinically node-negative (intact & postop) 45–50.4 Gy

  1. Superior start: aortic bifurcation into the common iliac arteries, or proximal IVC / common iliac veins — whichever is more superior (typically L4–L5).
  2. Vessel margin: ~5–7 mm around each iliac vessel, full circumference of artery and vein; exclude bone, bowel, bladder, muscle. Up to 10 mm anteriorly where clinically indicated; cover the space between psoas major and vertebral body posteriorly.
  3. Interspace: external–internal iliac contour interspace ~1.5–3 cm (patient-dependent).
  4. Presacral: include prevertebral / presacral / posterior mesorectal nodes to the bottom of S3.
  5. Posterior off internal iliac: extend to the anterior edge of piriformis, following the pudendal and inferior gluteal arteries.
  6. Inguinal transition: where external iliac vessels cross beneath the inguinal ligament into the inguinal canal — begin tapering (often clearest on coronal).
  7. External iliac end: when vessels are completely lateral to the most medial acetabulum (~mid-femoral head / fovea) — taper off.
  8. Obturator width: 1–2 cm, to the posterior edge of obturator internus.
  9. Obturator anterior/taper: begin tapering at the top of the SV (or top of the postop bed), extending ~1 cm anterior to the anterior edge of obturator internus.
  10. Obturator end: where the SV join the prostate, or ~mid-portion of the postop CTV bed.

Node-positive modifications boost

  1. Follow steps 1–10 for the elective envelope.
  2. Identify suspicious nodes (criteria table, next page); review with diagnostic radiology.
  3. Elective volumes extend ~5–7 mm around clinically suspicious nodes (may locally alter steps 1–10).
  4. Residual (shrunken) gross nodes post-ADT form the primary boost volume — contour pre- and post-ADT; boost the post-ADT volume.
Consensus caveats (NRG)

CTV excludes bone, bladder, muscle and bowel. Perirectal / mesorectal nodes are not routinely included (only for highly selected T4 disease). In postop cases, aberrant nodal spread is common — incorporate pathology, operative report and PSMA/MRI; identify and consider including surgical clips.

4.2  Elective nodal irradiation (ENI) — contested indications

The NRG atlas defines how to contour nodes but explicitly does not address whether to treat them (out of scope). Indications are presented below as named positions.

Salvage setting (FROGG / SPPORT): RTOG 0534 SPPORT showed prostate-bed RT + short-term ADT + pelvic nodal RT improved 5-yr freedom-from-progression (87.4% vs 81.3% [PBRT+ADT] vs 70.9% [PBRT alone]) and delayed second salvage ADT — but no MFS/OS benefit demonstrated, and PLNRT increased acute GI and haematological toxicity. ENI can be considered, and should be discussed with patients who had minimal/no nodal dissection and high microscopic-nodal risk (ISUP GG4–5, pT3/4, preop PSA >20, persistent PSA with clear margins, pre-salvage PSA >0.35). Nodal RT without concurrent ADT is of unknown benefit — consider ADT alongside ENI.

pN1 at prostatectomy (FROGG 6.3): patients with pathologically involved nodes being considered for salvage should receive pelvic nodal RT in addition to prostate-bed RT.

Intact high-risk (POP-RT / SPPORT-era practice): whole-pelvis RT is an accepted option for high / very-high-risk disease and clinically node-positive disease; POP-RT demonstrated improved biochemical failure-free and disease-free survival with prophylactic whole-pelvis RT in high-risk patients. Indication remains an area of active trial inquiry.

SBRT to the prostate bed remains investigational (SCIMITAR II 30–34 Gy/5#; no biochemical outcomes yet) — deliver only within a trial. Both ESTRO ACROP and FROGG regard the delineation recommendations as applicable irrespective of fractionation regime.

5.2  ADT (post-prostatectomy salvage) — brief

  • Add short-term ADT for higher-risk salvage (RTOG 9601 OS benefit likely limited to pre-salvage PSA >0.6; GETUG-16 MFS benefit; SPPORT FFP benefit). RADICALS-HD: long-course (24 mo) improved MFS vs short in higher-risk; 6 mo did not improve MFS vs none but delayed second salvage ADT.
  • Risk-stratify (ISUP GG≥4, PSA-DT <12 mo, pre-salvage PSA >0.6, ≥pT3b); 6–24 months, longer for multiple high-risk features. Patients receiving ENI should be considered for concurrent ADT.

Primary source: ESTRO ACROP consensus guideline on IGRT for localized prostate cancer (Ghadjar 2019). Corroborating margin guidance: ACR Appropriateness Criteria; Oehler 2014.

Scope of the guideline

The Ghadjar guideline applies only to patients without prior prostatectomy, eligible for definitive EBRT. IGRT of the prostate bed is explicitly a separate topic and is not covered — use the bed rules in §3.5 (Dal Pra), for which no dedicated postoperative IGRT guideline exists.

5.4  Intact prostate — IGRT method & margin framework

The guideline reviews (a) inter- and intra-fractional prostate motion, (b) technical IGRT approaches, (c) clinical control/toxicity, and (d) margin selection, and recommends daily online IGRT. Example CTV→PTV margins are given for the three most common IGRT scenarios; margins scale with the precision and timing of the correction and must be institution-derived (e.g. van Herk recipe), anisotropic (reduced posteriorly to spare rectum), and matched to delivery/QA capability.

Motion & delivery principles
  • Intrafraction motion grows with treatment time — minimise beam-on time (VMAT) and consider tracking for SBRT / ultra-hypofractionation, where the 2–4 mm regime and MRI guidance are most relevant (cf. MIRAGE: 2 vs 4 mm reduced toxicity).
  • Rectal distension on the planning CT worsens biochemical control via geographic miss — enforce the empty-rectum / comfortably-full-bladder protocol and re-sim if the reference anatomy is non-representative.
  • Only adopt tight margins when matched by appropriately precise delivery and QA; the margin, IGRT method and delivery technique are a single coupled decision.

5.5  Prostate bed — IGRT recap (Dal Pra)

  • Preferred method: CBCT with soft-tissue and/or surgical-clip matching, daily or at minimum 3×/week. Bone-only match or no image guidance are not recommended; RTT online-matching training is critical.
  • No implanted fiducials in the bed; no routine rectal balloon or hydrogel spacer.
  • Rectum/bladder filling variation has a greater impact postoperatively than for the intact gland — maintain an empty rectum and comfortably full bladder. Apply the anisotropic upper/lower-bed PTV margins of §5.3.
Physics validation

Margin values above are examples. Derive departmental CTV→PTV margins from a local van-Herk analysis of setup, intrafraction and delineation uncertainties for each plan class, and re-validate when IGRT technique or fractionation changes.

Split the bed at the level where the CTV leaves the pubic symphysis (upper vs lower bed) and apply anisotropic margins — the larger antero-posterior margin in the upper bed where motion is greatest (FROGG 7.5). Use non-uniform margin expansion in RayStation (Anterior/Posterior/Superior/Inferior/Left/Right fields), not a uniform value.

7.3  Optimisation & evaluation helpers

  • Wall ROIs: Rectum_Wall = Rectum − (Rectum ⊖ 3mm); likewise Bladder_Wall. Report V-dose on walls where luminal filling varies.
  • PTV eval / hotspot control near VUA: zPTV_eval = PTV − (Rectum ⊕ 3mm); add a zVUA_avoid planning ROI to suppress dosimetric hotspots at the anastomosis.
  • Skin trim: PTV −= (External ⊖ 3mm) to prevent build-up-region PTV.
  • Rings: zRing = (PTV ⊕ 2cm) − (PTV ⊕ 3mm) for conformality control.

7.4  Registration & QA

  • Rigid CT–MRI (and CT–PSMA) fusion: initialise on pelvic bone (symphysis closure), then locally refine at the external urethral sphincter / peri-prostatic fat or fiducials. Use deformable registration with caution given bladder/rectal filling differences; verify at the target.
  • Contour QA: review every target in axial + sagittal + coronal (mandatory at apex/base and at the VUA). Confirm no CTV–rectal-wall overlap (adherence predicts toxicity / control).
  • IGRT: daily CBCT, soft-tissue ± clip match; document upper/lower-bed match priority.
