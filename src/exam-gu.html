A: 60yo

G: M

R: Western countries

RF:

T1: no muscle invasion

T2: muscle invasive

T3: perivesical soft tissue

T4: adjacent organ invasion

N1: single pelvic node

N2: multiple pelvic node

M1: DM

Outcomes:

5 yr OS:

  • Localised node negative: 55%
  • Node positive: 30%

Distant relapse: 20-30%

Node relapse 10-15%

  • Worse for variant histology

5 yr bladder preservation: 80%

F/u:

  • q3monthly surveillance cystoscopy for 2 yrs, then q6mo for year 3-5 then annual
  • q6monthly CTCAP

Pre-invasive path

Tis: flat, carcinoma in situ

  • low, intermediate, high grade

Ta: papillary neoplasm

  • papillary urothelial neoplasm of low malignant potential
  • low grade papillary neoplasm
  • high grade papillary neoplasm

Invasive:

Urothelial cell carcinoma: classified by flat or papillary.

  • Flat is always high grade
  • Subtypes: micropapillary, sarcomatoid, nested, plasmacytoid

Variant:

  • SCC 3-5%
  • AdenoCa
  • Sarcoma: RMS
  • Small cell NEC – RT + cis/etop
  • Melanoma
  • Lymphoma

IHC: Uroplakin, GATA3, CK7, CK20, AE1/AE3

Note: core biopsy must sample detrusor muscle

  • Exception is cancer in diverticulum which lacks muscle wall

Multiple GU cancer theories:

Monocloncal seeding:

  • malignant clone travels through GU tract and implants into another site resulting in a secondary primary cancer

Field cancerization

  • Epigenetic changes due to exposure to a common carcinogen lead to accumulation to mutations, dysplasia and malignancy

Mgmt – pre-invasive & T1N0M0

1.Cystoscopy + maximal TURBT

  • Repeat if R1 as BCG only effective for eliminating microscopic disease

2.Intravesical MMC or BCG

  • commence by 2 mo post op to avoid macro regrowth
  • Low risk: Ta, low grade, unifocal, <3cm
  • Intermediate: not low or high risk
  • High risk: T1, G3, CIS, multifocal/diffuse, recurrent, large >3cm

Induction

  • ALL: weekly BCG for 6 weeks
  • Low risk can have MMC

Maintenance:

  • Check cystoscopy
  • Low risk: TURBT +/-MMC
  • Intermediate risk: intravesical BCG at month 3, 6, 12
  • High risk: if non response → cystectomy. If good response then maintenance BCG q6mo for 3 yrs

50% or more LR by 10 years for high risk

  • Rechallenge BCG if >6mo
  • cystectomy if <6mo
  • or adj RT

BCG toxicity: systemic TB (if bladder injury to ongoing haematuria due to systemic seeding), irritative bladder sx, bladder stricture

Mgmt – T2NanyM0

1.Cystoscopy + maximal TURBT +/- HUN management for all

Cystectomy:

NA chemotherapy if for cystectomy OR N+

  • ddMVAC: methotrexate, vinblastine, Adriamycin, cyclophosphamide
    • 5% OS benefit, may be up to 15% for higher risk
  • Cis/gem/Durvalumab + adj durva for 1 yr vs cis/gem had 8% 5yr OS benefit and 20% EFS – NIAGARA (on PBS now)
  • Enfortumab vedotin + pembro showing promising pCR rates ~60%

Cystectomy + PLND with at least 10 LN

  • Indications: young, fit, N-ve, non TCC histology
  • males: removal of prostate, SV, distal vas deferens, proximal urethra
  • females: removal of uterus, anterior vaginal wall, all urethra
  • all: need urinary diversion through neo-bladder using ileum or cutaneous stoma via ileal conduit
    • Neo-bladder is continent, ileal conduit/stoma can be continent or non-continent
  • G3 toxicity rate of 20%, G5 2% in one series (Hirobi et al), QoL is poor

Adjuvant nivolumab (CHECKMATE 274 – improved DFS)

  • if ypT2+ or pN+ or pT3+

Adjuvant radiotherapy: 50.4Gy/28# (BART trial – improved LRC and maybe OS without increased tox)

  • if SM+ve, pT3-4, N+, ≤10 nodes dissected
  • Pre-IO era, no data integrating IO and RT
  • CTV: cystectomy bed + pelvic nodes, spare stoma. Boost SM+ve

TMT:

  • Equivalent survival outcomes - Zlotta et al. Better QoL.
  • Selective bladder preservation:
  • functional bladder
  • willing to attend surveillance cystoscopies
  • unifocal tumour without extensive CIS
  • non-variant histology
  • maximal TURBT completed
  • treated HUN
  • 55Gy/20# preferred (pooled analysis of BC2001 & BCON)
  • 64Gy/32# with concurrent MMC/5FU (BC2001 trial)
  • Concurrent weekly cisplatin is alternative (TROG 97.01 & 99.06 analysis)
  • Dose escalation to 70Gy and adaptive RT not shown to be superior (RAIDER)
  • Carbogen + nicotinamide if unfit for chemo (BCON)
  • if N-ve not role for ENI (Tunio et al)
  • if N+ then boost involved nodes to 60Gy and ENI 50Gy ENI EQD2
  • 20% need salvage cystectomy, more morbid than upfront surg

Palliative RT:

  • 21Gy/3# 2nd daily non-inferior to 35Gy/10# for sx relief (MRC trial)
  • 70% haematuria relief at 3 mo

Zlotta et al:

  • Retrospective review with matched cohort analysis showed TMT is comparable to cystectomy for 5 year CSS, DFS and beneficial for OS 66% vs 73%

BC2001:

P: MIBC, unresectable

I: 64Gy/32# or 55Gy/20# +/- MMC/5FU

whole bladder RT vs modified bladder RT (80% dose to whole bladder, 100% to GTV)

No ENI, 30% had NA CT

O: CRT vs RT:

  • 5 yr OS 48% vs 35%
  • Invasive bladder recurrence 11% vs 19%
  • Nodal relapse 5% vs 7%
  • 5 yr LRC 60% vs 50%
  • Cystectomy 14% vs 22%
  • G3/4 tox: 8% vs 16%
  • Benefit persists at 10 yrs
  • Modified bladder RT non-inferior

-55Gy/20# had better LRC and isotoxic, hypothesised due to less repopulation time

BCON:

P: MIBC receiving radical RT

I: radical RT +/- carbogen and nicotinamide

O: 3 yrs OS 45% vs 50% however no sig difference at 10 yrs ~ 30%. Tumour necrosis predictive for greater benefit

P: High risk MIBC – any of: T3-4, N+, SM+, ≤10 nodes dissected

I: Post-op pelvic RT 50.4Gy/28# vs no RT.

Chemo allowed in neoadj or adj setting. No IO.

O: 10% reduction in LRR at 2 yrs. 2 yr OS 70% vs 57%. No G3 tox.

-Addition of adj nivo to cis/gem chemo improved mOS from 50 to 70 months

Neoadjuvant gem/cis/durva compared to gem/cis had 8% 5 yr OS benefit.

Management

SOC:

  • Total nephrectomy + LN sampling:
    • >7cm
    • if T3-4, node positive, WHO grade 4, then PA LND
  • Nephron sparing - partial nephrectomy
    • <7cm, within renal capsule, no vessel invasion
    • DTPA scan to assess differential function
    • small tumour, patient at risk of CKD or single kidney or bilateral disease
    • 10 yr CSS no diff with partial vs total nephrectomy
    • eGFR drop of 10% overall

Adjuvant pembrolizumab for 1 year

  • T2 WHO grade IV, T3-4, N+
  • 5% OS benefit at 4 yrs (KEYNOTE 564)
  • Addition of Belzutifan improves DFS (Litespark 022, abstract only)

No benefit to adjuvant RT

SABR (Fastrack, iROCK)

  • Not first line as no RCT data, operable candidates should still have surgery
  • Biopsy is preferred to confirm pathology for non-surgical options
    • If solid component available to bx
  • <4cm 26Gy/1# (T1a)
  • 4-10cm 42Gy/3#
  • Exclusions: ≥T2b, eGFR ≤ 30mL/min, abutting bowel,
  • Relative exclusions: single kidney, poor contralateral function, surgical or surveillance candidate

Active surveillance:

  • Elderly pt, fit for treatment
  • Lesions <1cm have up to 40% chance of being benign, can pursue AS for up to 4cm
  • Growth rate <5mm/year
  • LE <5years
  • CT, MRI or US q3-6mo for 2 years, then q6-12mo

Alternatives:

  • RFA, MWA, cryoablation: <3cm, non-central, away from vessels
  • >95% LC at 5 yrs, though low level evidence

Oligometastatic:

  • If long progression free survival consider ablative local therapy (SABR) as per ESTRO/EAU consensus guidelines
  • Can prevent local progression, improve PFS, delay systemic therapy

Metastatic:

  • TKI works faster, IO slower but possibly more durable
  • First line: doublet IO (ipi/nivo) or IO/TKI (pembro/axitinib or nivo/cabozatinib)
  • Less fit favourable IMDC - TKI alone – sunitinib, pazopanib
  • TKI tox: HTN, diarrhoea, fatigue, hand-foot syndrome, mucositis, hypothyroid, hepatotoxicity, proteinuria, bleeding/thrombosis.
  • Role for nephrectomy:
    • Avoid upfront cytoreductive nephrectomy unless symptomatic (CARMENA)
    • May be a role for delayed CN in responders to systemic therapy though trial was underpowered (SURTIME)

Management

Surgery is mainstay with improved LC compared to RT. However OS no different as salvage surgery can be used ~ 50%

Tis, T1 & confined to glans

  • 5FU or imiquimod for Tis
  • Glands resurfacing
  • Moh’s surgery
  • Glansectomy
  • Interstitial brachytherapy (<4cm, <1cm in corpora spongiosum): HDR BT 54Gy/3# BD, risk of urethral stricture up to 40%, LC 80%

T2+ or not confined to glands:

  • Partial penectomy if residual length sufficient for standing urination
  • Total penectomy

Nodal management:

  • T1: Dynamic SLNBx. If SLN+ → inguinofem LND
  • if inguinal node +: bilateral inguino fem LND
  • if 1-2 nodes
  • if ≥3 inguinal node + or ENE → pelvic LND
  • if ≥3 inguinal node, ENE or pelvic node + → adj groin and pelvic RT + cisplatin

Adjuvant chemoRT (from InPACT guidelines):

  • SM+ - adj RT to primary: 66Gy/33#
  • N+ - adj RT to nodes: 57 (macroscopic), 54Gy (high risk), 50Gy (low risk)
  • ENE or multiple nodes: adj chemo

Multiple clinical node + or unresectable:

  • Neoadjuvant TIP x4 (paclitaxel, ifosfamide, cisplatin) → reassess
  • Definitive chemoRT: 70Gy/35# with cisplatin

Consider penile reconstruction options

Post-op pelvic RT doses (InPACT guidelines):

  • Consider each side and region (inguinal vs pelvic) separately
  • Dissected pN0 = no RT
  • Dissected pN1 (single node no ENE) = 45Gy
  • Dissected multiple nodes or ENE = 54Gy
  • Macroscopic disease = 57Gy boost to involved node in groin, 54Gy in pelvis
  • Undissected pelvis in high risk groin (2+ or ENE groin nodes) = 54Gy
  • If positive pelvic nodes include CI to at least 2cm above involved node = 45Gy
  • Prepubic fat = 54Gy in all
