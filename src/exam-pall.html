Bone mets

Metastatic cascade · Mechanism of pain · SBRT · Volumes

Metastatic cascade

  • Epithelial – mesenchymal transition: loss of e-cadherin in primary tumour, increase in matrix metalloproteinase which degrades ECM → cancer cells escape local environment
  • VEGF increases permeability → Intravasation into blood vessels
  • TGF-B and PDL1 assist in immune evasion in circulation
  • Integrins and intercellular adhesion molecules assist in extravasation
  • Mesenchymal – epithelial cascade: cancer cells seed into new environment, angiogenesis and cell proliferation occurs via VEGF and cell signalling pathways (MAPK, PI3K, JAK-STAT)

Pathophysiology

  • Cancer cells disrupt normal bone remodelling and cause increased growth factor release → cancer cell proliferation
  • Most bone mets occur in axial skeleton bone as it is rich in highly vascularised red marrow and there is more bone turnover due to loading

Common primaries

  • Lytic: myeloma, thyroid, renal, choriocarcinoma, melanoma
  • Sclerotic: prostate, SCLC, hodgkin lymphoma
  • Mixed: breast, NSCLC GI , SCC

Clinical oncology

Bisphosphonates (Zoledronic acid)

  • Inhibits osteoclast activity
  • Larger evidence base, more cost-effective and widely available
  • Reduces skeletal related events, bone pain, hypercalcaemia, osteoporosis
  • Provides 2-3% 10yr OS benefit in post-menopausal breast cancer (EBCTCG MA)
  • Contraindicated in severe renal dysfunction, vit D deficiency, small risk of osteoradionecrosis (~1% but increases with cumulative exposure up to 5%)
  • IV injection, q1 or 12mo for bone met vs OP

RANK-L ligand inhibitors (Denosumab)

  • Prevents osteoclast activation
  • Less studied, more expensive
  • Reduces SRE, bone pain, hypercalcaemia and OP
  • Can be used in renal impairment, slightly lower risk of ONJ
  • SC injection, q1 or 6mo for bone met vs OP
  • MA has shown denosumab is superior for reducing SRE or hypercalcaemia of malignancy in solid tumours with bone mets

Mechanism of pain

  • Uncomplicated: constant dull ache from pressure on surrounding bone or soft tissue
  • Complicated: sharp sudden pain with movement due to unstable structural defect
  • Neuropathic: burning shooting pain and hyperalgesia due to nerve irritation

Prophylactic fixation

  • Mirel’s score (estimates # risk with RT) suggests consider prophylactic fixation if ≥8 (≥15% fracture risk)
  • Type: lytic, blastic, mixed
  • Diameter: <1/3, >2/3
  • Site: upper, lower, trochanteric
  • Pain: mild, mod, functional

Palliative radiotherapy

  • 8Gy/1# vs 20Gy/5#
  • Equivalent pain relief ~ 60% response rate (Chow et al MA)
  • Higher retreatment rate with single fraction – 20% at 6mo vs 8% at 6mo
  • No diff in toxicity or # risk
  • Up to 1/3rd of patients may experience a pain flare, most commonly within 5 days but can occur up to 10 days post RT

Re-treatment

  • Retreatment provides pain relief in 50%. Patients within initial good response are more likely to report.
  • Single fraction is similar in efficacy to multifraction
  • A minimum 4 week interval is suggested

Bone SABR

  • Per eVIQ: 18Gy/1#, 24Gy/2# or 30Gy/5#
  • For lesions with >30% circumferential cortical involvement, soft tissue extension or weight bearing bones preference is for multi-fraction SABR to reduce # risk
  • 12Gy/1# if >4cm or 16Gy/1# if <4cm has better pain response (77% vs 46%) and 100% vs 75% LC at 2 yrs with similar toxicity to 30Gy/10# (MDACC phase II RCT, Nguyen et al)
  • SBRT has excellent LC >85% at 2 yrs, 5% # risk mostly in rib or femur and assoc with ↑ PTV size, 7% pain flare, 5% fatigue (MASTER BONES MA)
  • ISRS MA has demonstrated similar excellent LC >90% however fracture rates up to 17%

Radionuclide

Sr-89

  • calcium analogue taken up proportional to osteoblastic activity, therefore preferentially taken up by bone mets
  • Pure B-emitter, ½ life of 50 days
  • Pain relief occurs from 2-6 weeks, 10% have pain flare which indicates good response
  • Lu-177-PSMA-617 is an alternative option in MPC
    • Binds to transmembrane PSMA enzyme, entire radio-ligand is internalised → released Lu-177 radioactive payload → B-particle emission has therapeutic effect, also low energy gamma rays which allows imaging
  • All cause myelosuppression

SBRT

Dose: 24Gy/3# as per ICRU 91, VMAT, MV FFF, alternate days

  • GTV/ITV:
    • 100% to receive >100% TD
  • PTV:
    • 98% to receive >100% TD
    • 100% to receive >95% TD
  • Dmax 125-140% within GTV
  • <50% of TD outside 1-2cm of PTV

Pre-sim: assess fracture risk and need for prophylactic fixation, optimise analgesia

Sim: supine, isolate and immobilise treatment area, consider breath hold if rib, fuse imaging if not well demarcated on CT

Volumes

GTV: met

CTV: +5mm cropped to bone if intraosseous lesion. If extraosseous lesion then extend margin outside bone

  • NB SABR-COMET protocol did not use CTV expansion

PTV: +3mm

Daily pre and post CBCT with bone match

Coverage

GTV: D100 >100%

PTV: D95 >100%

Spine SABR

Indications · Dose fractionation · SRS - ISRS spine SABR guidelines · The ISRS CTV expansions

Indications

  • Oligometastatic disease
  • Retreatment of bone metastasis for pain or local control without cord compression

Contraindications

  • Treatment ≥ 4 vertebral adjacent bodies
  • Bilsky score 1c+

Bilsky score

0: no epidural disease

1: indenting thecal sac (1a,b) or abutting cord (1c) but no compression

2: cord compression with visible CSF

3: cord compression without CSF

SINS score

>6 warrants neurosurgical review for stabilisation

  • Location: junctional, rigid, semi-rigid, mobile
  • Pain: incident, constant, nil
  • Compression of VB
  • Posterior element involvement
  • Alignment
  • Type of lesion: blastic, lytic, mixed

Role of surgery

  • Decompression only (laminectomy)
  • Stabilisation +/- decompression
  • Separation
  • Corpectomy + stabilisation
  • Carbon fibre PEEK screws to assist with RT post-op

Dose fractionation

  • 20Gy/1#, 24Gy/2#
  • Doses higher than 20Gy per fraction are at greater risk of causing VB fracture
  • Multifraction and single fraction pain response is similar
  • Single fraction may have higher VB # risk but better LC
  • New or progressive vertebral fracture risk is ~15%
  • Pain flare risk is 15%
  • SBRT with 24Gy/2# vs 20Gy/5# provides higher complete pain response (33% vs 16% at 6mo) and less retreatment (15% vs 35%) (Saghal et al)
  • If treating near cord → require at least 3mm of separation between cord and disease (Bilsky 0-1)

Spine retreatment

  • Above fractions or consider 20Gy/10#
  • 50% recovery if >6mo passed, no single course >48Gy EQD2, total Dmax <68Gy EQD2. A/b 2

Liver mets

Pathophysiology · Fractionated palliative RT · SABR · OARs

Pathophysiology

  • PV drains majority of blood supply from GIT, thus seeding to liver is common
  • Favourable microenvironment rich in nutrients for cellular growth and pro-inflammatory cells (Kpuffer cells)
  • Liver is constantly exposed to GIT antigens resulting in immune tolerance and easier immune evasion

See HCC for imaging

Fractionated palliative RT

  • Diffuse multifocal disease, unsuitable for focal therapies
  • whole liver or partial liver can be treated for pain, bloating, jaundice, deranged LFTs secondary to liver mets
  • 10Gy/2# consecutive days, 8Gy/1#, 21Gy/7#
  • eVIQ suggest generous margins – 1.5-2cm for CTV & PTV if no 4DCT
  • Up to 70% pain response with whole liver single fraction RT

SABR

  • Ideally 1-3 mets, combined diameter ≤10cm with adequate liver function (CP B7 or less if cirrhotic)
  • ≥5mm margin from GI organs
  • Consider other focal therapies with more established outcomes in fit patients: resection, MWA/RFA
  • 3# regime preferable (42Gy/3#, 45Gy/3# - 3rd daily)
  • Alternatives: 50Gy/5# as per HCC
  • HyTEC analysis showed promising outcomes with SBRT: 1yr LC 90%, 3yr LC 75%, BED >100Gy assoc with better LC
  • There is established dose response with higher dose per fraction increasing LC (e.g. 60Gy/5# at 100% 2yr LC vs 30Gy/5# had 50% 2 yr LC – Rule et al)

SABR

Dose: 54Gy/3# 3rd daily, as per ICRU 91, MV, FFF

Sim: Supine, arms above head, vac bag, 4DCT or EEBH, triple phase contrast, fuse MRI, anti-emetic pre-med

Brain mets

Pathophysiology · WBRT · SRS - cavity · OARs

Pathophysiology

  • Most common intracranial tumour
  • Tumour cells lodge in cerebral microvasculature
  • Adhesion to BBB via integrins, ICAM/VCAM
  • Disruption of BBB through MMP and VEGF secretion
  • Cells transmigrate across BBB and adapt and grow with brain parenchyma
  • Leaky tumour vessels leads to vasogenic oedema
  • Most common metastatic site is at grey-white junction due to vessel calibre change

Common primaries

  • Overall: lung, breast, melanoma, colon
  • Haemorrhagic: Melanoma, RCC, thyroid, choriocarcinoma, testicular

Raised ICP mgmt

  • Dexamethasone, BP management
  • Ventriculostomy and decompression with temporary EVD
  • VP shunt
  • Decompressive craniotomy

Radionecrosis

  • Strong association with treatment volume and dose
  • 6-18 months post RT
  • Fractionated SRS for lesions >2cm reduces risk (Minniti et al)
  • Diagnosis: surveillance MRI, MRA, MR with spectroscopy, biopsy
  • Mgmt: steroids, bevacizumab, Bosweillic acid, resection, laser interstitial thermal therapy has low evidence, HBOT has insufficient evidence (ISRS guidelines)

WBRT

  • 30Gy/10#, 20Gy/5#
  • For mNSCLC pts there is likely no benefit compared to BSC in those with poor PS and limited LE (QUARTZ trial)
  • HA-WBRT and memantine can reduce neurocognitive toxicity
    • HA-WBRT associated with less cognitive decline (executive function, learning and memory) with no difference in OS vs WBRT (Brown et al)
    • Hippocampi D100 ≤9Gy, Dmax <16Gy, mets need to be ≥5mm from hippocampi
    • Memantine reduced time to cognitive decline and probability of cognitive decline. Some results did not reach significance due to patient attrition (RTOG 0614)

SRT

  • <15mm: 20Gy/1#
  • <2cm: 24Gy/3#
  • ≥ 2cm: 30Gy/5#
  • Aiming BED10 of 50Gy, fractionation trades dose intensity for brain protection
  • 1cm – 4cm is approximate size guide for standard linac
  • Subcm lesions can be treated with gamma knife, cyber knife, ZapX
  • Tumour volume more important than number
  • Reduced cognitive decline compared to WBRT (Brown et al)
  • Can be deferred if asymptomatic and being treated with EGFR TKI
  • SRS up to 20 BM: ph III RCT found SRS vs HA-WBRT in 5-20 BM had fewer pt reported symptoms and less interference in function (Aizer et al)
  • pre-op vs post-op SRS: less RN risk, smaller volume, less LM disease risk – no ph III data yet

Cavity SRT

  • <3cm: 24Gy/3#
  • ≥ 3cm: 30Gy/5#
  • Indications for resection: large ≥ 4cm, symptomatic, pathology uncertainty, oligometastatic in young fit patient
  • Surgical resection and post-op WBRT was historically shown to improve OS and LC compared with WBRT alone in patients with a single met and good PS (Patchell I & II)
  • Modern trials have shown post-op SRS provides comparable OS to post-op WBRT with less neurocognitive toxicity but higher distant brain relapse requiring MRI surveillance/salvage (Brown/NCCTG N107C)
  • Post op SRS improves LC when compared to no post-op RT (70% vs 40% - Mahajan et al MDACC)

SRS - cavity

Dose: 24Gy/3#, alternate days, as per ICRU 91, MV, FFF

Pre-sim: advise patient to cease driving, obtain MRI, pre-med antiemetic

Sim: supine, arms down, TPS head shell, 1mm slices, fuse planning MRI

  • Gamma knife requires stereotactic frame immobilisation

Volumes (Soliman et al)

GTV: enhancing residual lesion

HRTV: enhancing surgical cavity on T1 + gad

CTV: +3mm margin + surgical tract

  • +5-10mm extension along bone flap beyond the initial region if in contact with dura pre-operatively
  • +1-5mm margin along sinus if in contact with venous sinus pre-operatively

PTV: +2mm

Infraction motion monitoring required if non-invasive immobilisation frame used

OARs

  • Both brain +PTV and brain – PTV doses are reported
  • brain + PTV:
    • V12Gy <10cc = G2 radionecrosis <15% for single fraction
    • V20 <20cc = G2 radionecrosis <10% for 3#
    • V24 <20cc = G2 radionecrosis <10% for 5#
  • optics: D0.035 <15Gy for 3#; -5Gy for 1#, +5Gy for 5#
  • Brainstem D0.035 <18Gy for 3#; -5Gy for 1#, +5Gy for 5#
  • Cord D0.035 < 17Gy, cochlea Dmax <10Gy for 3#
  • Non coplanar beams can reduce normal brain dose and improve dose fall off and are often preferred in SRS
