Primary tumour: GTV-P to CTV-P (Grégoire 2018)

Draw GTV-P from examination, endoscopy, contrast CT, MRI and PET together. Then grow it twice by 5 mm and edit each step for anatomy: air, uninvolved bone and intact fascia stop the spread of disease.

  • After induction chemotherapy: draw GTV-P on the pre-chemo extent (fused imaging), not the post-chemo remnant.
  • Site-specific rules (larynx cartilage, pre-epiglottic space, tongue base, retromolar trigone) are tabulated in the 2018 paper and are worth having open.

Nasopharynx (Tang 2025; Lee 2018)

  • Use MRI (T1 post-contrast, fat-saturated) for skull base, cavernous sinus, perineural and parapharyngeal spread.
  • High-risk CTV around GTV with a margin, and a low-risk CTV that covers the next anatomical sites in the local stepwise pattern of spread (e.g. whole nasopharynx, retropharyngeal nodes, skull base foramina, pterygoid fossae, posterior nasal cavity / maxillary sinus, parapharyngeal space).
  • The 2025 guideline gives margin design and the approach after induction chemotherapy; open its atlas for the exact boundaries.

Neck node levels (Grégoire 2014)

Which levels to treat

  • Node-negative neck: by primary site and stage, per Grégoire 2018 (Oral Oncol) tables. Oropharynx typically II–IV ± VIIa; oral cavity I–III (± IV); larynx/hypopharynx II–IV ± VI.
  • Node-positive neck (Biau 2019): extend one level beyond involved levels, add VIIb or Ib depending on the involved level, include the adjacent muscle with ENE, and use a margin around GTV-N for the high-dose CTV.
  • NPC: Tang 2025 neck guideline covers sparing of lower neck in N0 sides and handling of equivocal nodes.

The node-positive margins and the extra levels differ by situation: check Biau 2019 Table 2 before finalising.

Post-operative volumes and perineural spread

Rebuild the pre-op tumour on the planning CT: fuse pre-op imaging, read the operation and pathology reports, and talk to the surgeon about flap position and close margins.

Post-op (DAHANCA, PATHOS, flaps), cSCC (HNCIG) and PNI pathways: protocol sections 5 and 7.

Post-op oral cavity and oropharynx (Evans 2025 ESTRO; Evans 2018)

  • High-risk CTV: the pre-op GTV location plus margin, the tumour bed, sites of positive / close margins, and nodal levels with ENE.
  • The flap itself is not a target; include the flap–native tissue junction, where recurrence occurs.
  • Elective CTV: the operated and at-risk undissected levels.

Perineural invasion (Ko 2014; Biau 2019 trigeminal)

  • Named-nerve PNI: trace the nerve from the primary back to the skull base foramen (and to the ganglion for clinical PNI). V2: foramen rotundum, pterygopalatine fossa. V3: foramen ovale. VII: stylomastoid foramen through the temporal bone.
  • Use MRI (thin-slice post-contrast, fat-saturated) to look for nerve thickening or foraminal enhancement.
  • Cutaneous SCC: HNCIG (Porceddu 2020) covers PNI, parotid and nodal levels for post-op RT.

Organs at risk (Brouwer 2015)

Contour the full set for every curative case so you can trade off doses: swallowing structures, salivary glands, oral cavity, cord and brainstem, cochlea, optic apparatus, mandible, larynx.

Tips

  • Swallowing (DARS): superior, middle and inferior constrictors, supraglottic and glottic larynx, cricopharyngeus, cervical oesophagus (Christianen 2011).
  • Parotid: include the deep lobe medial to the mandible; follow Brouwer's figures for the vessels that run through the gland.
  • Oral cavity, lips and buccal mucosa are separate structures in Brouwer: decide which your constraints refer to.
