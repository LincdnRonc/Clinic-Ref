Benign musculoskeletal conditions

Low-dose RT for heterotopic ossification, painful osteoarthritis, plantar fasciitis and early Dupuytren's disease. Use the lowest effective dose and explain the small theoretical cancer risk.

Heterotopic ossification prophylaxis (hip)7/1Within 4 h before surgery, or within 72 h after. 5.5/1 post-op was less effective. Spare the medial third of acetabular hardware
Painful osteoarthritis3/6 (0.5 Gy/fx) 2–3 times a weekA second series may be given. Knee OA sham-controlled trial: 4-month response 42% (sham) vs 70% (3 Gy)
Plantar fasciitis3–6 Gy/6 (0.5–1 Gy/fx) 2–3 times a week; may repeat80% improve with conservative treatment by 12 months

Other benign conditions

Low doses, small fields, and a documented discussion of the small risk of a radiation-induced cancer, especially in younger patients.

Principles

  • Confirm the diagnosis and that standard non-radiation options have been tried or are unsuitable.
  • Shield the thyroid, breast, lens and gonads; use the lowest effective dose and the smallest field.
  • German (DEGRO) guidelines are the main source of dose recommendations for benign disease.
  • Avoid RT for benign disease in pregnancy and, where possible, in children and young adults.
