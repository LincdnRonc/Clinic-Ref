Thymoma and thymic carcinoma

R0 resection is the mainstay of treatment. PORT is for invasive or incompletely resected disease, and more readily for thymic carcinoma. Neoadjuvant RT is not recommended.

  • Stage II: PORT reduces local recurrence from about 30% to 10% (controversial). Stage III: from >50% to <25%, with an OS benefit.
  • About 40% of thymomas recur, usually around 4 y; late recurrences occur.
  • Volumes: GTV = visible tumour or tumour bed with clips. CTV = surgical bed and clips; generally no elective nodes. PTV = CTV + 0.5–2 cm (or ITV + 0.5 cm).

Pleural mesothelioma

Systemic therapy (nivolumab/ipilimumab) is the backbone. RT is mainly palliative. Hemithoracic IMRT after lung-sparing surgery should be given on trial or in expert centres. Prophylactic tract RT is no longer routine.

Role of RT (ASCO 2025)

  • Palliation: 8/1, 20/5 or 30/10.
  • Tracts: do not routinely give prophylactic tract RT (SMART). Treat the tract if it is histologically positive. Historically 21/3.
  • After EPP: hemithoracic IMRT 50–54 Gy (R0) or 54–60 Gy (positive margins). Lung V20 <7% goal; fatal pneumonitis occurred even with V20 <20%.
  • After pleurectomy/decortication: hemithoracic IMRT is toxic; use only on trial. Do not give neoadjuvant RT if lung-sparing surgery is planned.

Thoracic re-irradiation

Re-irradiation can be curative for isolated recurrence. Base the decision on target size, location, time since prior RT and cumulative OAR dose. Be most careful centrally.

Approach (ARS)

  • Expected failure: after early-stage SBRT ≤10% local and 10–20% nodal. After LA-NSCLC, 25–50% local failure by 2 y.
  • Peripheral recurrence after SBRT: SBRT usually appropriate; daily conventional RT may be appropriate.
  • Central or nodal recurrence: daily conventional chemoRT usually appropriate; SBRT usually not.
  • Cumulative limits (EQD2, α/β 3): spinal cord 75 Gy, oesophagus 100 Gy. Reirradiation cord Dmax <57 Gy has been used.

Cardiac radioablation for ventricular tachycardia

A single 25 Gy fraction to the arrhythmogenic scar can substantially reduce refractory VT episodes. It is still investigational; use only in a cardiology-led programme.

Evidence
