Usual treatment thresholds on the day

  • Typical parameters to proceed with a weekly concurrent dose: neutrophils ≥1.0–1.5 × 10⁹/L, platelets ≥100 × 10⁹/L, adequate creatinine clearance for the drug. The exact thresholds are in each eviQ protocol. Verify
  • Chemotherapy is usually given before the RT fraction on the same day; do not delay RT for a held chemotherapy dose unless unwell.
  • Missed concurrent doses are generally omitted, not made up after RT finishes.
  • Febrile neutropenia: RT is usually paused until afebrile and recovering; replan the overall treatment time (see the gap calculator in Tools).

Radiation recall and drugs to keep away from RT

  • Recall: anthracyclines, taxanes, gemcitabine, capecitabine, methotrexate, vemurafenib; can occur weeks to years after RT. Manage with topical or systemic steroid and stopping the drug if severe.
  • Avoid concurrently: doxorubicin, full-dose gemcitabine, bleomycin with lung fields, methotrexate with or after cranial RT, dactinomycin.
  • Usually acceptable to continue with palliative RT: endocrine therapy, bone agents, most single-agent platinum or fluoropyrimidine schedules outside the field of concern. Practice varies: agree with the medical oncologist.
  • Simple rule when unsure: hold a cytotoxic for about a week before and after palliative RT that includes bowel, lung, oesophagus or large marrow volumes. Verify

Managing toxicity during chemo-RT

First steps in clinic. Follow eviQ and local febrile neutropenia pathways.

Febrile neutropenia

  • Temperature ≥38.0 °C with neutrophils below 1.0 × 10⁹/L (or expected to fall): medical emergency; first antibiotic dose within 30–60 minutes per the local sepsis pathway. Verify
  • eviQ toxicity management has the immediate management pathway.

Nausea and vomiting

  • High emetic risk (cisplatin): NK1 antagonist + 5-HT3 antagonist + dexamethasone, ± olanzapine; delayed-phase cover for days 2–4.
  • Breakthrough: metoclopramide 10 mg tds, olanzapine 2.5–5 mg nocte, cyclizine; check for constipation from ondansetron.
  • Check renal function, magnesium and potassium in anyone vomiting on cisplatin.

Cisplatin: kidneys, ears, nerves

  • Rising creatinine: withhold, hydrate, recheck; change to carboplatin if clearance does not recover.
  • New tinnitus or hearing loss: audiometry; change to carboplatin or stop.
  • Replace magnesium (drives refractory hypokalaemia and hypocalcaemia).

Fluoropyrimidines

  • Grade ≥2 diarrhoea (4–6 stools over baseline) or hand–foot syndrome: stop capecitabine until grade ≤1, loperamide, then restart at a reduced dose.
  • Severe early toxicity (first cycle mucositis, diarrhoea, neutropenia): think DPYD deficiency; uridine triacetate is the antidote where available.
  • Chest pain: stop, ECG and troponin, do not rechallenge without cardiology advice.

Skin, mucosa and nutrition

  • Chemo-RT mucositis and oesophagitis: early dietitian review, analgesic ladder (see supportive drugs), low threshold for enteral feeding in head and neck.
  • Cetuximab rash versus radiation dermatitis: see agents table.
  • Weekly weight; more than 10% loss should trigger feeding review.
