Glioblastoma and high-grade glioma

ESTRO/EANO 2025 recommendations and RTOG 1205.

Selection

  • MDT decision weighing risks, benefits and alternatives (Rec 1).
  • Consider with KPS ≥60 and >6 months from the first course, regardless of age or MGMT status (Rec 2).
  • After gross total resection, only with favourable prognostic factors (Rec 3).
  • Lesions generally ≤5–6 cm; larger, multifocal or leptomeningeal disease is excluded. Prospective data exist only up to 6 cm (Statement 4).
  • Expect longer progression-free survival, not proven overall survival benefit (Statement 1).

Reirradiation evidence is mostly retrospective and consensus-based. Figures here are cited to their source; anything marked Verify was entered from memory. Constraints are starting points for an individual decision, not guarantees of safety. Have a colleague and physics review each case.

Imaging and volumes

  • Contrast-enhanced T1 MRI is required; advanced MRI or amino-acid PET to separate recurrence from pseudoprogression or necrosis (Rec 4).
  • GTV: enhancing T1 lesion, new or progressive T2/FLAIR change and amino-acid PET-avid regions (Rec 6).
  • GTV-to-CTV margin is not mandatory; 3–5 mm optional. CTV-to-PTV at most 3 mm (Rec 7).
  • Rigid registration for delineation and dose accumulation (Rec 5).

Systemic therapy and evidence

  • RTOG 1205 (Tsien 2023, n=170, progression ≥6 months after chemo-RT): bevacizumab ± 35 Gy/10. Median OS 10.1 v 9.7 months (HR 0.98); median PFS 7.1 v 3.8 months; 6-month PFS 54% v 29%; acute grade ≥3 treatment-related events 5%, no delayed high-grade events.
  • No routine concurrent or maintenance systemic therapy can be recommended (Statements 8–9); target and dose do not change if it is given (Rec 14).
  • Follow-up: MRI every 3 months, toxicity check at 6 weeks; do not call progression on the 6-week scan (Rec 16).

Sources: Andratschke 2025 (ESTRO/EANO); Tsien et al. J Clin Oncol 2023;41:1285–95.

Brain metastases: a second course

Repeat SRS, SRS after WBRT, and repeat WBRT.

Repeat SRS to the same lesion

  • Meta-analysis (Singh; 347 patients, 462 lesions): 1-year local control 69%, radionecrosis 16%.
  • Multicentre NSCLC series (Kowalchuk 2021): second SRS median 18 Gy margin dose; 1-year local control 79%; radionecrosis 20%, symptomatic 7%. Risk rose with maximum dose ≥40 Gy at either course and second-course V12Gy >9 cm³.
  • Local control was better for tumours ≤1 cm³.
  • First confirm it is tumour, not necrosis: perfusion MRI, amino-acid PET or resection.
  • Consider fractionated SRS, resection plus cavity treatment, or LITT for larger lesions. Verify

SRS after WBRT

  • Prospective phase 2 (Maranzano 2019): KPS ≥70, lesions ≤20 mm, dose ≤20 Gy (median 18 Gy), median 15 months after WBRT: 2-year control 81%, no radionecrosis, median OS 14 months.
  • An earlier series from the same group with wider selection had 6% radionecrosis.

Repeat WBRT

  • Reserve for multiple recurrences when SRS is not feasible, good performance status and a long interval. Commonly 20–25 Gy in 10 fractions. Verify
  • Expect modest benefit and cognitive cost; discuss best supportive care.

Spine metastases and the spinal cord

ESTRO–ISRS 2026 recommendations, Sahgal and Nieder limits.

SBRT reirradiation: ESTRO–ISRS 2026

  • SBRT reirradiation after conventional RT or prior SBRT is effective and safe in selected patients, and is a recommended option.
  • Evidence base: 20 studies, 1538 metastases; median reRT dose 24 Gy in 2 fractions (range 16–30 Gy in 1–5) after a median 30 Gy/10.
  • Baseline SINS strongly recommended; SBRT only for stable or potentially unstable spines (SINS 0–12). Prior surgery is not an exclusion.
  • MRI for cord and thecal sac delineation; CT myelography if MRI is not possible.
  • Use the HyTEC 2021 cord constraints; repeat SBRT to the same level did not carry more adverse events than SBRT after conventional RT.

Earlier ISRS review (Myrehaug 2017): median 1-year local control 76%; vertebral compression fracture 12%.

Second SBRT after SBRT failure

  • Thibault 2015 (40 patients, 56 segments): most often 30 Gy in 4 fractions (range 20–35 Gy in 2–5) after a first course of median 24 Gy/2; 1-year local control 81%; median OS 10 months.

Cord compression, second course

  • Conventional reirradiation (8 Gy/1 to 20 Gy/5) improved motor function in about 40% in one series (Rades 2005).
  • Keep cumulative cord BED within the Nieder conditions where survival is expected beyond a few months; consider surgery or SBRT (with separation) for radioresistant histology.

Painful bone metastases

NCIC CTG SC.20.

Retreatment for pain

  • SC.20 (Chow 2014; n=850; TROG participated AU): 8 Gy/1 v 20 Gy in multiple fractions (5 fractions, or 8 if prior multi-fraction RT to spine or whole pelvis).
  • Pain response at 2 months, intention to treat: 28% v 32% (non-inferior). Per protocol: 45% v 51% (non-inferiority not met).
  • Less acute toxicity with 8 Gy: lack of appetite 56% v 66%, diarrhoea 23% v 31%.
  • Practical: 8 Gy single fraction is reasonable; expect about half of assessable patients to respond. Wait at least 4 weeks after the first course before judging failure. Verify
  • Spine: account for cord dose as above. Consider SBRT when durable control matters.

Source: Chow E et al. Lancet Oncol 2014;15:164–71. eviQ protocol 268 summarises the same trial.
