Basal and cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma

Definitive RT is an excellent option where surgery would be disfiguring. PORT is for gross PNI, uncorrectable close or positive margins, recurrence, T3+ SCC and nodal disease. Adjuvant cemiplimab after PORT is now an option for high-risk SCC (C-POST).

Post-op cSCC (HNCIG) and perineural pathways: Head and neck contouring guide, section 7.

PORT indications (ASTRO 2019)

  • Gross (clinical or radiological) PNI.
  • Close or positive margins that cannot be fixed with more surgery, especially SCC.
  • Recurrence after a margin-negative resection.
  • T3+ SCC; desmoplastic or infiltrative SCC with immunosuppression.
  • BCC: conditionally, if involving bone or infiltrating muscle.
  • Nodes: after neck dissection, nodal RT may be omitted for a single small node (<3 cm) without ENE. Adjuvant nodal RT 60–66 Gy; elective 50/25 or 54/30.
  • Concurrent carboplatin does not help after resection (TROG 05.01).

Melanoma

Adjuvant nodal RT improves regional control but not survival, and adjuvant immunotherapy has largely replaced it. Consider RT for high-risk nodal disease when systemic therapy is not used, for desmoplastic melanoma and for palliation.

When to use RT

  • Adjuvant nodal RT (TROG 02.01 criteria): consider after lymphadenectomy for high-risk features such as ENE. Also thick (>4 mm) ulcerated primaries or satellitosis, and SLNB-positive without completion dissection.
  • Desmoplastic melanoma: consider adjuvant RT to the primary (TROG 08.09 RTN2 is testing this).
  • Doses: adjuvant 48/20 (TROG 02.01) or 60–66 Gy conventional; 30/5 is also used. Definitive 64–70 Gy or 50–57.5/20–23. Palliative 30/10 or 20–30/5.

Merkel cell carcinoma

Merkel cell carcinoma is very radiosensitive. Give adjuvant RT to the primary bed with wide margins and assess regional nodes in all patients. Use immunotherapy for advanced disease.
