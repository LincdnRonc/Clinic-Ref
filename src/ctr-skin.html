Primary skin cancer: BCC and cSCC

There is no single contouring atlas. Australian practice uses clinical margins that depend on how well-defined the lesion is and on its risk features. Draw the GTV in clinic: palpate, use good light and a dermatoscope, and photograph the marked lesion.

Volumes

VolumeDefinition
GTVVisible and palpable tumour, marked on the skin (wire on CT). Depth from examination, ultrasound, CT or MRI for thick or fixed lesions.
CTVGTV + a radial margin, commonly about 5–10 mm for small, well-defined BCC and 10–20 mm for high-risk SCC, infiltrative or morphoeic BCC, or recurrent lesions. Deep margin to the next anatomical barrier (fascia, perichondrium, periosteum) or by imaging.
Field (electrons or orthovoltage)Electrons: the field edge goes beyond the CTV because the isodoses constrict at depth (about 1 cm, more for higher energies). Orthovoltage: a smaller extra margin is needed. Bolus to bring the dose to the skin surface.

These margins are ranges used in practice, not from one guideline. Check your department's skin protocol.

Post-operative cutaneous SCC of the head and neck (HNCIG, Porceddu 2020)

Rebuild the pre-op lesion using clinical photographs, the operation note, the pathology report and pre-op imaging. The graft or flap does not mark the tumour site.

CTV

  • Primary: the surgical bed with a margin around the original tumour site, wider where margins were close or positive.
  • Nodes: treat the involved parotid and neck basin. For parotid involvement, include the parotid and the upper neck levels it drains to. Choose levels using the facial site's drainage (anterior face → parotid, Ib, II; scalp and ear → parotid, II, V, retroauricular and occipital).
  • Perineural invasion: named-nerve or clinical PNI: trace the nerve back to the skull base (see the head and neck protocol, Ko 2014, and the trigeminal atlas, Biau 2019). Use MRI to look for nerve enhancement.

Merkel cell carcinoma and melanoma nodal RT

Both rely on trial protocols rather than atlases.

Merkel cell carcinoma

  • Primary: wide margins around the scar or tumour, because of in-transit spread (larger than for SCC; often several centimetres where anatomy allows).
  • Nodes: the draining basin, or a basin with a positive sentinel node, by trial or departmental protocol.

Melanoma: adjuvant nodal RT (ANZMTG 01.02 / TROG 02.01)

  • The dissected nodal basin (parotid and neck, axilla, or groin), defined from the operative bed, clips and scar, as in the trial's field definitions. Now rarely used, because of adjuvant systemic therapy.

Check the trial protocols for basin borders.
