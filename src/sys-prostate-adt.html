Sequencing with RT

  • Neoadjuvant ADT usually 2–3 months before RT in older trials; MARCAP sequencing analysis favoured concurrent and adjuvant ADT when whole-pelvis RT is not used.
  • Flare cover with bicalutamide when starting an LHRH agonist in metastatic or bulky disease.
  • ARPIs continue through RT.
  • PSA and testosterone at baseline, 3–6 months, then at each review; testosterone recovery after stopping takes about 6–12 months after 6 months of ADT and can exceed 2 years after long courses, especially over age 70. Verify

Managing ADT side-effects

Tell the patient, tell the GP, and set up monitoring at the first injection.

Baseline and monitoring

  • Blood pressure, weight, fasting glucose or HbA1c, lipids; repeat at 3–6 months then yearly.
  • DEXA at baseline if ADT planned for ≥6–12 months or other risk factors; FRAX; calcium and vitamin D.
  • Cardiovascular risk review with the GP; exercise prescription.
  • With abiraterone: potassium, LFTs and BP fortnightly for 3 months then monthly. Verify

Hot flushes

  • Affect most men on ADT.
  • Venlafaxine 37.5–75 mg daily; medroxyprogesterone 20 mg daily or cyproterone 50–100 mg daily are more effective (Irani 2010) but cyproterone carries hepatic and meningioma risk and may affect PSA interpretation. Verify
  • Gabapentin up to 900 mg/day; oxybutynin 2.5–5 mg bd.
  • Acupuncture has supportive data; intermittent ADT where appropriate.

Bone

  • Bone density falls most in the first year.
  • Treat if T-score ≤ −2.5, fragility fracture, or high FRAX: denosumab 60 mg 6-monthly, zoledronic acid 5 mg yearly, or alendronate 70 mg weekly. Verify
  • This is the osteoporosis dose, not the bone-metastasis dose (see bone agents).

Metabolic and cardiovascular

  • Weight gain, sarcopenia, insulin resistance, lipid rise; small increase in cardiovascular events, mostly in men with prior disease.
  • Supervised resistance and aerobic exercise has the best evidence for fatigue, body composition and mood.
  • Consider a GnRH antagonist in men with recent cardiovascular events (HERO was hypothesis-generating; PRONOUNCE did not confirm).

Sexual, breast, mood and cognition

  • Loss of libido and erectile dysfunction: PDE5 inhibitor, vacuum device, intracavernosal therapy; refer early.
  • Gynaecomastia with bicalutamide 150 mg: tamoxifen 10–20 mg daily, or prophylactic breast-bud RT (commonly 8–10 Gy in 1 fraction; 12–15 Gy/3 also used). Verify
  • Depression and cognitive slowing: screen; anaemia (normochromic, mild) is expected.

Drug interactions worth knowing

  • Enzalutamide and apalutamide induce CYP3A4/2C9/2C19: lower levels of warfarin, DOACs, some statins, opioids, clopidogrel activation changes.
  • Abiraterone inhibits CYP2D6; needs prednisolone to prevent mineralocorticoid excess; stress-dose steroids when unwell.
  • Relugolix: avoid P-gp inhibitors at the same time of day.
