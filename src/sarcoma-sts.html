Extremity and trunk soft tissue sarcoma

For grade 2–3 tumours, preoperative 50/25 then limb-sparing surgery 3–6 weeks later. Pre-op RT gives the same control as post-op RT, with less fibrosis and oedema but twice the wound complications. Grade 1 tumours with clear margins need surgery alone.

Decisions

  • Grade 1: wide excision alone (LR <15%). RT for positive margin, recurrence, or where salvage surgery would not be possible.
  • Grade 2–3: pre-op 50/25 → surgery. Hypofractionated 42.75/15 (HYPORT-STS) or 5-fraction schedules (UCLA, Poland) are options.
  • Post-op boost after pre-op RT: not proven to help (no randomised trial). Consider 16–18 Gy for R1 and 20–26 Gy for R2.
  • Post-op RT (if not given pre-op): 50 Gy then cone-down to 60–66 Gy; ≥64 Gy for positive margins. Indicated for margin <1 cm; consider for >10 cm.
  • Chemo: doxorubicin + ifosfamide for selected high-risk patients (meta-analysis OS OR 0.56).

Volumes and limits

  • Pre-op (RTOG 0630): CTV50 = GTV (including suspicious T2 oedema) + 3 cm longitudinal and 1.5 cm radial (G2–3 and ≥8 cm); + 2 cm and 1 cm for G1 or <8 cm. Edit at bone and fascia. PTV 5 mm with daily IGRT (1–1.5 cm without).
  • Post-op: CTV50 = bed + scar + pre-op GTV + 4–5 cm longitudinal and 1.5–2 cm radial (VORTEX tested 5 cm/2 cm → 66 Gy at 2 cm). Boost: pre-op GTV + 2 cm longitudinal and 1.5 cm radial.
  • Spare a 1.5–2 cm strip of limb circumference; never treat the entire circumference to >50 Gy.
  • Bone (fracture risk): V40 <64%, mean <37 Gy, max <59 Gy. Skin/subcutaneous V20 <50% and bone V50 <50% (RTOG 0630).

Retroperitoneal sarcoma

Complete en bloc surgery at a sarcoma centre is the key. STRASS showed no overall benefit from pre-op RT, but there may be a benefit in liposarcoma. Post-op RT is not recommended.

Pre-op RT if used

  • Dose: 45–50 Gy in 1.8–2 Gy fractions (STRASS: 50.4/28). Optional IORT boost 10–15/1.
  • CTV: GTV + 1.5 cm, cropped at bone and retroperitoneal compartment. PTV = ITV + 5 mm with IGRT (9–12 mm without).
  • OARs: stomach and duodenum V45 ≤100%, V50 <50%, max 56 Gy. Bowel bag V15 ≤830 cc, V45 <195 cc (possibly too conservative). Usually one kidney is in the field; document function of the other.
STRASS (EORTC 62092)266 · 75% liposarcoma · ± 50.4/28 → en bloc surgeryNo overall benefit in abdominal RFS (3 y about 60%). Liposarcoma subgroup: 3 y abdominal RFS 60 → 72% (HR 0.64).

Chordoma, chondrosarcoma, desmoid and Kaposi sarcoma

Chordoma and chondrosarcoma need high doses (≥70 Gy, usually protons). Desmoid tumours are observed first; RT 50–56 Gy for progressive unresectable disease.

Chordoma and chondrosarcoma

  • Chordoma: 72–76 Gy RBE (5 y LC 60–80%). CTV1 54 Gy RBE = pre-op tumour + 1–2 cm.
  • Chondrosarcoma: 70–72 Gy RBE. R1/R2 or inoperable ≥70 Gy.
  • Osteosarcoma: RT if inoperable (70 Gy) or after R1/R2 resection (64–68 Gy).

Desmoid tumour

  • Active surveillance first. Surgery first-line only for abdominal wall tumours.
  • RT: 50–56 Gy for progressive unresectable disease (3 y LC about 80% with 56 Gy); post-op 50 Gy only in selected cases. R1 alone does not mandate RT.

Kaposi sarcoma

  • HIV-associated: start antiretroviral therapy first.
  • RT: 8–12/1 for palliation, or 30/15. Total skin electrons 4 Gy weekly × 6–8 for widespread disease.
