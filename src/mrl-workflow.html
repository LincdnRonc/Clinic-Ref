Online adaptive workflow

On the day you are editing, not delineating from scratch. Spend your minutes on the target and the dose-limiting OAR nearest the PTV, and leave distant structures as propagated.

Before the patient is on the table

  • Review the reference plan: which OAR is dose-limiting, how close it is, and whether the PTV was compromised.
  • Look at the reference T2 and the previous fraction's contours so the daily image looks familiar.
  • Note two or three vascular landmarks next to the GTV for when the lesion is faint.

Editing propagated contours (ATS)

  1. Registration first: check the rigid match on the target organ (liver, pancreas, prostate), not bone.
  2. GTV next: confirm or edit in all three planes.
  3. Closest OARs within a ring: many groups recontour only within about 2 cm of the PTV (e.g. a 2 cm isotropic PTV expansion in liver SMART, Mayinger 2021). Follow departmental protocol.
  4. Deformation fails at bowel: propagated stomach and duodenum are least reliable; check every slice near the PTV, especially where gas and fluid have moved.
  5. Don't chase distant edges: they barely affect optimisation and cost time.

Gas and bulk density

  • Unity calculates dose on the daily MR using bulk electron-density overrides per structure, not real HU.
  • Caution New gas pockets near the PTV (stomach, duodenum, colon, rectum) are not in the reference densities. Follow your physics protocol on contouring and overriding them: at 1.5 T the electron return effect changes dose at tissue–air interfaces.

Time and safety

  • Work in a fixed order every time so nothing is missed under pressure.
  • Second check (RT or physicist) on the GTV and dose-limiting OAR.
  • Look at the verification scan or cine before beam-on: stomach filling and peristalsis change over a 20–40 minute session.
