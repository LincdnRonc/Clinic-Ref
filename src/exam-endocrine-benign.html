Oncocytic carcinoma of thyroid (Hurthle cell):

  • FTC variant with higher tendency for LRR
  • Less iodine uptake than FTC
  • Slightly worse survival – 10 yr OS 80%

Work-up

Thyroid US concerning features:

  • Taller than wide
  • Diffuse, infiltrative margins
  • Solid or heterogenous
  • Microcalcifications
  • Capsule invasion or ETE

I-131:

  • Cold nodule has 10% chance of malignancy
  • Hot nodule 3% change of malignancy
  • If not a well diff thyroid cancer → do FDG PET scan

Management

Biopsy

  • FNA:
  • Cytology allows diagnosis of PTC or suspicious lesion (Bethesda III-IV)
  • Less invasive than core bx
  • Both core and FNA are unable to dx FTC as this needs extensive vascular and capsule sampling for invasion
  • Less tissue than core for molecular testing
  • Consider diagnostic hemithyroidectomy if sufficient suspicion / FTC

Thyroidectomy

  • Hemithyroidectomy for diagnostic purposes OR therapeutic if:
    • Unifocal
    • no ETE or capsular invasion
    • <4cm
    • No LN
  • Total thyroidectomy
    • Oncocytic carcinoma
    • Prior RT (↑ recurrence risk)
    • Doesn’t fit above

Cervical LN mgmt:

  • LND of VI AND VII if:
    • T4
    • N+
    • ETE
  • Add lateral neck dissection if involved
  • SLNBx not routinely used

Adjuvant RAI – I-131

  • Indications (as per ATA):
    • To ablate remnant thyroid tissue do TG can be used for surveillance, to eradicate microscopic residual disease/micromets, to allow use of RAI imaging as surveillance
    • Aggressive histology: tall cell, columnar, hobnail
    • ETE
    • Multiple nodes or node >3cm
    • FTC with extensive vascular invasion >4 foci
    • R2 resection
    • M1
    • Post-op TG high
  • Can perform a diagnostic I-123 scan prior to assess iodine uptake
  • S/e of RAI: nausea, sialadenitis/xerostomia, dysgeusia, lacrimal duct obstruction, myelosuppression, temporary gonadal dysfunction, dose-related secondary malignancy risk.
  • CI: pregnancy, breastfeeding

Adjuvant EBRT:

  • Rarely used in differentiated thyroid cancer
  • Indicated if:
    • No Iodine uptake
    • Unresectable disease
    • Residual or recurrent disease not amenable to RAI or re-resection

Anaplastic thyroid cancer:

  • Assess and manage airway!
    • Steroids, FNE, tracheostomy, bronchoscopy + tracheal stent, surgical decompression, RT once airway secured
  • If resectable (rare): surgery → prompt adj RT 66-60Gy (R1-R0) → adjuvant systemic therapy
  • If borderline resectable: neoadjuvant systemic treatment → reassess
  • If unresectable (common): systemic treatment + RT
    • Definitive RT 66Gy/33# including level 2-4, VI and upper mediastinal nodes to carina with concurrent carbo/taxol
    • Palliative RT 20Gy/5# for local control, given airway compromise risk)
  • Systemic therapy:
    • If BRAFm: dabrafenib & trametinib upfront
    • Others: carboplatin, paclitaxel

F/u:

  • Neck US, tumour markers

Dose: 66Gy/33# with concurrent carboplatin/paclitaxel

Pre-sim: check tumour markers, discuss at MDT, airway assessment, SP/DT

Sim: supine, chin extended, TPS for head and shoulders, 1cm bolus over neck, fuse PET

Volumes:

GTV

CTV6600: GTV + 5mm

PTV6600: +5mm

CTV 5400: elective central neck nodes (level VI) + upper mediastinal (VII) from hyoid to aortic arch

PTV: +5mm

RAI

I-131:

  • B and gamma emission, t ½ 8 days, mean energy 190 KeV

Indications:

  • Ablate remnant thyroid tissue
  • Adjuvant treatment for DTC where there is high risk of micromets
  • Recurrent or metastatic DTC

Prep:

  • Low iodine diet for 2 weeks
  • Exclude pregnancy
  • Achieve TSH stimulation (withhold thyroxine or give TSH)

Administration:

  • Oral tablet given in radioisotope protected room
  • Follow time, distance, shielding and contaminated waste removal protocols
  • Mostly excreted via urine – hydrate, void, separate toilet, double flush, wash hands
  • DC home after ~ 1 week (radiation levels at 1m <25 μSv/h)
  • Advise separation, avoid children/pregnant women

Post-RAI

  • I-131 scan 1 week post treatment to identify areas of disease/remnant tissue (background I-131 is cleared but activity in iodine uptaking tissue is retained)

Thyroid eye disease

Epidemiology · Pathophysiology · Management · EBRT · Technique

Epidemiology

I: Assoc with grave’s disease

A: 20-40yo

G: F

R:

RF:

E: poorly controlled Grave’s disease, smoking

Sx:

  • Bilateral
  • Diplopia (use prisms or eye patch)
  • Conjunctival inflammation
  • Ophthalmoplegia
  • Exopthalmos
  • Periorbital oedema

Pathophysiology

  1. TSHR Ab target extraocular orbital fibroblasts and adipocytes
  2. Activation of fibroblasts → chronic inflammation, release of glycosaminoglycans
  3. Oedema and fibrosis of extra-ocular muscles
  4. Vision changes, ophthalmoplegia, dry eyes, exophthalmos

RT:

  • Sterilises inflammatory T cells
  • Reduces inflammatory cytokine response

Management

Consider biopsy to confirm diagnosis, ophthalmologist and endocrinologist review

Indications:

  • active moderate–severe Graves orbitopathy, especially diplopia/motility restriction
  • steroid-refractory/contraindicated or adjunct to IV steroids

Contraindications:

  • pregnancy, diabetic or hypertensive retinopathy, age <35 relative, prior orbital RT

2021 EUGOGO guidelines:

  • Vision threatening → emergency decompression surgery
  • Control Grave’s disease – carbimazole
  • First line high dose IV methylprednisolone over 12 weeks
  • Local injections of triamcinolone if steroids CI
  • 2nd line orbital radiotherapy for active mod-severe disease in presence of diplopia or extraocular motility restriction
    • limited number of small RCT (<50 pts each) - findings controversial but suggest ~50% may experience some benefit, particularly with eye movement and diplopia
    • RT can be combined with steroids
  • 2nd line systemic Rx: cyclosporine, azathioprine, tocilizumab, tiruximab
  • Strabismus surgery for extraocular muscle dysfunction

EBRT

Dose: 20Gy/10#, VMAT, daily treatment, BD compensation

Pre-sim: discuss at MDT, baseline ophthalmologist assessment, endocrinologist review

Sim: Supine, arms down, TPS head shell, fuse MRI

Volumes:

GTV: abnormal orbital soft tissue

CTV: entire posterior orbit, exclude globe

PTV: +3mm

Technique

  • Partial arc to avoid beam entry through contralateral eye if unilateral

Daily CBCT with bone match

OARS:

  • Contralateral lens <5Gy , ipsilateral <20Gy, ALARA
  • Globe ALARA
  • Optic nerve / chiasm <20Gy
  • Pituitary ALARA

Toxicities

Acute: peri-orbital oedema, opthalmoplegia, conjunctivitis, fatigue, symptom flare

Late: dry eye, cataract, optic neuropathy, retinopathy (risk ↑ with diabetes), 2nd malignancy, very rare hypopituitarism

Heterotopic ossification

Epidemiology · Pathophysiology · Management · EBRT

Epidemiology

I: up to 20% post hip athroplasty

A: elderly

G: nil
R: nil

RF:

E: orthopaedic surgery, neurological injury, fracture, soft tissue burn

I:

G: fibrodysplasia ossificans

I:

Sx:

-pain, restricted movement, swelling around affected region

- Classic presentation in hip after THR

Pathophysiology

  1. Trauma to soft tissue surrounding bone
  2. Inflammatory response with pro-inflammatory cytokine release (TNF-a, IL-1, IL-6) attracts mesenchymal stem cells
  3. Activation into osteoblasts
  4. Production of bony spurs in area where bone does not normally exist

RT:

  • Inflammatory cytokine → cause mesenchymal to osteoblast differentiation
  • RT inhibits mesenchymal cell differentiation

Brooker grading system:

  • I-IV, represents degree of bony spur growth and fusion around hip joint
  • IV – ankylosis

Management

  • Surgical excision – 12-18 months after initial surgery to avoid further HO
    • Used in conjunction with preventative measures
  • Preventative measures:
    • NSAID – caution with GI and renal toxicity
    • 8Gy/1# RT 8 hours prior to or 24 hours post op (up to 72 hrs post-op ok).
    • RT and NSAID are equally effective.

EBRT

Dose: 8Gy/1# 24 hours post-surgery, AP/PA

Pre-sim: co-ordinate timing with orthopaedic surgery

Sim: Supine, arms on chest, vac bag

Field:

Cover entire acetabulum, superior, ilium, femoral head and lesser trochanter, proximal 1/3rd of hip replacement with 2-3cm margin to cover periarticular tissue at risk

Block out bladder

Tox:

Acute: fatigue, impaired wound healing, dermatitis

Late: skin change – fibrosis, telangiectasia, minor fracture risk, 2nd malignancy

Keloid

Epidemiology · Pathophysiology · Management · EBRT · Toxicities

Epidemiology

I: common

A: <30yo
G: F

R: African, Hispanic, asian

RF:

E: prv keloid scar, piercings – particularly ear lobe, upper body, areas of high skin tension, dark skin, hormonal fluctuation

I:

G: fhx of keloid scar

I:

Pathophysiology

  1. Trauma to soft tissue
  2. Local inflammatory response with pro-inflammatory cytokine release (TNF-a, IL-1, IL-6, TGF-B)
  3. Activation of fibroblasts
  4. Deposition of collagen and reduced collagen breakdown
  5. Fibrous tissue which grows beyond the wound size

Occurs over weeks – months, unlikely to regress spontaneously

RT:

-Sterilises fibroblasts

Management

  1. Surgical excision
  2. Up to 80% recurrence risk
  3. Silicone get sheets/dressings
  4. 12-24hr/day
  5. More effective in prevention than treatment, modest improvement in existing keloid
  6. Intralesional corticosteroid injection
  7. every 4-6 weeks, 3-6 times
  8. 50% will recur by 5 yrs if used as solitary Rx
  9. Cryotherapy + steroid injection
  10. Adjuvant RT 16Gy/4# to 5mm around scar 24 hours post-op (up to 72hrs is ok)
  11. Reduces recurrence risk to <20%

EBRT

Dose: 16Gy/4# to scar, orthovoltage 125kV

Pre-sim: wound closed, time with operation

Mark-up: 10mm around scar, consider including entire surgical bed, custom Pb shield to block surrounding area, Pb lead behind ear

Toxicities

Acute: dermatitis, fatigue, pain, delayed wound healing

Late: skin change – telangiectasia, hypo/hyperpigmentation, atrophy, 2nd malignancy

AVM

Epidemiology · Pathophysiology · Management · SRS · Toxicities

Epidemiology

I: Rare

A: 20-40y

G: M

R: nil

RF:

E: prior haemorrhage ↑ re-bleed risk

I:

G: Hereditary haemorrhagic telangectasia (HHT)

I:

Sx

-Intracranial – most commonly presents with haemorrhage – neurology, seizure

-Spinal – gradual progressive myelopathy

Risk

  • 2-3% annual bleeding risk
  • Risk increases if:
  • Exclusive deep venous drainage
  • Prior bleed (4-5%)

Pathophysiology

  1. Congenital abnormal connection between artery and vein without intervening capillary bed
  2. High pressure flow → tension on vessel walls
  3. Exclusive deep venous outflow increases haemorrhage risk further

RT

-Gradual obliteration of nidus:

  • Vessel thickening
  • Progressive thrombosis
  • Endothelial cell damage

-Response period of up to 2-3 yrs

-Haemorrhage risk remains until obliteration

Speltzer-Martin supplemental grading

  • Predicts surgical morbidity/mortality
  • Age, size, eloquent vs non-eloquent area, superficial vs deep venous drainage, prior bleed, compaction of nidus
  • Score of ≥6 is high risk → surgery not recommended

Management

Speltzer-Martin <6

  • Surgery
  • Offers immediate cure

Inoperable, <3.5cm:

  • SRS: 16Gy-20Gy/1#
  • 16Gy for >3cm, 20Gy for <3cm
  • 25Gy/5# for large/close to OAR
  • 70-80% obliteration rate. For larger ones, success rate drops from 30-70%
  • Can take 3-5 years for obliteration

Speltzer-Martin grade VI+:

  • Conservative/palliative approach

Endovascular embolization:

  • Adjunct to surgery or SRS to reduce nidus flow and size

SRS

Dose: 20Gy/1#, SRS as per ICRU 91

Pre-sim: MRI, discuss at MDT with Nsx, high volume centre

Sim: supine, arms down, TPS for head, fuse MRI OR on treat on MR linac

Volumes: Nidus

Toxicities

Acute: oedema, headaches, seizure

Late: radiation necrosis, neurological deficit, cyst formation

Other benign

Fibrotic

Fibrotic

  • Dupytren’s/ledderhose: 15Gy/5# + 15Gy/5# split course over 2-3 months. Or 30Gy/10#. Stops fibroblast and myofibroblast activation/proliferation
  • Peyronies disease: 30Gy/15#, 20Gy/10#. Resolves acute inflammatory phase

Inflammatory

  • Reduce inflammation, pain, prevent further damage
  • OA: 3Gy/6# or 6Gy/6#
  • Plantar fascitis: 6Gy/6#, 6Gy/12#, 3Gy/6#

Sialorrhea

  • 10Gy/2# to bilateral submandibular and inferior 2/3rd of bilateral parotid
  • ALS, Parkison’s, stroke (neurological conditions)
  • SP review, Anticholinergic meds -atropine, hycosine, surgery, Botox

Gynaecomastia

  • 12Gy/2# or 10Gy/1# to all glandular tissue. Prophylactic dose. Reduces onset and pain. 20Gy/5# if established.
  • Other: tamoxifen, stop causative medication, excision, liposuction, change ADT (classically assoc with bicalutamide)
