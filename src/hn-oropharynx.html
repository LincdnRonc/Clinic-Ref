Oropharynx: who needs what (ASTRO 2024, HPV+)

Most HPV+ OPC is cured with either TORS ± adjuvant RT or definitive (chemo)RT. Choose so the patient gets one modality where possible. Cisplatin remains standard; cetuximab is inferior. De-escalation outside trials is not yet standard.

Definitive RT

  • RT alone: T1–2 N0, or T1 with a single node ≤3 cm. T2 with a single node ≤3 cm: RT alone or chemoRT.
  • ChemoRT: T3–4, ≥2 nodes, or a single node >3 cm. Cisplatin 100 mg/m² q3w or 40 mg/m² weekly. If not fit for cisplatin: cetuximab, carboplatin/5-FU or a taxane.
  • Dose: 70 Gy in 33–35 fractions with chemo. RT alone: 66–70 Gy accelerated or hypofractionated, or 68–70 Gy conventional. Elective nodes EQD2 ≥46 Gy.
  • No induction chemotherapy and no immunotherapy outside trials.

After TORS

  • ChemoRT: pT3–4 or pN+ with a positive margin or ENE.
  • PORT: pN+ with pT3–4, ≥2 nodes or a single node >3 cm. pT3–4 N0. Conditionally for PNI/LVSI or close margins.
  • Observe: pT1–2 with a single node ≤3 cm and no other risk factors.
  • Dose: 60–66 Gy to positive margin or ENE; 56–60 Gy to the bed and involved nodal levels; EQD2 ≥50 Gy to uninvolved levels in a node-positive neck.

Volumes

  • GTV to CTV_high ≤5 mm. CTV to PTV 3–5 mm.
  • Unilateral RT: T1–2 tonsil confined to the fossa with N0 or a single node ≤3 cm (ASTRO). MDACC series: 6 y contralateral neck failure 2%.
  • May omit: contralateral high level II, retrostyloid and retropharyngeal nodes if the contralateral neck is node-negative and there is no extensive soft palate, posterior pharyngeal wall or nasopharyngeal involvement. Level IB if neck-negative and no oral cavity involvement. Level V if neck-negative and no nasopharynx or hypopharynx involvement.

Key oropharynx trials

Cetuximab failed as de-escalation. Dose reduction, TORS-based and adaptive approaches are promising but not yet standard of care.

Cisplatin vs cetuximab

RTOG 1016HPV+ · 70 Gy over 6 weeks · cetuximab vs cisplatinCisplatin better: 5 y OS 78 → 85%, PFS 67 → 78%, LRF 17 → 10%. Acute G3–4 about 80% and late G3–4 about 20% in both arms; cisplatin had more G3–4 events per patient (2.35 → 3.19).
De-ESCALaTElow-risk HPV+ · cetuximab vs cisplatin2 y OS 89 → 98% and recurrence 16 → 6% favouring cisplatin. No toxicity advantage for cetuximab.
TROG 12.01 ANZlow-risk HPV+ · 70/35 with weekly cisplatin vs cetuximabNo significant difference in G3+ events (p=0.11). 3 y failure-free survival 80% (cetuximab) vs 93% (cisplatin).
