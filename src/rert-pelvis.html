Locally recurrent rectal cancer

Systematic review data and SABR series.

Conventional reirradiation (Guren 2014 review)

  • 375 patients in seven studies; first course median 50.4 Gy, 8–30 months earlier.
  • Schedules: hyperfractionated 1.2–1.5 Gy twice daily, or 1.8 Gy daily, with concurrent fluoropyrimidine, to a median 30–40 Gy (for example 40.8 Gy in 1.2 Gy twice daily, Valentini) to GTV plus 2–4 cm.
  • Median survival 39–60 months when resected, 12–16 months when palliative; symptom relief in 82–100%.
  • Acute diarrhoea 9–20%; late toxicity poorly reported.
  • Curative intent: hyperfractionated chemoradiation then radical resection. Short life expectancy: once-daily treatment.
  • Keep volumes small with conformal technique; small bowel and bladder are the organs at risk.

SABR for unresectable recurrence

  • UK multicentre series (69 patients, 81 lesions): 30 Gy in 5 fractions, daily or alternate days, after >6 months, ≤3 sites.
  • Later analysis found 30 Gy in 5 fractions often insufficient for durable control; hence isotoxic escalation (see pelvic SABR tab).

Sources: Guren MG et al. Radiother Oncol 2014; UK multicentre SABR series, Radiother Oncol 2021;162:1–6.

Pelvic SABR reirradiation: nodes and sidewall

International Delphi (Slevin 2021), UK SABR Consortium guidance 2025 and the Leeds data.

What is agreed and what is not

  • Delphi 2021: consensus on 29 of 44 statements, mostly patient selection, investigations, planning and delivery.
  • No consensus on a minimum interval between courses, the number or size of lesions, or cumulative OAR constraints.
  • UK guidance 2025: proposed dose 30 Gy in 5 fractions, with isotoxic escalation up to 45 Gy in 5 against cumulative OAR constraints when image verification is adequate.
  • Recovery: for 6–18 months since prior RT, 30% recovery may be applied to the original dose in the OAR calculation.
  • Most treated patients have nodal recurrence after prostate RT; median interval in the Leeds cohort 53 months.

Practical

  • Bowel in or abutting the high-dose region, and tumour invading bowel or bladder, are the main reasons not to proceed. Verify
  • Alternate-day fractionation is commonly used.
  • Have physics do EQD2 3D summation where there is high-dose overlap.

Selection and work-up

  • Biopsy-proven local recurrence, usually ≥2 years after primary RT, with PSMA PET and MRI excluding metastases. Verify for the interval.
  • ESTRO ACROP Delphi (salvage SBRT): consensus on no age limit, ECOG 0–1, satisfactory urinary flow, exclusion of metastatic disease, and target defined on MRI.
  • No consensus on concurrent ADT, how to account for the first-course dose, or whether Phoenix criteria apply after salvage.
  • Focal salvage suits unifocal disease with imaging–pathology concordance; whole-gland otherwise.

Schedules reported

  • HDR brachytherapy: 19 Gy in 1 fraction (focal), or 12–13.5 Gy × 2 (24–27 Gy). Dose-escalated focal HDR up to 30 Gy in 1–2 fractions was reported as safe in the F-SHARP phase 1/2 (ASTRO 2024).
  • SBRT: commonly 30–36 Gy in 5–6 fractions. Verify
  • LDR (RTOG 0526): late grade 3 GI/GU events 14%; 10-year biochemical PFS 46%.
  • Systematic review (2021): 2-year bRFS about 74% HDR, 71% LDR, 55% SBRT; late grade ≥3 GU 7.9% HDR, 13.6% LDR, 2.7% SBRT.
  • Cumulative rectum and bladder limits from one SBRT series: see limits.

Sources: Valle LF et al. Eur Urol 2021;80(3); Jereczek-Fossa BA et al. Cancer Treat Rev 2021;98:102206; Crook J et al. RTOG 0526, IJROBP 2019; Front Oncol 2021;11:681448.

Gynaecological recurrence after pelvic RT

Mostly brachytherapy; evidence is retrospective.

Options

  • Central recurrence: pelvic exenteration is the surgical standard, with high morbidity.
  • Image-guided interstitial brachytherapy is the best-suited reirradiation technique for central and paracentral recurrences: more conformal than EBRT or SBRT in dosimetric comparisons (Bockel 2021).
  • Reported 2–3 year local control about 50% with interstitial brachytherapy.
  • SBRT for nodal and sidewall relapse: local control about 80% at 1 year and 50% at 2 years.
  • HDR schedules of 4–6 Gy twice daily for 5–10 fractions (EQD210 about 40–50 Gy) are common.
  • Better outcomes with small recurrences, longer interval, complete response and higher target dose.

Risks and consent

  • Fistula, bowel obstruction, vaginal necrosis and ureteric stenosis are the feared late effects; quote unit data where available. Verify
  • Refer to a centre with interstitial experience.
  • Cumulative EQD2 limits for rectum, bladder, bowel and urethra have been proposed from published series (Faundez 2026) but are not standardised.
