Thorax — Target Volume Delineation Protocol

One reference for the three curative lung settings: SABR (peripheral, chest-wall-adjacent, central and ultracentral), definitive (chemo)radiotherapy for stage II–III NSCLC, and post-operative radiotherapy (R1/R2 and pN2). Mediastinal and hilar nodal stations are defined on CT with their landmarks, using the IASLC map as drawn by Lynch 2013.

Governing principles of this protocol
  • SABR treats only what you can see. GTV = CTV; the motion envelope (iGTV, ITV) and a 5 mm PTV do the rest. Dose follows the tumour's position relative to the chest wall and the proximal bronchial tree.
  • Conventional RT is involved-field. Primary and PET- or biopsy-positive nodes are the GTV; elective nodal irradiation is not used. Microscopic margin is small (about 5 mm) and is folded into the ITV in the departmental 4D workflow.
  • Post-op RT is station-based. The CTV is built from the resected involved stations, the bronchial stump and the ipsilateral hilum, plus stations 4 and 7, using the pathology report station by station.
  • Where the departmental protocol and an international guideline differ, both are shown and the page states which it follows. Values not found in a supplied source are marked CHECK.

1Scope, simulation, 4D-CT and imaging

Covers photon EBRT for primary NSCLC and lung oligometastases treated with SABR, definitive conventional or moderately hypofractionated RT, and post-operative RT. Limited-stage SCLC volumes are summarised in Section 5b (Levy 2026); SCLC doses and constraints from the departmental protocol are listed in Sections 7–8 for completeness. Palliative volumes are summarised in Section 7.

1.1 Before simulation

  • MDT and work-up. All patients discussed at the lung MDT. Definitive RT needs pulmonary function tests; there is no PFT cut-off that excludes treatment. Interstitial lung disease carries a high pneumonitis risk; estimate ILD mortality with the ILD-GAP index and weigh it against cancer mortality (Dept V4).
  • PET timing. Departmental: PET within 4 weeks of simulation for stage II–III and within 6 weeks for stage I, otherwise repeat a planning PET in treatment position (Dept V4). ESTRO-ACROP recommends PET-CT within 3 weeks before the start of treatment (Nestle 2018, "R"). With induction chemotherapy, PET before chemo and a contrast CT after it are mandatory (Nestle 2018, "M").
  • Nodal staging. Biopsy (EBUS, EUS or mediastinoscopy) for nodes ≥1 cm short axis or PET-avid nodes when it changes the volume (Nestle 2018).
  • Motion assessment. Consider abdominal compression or breath-hold when tumour excursion is ≥1.5 cm or for lower-lobe tumours; check comprehension, dentures (breath-hold) and stomas (compression) before choosing (Dept V4).

Section 2 · Scenario A

2SABR: peripheral, chest-wall-adjacent, central and ultracentral

Primary sources: departmental lung protocol V4 (2024); NRG SBRT lung physics template (2022); Kong 2011 (PBT and central zone); UK SABR consensus (Diez 2022). Not reviewed: Guckenberger 2017 ESTRO-ACROP SBRT.

2.1 Who is suitable

  • T1–T2b primary NSCLC up to 5 cm, or lung oligometastases meeting the same criteria (Dept V4).
  • Histological proof where safe; otherwise the MDT accepts a clinical diagnosis from serial CT, PET and risk factors (Dept V4).
  • Able to lie in treatment position for about 30 minutes (Dept V4).
  • Not ultracentral: the PTV must not abut or overlap the trachea, proximal bronchial tree, oesophagus, pulmonary vein or pulmonary artery (Dept V4).

2.4 Edge-by-edge rules for the SABR GTV

SABR has no anatomical CTV borders; what matters is where the GTV edge is drawn at each interface.

InterfaceRule
Lung parenchymaLung window (1600/−600), zoomed so the tumour fills the view (Dept V4; NRG).
VesselsSeparate tumour from vessel by scrolling in transverse, sagittal and coronal views with the cross-hair (Dept V4). Use soft-tissue window, ideally with contrast, to avoid drawing vessels into the GTV (NRG).
Atelectasis / fibrosisPET may be needed; IV contrast helps separate subtle atelectasis from tumour (NRG). Collapse or consolidation next to the tumour justifies a larger margin (Dept V4).

2.5 Step by step (MIM 4D workflow, cranial → caudal)

  1. Open the 4D + PET page. Confirm you are on the 50% phase (top-right of the viewer).
  2. Start two slices above the first visible tumour on lung window and scroll caudally; begin GTVp_EX on the first slice where tumour, not partial volume, is seen. Check that slice in sagittal and coronal.
  3. Draw each slice on lung window. Switch to mediastinal window wherever the tumour touches pleura, a fissure, vessels or mediastinum. Use the PET only to decide what is tumour, not to set the edge.
  4. Finish on the last caudal slice with tumour, again checking the cranio-caudal extent in sagittal view.
  5. Select all phases and run propagation ("Create ITV" in MIM, about 5 minutes). Rename the result iGTVp.
  6. Step through every phase and play cine; edit any phase where the propagated contour misses tumour.
  7. Create the MIP, draw GTVp_MIP, then build ITVDOSE = iGTVp ∪ GTVp_MIP and PTVDOSE = ITVDOSE + 5 mm (Boolean expand tool, avoid hard edge).
  8. For central tumours draw BRONCHUS_PROX and its 2 cm expansion now, and the heart and great-vessel PRVs on the 50% phase propagated to all phases, before choosing the fractionation.
  9. Save the session as RO_FINAL with the planning CT (not the 4D-CT) as the associated series.

Steps 5–9 follow the departmental MIM 4D ITV work instruction (2018); steps 1–4 combine Dept V4 contouring tips and NRG.

Contouring the proximal bronchial tree

  • Extent: distal 2 cm of trachea, carina, both main bronchi, both upper-lobe bronchi, bronchus intermedius, middle-lobe and lingular bronchi, and both lower-lobe bronchi; stop each lobar bronchus at its segmental bifurcation (Kong 2011; NRG).
  • Wall: mediastinal window, to the outer cartilage rings, lumen included (NRG; Kong 2011). Trachea is drawn from the cricoid down to where the PBT begins (NRG).
  • Quick method: threshold-autocontour bronchial air on lung window (MIM raw values 200–1000), then expand 1 mm to include the wall; check in all three planes (archived SABR protocol, CHISEL method).
  • Central zone: PBT expanded 2 cm in all directions except superiorly (Kong 2011); departmental structure BRONCHUS_PROX+2CM.
Wording differs Dept V4 defines BRONCHUS_PROX as "trachea 2 cm above carina to bifurcation of primary bronchi". This page follows Kong 2011 and NRG (to the segmental bifurcation of each lobar bronchus), which matches the lobe-by-lobe images in the archived departmental SABR protocol.

2.7 Pitfalls (SABR)

  • Irregular breathing. 4D-CT artefacts mislead the ITV; widen the margin and avoid single fraction (Dept V4).
  • Failed propagation. Large-amplitude tumours can be under-covered on end-inspiration phases; always cycle all phases and compare with the MIP.
  • MIP next to soft tissue. The MIP loses the tumour edge against diaphragm, heart or mediastinum (Nestle 2018); rely on the phase contours there.
  • When the smallest margin is not enough (Dept V4): highly mobile tumours, two separate targets matched on one CBCT, uncertain extent next to collapse, consolidation or effusion, lepidic adenocarcinoma.
  • Lung DVH. Subtract GTV_EVAL only, not the PTV (Dept V4; Kong 2011).
  • Lower-lobe tumours need a pre-treatment mock-up; check beam clearance for all patients (Dept V4).

3Definitive RT / chemoRT for stage II–III NSCLC

Primary sources: Nestle 2018 ESTRO-ACROP (recommendation grades M = mandatory, R = recommended, O = optional, D = discouraged); departmental lung protocol V4 (2024) and V3 (2021, margin rationale).

3.1 Indications and schedule

  • Stage II–III, fit: 60 Gy/30 # with concurrent chemotherapy, ideally both starting day 1 (chemo at least within week 1). Sequential chemoRT if the volume is too large to treat safely, toxicity is a concern or the patient is not fit for concurrent (Dept V4).
  • Not for concurrent chemo (or consolidation RT for mediastinal oligoprogression): 55 Gy/20 # (Dept V4).
  • Too large for radical dose: induction systemic therapy, an isotoxic approach (highest dose that meets constraints, 40–50 Gy/20–25 # with chemo in the departmental table) or adaptive replanning if constraints are only just exceeded (Dept V4).
  • Consolidation immunotherapy after chemoRT: re-image about 4 weeks after RT to confirm eligibility (Dept V4 follow-up table).

3.5 After induction chemotherapy

  • Primary: contour the current (post-chemo) extent on the new CT, with the pre-chemo imaging alongside (Nestle "R"). Department helper: GTVp_PRECX from fused diagnostic PET/CT (Dept V4).
  • Nodes: include every initially involved node or station from pre-chemo clinical, pathological and imaging information, even if the node has disappeared (Nestle "M"). Helper: GTVn_PRECX.
  • The same principle (post-chemo primary, pre-chemo nodes) applies to limited-stage SCLC (Dept V4).

3.6 Step by step, cranial → caudal

  1. Thoracic inlet (cricoid to manubrium): mediastinal window. Look for station 1 and 2 nodes on PET; separate them from thyroid, vessels and oesophagus. For apical tumours, draw the ipsilateral brachial plexus first (Section 8).
  2. Upper mediastinum (manubrium to top of aortic arch): stations 2R/2L, 3a, 3p. Check the left brachiocephalic vein is not drawn as node.
  3. Aortic arch to carina: 4R (to lower azygos border), 4L, 5 and 6. Use a sagittal view to place the arch and azygos landmarks.
  4. Primary: switch to lung window; contour the primary on the 50% phase slice by slice, returning to mediastinal window where it meets mediastinum, hilum or chest wall. Exclude atelectasis outside the PET edge if the PET separates it (Nestle "O").
  5. Carina to lower lobe bronchi: station 7, hilar 10R/10L and interlobar 11. Coronal view on lung window shows the bronchus intermedius and lower-lobe bronchus landmarks.
  6. Below: stations 8 and 9 down to the diaphragm when involved.
  7. Propagate GTVp_EX and GTVn_EX separately to make iGTVp and iGTVn; check every phase.
  8. Expand each by 5 mm to ITVp and ITVn, edit off bone, heart, great vessels and oesophagus unless invaded, union to ITVDOSE, check in cine, expand 5 mm to PTVDOSE.
  9. Write the matching priority (primary, mediastinum, OAR, or ranked) in the prescription notes (Dept V4).

3.7 Pitfalls (definitive)

  • Stale PET. Tumours progress if the scan is more than about a month old; repeat PET in treatment position (Dept V4).
  • Co-registering a diagnostic PET acquired arms-down. View it side by side rather than fusing it (Nestle "R").
  • Dropping a node that shrank after chemo. Pre-chemo involvement defines the nodal GTV (Nestle "M").
  • Contouring with one window. Lung window under-draws mediastinal invasion; mediastinal window over-draws spiculated lung edges.
  • Primary and nodes move differently. One combined propagation can under-cover one of them; propagate separately (Nestle "R"; Dept V4).
  • Manual PTV edits to meet constraints are discouraged (Nestle "D"); use isotoxic dose or replanning instead.

3.8 Superior sulcus (Pancoast)

  • Mask immobilisation; 4D-CT not required; MRI in treatment position if chest wall, plexus or vertebra is involved (Dept V4).
  • Neoadjuvant 46 Gy/23 # for stage II–III (Dept V4). Draw the ipsilateral brachial plexus (Section 8.1) and the vertebral body.
  • Hypofractionated 60 Gy/15 # is not used when the brachial plexus would receive the full dose (Dept V4).

5.5 Step by step

  1. Read the operation note and pathology station by station (Section 4.4): which stations were dissected or only sampled, which were positive, extranodal extension, margin status, pre-op chemo and initial nodal stage.
  2. Fuse the pre-operative PET/CT (GTVn_PREOP) to locate originally involved nodes and the original tumour position.
  3. On the planning CT (mediastinal window, contrast), find the bronchial stump from the staple line and contour it with the ipsilateral hilum (10).
  4. Contour each required station on the Section 4 borders, cranial → caudal, including 4 (ipsilateral) and 7, the involved stations, any station between two involved stations, and the adjacent stations if nodes were only sampled. Add mediastinal pleura next to the resected bed if relevant. Union = rCTV.
  5. Expand 1 cm to CTV, remove vertebral body, then clip to the maximum upper and lower limits in 5.4.
  6. Check motion on the 4D-CT and add the PTV (0.5–1 cm; Lung ART at least 0.5 cm axially and 1 cm cranio-caudally).

5.6 Pitfalls (PORT)

  • Inter-observer variation is large for PORT volumes (Spoelstra 2010, cited by Lynch 2013); use the station rules and peer review at the lung audit meeting (Dept V4).
  • Heart dose. In Lung ART the PORT arm had mean heart dose 13.4 Gy overall and 17.0 Gy for left-sided tumours (supplement, App. 11) alongside excess cardiopulmonary toxicity; keep the CTV inside the limits and the heart as low as possible.
  • After pneumonectomy the remaining lung tolerates much less: departmental V20 <10% and MLD <8 Gy against <30% and <15 Gy after lobectomy (Section 8).
  • Downstaged nodes. Stations involved before neoadjuvant chemo stay in the rCTV (Lung ART).
