Pancreas and Stomach — Target Volume Delineation Guide

Target volumes for pancreatic cancer — neoadjuvant and definitive chemoRT, dose-escalated RT and SBRT, and adjuvant RT after resection — and for gastric cancer (nodal anatomy, post-op and definitive volumes). Oesophagus and GOJ are in a separate protocol. Abdominal OAR definitions and SABR constraints are in the Liver SBRT OAR guide.

GTV High-dose CTV Low-dose / elective CTV Post-op CTV ITV / PTV
ESTRO-ACROP pancreas (Brunner 2021) · NRG international consensus (Sanford 2025) · RTOG post-op (Goodman 2012) · ASTRO 2019 · NRG template 2021 · gastric atlas (Wo 2013) · TROG 03.02 · TOPGEAR — Version 1.0 · Nov 2026
Governing principles of this guide
  • Locally advanced pancreas: treat the gross tumour, not elective nodes (ESTRO-ACROP). The newer dose-escalated approach (NRG 2025) adds a low-dose CTV built around the arteries and nerve plexuses (the "triangle"), not around nodal stations.
  • Motion is the biggest margin. Assess it for every patient (4D-CT or cine-MRI) and manage it when tumour motion exceeds 5 mm (NRG 2025). Without 4D information the PTV has to be large (1.5 cm axially, 2 cm cranio-caudally; ESTRO-ACROP).
  • After resection the CTV is built from vessels and anastomoses that can be seen on CT (RTOG; Goodman 2012), with an asymmetric para-aortic volume.
  • Duodenum, stomach and small bowel set the dose. Fast, use the same oral-contrast routine every day, and contour luminal OARs fully around the PTV.
  • No departmental (Liverpool) upper GI protocol was in the folder; departmental items are marked CHECK.

1.2 Simulation for stomach

  • Supine, arms up, body cradle; scan from above the sternal notch (if lung DVH needed) to about L3 (TROG 03.02).
  • TROG 03.02 avoided oral contrast in the planning scan because dense contrast distorts the dose calculation; if needed, scan twice and fuse. Modern practice: fast and use a fixed small drink to reproduce gastric filling CHECK local.

Section 2 · Scenario A

2Pancreas: neoadjuvant and definitive conventional chemoRT

2.1 Indications (ASTRO 2019, conditional unless stated)

  • Resectable: neoadjuvant therapy conditionally recommended.
  • Borderline resectable or selected LAPC for downstaging: chemotherapy then conventional chemoRT, or chemotherapy then multifraction SBRT.
  • LAPC not for surgery: chemotherapy then conventional chemoRT, dose-escalated chemoRT, or multifraction SBRT without chemotherapy.
  • After resection: adjuvant conventional chemoRT for selected high-risk patients (node-positive, margin-positive); adjuvant SBRT only on a trial or registry.

2.2 Volumes GTV ITV/PTV

VolumeNeoadjuvant chemoRTDefinitive chemoRT (LAPC / recurrence)
GTVPrimary on the pancreatic phase plus nodes ≥1 cm short axis or PET-avid, using all diagnostic imaging. After induction chemo, review baseline imaging but draw the GTV on current imaging (ESTRO-ACROP).
Primary CTVTumours ≥3 cm: no extra CTV needed. Tumours <3 cm (especially head): consider a CTV margin, because CT under-calls small tumours (median about 2–6.5 mm) (ESTRO-ACROP). NRG trial template: GTV (or IGTV) + 0.5–1.5 cm.
ex-GTVOptional CTV = pre-induction GTV (as in CONKO-007); evidence supports either approach—
Elective nodesOptional: weak recommendation for BRPC, moderate for resectable; if used, as for post-op (Section 4)Not recommended
ITVTumour (or CTV) on all 4D phases — commonly the 3D scan plus maximum inhale and exhale, checked in cine
PTVWith 4D: ITV + 0.5–1 cm (department set-up data). Without 4D or gating: GTV/CTV + 1.5 cm axially and 2 cm cranio-caudally (ESTRO-ACROP Table 3).

2.3 Step by step

  1. Open the pancreatic-phase planning CT with MRI and the portal-venous phase alongside. Identify celiac axis, CHA, SMA, SMV, portal vein and splenic vessels first.
  2. From the top of the tumour downward, draw the hypodense mass and any soft-tissue cuff around vessels; include involved nodes.
  3. Decide the primary CTV by size and site (above), crop off duodenum, stomach and bowel unless invaded.
  4. Contour the GTV on inhale and exhale (or all phases), union to ITV, play cine.
  5. Add the PTV from department data; contour duodenum and stomach completely and other bowel at least at PTV level.

3Pancreas: dose-escalated RT and SBRT

3.1 Two schools

ItemESTRO-ACROP SBRT (Brunner 2021)NRG consensus dose-escalated RT (Sanford 2025)
TargetTumour volume only; ITV usually taken as CTV; no elective nodes; often no CTV around nodeshigh-dose GTV/CTV plus low-dose elective CTV ("triangle") wherever dosimetrically feasible
PTVITV + 0–5 mm (institutional)5 mm non-adaptive; 3 mm adaptive
FractionationMostly 5 # (North America); up to 12 # in Europe, risk-adapted (more fractions the closer the PTV to stomach/duodenum)5 #: 50 Gy / 33 Gy; 15 #: 67.5 Gy / 37.5 Gy; 25 #: 75 Gy / 45 Gy (high / low, BED10 ≈100 / 47–55)
This page follows

NRG 2025 for dose-escalated or MR-guided adaptive SBRT (it matches the MR-linac workflow), and ESTRO-ACROP GTV-only SBRT when the low-dose volume cannot be fitted around bowel. ASTRO 2019 doses (Section 8) for standard SBRT.

3.2 High-dose GTV/CTV — all tumours (NRG 2025)

  1. All gross disease (primary and nodes >1 cm or with malignant enhancement) from MRI, CT and PET.
  2. Any of celiac artery, SMA, SMV or portal vein within 5 mm of gross disease: include the whole vessel diameter on that slice and extend 5 mm along it, full circumference (tail tumours: also the splenic artery).
  3. Include the hazy soft tissue opacifying fat around the tumour.

3.3 Low-dose CTV — head, neck and proximal body

  1. Part A: GTV (primary + nodes) + 10 mm isotropic, consider pre-chemo extent; crop off stomach and bowel unless invaded or at risk. Add the whole pancreatic head.
  2. Part B starts 10 mm above the celiac take-off: cover the celiac artery from aorta to its bifurcation + 5–10 mm.
  3. Cover the CHA to at least the gastroduodenal artery + 5–10 mm; consider the portal vein at that level. Extend back to the anterior aorta and IVC (fat with the nerve plexuses), about 5–10 mm lateral to the aorta.
  4. Laterally include the portal vein through the portosplenic confluence; keep the left border 5–10 mm from celiac and SMA; exclude stomach and bowel.
  5. Anteriorly the SMA + 5–10 mm (SMV optional at that level); the posterior border can move forward off the aorta here.
  6. Stop at the first jejunal branch of the SMA. Union A + B.

3.4 Low-dose CTV — distal body and tail (NRG 2025)

  • Part A: GTV + 10 mm (crop off kidney and spleen unless invaded) plus the whole body (body tumours) or whole tail (tail tumours).
  • Part B from the celiac take-off: splenic artery + 15–20 mm circumferentially; celiac axis + 5–10 mm optional (half the panel included it); join celiac and splenic volumes through intervening pancreas if appropriate; stop at the first jejunal branch of the SMA.

4.2 Building the CTV

  • Union of all expansions; posterior edge follows the front of the vertebral body, at most 0.5 cm into it.
  • If the PJ cannot be found, build the CTV without it. Edit the CTV off liver or stomach where it protrudes.
  • ESTRO-ACROP adds, from post-resection relapse mapping (Dholakia), a volume around CA and SMA of 2–3 cm to the right and 1–2 cm in other directions, with particular care for the space bounded by CA/SMA (medial), portal vein (anterior) and IVC (lateral).
  • PTV: department set-up data (NRG template example: CTV expanded with an institution-specific margin) CHECK.

Section 6 · Scenario D

6Stomach

6.1 Where gastric RT stands

  • Pre-operative chemoRT is not standard. In TOPGEAR (AGITG/TROG/EORTC/CCTG, 574 patients), adding pre-op chemoRT to perioperative chemotherapy raised pCR (17% vs 8%) but not survival (median OS 46 vs 49 months, HR 1.05; 5-year survival about 45% in both arms) (Leong 2024).
  • Post-operative chemoRT (INT-0116 era) remains an option after inadequate (e.g. D0/D1) surgery or R1 resection without peri-operative chemotherapy; 45 Gy/25 # to the tumour bed, anastomoses and stumps, and regional nodes, individualised to primary site, nodes and operation (Leong 2003, following INT-0116).
  • Palliation (bleeding, obstruction) and definitive chemoRT for unresectable disease use the same nodal anatomy without anastomoses (Leong 2003).

9Organs at risk and constraints

Contour stomach and duodenum completely, other bowel at least at PTV level, both kidneys separately, liver and spinal canal; follow the RTOG upper-abdominal atlas (Jabbour 2014) as in the Liver SBRT OAR guide, which also holds the UK 2022 3- and 5-fraction SABR limits. Stomach/duodenum internal risk volumes from 4D-CT are optional (ESTRO-ACROP).
