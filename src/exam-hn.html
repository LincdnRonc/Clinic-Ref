Oropharynx

Epidemiology · Subsites · Management · Curative RT · Post-op RT

Epidemiology

I: 2nd most common H&N primary

A: 50-60

G: M>F

R: Developing world, Europe, Caucasian

RF

E: smoking, alcohol, betel nut chewing, pollution, asbestos, nickel, tar, prv RT, poor oral hygiene, sexual RFs

I: HPV – 16 makes up 90%

G: Fanconi anaemia

I: HIV, solid organ transplant

Staging

  • T1: ≤2cm
  • T2: 2-4cm
  • T3: ≥ 4cm
  • T4: invading oesophagus, bone, larynx

HPV +ve

  • cN1: ipsilateral nodes <6cm
  • cN2: contralateral nodes
  • cN3: any >6cm
  • pN1: <4 nodes
  • pN2: >4 nodes
  • M1 = stage IV

HPV -ve

  • cN1: single node, ipsilateral, <3cm
  • cN2a: single, ipsilateral, 3-6cm
  • cN2b: multiple, ipsilateral, <6cm
  • cN2c: contralateral node
  • cN3: ENE or >6cm
  • pN is same except N1a with ENE is pN2a
  • M1: distant nodes/mets

T4 or N2 or M1 = stage IV

F/u

  • 3 month PET → repeat in 6 weeks if equivocal → salvage neck dissection if persistent dx

Outcomes

  • HPV +ve: 5 yr OS 80-90%
  • HPV -ve 5 yr OS 50%
  • HPV+ve generally does better even in metastatic disease and has late distant relapse

Subsites

  • BoT, posterior pharyngeal wall, soft palate, tonsil, vallecula (between BoT & anterior epiglottis)

Path

DDx

Benign: brachial cleft cyst, tonsilitis, abscess, cyst, reactive/inflammatory node, erythroplakia, leukoplakia

Malignant: SCC, salivary gland carcinoma, EM plasmacytoma, melanoma, lymphoma

Pre-invasive

  • Erythroplakia: red plaque. 30-50% invasive risk, more likely to be CIS
  • Leukoplakia: white plaque. 5-10% invasive risk can be hyperplasia, dysplasia, less likely CIS

Invasive

  • SCC – non-keratinising, basaloid (both are HPV+), keratinising (HPV-)
  • Grade 1-3 only applies to HPV-ve SCC
  • IHC: p16+ve (>70% block nuclear/cytoplasmic stain), CK5/6, p40, p63
  • Other: HPV ISH/PCR +ve, EBER ISH -ve
  • Best outcomes if concordant HPV and p16+

HPV carcinogenesis

  • HPV 16 & 18 infection of basal epithelial cells (via microabrasions)
  • Viral genome integration into host DNA → loss of E2 regulation
  • E6 and E7 oncoprotein overexpression:
    • E6 → p53 degradation → impaired apoptosis, genomic instability
    • E7 → Rb inactivation → release of E2F → G1/S progression → uncontrolled cell cycle progression
  • Result: cell cycle dysregulation + accumulation of mutations → malignant transformation
  • Latency of 20+ years

EGFR overexpression

  • Common in H&N SCC – 85%, more common in HPV-
  • Upregulation of downstream pathways (MAPK, PI3K, JAK/STAT) increases cell growth, survival, angiogenesis
  • Cetuximab is EGFR targeted therapy but assoc with worse OS compared to cisplatin
  • Does not predict response to IO
  • Worse prognosis

Management

  • Note: Avoid trimodality Rx where possible to minimise toxicity

Well lateralised tonsil with N0-1

  • <1/3 of lateral hemi-oropharynx, <1cm into BoT or soft palate, no deep or muscle invasion
  • Definitive RT +/- cisplatin with unilateral neck RT: 70Gy/35# or 55Gy/20#
  • TORS with unilateral LND

Early stage operable (T1-2N0-1)

  • Should have lateralised tumour with good exposure
  • TORS + bilateral LND – ORATOR
  • Adj RT indicated if: T3/T4, LVI, PNI, SM <2mm, ENE, large node ≥3cm, ≥2 nodes
  • Adj cisplatin if: ENE, SM+ - improves OS/PFS (pooled analysis of EORTC 22931 and RTOG 9501)

Inoperable (T2Nany)

  • Definitive chemoradiotherapy with cisplatin (MACH-NC MA)
  • If unfit for cisplatin consider cetuximab (Bonner trial) though OS inferior (RTOG 1016), or carbo/5FU (French GORTEC trial)
  • Alternate fractionation: 6# per week (DAHANCA), accelerated hyperfractionation (MARCH MA, RTOG 9003)
    • Hyperfractionation improves OS by 8% at the expense of acute mucositis, acceleration improves LRC
    • Hypofractionation is non-inferior for LRC and similar for tox: 55Gy/20# (HYPNO)

Metastatic

  • Chemoimmunotherapy: cisplatin/5FU/pembro (10% OS benefit vs chemo + cetuximab)
  • IO monotherapy – ORR 20% alone vs 40% with chemo, however still beneficial. Ideally CPS >20%
  • Palliative RT

Re-irradiation

  • 60Gy/30-40# OD-BD

De-escalation of HPV+

  • No ph III evidence supports this
  • Ph III RTOG1016/De-escalate trial & TROG 12.01 – worse OS with 70Gy RT + cetuximab vs cisplatin - 5 yr OS 78% vs 85%
  • Ph II/III NRG HN005 – pts with HPV <10pyhx 60Gy + cisplatin or nivo was inferior to 70Gy with cisplatin for 2 year PFS
  • Ph II ECOG E3311 with risk adapted adjuvant RT (obs, 50-60Gy, 66Gy) with cisplatin showed acceptable 2 yr PFS and OS for 50Gy vs 60Gy in intermediate risk group (T1-2, N1-2, SM-) though not powered for efficacy comparison between arms
  • Other pending studies: PATHOS (ph III of ECOG E3311) and F-MISO PET to de-escalate hypoxia-negative disease

ORATOR, ph II

P: T1-2, N0-2, OP SCC

I: TORS + neck dissection +/- adj Rx vs 70Gy +/- cisplatin

O: Used MDADI scores which was worse for tonsil. TORS: worse social eating, swallow and trismus. RT: worse tinnitus, hearing loss, xerostomia. PFS & OS no diff. Trimodality toxicity worst. Bimodality toxicity (sug + RT or CRT) are equivalent

ORATOR II: TORS vs de-escalated CRT 60Gy – 2 deaths in surgical arm, closed early

Pooled analysis of EORTC 22931 and RTOG 9501

  • in adj setting if SM+ or ENE then cisplatin adds 6% 5 yr PFS benefit

Bernier et al

  • Post-operative concurrent 3 weekly cisplatin chemotherapy + RT improved OS by ~7% at 5 yrs as well as PFS. 20% more G3 toxicity.

MACH-NC MA

  • Concurrent cisplatin adds 6.5% 5 yr OS benefit
  • Platinum based chemo has best OS benefit
  • Concurrent > induction chemo
  • Benefit reduces with age, no benefit >70yo

MARCH-MA

  • altered fractionation vs conv RT alone improves 5 yr OS by 3%
  • hyperfractionation specifically adds 8% 5 yr OS benefit vs conv RT alone
  • accelerated RT has smaller benefit, mainly LRC benefit
  • chemoRT has better 6-5% 5-10 yr OS benefit vs hyperfrac RT alone
  • Altered fractionation increases acute tox – mucositis and feeding tube need during RT

DAHANCA 6&7

  • 6# per week improved 5 yr LRC by 10% and DFS. Worse acute mucositis

Bonner trial

  • Improved median OS with RT + cetuximab vs RT alone – 50 vs 30 months

HYPNO

P: locally adv H&N SCC

I: 55Gy/20# 5fx/week vs 66Gy/33# 6fx/week. Cisplatin allowed.

  • NB control arm deemed biologically equiv to 70Gy/35#

O: 55Gy/20# non-inferior at 3 yrs for LRC and G3 tox

Curative RT

Dose: 70Gy/[63Gy]/56Gy/35# 10# per fortnight with BD compensation with:

  • concurrent cisplatin 100mg/m2 3 weekly OR 40mg/m2 weekly if less toxicity desired (ototoxicity, renal impairment, neuropathy)
  • cetuximab OR carbo/taxol (NCCN category 2b)

Pre-sim: dietician, speech pathologist, dental review, prophylactic PEG/RIG insertion if treating bilateral neck, bloods, audiometry, smoking cessation

Sim

  • Supine, arms down, neck extended, TPS over head and neck
  • 1cm bolus for involved nodes
  • CT with IV contrast
  • Fuse PET and MRI

Oral cavity

Epidemiology · Subsites & 1st echelon · Management · Post-op RT · Osteoradionecrosis

Epidemiology

I: Most common H&N ca globally

A: 60

G: M

R: South and central America

RFs

E: smoking, poor oral hygiene, chronic dental infection, betel nut chewing, ETOH, periodontal disease

I: chronic oral candidiasis

G: Fanconi anaemia

I: HIV, solid organ transplant

Staging

T1: <2cm, DOI <5mm

T2: 2-4cm, DOI <10mm

T3: >4cm, DOI >10mm

T4: fixed/invading structures outside oral cavity

N1: single, ipsi, <3cm

N2a: single, ipsi, 3-6cm

N2b: multiple, ipsi, 3-6cm

N2c: contralateral, <6cm

N3: >6cm, ENE

M1: DM

WPOI:
- Graded from 1-5 by borders (pushing vs infiltrative),

  • 4: separated tumour islands and budding
  • 5: dispersed tumour satellites >1mm from invasive front

Subsites & 1st echelon

  • Lip – 1a if central, 1b if lateral
  • Buccal mucosa – pre-auricular, 1b
  • Gingiva / alveolar ridge – 1a/1b
  • RMT- 1b
  • FOM – 1a or 1b
  • Hard palate – RP, 1b, 2
  • Tongue – 1a if tip, 1b if lateral

Path

Pre-invasive: see OPSCC

  • SCC – verrucous, acantholytic, spindle cell, adenosquamous, papillary
  • Salivary gland carcinoma
  • Mucosal melanoma
  • EM plasmacytoma
  • Lymphoma
  • Sarcoma, Kaposi’s sarcoma
  • Cutaneous SCC or BCC

Benign

  • Odontogenic cyst,
  • Odontoma
  • Cyst
  • Abscess

Management

Curable

Surgery

  • WLE + neck node dissection is mainstay (Iyer et al)
  • SLNBx reasonable if DOI <4mm and high volume centre, neck dissection if DOI >4mm (Cruz et al)
  • If within 1cm of midline should do bilateral neck dissection
  • SLNB vs LND reduces fibrosis, shoulder dysfunction and sensory impairment
  • Total glossectomy is accompanied with laryngectomy as risk of aspiration is very high without tongue to propel food bolus
  • If borderline resectable can trial downstaging NA induction chemo (TPF) if MDT agreeance.
  • If clearly unresectable proceed to definitive CRT accepting worse OS
  • Peri-op pembro improves EFS by 10%

Adj treatment

  • Adj RT and chemo follows same principles as OPSCC: T3/T4, 2 or more nodes, LVI, PNI, SM+ve not re-excisable, close SM <2mm, node >3cm, ENE (pooled analysis EORTC and RTOG trials)
  • Adj RT should include the entire surgical bed, including nodal dissection bed even if N-
  • Consider omission of adj chemo unless multiple high risk features: T3/T4 + N2-N3 + ENE (OCAT)
  • Peri-op immunotherapy for locally advanced resectable improves 3 yr EFS by 11% and possibly OS (KEYNOTE 689)

Metastatic

  • cisplatin based chemotherapy, cetuximab, immunotherapy
  • Palliative RT

Iyer et al

P: retrospective, locally advanced oral cavity SCC

O: OS and LRC better with surgery than definitive CRT. 5 yr OS 60% vs 30%

Senti-MERORL

P: T1-2N0 oral cavity SCC

I: SLNBx vs neck node dissection

O: SLNB non-inferior for 2 year RFS. OS similar. Less shoulder dysfunction, fibrosis, sensory deficit.

OCAT

P: Resected locally advanced oral cavity SCC

I: Adj treatment with RT 60Gy/30# vs CRT vs accelerated RT 60Gy/30# 6 fx/week

O: 10 yr LRC 60% for all. 10 yr OS 40% for all. CRT had DFS benefit if T3-4, N2-3 and ENE

KEYNOTE 689

most HPV negative

PORT +/- chemo if ENE or close SM <1mm

O: 3 yr EFS benefit of 11%, estimated 10% OS benefit (not statistically sig)

Dose: 66Gy (SM not re-excisable, ENE) / 60Gy/56Gy/30-33#.

Concurrent IV cisplatin if ENE or SM+ve as per OPSCC

Pre-sim: ensure scar healed, DT/SP, PEG if bilateral neck treatment, dental rv, audiology if cisplatin

Sim

Supine, arms down, neck extended, bite block, tongue depressor, TPS over head and shoulders

1cm bolus if cervical N+

Osteoradionecrosis

  • Non-healing exposed irradiated bone, >3-6 months post RT, without persistent/recurrent tumour
  • Higher risk after marginal mandibulectomy (≥1cm of residual bone) vs segmental mandibulectomy (poorly vascularised bone left vs reconstruction)
  • Mandible has less blood supply than maxilla so at higher risk
  • RT doses >60Gy

Pathophys

  • Radiation induced vascular damage (endarteritis obliterans)
  • chronic inflammation and activation of fibroblasts lead to fibrosis
  • osteocyte death and reduced osteoblast activity → hypovascular, hypoxic and hypocellular tissue → impaired healing → necrosis
  • Dental extraction can precipitate by causing non healing injury

Mgmt

  • Exclude recurrence
  • Treat concurrent infection, oral hygiene (chlorhexidine mouthwash), regular dental reviews, avoid further trauma (dentures)
  • PENTO regime: pentoxifylline + vitamin E to improve vascularity and reduce fibrosis
  • HBO – mixed evidence
  • Debridement or mandibulectomy + vascularised free flap reconstruction

Larynx

Epidemiology · Subsites · Management · Small field larynx EBRT · Definitive RT

Epidemiology

I: 3rd most common H&N ca

A: 70+

G: M

R: Caucasian, countries with high smoking prevalence

RFs

E: smoking, alcohol, asbestos, nickel, tar, fuel pollution

I: nil

G: Fanconi anaemia, Plummer vinson syndrome

I: HIV, solid organ transplant

Staging

T1: single subsite

T2: extension into adj site, impaired cord mobility

T3: fixed cord, inner cortex thyroid cartilage or paraglottic space

T4: invasion to surrounding organs beyond larynx, through thyroid cartilage

N1, single ipsi <3cm

N2a single ipsi 3-6cm

N2b: multiple ipsi, <6cm

N2c: contralateral

N3: >6cm, ENE

M1: DM

Subsites

  • Supra-glottis: epiglottis, arytenoid, false vocal cord, AEF
  • Glottis: true vocal cord, ant & post commissure, 5mm below cords
  • Best prognosis, early presentation with hoarse voice
  • Subglottis: 5mm below true cords to caudal cricoid
    • Late presentation and worst prognosis

Lymphatics

  • Glottis has sparse lymphatic drainage, rarely N+ ~20% with T4, <5% for T1-2
  • Supraglottis: level 2,3
  • Subglottis level 3 and paratracheal

Path

  • SCC (95%) – classical, verrucous, sarcomatoid, papillary
  • Sarcoma
  • Melanoma
  • Plasmacytoma
  • Lymphoma
  • Verrucous SCC: exophytic cauliflower lesion with low grade and ‘elephant feet’ (pushing edge)
    • Rare nodal involvement, reactive nodes common
    • RT ineffective and can cause anaplastic transformation!

Notes

  • Innervation by recurrent laryngeal nerve from vagus nerve
  • Bulky tumour risks airway obstruction

F/u

  • No 3 month PET for glottic. PET for all others

H&N synoptic pathology report

  • procedure/specimen
  • subsite/laterality
  • tumour size
  • DOI
  • histological type/grade
  • WPOI
  • bone/skin/muscle invasion
  • PNI including nerve calibre/named nerve
  • LVSI
  • margins
  • nodal levels, nodes examined/involved, largest deposit, ENE presence/extent
  • TN< stage
  • ancillary tests

Management

Tis

  • laser excision
  • small field larynx hypofractionated RT

T1-2N0 glottis

1) Transoral microsurgery with CO2 laser – Helsinki et al

  • Limited to superficial lesions, voice quality more impacted (wider glottic gap → breathier, hoarse voice), day procedure

2) Small field hypofractionated larynx RT – Yamakazi et al

  • RT preferred if near anterior commissure (as will have to have both cords operated on) or upfront poor voice quality
  • Tis-T1: 63Gy/28# in 2.25Gy/#
  • T2: 65.25Gy/29# in 2.25Gy/#
  • Has late toxicity of hypothyroidism, dysphagia
  • NB: impaired cord mobility and subglottic extension are poor prognostic factors
  • Numerically worse LC with partial larynx RT

3) Partial laryngectomy

  • Voice quality poor, better margin control than TOMS

Other T1-2 Nany

  • H&N radiotherapy. Consider chemotherapy if bulky.
  • Laryngectomy

T3-T4, N+

  • Definitive chemoradiotherapy (RTOG 9111)
  • 40% will require salvage laryngectomy (50% if T4), OS post salvage laryngectomy similar to upfront surgery (subset analysis of RTOG 9111)
  • Higher aspiration risk
  • Induction chemotherapy followed by RT (VALSG):
      • Cis/5FU q3weekly x 3. Addition of docetaxel is more tox but improves LRC
      • shown to be equivalent to surgery +/- adj Rx for OS but inferior for LRC
      • Induction chemo followed by RT is similar to definitive CRT for long term 10 yr OS
  • Laryngectomy
    • Indicated if T4 with cartilage penetration or extralaryngeal spread given risk for LRR and cartilage necrosis OR in non-functional larynx
    • PORT +/- chemo follows same indications as OPSCC

P: T1-2N0 glottic ca

I: transoral microsurgery with CO2 laser vs definitive radiotherapy

O: more breathy hoarse voice with surgery. OS equivalent – 98% at 5 yrs

Yamakazi et al

P: T1-2N0 glottic ca

O: Hypofractioned had better 5 yr LC 92% vs 77%

P: locally advanced laryngeal cancer

I: laryngectomy +/- adj RT vs induction cis/5FU > definitive RT if >50% response. If <50% response then surgery

No OS diff

RTOG 9111

induction TPF (docetaxol, cisplatin, 5FU) improved larynx preservation compared with PF, but with more haematologic toxicity, especially neutropenia

Laryngectomy toxicity

  • Pharyngocutaneous fistula (most common complications ~ 20%)
  • Loss of voice → voice prosthesis, electrolarynx, oesophageal speech, tracheoesophageal speech
  • Stoma care
  • QoL
  • Dysphagia
  • Aspiration
  • LRTI
  • Reduced small and taste due to loss of nasal airflow
  • Peri-operative risk: bleeding, infection, perforation

Voice rehab

TEP voice prosthesis

  • surgically created tract between trachea and oesophagus with one-way valve/prosthesis inserted into tract
  • Patient occludes the stoma or uses hands-free valve → air from lungs is diverted into oesophagus/pharynx → mucosa vibrates → mouth shapes sound into speech
  • Gives most fluent speech

Oesophageal speech

  • Patient swallows small amounts of air into the oesophagus
  • Air is released back up → pharyngoesophageal segment vibrates → speech
  • No device needed, but hard to learn, short phrases, quieter/less fluent

Electrolarynx

  • Handheld vibrating device placed on neck/submental area
  • Vibration enters oral cavity and is shaped by tongue/lips
  • Easiest to use, but voice sounds mechanical/robotic

Hypopharynx

I: Rare, 6% of all H&N ca, pyriform sinus most common

A: 70

G: M

R: Caucasian

RFs

E: smoking, ETOH, betel nut chewing, fuel air pollution, asbestos, coal dust/fumes

Staging

T1: <2cm, one subsite

T2: 2-4cm, multiple subsites

T3: fixed hemi larynx, into oesophagus, >4cm

T4: through cartilage, oesophageal wall, mediastinum, prevertebral fascia, carotid encasement

N same as HPV -ve OPSCC

M1: DM

Subsites

Extends from bottom of vallecula to bottom of cricoid

  • Post cricoid space, pyriform sinus, posterior pharyngeal wall
  • Drains to level 2-4, RP
  • Early nodal and haematogenous spread
  • Posterior cricoid space drains to level VI and paraoesphageal
  • Presents late

Path

SCC – basaloid, papillary, verrucous

Often presents at advanced stage

Outcomes

Outcomes worse than other H&N sites, pyriform sinus does worst

Early stage

  • 5 yr OS 50-80%
  • Larynx preservation 80%

Late stage

  • 5 yr OS 30%
  • Larynx preservation 60%

Management

T1-T2

  • If functional larynx and pharynx -> definitive RT
  • If impaired speech and swallow -> least invasive surgical option: Transoral excision + neck dissection

T2-T4 N any

  • Pharyngolaryngectomy preferred if non functional larynx and pharynx
    • Partial pharyngectomy suitable in select small lesions where laryngeal function can be preserved. However for most, particularly pyriform/posterior wall, surgery is commonly total laryngopharyngectomy
    • Flap reconstruction or gastric pull up
  • Definitive CRT (extrapolated from RTOG 9111 for larynx)
  • Consider induction chemotherapy (EORTC 24981)

60% of pts in the CRT arm alive at 5 yrs preserved larynx

35% of all pts in CRT at 5 yrs preserved larynx (significant number of deaths).

OS 10% at 10 years.

Recurrent

Epidemiology · Management

Epidemiology

  • 20% risk of second H&N primary with field change from smoking

Tumour factors

  • Second primary may respond better to re-RT than recurrence of original disease as radioresistant clones are selected in the latter
  • Higher volume = harder to achieve LC and more toxicity

Treatment factors

  • Salvage surgery such as pharyngolaryngectomy is generally associated with higher morbidity than upfront surgery – surgical planes less defined, worse lymphedema, worse recon/flap healing, higher salivary leak and carotid blow out risk
  • Time from first course of radiation <2 years and existing organ dysfunction → worse OS (Ward et al – patient selection for re-irradiation of H&N SCC)
  • Volume of disease and proximity to OARS influences technique – VMAT vs SBRT
    • SBRT ideally for smaller volumes <25cc
  • Chemo + RT should be considered if fit enough
  • Brachytherapy ~ 20-48Gy in 3-4Gy/# BD. Dose is prescribed to periphery, centre will receive ~150%+. LC ~70%
  • Systemic therapy alone has worst outcomes

Management

Options

  • salvage surgery, definitive intent re-irradiation, palliation, systemic rx
  • Toxicity of re-RT: higher risk of osteoradionecrosis, aspiration, swallow dysfunction and PEG dependence, carotid blowout (<5% 8 months after re-RT, MacDonald 2012)
  • In IMRT era Re-RT 2 yr LC ~ 50%, G3 toxicity ~ 30%, G5 tox 3%, 2 yr OS 45% (Reirradiation with IMRT for recurrent H&N MA)

Re-irradiation doses

  • 60Gy/30# → this is almost always the desired dose, LC ~ 80%
  • 35-40Gy/5# (EQD2 of 60Gy) improved LC/OS vs < 6Gy per #
    • SBRT may have less toxicity
  • 65Gy/54# BD
  • Quadshot: 3.7Gy BD x 2 days, repeated up to 5 cycles q4weekly. 65% symptom response. 5% G3 toxicity (mostly mucositis)
