Advanced-stage Hodgkin lymphoma

Systemic therapy is curative (BEACOPPesc, BV-AVD, or nivolumab-AVD). RT is reserved for residual PET-positive masses ≥2.5 cm, and sometimes for initially bulky sites.

Role of RT

  • After BEACOPPesc: 30 Gy only to PET-positive residual masses ≥2.5 cm (HD15). PET-negative patients had 4 y PFS 93% without RT.
  • After PET-negative chemo: no consolidation needed (EORTC 20884: no EFS benefit in CR).
  • Residual PET-avid masses: 36–45 Gy (ILROG).
  • SWOG 1826: nivolumab-AVD improved on BV-AVD and is a new standard.
  • Relapsed/refractory: ISRT before or after transplant for limited or bulky relapse.
GHSG HD15 (2012)BEACOPP regimens · RT 30 Gy only if PET-positive residual4 y PFS 93% for PET-negative vs 86% for PET-positive residual.
EORTC 20884 (2003)421 · MOPP-ABV · CR → ± IFRT 24 GyIn CR, 5 y EFS 84 vs 79% (no benefit). Partial responders who received RT had EFS similar to the CR group.

ISRT principles and late effects

Treat the pre-chemo involved sites (ILROG), not whole fields. Keep heart, breast and lung doses low; cardiac and second-cancer risks dominate late mortality.

ISRT contouring step by step: Lymphoma ISRT contouring guide, section 3

ISRT volumes (ILROG)

  • GTV: pre-chemo involved nodes on PET/CT fused to the planning CT, and any residual disease.
  • CTV: pre-chemo extent, edited off normal tissues that were displaced (lung, muscle, bone). Do not include initially normal nodes. Add margin craniocaudally for uncertainty when image fusion is poor.
  • Use 4DCT/ITV or DIBH for mediastinal disease; DIBH helps most with upper mediastinal involvement and allows smaller PTV margins.
  • PTV margin per institution.

Since this study, SWOG 1826 showed 3-yr PFS of 98% in Stage III HL in peds treated with N+AVD x6. Thus there may no longer be a role for routine ISRT for PET2+ in Stage I-III HL treated with this regimen, but there is still a role for ISRT for refactory PET6+. Two to three cycles of chemo followed by ISRT may be useful for those who want less chemotherarpy.

This trial's 10 year follow-up confirms long term PFS benefit with INRT in PET2 negative, without detriment to OS or increase in secondary cancer. The study also shows that additional cycles of chemo cannot replace INRT. There is no benefit in OS, likely due to salvage therapy. For comparison HD17 did show noninferiority without INRT in PET negative unfavorable HL when BEACOPP is used. GHSG HD10 used a slightly de-escalated regimen, ABVD x2 plus 20 Gy.

PET2 positive. These trials not only ask who can avoid radiation, but also give data on who can avoid death and transplant when using RT for PET positive at cycle 2-3. In H10, RAPID, and HD16, PFS was 80-95%, allowing a large majority to avoid salvage therapy and transplant. Some prefer treatment with response adapted chemotherapy, but this is mainly studied in advanced HL, not early stage.

Adverse effects and 2nd malignancy. In HL for some there is a careful balance between cure, adverse effects, and secondary malignancy. There was no increase in secondary malignancy in EORTC with RT - small INRT fields were used. Effects past ten years are unknown for this specific study, but ten years is a good follow-up period. Increasing doses of doxorubicin, ASCT, and CAR-T also increase 2nd cancer risk (Neppelenbroek 2024, McDonald 2023). CAR T cell has reported secondary malignancies of 4-16%, and consequently FDA now requires boxed warnings (Cappell & Kochenderfer 2023, Suran 2024). Neither ASCT nor CAR T have shown improvement in OS in randomized trials in HL (Schmitz 2022 and Linch 1993). Concerning cancer survivorship, female patients who undergo radiation are required to undergo more frequent screenings for breast cancer (NCCN 2024). It is unclear if this is needed with INRT; site of 2nd cancer was not reported in H10. Would a patient rather have INRT or "go through this again" with the chance of salvage chemo with ASCT or CAR-T? Patients should be offered INRT.

Radiation planning. INRT volumes in the study strictly covered the initial volume of nodes prior to chemotherapy, also taking into account anatomical displacement. There was no allowance for extra elective volume per expert opinion. In other words the smallest volumes possible should be implemented to reproduce these results.

-HL initially treated with RT alone with large fields (STNI)
-ABVD chemo later developed, and eventual NCIC ECOG HD6 showed chemo may have better OS than RT. But there are criticisms to the study
-As a result, RT use decreases over time some regions

Commentary
Background. Both pediatric and adult patients are eligible. In order to preserve fertility and reduce late effects, adult HL regimens are now being trialed in the pediatric population.

SER. As in essentially all other clinical trials for early stage HL, RT remains mandated for those with SER, anticipated in 15%. The investigators write "Therefore, there is no data to support omitting radiotherapy among patients with SER for patients with Stage I/II disease." RT for SER shows good results in studies, and there is no evidence that response adapted chemo for something that already seems chemo resistant is superior. In H10, RAPID, and HD16, PFS in PET2-3 SERs treated with RT was 80-95%. In AHOD0431, when ISRT was required for low risk relapse after CR, 84% avoided transplant. In RATHL in adults, 91% who had optional radiation for refractory were able to avoid transplant. In the HD12+9 combined analysis in advanced lymphoma there was PFS and OS benefit with radiation for refractory disease.

RER. This study was designed prior to the publication of long term follow-up of EORTC H10 in adults, which showed superiority of INRT in CR in favorable HL and not-noninferiority of removal INRT in CR in unfavorable. An amendment adding ISRT to the RER arms in AHOD2131 seems called for, at least for favorable HL in adults in the control arm, though perhaps COG anticipates that mainly young adults will be enrolled. In AHOD0031, an analogous study to H10 evaluating IFRT in RER in pediatrics, the benefit with IFRT in CR at four year was p=0.11, and the trial does not yet have the long term follow up that EORTC H10 does. In H10 additional cycles did not compensate for omission of ISRT. HD17 showed no benefit with RT in unfavorable HL with RT after PET CR when BEACOPP is used, but HD16 did not show noninferiority in with omission of RT in PET negative favorable early stage. In AHOD0431 for low risk pediatric lymphoma, a post-hoc analysis showed benefit with IFRT in RER, but this was non randomized. Concerning the experimental arm, in SWOG 1826, evaluating nivolumab in Stage III-IV lymphoma ages ≥12, only ≤1% were treated with radiation, but this trial had extremely strict criteria for allowing RT. In SWOG 12% in the nivolumab arm were still Deauville 4-5.

Radiation planning. IMRT, VMAT, and protons are now recommended. ISRT is now 30 Gy. For age <15, 21 Gy with 9-10 Gy boost to SER sites can be given. Prior COG protocols showed risk of in field relapse with 21 Gy in SER, suggesting a role for dose escalation. The trial recommends to follow ILROG guidelines for CTV.

Commentary
Although phase II thus far, some are interested in this approach to omit radiation.

Radiation for low risk relapsed and refractory disease allowed 84% to avoid SCT in AHOD0431, and ISRT is still required for SER in open protocol AHOD2131. The Checkmate 744 approach does not allow those with standard risk and good response to avoid transplant.

Response can be difficult to assess after nivolumab. The LYRIC criteria propose instructions for assessing indeterminate response from immunotherapy (see Cheson 2016 - the criteria are too lengthy to outline here). Clinicians however should use caution in applying the LYRIC criteria too liberally as an excuse to exclude RT without evidence. The LYRIC authors themselves write in their proposal that "There are not at present sufficient data to rigorously support the details of the choices made here."

Classic HL

  • Nodular sclerosing ~70%

    • Rich in lacunar cells
  • Mixed cellularity ~30%
    • Male, 50+
    • HIV, EBV

  • Lymphocyte predominant ~5%
    • Many lymphocytes, few RS cells
  • Lymphocyte depleted <5%

    • HIV
    • Poor prognosis

  • Fibrotic or sclerotic background
  • IHC: CD 45-, CD30+, CD15+, EBV/LMP1

Nodular lymphocyte predominant HL

  • M predominant, 40-50yo
  • Variant of RS cells → popcorn cells in a nodular B cell lymphocyte rich and histiocyte background
  • IHC: CD20+, CD19+, CD45+
  • managed as per indolent B cell lymphoma
  • 20-30% risk of transformation to DLBCL

Management

PET adapted approach following EORTC HD 10

Early stage favourable

ABVD x2 → PET2

  • ISRT 20Gy/10# OR
  • 2 additional cycles of ABVD. Option to drop bleomycin
