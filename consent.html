With reconstruction

  • Major reconstruction complications are common with PMRT (about 40% in one series). Reconstruction failure is about 10%.
  • Irradiating a tissue expander carries more risk than irradiating the permanent implant.
  • Infection 6 → 13% and skin necrosis 3 → 8% with PMRT in one implant series.

Consent checklist for SRS

  • Aim is to stop growth; the tumour stays visible on scans.
  • Early swelling at 6–24 months is expected and may briefly worsen imbalance, tinnitus or facial sensation.
  • Hearing may keep falling over years, with or without SRS.
  • Rare: facial weakness, trigeminal numbness or pain, hydrocephalus (report headache, gait change, confusion, incontinence), malignant change.
  • Single session; frame or mask; short steroid course if symptomatic.
  • Lifelong MRI follow-up.
  • Offer hearing rehabilitation, vestibular physiotherapy and tinnitus support whatever is chosen. Dizziness and headache drive long-term QoL more than the modality.

Managing bowel toxicity

  • High-dose probiotics during pelvic RT modestly reduce days with loose stools (ProRad, gynae).

Consent: second cancer after prostate RT

  • VA cohort (~150,000 men): potentially in-field second cancer 3.7% with RT vs 2.5% without. Bladder 1.8 vs 1.4%, rectal 0.4 vs 0.3%, leukaemia 0.7 vs 0.5%.
  • Relative risk climbs with time: HR 1.24 at 1–5 y, 1.50 at 5–10 y, 1.59 at 10–15 y. Matters more in younger men with long life expectancy.

Managing urinary symptoms

  • Frequency/obstruction: tamsulosin 0.4 mg daily.
  • Dysuria: phenazopyridine 200 mg (source lists QID; turns urine orange, false-positive nitrites).
  • Urgency/spasm: oxybutynin 5–10 mg daily.
  • Exclude UTI with culture, not dipstick alone (leucocyte esterase often false positive during RT).

Managing radiation proctitis

Bleeding-predominant is the most common type and usually settles with time.

  1. Optimise bowel habit and stool consistency.
  2. Start vitamins A/E/C (A 10,000 IU, E 400 IU TDS, C 500 mg TDS; 90-day course) or metronidazole 400 mg TDS for 30 d.
  3. If bleeding affects QoL: sucralfate enema 2 g BD for 30 d (77% improve by two grades); consider holding anticoagulation.
  4. Persistent: APC (treatment of choice; rectal ulcers in 26%), formalin, or hyperbaric oxygen.

Inflammation-predominant: loperamide, fibre/bulking agents, rectal steroids (hydrocortisone 90 mg BD may beat betamethasone 5 mg BD).

Sexual function and fertility

  • About half of potent men get new ED after RP, a little under half after EBRT; PDE5 inhibitor or Trimix restores function in ~3/4. Sildenafil improves RT-related ED in ~60–70%.
  • Azoospermia from 0.5–2 Gy to the testis; testosterone production impaired at ~14 Gy.
  • Penile bulb dose predicts ED (HYPO-RT-PC), especially in younger men.
  • UTSW neurovascular-sparing SBRT: no sexual benefit (≈80% kept erections in both arms with spacer and no ADT).

Toxicity and follow-up

  • Acute: frequency, urgency, haematuria, spasm, bowel upset. Send urine culture for suspected UTI (leucocyte esterase often false positive during RT).

  • Cystectomy follow-up: retained urethra needs surveillance. 35% develop CKD 3.

General gynae

From the General gynae page, where sources and trials are cited.

Toxicity management

  • High-dose probiotics (L. plantarum) during pelvic RT reduced days with loose stools (ProRad).
  • Chronic proctitis: optimise stool; vitamins A/E/C (90 d) or metronidazole 400 mg TDS (30 d); sucralfate enema 2 g BD; APC for persistent bleeding.
  • Vaginal dilators: start 4–6 wk after treatment, a few minutes 2–3× weekly, ≥9–12 mo; regular intercourse lowers G2 stenosis (23% vs 36%, EMBRACE I).
  • Insufficiency fractures, lymphoedema, ovarian failure and sexual dysfunction: discuss at consent.

Notes by phase

Acute
  • In QUARTZ, drowsiness, hair loss, nausea and dry or itchy scalp were all more frequent with WBRT. Serious adverse events were not.
  • For new headache, vomiting or deficits during RT, think of oedema: increase dexamethasone and image if it doesn't settle.
Early-delayed
  • Somnolence is more common and more marked in children. Reassure patients and families, and use steroids if needed.
  • Memory decline at ~4 months was 52% with SRS + WBRT vs 24% with SRS alone (Chang). Some recovery is possible.
Late
  • Severe dementia developed in 11% of 1-year survivors who had 3–6 Gy fractions (DeAngelis 1989). Onset was 5–36 months (median 14). It wasn't seen with ≤3 Gy per fraction. This is why long-survivors get ≤3 Gy per fraction.
  • For hearing, cochlear dose equals the prescription in conventional WBRT. The risk adds to cisplatin ototoxicity.
  • Hypopituitarism and second tumours matter mainly for long survivors (PCI, PCNSL, young patients). Check endocrine function if symptoms suggest it.
