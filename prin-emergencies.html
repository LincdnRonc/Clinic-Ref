Metastatic spinal cord compression

Dexamethasone now, whole-spine MRI within 24 hours, spinal surgical opinion, then RT. Full detail: Bone and spine: cord compression.

First steps

  • Suspect it in anyone with cancer and new back pain plus any neurological symptom; do not wait for weakness.
  • Dexamethasone 16 mg daily (e.g. 8 mg twice daily, or a 10 mg loading dose then 4 mg four times daily for severe deficits) with a PPI and glucose monitoring. Higher doses add toxicity without clear benefit.
  • Flat bed rest and spinal precautions if pain suggests instability, until stability is assessed (SINS).
  • Whole-spine MRI (multiple levels in about a third of patients).
  • A tissue diagnosis is needed before RT unless the cancer is already known or the MDT accepts a clinical diagnosis in an emergency. Check for lymphoma, myeloma and germ cell tumours, where chemotherapy may be preferred.

Surgery or RT alone

  • Patchell (2005): decompressive surgery plus RT kept more patients walking than RT alone, in fit patients with a single level of compression, prognosis >3 months and paraplegia for <48 h; radiosensitive tumours were excluded.
  • RT alone: 8/1 is non-inferior to 20/5 for ambulatory status at 8 weeks in patients with limited prognosis (SCORAD III; ICORG 05-03). Use 20/5 or 30/10 for better prognosis or after surgery.
  • SBRT is for selected oligometastatic or post-separation-surgery cases, not for high-grade compression without decompression.

Superior vena cava obstruction

Assessment and treatment

  • Usually from lung cancer (especially small cell) or lymphoma; also thrombosis around central lines.
  • Symptoms develop over weeks as collaterals form; it is rarely immediately life-threatening unless there is laryngeal or cerebral oedema or airway compromise. Those need urgent stenting and airway management.
  • Get a tissue diagnosis first where possible: steroids and RT can obscure lymphoma histology, and chemotherapy may be the best treatment (SCLC, lymphoma, germ cell).
  • Endovascular stenting relieves symptoms fastest (often within 24–72 h) and is first choice for severe symptoms. Anticoagulate if there is thrombus.
  • RT for NSCLC or when chemo is not suitable: e.g. 20/5 or 30/10, or radical treatment if the disease is curable. Symptom relief takes 1–2 weeks.
  • Steroids are for lymphoma (after biopsy) or symptomatic oedema; benefit in carcinoma is unproven.

Airway compromise and bleeding

Airway compromise

  • Stridor or rapidly worsening dyspnoea: secure the airway first (ENT or respiratory: tracheostomy, debulking, stenting, laser), then treat.
  • RT causes early oedema and can transiently worsen obstruction: give dexamethasone and start with a protected airway when possible.
  • Palliative thoracic RT: 8/1, 17/2 (one week apart), 20/5 or 30/10 by performance status. Endobronchial brachytherapy helps intraluminal disease.

Haemostatic RT

  • Effective for bleeding from bladder, rectum, cervix, stomach, lung (haemoptysis), skin and fungating breast tumours; most respond within days.
  • Schedules: 8/1 (repeatable), 20/5, 30/10. Cervix: up to 8/1 repeated weekly for up to 3 doses, or vaginal brachytherapy if available.
  • Resuscitate, reverse anticoagulation where safe, and involve interventional radiology (embolisation) and endoscopy in parallel; tranexamic acid as adjunct.

Raised intracranial pressure and leptomeningeal disease

Raised intracranial pressure

  • Headache worse lying down, vomiting, drowsiness, papilloedema. Dexamethasone (e.g. 8–16 mg/day), neurosurgical review for mass effect or hydrocephalus (shunt or resection).
  • RT follows stabilisation: SRS, surgery, HA-WBRT or supportive care according to prognosis (Brain metastases).

Leptomeningeal disease

  • Diagnosis: MRI brain and whole spine with gadolinium; CSF cytology (repeat up to twice if negative). Common in breast, lung (EGFR/ALK), melanoma and haematological cancers.
  • RT is for symptomatic bulky sites, cauda equina, cranial neuropathies and CSF flow blockage: involved-field 20/5 or 30/10, or whole brain (helmet) volumes (Whole brain guide, helmet section).
  • Proton craniospinal irradiation improved CNS progression-free and overall survival compared with photon involved-field RT in a randomised phase II trial in breast and NSCLC (Yang, JCO 2022) Verify; it is not widely available.
  • Systemic therapy with CNS activity (osimertinib, lorlatinib, intrathecal chemotherapy) is usually the backbone.
