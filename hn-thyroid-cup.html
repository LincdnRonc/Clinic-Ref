Toxicity & consent

Thyroid cancer

Differentiated thyroid cancer is treated with surgery, RAI and TSH suppression. EBRT is for gross residual or RAI-refractory disease. Anaplastic carcinoma needs rapid assessment for BRAF and trimodality treatment with ≥60 Gy.

Decisions

  • Differentiated (papillary, follicular, Hürthle): thyroidectomy + central/lateral neck dissection if node-positive. RAI for pT2+ or raised thyroglobulin: 30 mCi (low risk), 100 mCi (T3+ or N+), 200 mCi (metastatic).
  • EBRT for differentiated disease: gross residual neck disease after RAI, bulky metastases, lesions with poor RAI uptake. Consider if >45–55 y with a high chance of microscopic residual. Evidence is mostly retrospective.
  • Medullary: the role of EBRT is less clear. It may benefit R1/R2, extrathyroidal extension and/or N+ disease. RET inhibitors for advanced disease.
  • Anaplastic: maximal safe resection, then PORT ± chemo; otherwise definitive RT. Doses ≥60 Gy with IMRT and trimodality therapy are associated with better survival. Test for BRAF V600E (20–50%).

Squamous cell carcinoma of unknown primary (neck)

Search hard: PET/CT before biopsy, examination under anaesthetic with tonsillectomy and base-of-tongue biopsy. Test HPV and EBV. Most hidden primaries are tonsil or base of tongue.

Decisions

  • Workup (ASCO 2020): HPV testing on level II–III nodes; EBV if HPV-negative. CT neck, then PET/CT. Primary found in tonsil 45%, base of tongue 40%, pyriform sinus 10%.
  • Node level hints: I → oral cavity; II → HPV+ oropharynx; III–IV → larynx/hypopharynx; V → skin or nasopharynx; IV/supraclavicular adenocarcinoma → below the clavicles. Intraparotid node → skin. p16+ but high-risk HPV-negative → skin.
  • cN1: neck dissection then RT, or RT alone.
  • cN2–3: neck dissection → RT ± chemo, or definitive chemoRT.
  • Mucosal volume: p16+ → oropharynx; EBV+ → nasopharynx. Hypopharyngeal mucosa is not routinely treated.
  • With neck dissection alone, about 50% fail at the primary. Treating the mucosa and both necks reduced contralateral nodal failure (44 → 14%) and primary emergence (44 → 8%) in one series. Overall: 5 y OS about 50%, subsequent mucosal primary 10–25%.

Oncocytic carcinoma of thyroid (Hurthle cell)

  • FTC variant with higher tendency for LRR
  • Less iodine uptake than FTC
  • Slightly worse survival – 10 yr OS 80%

Work-up

Thyroid US concerning features

  • Taller than wide
  • Diffuse, infiltrative margins
  • Solid or heterogenous
  • Microcalcifications
  • Capsule invasion or ETE

I-131

  • Cold nodule has 10% chance of malignancy
  • Hot nodule 3% change of malignancy
  • If not a well diff thyroid cancer → do FDG PET scan

Management

Biopsy

  • FNA:
  • Cytology allows diagnosis of PTC or suspicious lesion (Bethesda III-IV)
  • Less invasive than core bx
  • Both core and FNA are unable to dx FTC as this needs extensive vascular and capsule sampling for invasion
  • Less tissue than core for molecular testing
  • Consider diagnostic hemithyroidectomy if sufficient suspicion / FTC

Thyroidectomy

  • Hemithyroidectomy for diagnostic purposes OR therapeutic if:
    • Unifocal
    • no ETE or capsular invasion
    • <4cm
    • No LN
  • Total thyroidectomy
    • Oncocytic carcinoma
    • Prior RT (↑ recurrence risk)
    • Doesn’t fit above

Cervical LN mgmt

  • LND of VI AND VII if:
    • T4
    • N+
    • ETE
  • Add lateral neck dissection if involved
  • SLNBx not routinely used

Adjuvant RAI – I-131

  • Indications (as per ATA):
    • To ablate remnant thyroid tissue do TG can be used for surveillance, to eradicate microscopic residual disease/micromets, to allow use of RAI imaging as surveillance
    • Aggressive histology: tall cell, columnar, hobnail
    • ETE
    • Multiple nodes or node >3cm
    • FTC with extensive vascular invasion >4 foci
    • R2 resection
    • M1
    • Post-op TG high
  • Can perform a diagnostic I-123 scan prior to assess iodine uptake
  • S/e of RAI: nausea, sialadenitis/xerostomia, dysgeusia, lacrimal duct obstruction, myelosuppression, temporary gonadal dysfunction, dose-related secondary malignancy risk.
  • CI: pregnancy, breastfeeding

Adjuvant EBRT

  • Rarely used in differentiated thyroid cancer
  • Indicated if:
    • No Iodine uptake
    • Unresectable disease
    • Residual or recurrent disease not amenable to RAI or re-resection

Anaplastic thyroid cancer

  • Assess and manage airway!
    • Steroids, FNE, tracheostomy, bronchoscopy + tracheal stent, surgical decompression, RT once airway secured
  • If resectable (rare): surgery → prompt adj RT 66-60Gy (R1-R0) → adjuvant systemic therapy
  • If borderline resectable: neoadjuvant systemic treatment → reassess
  • If unresectable (common): systemic treatment + RT
    • Definitive RT 66Gy/33# including level 2-4, VI and upper mediastinal nodes to carina with concurrent carbo/taxol
    • Palliative RT 20Gy/5# for local control, given airway compromise risk)
  • Systemic therapy:
    • If BRAFm: dabrafenib & trametinib upfront
    • Others: carboplatin, paclitaxel

F/u

  • Neck US, tumour markers

EBRT – Definitive MTC

Dose: 66Gy/33# with concurrent carboplatin/paclitaxel

Pre-sim: check tumour markers, discuss at MDT, airway assessment, SP/DT

Sim: supine, chin extended, TPS for head and shoulders, 1cm bolus over neck, fuse PET

Volumes

GTV

CTV6600: GTV + 5mm

PTV6600: +5mm

CTV 5400: elective central neck nodes (level VI) + upper mediastinal (VII) from hyoid to aortic arch

PTV: +5mm

RAI

I-131

  • B and gamma emission, t ½ 8 days, mean energy 190 KeV

Indications

  • Ablate remnant thyroid tissue
  • Adjuvant treatment for DTC where there is high risk of micromets
  • Recurrent or metastatic DTC

Prep

  • Low iodine diet for 2 weeks
  • Exclude pregnancy
  • Achieve TSH stimulation (withhold thyroxine or give TSH)

Administration

  • Oral tablet given in radioisotope protected room
  • Follow time, distance, shielding and contaminated waste removal protocols
  • Mostly excreted via urine – hydrate, void, separate toilet, double flush, wash hands
  • DC home after ~ 1 week (radiation levels at 1m <25 μSv/h)
  • Advise separation, avoid children/pregnant women

Post-RAI

  • I-131 scan 1 week post treatment to identify areas of disease/remnant tissue (background I-131 is cleared but activity in iodine uptaking tissue is retained)

Unknown primary

From the exam summary.

Epidemiology

<5% of HN cancers

Usually presents as cervical node

Presentation

  • Enlarging painless node
  • Malignant: cranial nerve palsy, constitutional symptoms, increase in number or size, firm fixed matted
  • Other H&N sx

Work-up

  • Smoking hx, acintopath
  • US guided core biopsy, p16, HPV ISH and EBV testing
  • EUA and pan-biopsy
  • Skin exam
  • MRI H&N
  • FDG PET
  • MDT discussion

Primary sites

OPSCC or cutaneous

  • p16 positive: OPSCC or cutaneous SCC
    • 15% of cSCC are p16 +ve
  • HPV ISH: more specific for OPSCC
  • Cystic node level II or III: OPSCC

NPC

  • EBV positive: NPC primary
  • Level V node

Other: melanoma, lymphoma, sarcoma

Management paradigm

Investigation/surgery

  • Neck dissection (unilateral vs bilateral as per above)
  • Consider diagnostic bilateral tonsillectomy & mucosectomy

RT +/- chemotherapy

Unilateral neck treatment

  • Single small node, suspicion for tonsil primary
  • Frail patient

Bilateral neck treatment

  • Multiple nodes, large single node >3cm, suspicion for midline primary, ENE
  • Small single node or frail patient → RT alone, unilateral treatment
  • Large node or bilateral nodes → CRT
  • Avoid trimodality treatment (increased toxicity → extrapolating from OPSCC data)
  • RT doses:
    • 70Gy: gross disease
    • 63Gy: involved nodal level
    • 56Gy: elective neck and at risk mucosal sites
