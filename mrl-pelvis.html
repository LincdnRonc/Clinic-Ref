Pelvis on the MR-linac

Breathing motion is minimal in the pelvis, so the standard T2 3D is sharp. The main challenges are day-to-day bladder, rectum and bowel changes.

Rectum and bladder

  • Rectal wall: mucosa grey, submucosa bright, muscularis propria dark; gas black. Contour the outer wall.
  • Mesorectal fascia: thin dark line around bright mesorectal fat; key for rectal boost targets.
  • Bladder: urine bright, wall dark; edit the dome carefully as volume changes daily.

Female pelvis

  • Cervix: dark fibrous stromal ring; tumour is intermediate-to-bright tissue disrupting it.
  • Uterus: bright endometrium, dark junctional zone, intermediate myometrium; position shifts with bladder filling.
  • Ovaries: identified by bright follicles.

Nodes, bowel and fiducials

  • Nodes are intermediate ovals in bright fat that end; vessels are black tubes that continue.
  • Small bowel: bright fluid, grey wall; edit the bowel bag near the PTV only.
  • Fiducials: small black voids, less conspicuous than on CT, bloom more on GRE.

Pelvis workflow tips

Consistent preparation is what keeps pelvic adaptive sessions short.

Pelvic workflow

  • Consistent bladder and rectal preparation reduces editing on the day.
  • Rectal gas matters twice: it pushes the anterior rectal wall into high dose, and at 1.5 T gas pockets cause electron return effect dose changes at the tissue–air interface.
  • Apex and base carry the most inter-observer variation: agree a rule (e.g. sagittal-based apex) and apply it every fraction.
