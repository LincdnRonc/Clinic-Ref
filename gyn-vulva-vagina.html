Vulva

Surgery with sentinel node biopsy for small tumours; adjuvant RT for nodes and high-risk margins; chemoRT for locally advanced disease. Never block the midline.

Vulval CTV rules, inguinofemoral margins and post-op volumes: Gynae contouring guide, sections 8–9

Management

  • T1a (≤2 cm, ≤1 mm invasion): wide local excision with 1 cm margin; no nodal staging.
  • Unifocal <4 cm, >1 mm invasion, cN0: radical local excision + SLNB (GROINSS-V). SLN-negative: observe (groin recurrence 2.3%). Lateralised (>2 cm from midline) → ipsilateral SLN.
  • SLN-positive: ≤2 mm, no ECE → ipsilateral groin/low pelvis RT 50 Gy instead of dissection. >2 mm or ECE → completion IFLND then RT (56 Gy to groin/pelvis if residual risk) (GROINSS-V II).
  • cN+ or ≥4 cm/multifocal: inguinofemoral lymphadenectomy (palpable nodes have ~50% false-positive rate; FNA first).
  • Adjuvant nodal RT: ≥2 positive nodes or ECE (GOG 37); many treat a single node because pelvic recurrence is rarely salvageable.
  • Adjuvant vulvar RT: margin <5–8 mm, LVSI, depth >9 mm; re-excise if margin is the only factor. Margin distance matters less than dVIN/lichen sclerosus in the margin. ~40% recur locally by 10 y.
  • Locally advanced/unresectable: chemoRT with weekly cisplatin to 57.6–64.8 Gy (GOG 205: cCR 64%, pCR 50%; GOG 279 to 64 Gy). Resect residual disease where possible.

  • CTV: whole vulva + 1 cm beyond tumour extension; +2 cm along invaded structures (anus, urethra, clitoris, bladder), ≥3 cm along vagina.
  • Nodes: bilateral inguinal, obturator, external and internal iliac; nodes lie anterior/anteromedial to femoral vessels, so no margin needed posterior/lateral.
  • Sim supine frog-leg on vac-lok (less skin fold), wire tumour/scar/nodes, BB at introitus, bolus over the bed if visible tumour; check TLDs early. Replan for oedema or shrinkage. Full and empty bladder scans for IMRT.
  • Femoral neck tolerance ~50 Gy.

Vagina

Rare; managed by extrapolation from cervix and anal data. RT is the main curative modality; brachytherapy is key.

Vaginal vault, salvage and primary vaginal cancer volumes: Gynae contouring guide, section 7

Management and doses

  • Tumours touching the cervix are cervical; touching the vulva are vulvar. ~80% of vaginal tumours are metastatic from elsewhere. 90% SCC (~80% HPV).
  • Nodes: upper two-thirds → pelvic; lower third → inguinal/femoral. Node risk ~6–14% stage I, 26–32% stage II, 75% III.
  • Thin (<5 mm) stage I: intracavitary BT alone, whole vagina 60–65 Gy and tumour +10–20 Gy; or EBRT + BT.
  • Stage II+ or >4 cm: chemoRT 45 Gy pelvis (± inguinal) + BT boost to 70–80 Gy EQD2 (85 Gy for bulky residual); sidewall/nodes 55–66 Gy (favour 60). Interstitial if >5 mm thick (e.g. 5 Gy ×5 BD single insertion).
  • HDR boost after 45 Gy: 6–7 Gy ×3. IVA: brachytherapy can cause fistula; consider exenteration.
  • Vaginal apex can move 1.5–2 cm AP: ITV with full/empty bladder.

Outcomes to quote

  • 5-y pelvic control stage I / II / III–IVA: 86% / 84% / 71%; CSS 85% / 78% / 58% (MDACC, 193 patients).
  • BT reduced death by ~20% across stages (SEER); chemoRT added ~6% 5-y OS (NCDB).
  • Late effects: vaginal stenosis/fibrosis ~50%, cystitis and proctitis common; fistula with IVA.

Vulva

Exam notes

AS's Phase 2 summary, as written and not source-checked. Where it disagrees with this page, go by this page.

Vulva

From the exam summary.

Epidemiology

I: uncommon

A: 60

G: F

R: Caucasian

RF

E: smoking, alcohol, lichen sclerosis, VIN, sexual RFs, genital warts, chronic vaginitis

I: HPV (1/3rd of cases)

G: nil

I: HIV, solid organ transplant

Staging

TNM

  • T1: a <2cm stromal invasion ≤1mm; b >2cm, stromal invasion >1mm
  • T2: into distal 1/3 vagina, urethra, anal canal
  • T3: into prox 2/3 vagina, urethra, bladder, rectum
  • T4: fixed to bone
  • N1: inguinofemoral node
  • N2: fixed node
  • M1: pelvic node / DM

FIGO

  • IA = ≤2 cm and DOI ≤1 mm
  • IB = >2 cm or DOI >1 mm confined vulva/perineum
  • II = adjacent perineal structures
  • III = inguinofemoral nodes
  • IVA = upper urethra, vagina, bladder, rectum, pelvic bone or fixed/ulcerated nodes
  • IVB = distant mets including pelvic nodes

Node drainage

  • Nodal mets correlate to tumour thickness
  • 1mm = <5%
  • 2mm = 10%
  • 3mm = 20%
  • 4mm-5mm = 30%
  • >5mm = 50%
  • LN is most important prognostic factor in localised vulva ca – correlated to DM and survival
  • Pattern: superficial IF → deep IF → EI pelvic node
  • If clitoral +ve → drain directly to deep IF or pelvic nodes

Follow-up

  • Close surveillance until skin healed with NS/RO
  • PET/MRI at 3 months
  • q3mo review for 2 years, then q6mo. Alternate with gynae-onc
  • If evidence of tumour progression → refer for surgical excision

Differential diagnosis

Benign – wart, lichen sclerosis, VIN, cyst, lipoma, abscess

Malignant - extramammary pagets dx, SCC, adenoCa (bartholins glands), melanoma, lymphoma, sarcoma, BCC

Path

  • SCC (90%) – verrucous, basaloid, papillary, keratinising
  • Melanoma (5%)
  • AdenoCa (<5%) – Bartholin’s gland – CK7, CEA, MUC1 +ve
  • Adenoid cystic carcinoma – CD117, CEA +ve
  • Sarcoma

IHC: CK5/6, p16, p63, p40

Growth patterns

  • Spray = finger-like tumour projections deeper than main tumour → higher local recurrence
  • diffuse = infiltrative growth with desmoplastic stroma → higher nodal positivity

Pre-invasive

Lichen sclerosis

  • chronic inflammatory process with lymphocytic cell infiltrate, oedema and fibrosis of dermis and overlying atrophic epidermis.
  • 5% lifetime risk of SCC

uVIN (2004)/HSIL (2014, current)

  • assoc with HPV 16, younger women.
  • Multifocal, white, red
  • <10% risk of progression to basaloid or warty SCC

dVIN

  • assoc with lichen sclerosis and TP53m.
  • Unifocal, white
  • 30-50% risk of progression to G1-2 keratinising SCC, can be rapid

Mgmt: Imquimod, excision, laser therapy, topical 5FU

Vulvar Paget

  • intraepithelial adenocarcinoma with large pale Paget cells
  • IHC: CK7, CAM5.2/AE1/3, EMA, MUC1, CEA
  • Assoc with synchronous, breast, rectum, bladder carcinoma

Palliative

  • Don’t frog leg
  • Treat through dressings
  • En face electron with markup
  • IDC for comfort/ease

Work-up

H+P, ensure assessment of cervix with CST and vagina due to share HPV field effect and risk of synchronous primaries.

Biopsy + mapping biopsy if diffuse growth

FDG PET

EUA –cystoscopy, colposcopy, proctoscopy if concern for involvement

Mgmt

Node and primary managed as separate entities

Surgery is cornerstone

Operable N0M0

  • Modified or radical vulvectomy + nodal assessment >1mm thickness or stromal invasion
  • WLE may be appropriate for select T1/ stage 1 cases
  • Reconstructive options can be considered post-treatment

Upfront SLNBx vs IF LND

  • SLNBx preferred if
      • tumour <4 cm
      • radiologically node-negative
      • DOI >1 mm
      • no prior vulvar surgery disrupting lymphatics
  • Unilateral node assessment only if lesion >2cm from midline, small, unifocal, N0

Adj RT to primary

  • SM <8mm (Heaps data is controversial, some data suggests <3mm for SCC)
  • DOI >5mm
  • Diffuse or spray growth patterns
  • LVSI
  • Large tumour >4cm
  • 50Gy/25# if R0
  • 60-66Gy/30# if SM+ or close
  • Positive margin should have re-resection if cosmetic and functional impact is acceptable

SLN +ve

  • If SLN macromet (>2mm) → inguinofemoral lymph node dissection → adj RT if ENE or ≥ 2 nodes – GROINS VII & GOG 37
    • If only 1 macromet SLN consider RT if large primary, large node, inadequate dissection, LVSI
    • Adj RT field: include pelvic node if multiple positive groin nodes, ENE, large/deep femoral nodes
    • Can omit negative groin if one side has -ve SLN. Generally bilateral pelvis is treated
  • If SLN micromet (<2mm) → inguinal node + distal half of ipsilateral EI RT to 50Gy
  • Consider adjuvant chemotherapy for all N+
  • If treating LN: should treat pelvis bilaterally and consider treating primary and mons pubis

Operable cN+

  • Radical vulvectomy + inguinofemoral LND
  • If fixed node then debulking rather than LND preferred to reduce morbidity
  • Adjuvant CRT if fixed/matted node upfront
  • Upfront DE CRT if ENE or ≥ 2 nodes – GROINS VIII ongoing

Inoperable (T3)

  • If nodes operable → LND
  • Neoadjuvant CRT 50Gy/25# with cisplatin → reassess for surgery

GROINSS-VI

Observation study, early stage cN0 with SLN -ve → observation alone → low nodal relapse rate

GROINSS VII

P: Early stage cN0 with SLN +ve

I: inguinofemoral LND vs inguinal and pelvic RT

O: nodal recurrence worse if >2mm LN without dissection, therefore surgery required

Criticised as RT dose was low – 45Gy-50Gy

GOG 37

P: vulva cancer, inguinofemoral dissection with pN+

I: ipsilateral pelvic LND OR 45-50Gy RT to bilateral groin and pelvis

O: Adj RT to groin and pelvis improved long term OS from 54% to 68% if 2 or more nodes, ENE, fixed matted node

GOG 279

P: inoperable vulva ca

I: NA RT 64Gy/50Gy/32# with cis/gem

O: pCR 73% however G3 tox >85%

GROINS VII

Ongoing trial investigating whether DE RT 56Gy + cisplatin eliminates need for inguinofem LND in SLN macromet >2mm or ENE

EBRT

Vulva R0 post-op: 50Gy/25#

Positive margins: 66Gy/33#

Elective nodes or low risk post-op nodal bed: 50Gy EQD2

Post-op high risk nodal bed (macro, multiple, ENE): 54-60Gy

Consider concurrent weekly cisplatin for N+ or gross disease

Pre-sim

  • surgical wounds healed
  • med onc review for concurrent chemo in N+

Sim

  • Empty rectum, full bladder
  • Supine, arms on chest, frog leg [for tissue separation and vulva exposure], vac bag
  • consider 1cm bolus over groin and vulva [may require treatment break or removal if severe reaction]
  • wire scars + 3cm radial margin on skin
  • Fuse MRI/PET

Volumes post-op

CTV boost (SM+) 6600: tumour bed + 2cm cropped to anatomical boundaries

CTVp 5000: surgical bed + pre-op tumour extent + 3cm skin margin + 2cm margin cropped to boundaries

PTVp 5000: +7mm

CTV involved node bed 5400: entire nodal bed including clips, seroma

PTV involved node bed 5400: +7mm

CTV elective nodes: involved node bed + next echelon (EI, ob & II

  • If pelvic organ involvement then cover EI, II, obturator, PS, mesorectum accordingly
  • If treating primary + nodes then cover subcut dermal lymphatics in mons bridge

PTV elective nodes 5000: +7mm

NB if treating groin after SLNB+ for micromet then can treat ipsilateral inguinofemoral node and distal half of external iliac to 50Gy as per GROINSS V II

Inf border = top of lesser trochanter or 2cm below saphenous/femoral junction

Toxcities

Acute: wound breakdown, G3 skin reaction, fatigue, cystitis, proctitis, diarrhoea, vaginal mucositis, myelosuppression

Late: wound breakdown, lymphoedema, cellulitis, chronic cystitis, chronic proctitis, sexual dysfunction, vaginal stenosis and shortening, pelvic facture, second malignancy, skin fibrosis/telangiectasia, chronic pain

Vagina

From the exam summary.

Epidemiology

I: rare

A: 60

G: F

R: Caucasian

RF

E: smoking, alcohol, VaIN/HSIL/LSIL, sexual RF, in utero DES

I: HPV

G: nil

I: HIV, solid organ transplant

Staging

T1a DOI <5mm, <2cm, b DOI >5mm, >2cm

T2: paravaginal tissue

T3: lower 1/3 vagina or pelvic side wall

T4: adj organ invasion

N1: pelvic node

M1: DM

Pathology

Pre-invasive

  • HSIL and LSIL – same as cervix

Malignant

  • Extension from cervix, vulva more likely than vaginal primary
  • SCC
  • AdenoCa
  • Melanoma
  • Lymphoma
  • Sarcoma – RMS

Almost all are related to HPV

5-year OS

~50-70% for stage III

~70% for stage II

Management

Proximal, small

  • consider radical hysterectomy, vaginectomy, PLND if R0 achievable

Distal or inoperable

  • If <7mm thick and <2cm:
    • cavity brachytherapy, TD 75-80Gy to 5mm depth

If >7mm thick or >2cm

  • definitive RT to vagina and nodes (45Gy to pelvis) + brachytherapy boost with interstitial needles, TD 75Gy to 5mm depth
  • Consider platinum based concurrent chemo – extrapolated from cervix data

Consider debulking nodal surgery and defunctioning if fistula

Alternative: pelvic exenteration + nodal dissection

No ph III data due to rarity

EBRT + BT

RT technique similar to cervix

45Gy/25# pelvis +/- inguinofemoral nodes + 21Gy/3# OR 24Gy/4# boost to tumour to HR CTV

+/- concurrent cisplatin

Consider placing fiducial markers if planned for BT boost post EBRT as tumour may shrink

If N+ boost to 50-55Gy

If lower 1/3rd vagina involved then sim in frog leg position as will need to cover inguinofemoral nodes
