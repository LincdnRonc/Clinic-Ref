Vulva

Surgery with sentinel node biopsy for small tumours; adjuvant RT for nodes and high-risk margins; chemoRT for locally advanced disease. Never block the midline.

Management

  • T1a (≤2 cm, ≤1 mm invasion): wide local excision with 1 cm margin; no nodal staging.
  • Unifocal <4 cm, >1 mm invasion, cN0: radical local excision + SLNB (GROINSS-V). SLN-negative: observe (groin recurrence 2.3%). Lateralised (>2 cm from midline) → ipsilateral SLN.
  • SLN-positive: ≤2 mm, no ECE → ipsilateral groin/low pelvis RT 50 Gy instead of dissection. >2 mm or ECE → completion IFLND then RT (56 Gy to groin/pelvis if residual risk) (GROINSS-V II).
  • cN+ or ≥4 cm/multifocal: inguinofemoral lymphadenectomy (palpable nodes have ~50% false-positive rate; FNA first).
  • Adjuvant nodal RT: ≥2 positive nodes or ECE (GOG 37); many treat a single node because pelvic recurrence is rarely salvageable.
  • Adjuvant vulvar RT: margin <5–8 mm, LVSI, depth >9 mm; re-excise if margin is the only factor. Margin distance matters less than dVIN/lichen sclerosus in the margin. ~40% recur locally by 10 y.
  • Locally advanced/unresectable: chemoRT with weekly cisplatin to 57.6–64.8 Gy (GOG 205: cCR 64%, pCR 50%; GOG 279 to 64 Gy). Resect residual disease where possible.

  • CTV: whole vulva + 1 cm beyond tumour extension; +2 cm along invaded structures (anus, urethra, clitoris, bladder), ≥3 cm along vagina.
  • Nodes: bilateral inguinal, obturator, external and internal iliac; nodes lie anterior/anteromedial to femoral vessels, so no margin needed posterior/lateral.
  • Sim supine frog-leg on vac-lok (less skin fold), wire tumour/scar/nodes, BB at introitus, bolus over the bed if visible tumour; check TLDs early. Replan for oedema or shrinkage. Full and empty bladder scans for IMRT.
  • Femoral neck tolerance ~50 Gy.

Vagina

Rare; managed by extrapolation from cervix and anal data. RT is the main curative modality; brachytherapy is key.

Management and doses

  • Tumours touching the cervix are cervical; touching the vulva are vulvar. ~80% of vaginal tumours are metastatic from elsewhere. 90% SCC (~80% HPV).
  • Nodes: upper two-thirds → pelvic; lower third → inguinal/femoral. Node risk ~6–14% stage I, 26–32% stage II, 75% III.
  • Thin (<5 mm) stage I: intracavitary BT alone, whole vagina 60–65 Gy and tumour +10–20 Gy; or EBRT + BT.
  • Stage II+ or >4 cm: chemoRT 45 Gy pelvis (± inguinal) + BT boost to 70–80 Gy EQD2 (85 Gy for bulky residual); sidewall/nodes 55–66 Gy (favour 60). Interstitial if >5 mm thick (e.g. 5 Gy ×5 BD single insertion).
  • HDR boost after 45 Gy: 6–7 Gy ×3. IVA: brachytherapy can cause fistula; consider exenteration.
  • Vaginal apex can move 1.5–2 cm AP: ITV with full/empty bladder.

Outcomes to quote

  • 5-y pelvic control stage I / II / III–IVA: 86% / 84% / 71%; CSS 85% / 78% / 58% (MDACC, 193 patients).
  • BT reduced death by ~20% across stages (SEER); chemoRT added ~6% 5-y OS (NCDB).
  • Late effects: vaginal stenosis/fibrosis ~50%, cystitis and proctitis common; fistula with IVA.
