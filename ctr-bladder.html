Bladder preservation (definitive chemoradiation)

There is no single international contouring atlas for the intact bladder. Volumes come from the large trials (BC2001, BCON, RAIDER), so follow the protocol your fractionation comes from. The bladder changes shape and fills during treatment, so volumes and IGRT go together.

Volumes

VolumeDefinition
SimulationBladder emptied just before simulation and each treatment in most whole-bladder protocols (consistent, smaller volume). Some tumour-boost and adaptive protocols use a comfortably full bladder: follow your protocol.
GTVResidual tumour or the TURBT site, from the cystoscopy map, the pre-TURBT MRI (and CT), and fiducials or lipiodol injected at cystoscopy where available.
CTV whole bladderWhole bladder outer wall + GTV, + extravesical extension where present. Add the prostatic urethra (men) when the tumour involves the bladder neck or trigone, or the prostate is involved.
CTV tumour (boost or reduced high-dose volume)GTV + a margin within the bladder wall, as in BC2001's reduced high-dose volume or RAIDER's dose-escalated tumour volume. Check the trial margin before use.
PTVAnisotropic and larger superiorly and anteriorly, where the bladder expands most (often 1.5–2 cm without adaptive IGRT). Smaller with plan-of-the-day or online adaptive RT.
Nodes (if treated)Pelvic nodes with a vessel-based margin as for the NRG prostate atlas (Hall 2021): obturator, internal and external iliac, presacral and distal common iliac. BC2001 and BCON treated the bladder only.

Margin numbers here are typical values, not from a single guideline. Use the PTV and boost margins from the trial or departmental protocol you are following.

Adaptive options

  • Plan of the day: small, medium and large plans made from scans taken at different fill times; the best fit is picked on each day's CBCT (RAIDER, HYBRID).
  • Online adaptive (CBCT- or MR-guided): re-contour the bladder daily, which allows tighter PTVs.
  • Check rectal filling: a full rectum pushes the bladder forward and up.

Post-cystectomy adjuvant RT (Baumann 2016; IBIS update, Verghote 2022)

Most recurrences after cystectomy are in the pelvic side walls and the cystectomy bed. The neobladder or ileal conduit and bowel are the dose-limiting OARs.

CTV

  • Pelvic side walls: obturator, internal and external iliac and distal common iliac nodes (and presacral), with a vessel-based margin, up to about the aortic bifurcation.
  • Cystectomy bed: added when the risk of local failure is high (pT4, positive margins). It lies between the pubic symphysis and the rectum, guided by surgical clips.
  • Ileal conduit: usually spared as an OAR. Neobladder: generally not a target; check the IBIS update for when to include the urethral anastomosis.

The bed borders and nodal upper limit differ between the 2016 guideline and the 2022 update. Use the update's tables.

Organs at risk (Gay 2012 pelvic atlas)

Rectum, bowel bag (or individual loops), femoral heads, and penile bulb or vagina. Contour the bowel bag well above the PTV for nodal fields.

Tips

  • Rectum: from the anorectal junction to the rectosigmoid (where it turns anteriorly); outer wall.
  • Bowel bag: the peritoneal cavity minus muscle and bone, to 1–2 cm above the PTV.
