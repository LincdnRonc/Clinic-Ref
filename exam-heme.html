Lugano staging:

I: 1 nodal area (nodal areas = spleen, thymus, Waldeyer’s ring, nodes)

II: 2 nodal areas, same side of diaphragm

III: nodal areas, both sides of diaphragm

IV: non-contiguous extranodal disease

1: CMR, no avidity

2: CMR, avidity > mediastinum

3: CMR, mediastinum < avidity < liver 4: PR, avidity > liver

5: no response or PD, new areas of avidity

  • LP if BMBx+, sanctuary site, neurologic sx or HIV

Pathology

Grading:

  • 1: centrocytes with <5 centroblasts per 1 hpf
  • 2: centrocytes with 6-15 centroblasts per 1 hpf
  • 3a: >15 centroblasts per 1 hpf intermingled with centrocytes
  • 3b: solid sheets of centroblasts
  • 3b treated as DLBCL

Increasing effacement of follicular nodal architecture with grade

FNA insufficient – only provides flow cytometry sample, need to assess histology, sampling error, insufficient material

IHC: LCA/CD45, CD79a, CD20, CD19

Genetic:

High grade transformation – 2-3% annual risk, 30% overall risk

  • Transformed disease from low grade lymphoma to high grade lymphoma – usually DLBCL
  • Prognosis worse than upfront HG lymphoma ~median OS 1-2 yrs
  • Sx: sudden progression of symptoms after quiescent period, unexpected extranodal disease, rapid B symptoms, symptoms out of keeping with known extent of dx

B sx:

  • >10% wt loss in 6 months
  • Drenching night sweats
  • Fevers >38

PET staging:

  • FL can be heterogenous
  • PET helps:
  • Identify highest SUV site for biopsy
  • Detect occult extranodal/high-grade sites

Management

Stage I-II: potentially curable ~ 50%

  • Definitive radiotherapy improves PFS & may improve OS – retrospective data
  • 24Gy/12# is non-inferior to 45Gy – Lowry trial
  • post-RT chemo with R-CVP improves PFS only – TROG 99.03
  • Implies FL is indolent, relapse is salvageble and does not cause immediate mortality, and has a long natural hx
  • Alternative: surveillance if Rx is morbid

Stage III-IV

  • Upfront treatment does not improve OS and is unlikely to reduce transformation risk – UK RCT, Mayo clinic and French retrospective studies
  • 15% may have spontaneous regression
  • ‘GELF’ criteria to treat:
  • Impending organ dysfunction
  • Symptomatic – B sx or spleen enlargement
  • Bulky dx >7cm or 3 LN regions >3cm
  • Cytopaenia, lymphocytosis
  • Serous effusions
  • Palliative RT: 24Gy/12# OR 4Gy/2# - FORT
  • Chemotherapy:
    • rituximab alone – low tumour burden. S/e: infuction reaction, infection risk
    • R-CHOP – young fit pts with high tumour burden
    • R-CVP (cyclophosphamide, vincristine, pred) or R-bendamustine – unfit pts with high tumour burden

Relapsed dx:

  • Bx to confirm low grade dx
  • Surveillance if no GELF criteria
  • Chemotherapy – different regime to avoid cross resistance
  • Stem cell transplant
  • CAR-T cells
  • Palliative RT

Lowry trial:

P: B cell lymphoma

I: 45Gy vs 24Gy/12# for LG, 30Gy/15# for HG

O: reduced dose RT non inferior for OS or PFS

FORT trial

P: low grade B-cell lymphoma

I: 24Gy/12# vs 4Gy/2#

O: 4Gy inferior for 5 yr PFS (90% vs 70%) but not for OS. Less tox.

Involved field RT

  • Historical standard, includes entire Ann Arbour lymph node region of +ve node (ie entire axilla, cervical chain etc)
  • Associated with higher acute and late toxicity
  • Based on 2D imaging

Involved site RT

  • Treats involved nodes with a margin for uncertainty and inclusion of any equivocal adjacent nodes
  • Requires fusion of pre-chemo and post-chemo PET. CC length maintained, radial margins shrunk to anatomical boundaries and extent of initial disease infiltration
  • Proposed by ILROG to reduce toxicity without compromising disease control. Modern pragmatic approach

Involved node RT

  • Treats only the known involved node with tight margins
  • Proposed by EORTC to reduce toxicity however requires accurate pre-chemo imaging and accurate image registration - all PET scans to be completed in treatment position

Marginal zone

Epidemiology · Natural history · Management · EBRT · Toxicities

Clinic page: Non-Hodgkin lymphoma

Epidemiology

I: 5-10% of NHL, >50% are gastric MALT

A: 60

G: F>M

R: Asia with higher rates of H.pylori

RF:

E: AI disease – Sjogrens – salivary gland MZL, SLE, Hashimotos – thyroid MZL

I:

Chlamydia – orbital MALT

H.pylori – gastric MALT

Campylobacter – SB MALT

G: nil

I: HIV, solid organ transplant

Work-up:

  • BMBx for nodal MZL required as PET variable
    • Not required for extranodal MZL
  • LP as per FL
  • Non gastric EN MZL is assoc with gastric MALT → consider endoscopy in all
  • Gastric MALT is often multifocal and diffuse throughout stomach

Pathogenesis:

  • H. pylori infection → chronic T-cell stimulation → B-cell proliferation
  • Early disease stage is antigen dependent → later accumulation of activating mutations makes it antigen independent and resistant to H.pylori eradication treatment

Natural history

  • Indolent, rarely metastasizes
  • Nodal presentation is uncommon, typically extranodal
  • If disseminated disease on PET consider further Ix of metastatic sites
  • Extra gastric MALT has higher distant mucosal relapse rate

Pathology:

  • Atypical centrocytes effacing architecture in marginal zone of lymphoid follicle

IHC: CD45, CD75a, CD20, CD19

Extranodal MZL: t(11:18), bcl-2+

Gastric MALT: t(11:18)(q21;q21) [API2-MALT1fusion] predicts resistance to H.pylori eradication

H.pylori testing:

  • Urea breath test (must cease PPI 4-6 weeks prior)
  • Stool antigen test
  • MCS test on bx

Transformation risk:

  • 1-2% annual risk of HG transformation

Management

Stage I-II

  • Radiosensitive and RT is curative

Gastric MALT:

  • Eradication of H.pylori with triple therapy (clarithromycin, amoxicillin, PPI) → recheck endoscopy + bx at 3 months, can take up to 18mo for response
    • 80% will achieve CR
    • Can rechallenge HP7
  • Definitive RT 30Gy/15# (1.5Gy/# to reduce toxicity if ulcer)
  • If persistent disease → repeat bx to exclude DLBCL transformation
  • Lower chance of response if t(11:18)(q21;q21)

Orbital, salivary gland, cutaneous MZL:

  • Doxycycline may have response
  • Definitive RT 24Gy/12#
  • Treat entire orbital contents including globe or entire gland
    • Exception is conjunctival MALT where preseptal tissue and conjunctiva is treated with electrons and posterior globe can be spared
  • Consider ‘palliative RT’ 4Gy/2# if amenable to close surveillance and <5cm
    • Low level data shows low dose can have good LC ~ 80%

Splenic MZL:

  • Splenectomy or rituximab preferred
  • Splenic RT: 10Gy/10# using adaptive shrinking field technique
  • Check FBC, EUC to monitor for cytopaenia 2nd daily (plt >30)

Stage III-IV

  • Chemotherapy: R-CHOP → if symptomatic only
  • Palliative RT: 4Gy/2#
  • Surveillance

EBRT

Dose: 30Gy/15# for gastric MALT, VMAT, daily without BD compensation

Pre-sim: PET scan, discuss in MDT

Sim: NBM 4 hours

Supine, arms above head, vac bag

4DCT or motion management if >10mm– EEBH or abdominal compression

Fuse PET

Pre-treatment antiemetic and PPI daily

Volumes:

ITV: entire stomach from GOJ to pylorus, +perigastric nodes on all phases

PTV: +5mm

Toxicities

Acute: fatigue, n/v, reflux, gastritis, epigastric discomfort, enteritis

Late: renal dysfunction, low risk of gastric ulcer, perforation, oesophageal stricture, chronic gastritis, 2nd malignancy

Notes:

Orbital MZL – CTV includes entire orbit, extra-ocular muscles, lacrimal gland, spare lens. Visual fixation to avoid orbital motion.

Acute toxicity: conjunctivitis

Late: dry eye, cataracts, retinopathy

Parotid: xerostomia, dental caries

Splenic MZL – field size reduction as spleen reduces in volume

Clinic page: Non-Hodgkin lymphoma

Epidemiology

I: most common NHL – 30%

A: 60

G: M

R: Caucasian

RF:

E: indolent NHL, AI disease – SLE, sjogrens dx

I:

G: FHx

I: HIV, solid organ transplant

IPI score

Predicts 3 yr OS and PFS

A – age>60
P- PS ECOG >2
L – LDH above ULN
E – extranodal site >1
S – stage III or IV		0-1: 90%
2: 80%
3: 65%
4-5: 60%

CNS prophylaxis/LP assessment indications:

All high risk disease:

  • HIV
  • BMB+ve
  • Testicular, breast
  • Occular
  • Renal, adrenal
  • >1 extranodal site
  • Double/triple hit lymphoma

Testicular DLBCL:

  • Most common testicular tumour in men >60
  • High risk of CNS involvement
  • Managed with orchidectomy, R-CHOPx6, RT to contralateral testis 30Gy, MTX for CNS prophylaxis
  • RT technique: frog leg, penis taped, scrotum resting on solid water block, AP photons, with 1cm bolus, PTV = CTV + 15mm
  • Monitor testosterone long term

Natural history

-Aggressive lymphoma, 60% advanced stage

-Transformed low grade lymphoma does worse, median OS 2 years

Pathology:

IHC: CD45, CD75a, CD20, CD19

Double expressor: 2 of: BCL-2, BCL-6, MYC overexpression on IHC

Double hit: rearrangement of MYC with BCL-2 or BCL-6 on FISH

Triple hit: rearrangement of MYC, BCL2 and BCL6 on FISH

  • Double and triple hit pts should all have R-DA-EPOCH and CNS prophylaxis

Increasingly aggressive disease with above

Consider systemic treatment escalation with triple hit

Hans algorithm to differentiate

Germinal B cell DLBCL

  • CD10+ve, BCL6 +ve

Activated B cell DLBCL – more aggressive

  • MUM1+ve

Beware tumour lysis syndrome in bulky mass >10cm

R-CHOP: rituximab, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine (Oncovin), prednisone

DA-R-EPOCH: dose adjusted + etoposide

CNS prophylaxis:

  • Most CNS relapses in DLBCL are parenchymal
  • IT MTX mainly treats the CSF/leptomeningeal compartment and gives poor brain parenchymal penetration
  • systemic high-dose MTX is preferred when prophylaxis is used in fit patients

Management

Stage I-II, non-bulky <7.5cm

R-CHOP x3 → PET3

- If D1-3

  • R-CHOP x1 - LYSA trial shows R-CHOPx4 w/o RT gives good OS
  • RT preferred if high risk location
  • if D4: RCHOPx3 +/- ISRT or ISRT 40Gy/20#

Stage I-II, bulky >7.5cm

R-CHOP x6 → PET6

  • If D1-3: ISRT 36Gy/18#
  • If D4: ISRT 40Gy/20#
  • UNFOLDER trial shows inferior EFS without RT in bulky dx

Stage III-IV
RCHOP x6 → PET6

  • if D1-3 → no RT required
  • if D4: ISRT 36Gy-40Gy/20#
  • If initial bulk, skeletal involvement or high risk local relapse site initially then consider consolidation ISRT 30Gy/15#
  • RICOVER showed worse PFS and OS without RT for bulky dx in all stages
  • German pooled analysis shows worse EFS with omission of RT in bone disease

Other:

  • R-POLA-CHOP: 4% DFS benefit at 5 yrs, not available via medicare but discuss with young fit patients

CNS prophylaxis:

  • For all high risk disease (see LP indications)
  • Use CNS IPI score
  • Intrathecal methotrexate

Primary refractory: D5 through R-CHOP or relapse <12 mo

  • Bx confirmation
  • CAR-T cells

Relapsed disease:

  • Bx confirmation
  • salvage chemotherapy → consolidation Auto SCT if PR or CR
  • CAR-T cells

Allogenic SCT is 3rd line+ for multiply relapsed disease

SCT:

  • Au SCT: Myeloablative BEAM chemotherapy is SOC
  • Allo SCT: major risk is GVHD
  • myeloablative chemo: busulfan + fludarabine BuFlu
  • myeloablative conditioning with TBI: 12Gy/6# + cyclophosphamide preferred if sanctuary site (testes/CNS) or ALL (in ALL GVHD is desirable)

RT & CAR-T therapy:

CAR-T:

  • Refractory within 12 months or 3rd line relapsed disease
  • leukaphoresis on pt blood -> CD3+ T cells selected out -> Insert gene for CAR to T cell overseas -> production of CAR-T -> lymphodepleting chemotherapy --> infuse CAR-T patient
  • Takes 4-6 weeks
  • CAR-T targets CD19 on B cell lymphoma, leukaemia or BCMA for myeloma
  • Requires admission for ~1 month post infusion, risk of cytokine release syndrome

Role of RT in bridging:

  • Cytoreduction
  • Symptom palliation
  • Increase immunogenicity locally and systemically
  • Reduce immunosuppressive tumour microenvironment

Dose: 20Gy/5# - 30Gy/10#, delivered between collection (apheresis) and infusion of CAR-T cells

If ‘oligo’ sites of disease (<5) then treat all sites – retrospective MVA analysis shows this improves OS

EBRT for D4 stage II-IV

Dose: 40Gy/36Gy/20# SIB, VMAT, daily without BD compensation

Alternatively deliver 30Gy/15# with sequential cone down 10Gy/5# boost

Pre-sim: ensure pre-chemo and post-chemo PET available, DTPA scan and pre-med if abdomen, commence 3-4 weeks post chemo

Sim: supine, arms away, vacbag, motion mgmt

IV contrast

Fuse both PETs

Volumes:

i/GTV: residual disease on post-chemo PET

ITV/CTV

+5mm crop to uninvolved anatomical boundaries

PTV4000: +5mm

ITV/CTV 3600: pre-chemo PET craniocaudally, axially respecting normal anatomical boundaries that were displaced not infiltrated

+ 5mm margin (safety margin as PET not in treatment position)

PTV 3600: +5mm margin

Notes:

If 2 nodal areas involved with <5cm of separation – encompass within 1 volume, otherwise 2 separate volumes

Primary CNS

Epidemiology · Pathology · Management · WBRT · GTVb: residual disease

Clinic page: Non-Hodgkin lymphoma

Epidemiology

I: Rare

A: 65+yo

G: M

R: nil

RF:

E: Hx of aggressive B lymphoma

I:

G:

I: HIV – is an AIDS defining illness, solid organ transplant

Outcomes:

Poor prognosis without treatment: <6mo

5 yr OS 70% with AuSCT

Median OS 10-18mo with WBRT

Pathology

More commonly a B cell lymphoma – DLBCL ABC type

Less commonly – T cell lymphoma

IHC: CD45, CD75a, CD20+, CD19+, MUM1+

MRIB:

Homogenously enhancing mass ‘fluffy cloud’

Unlikely to have haemorrhage

Diffusely infiltrative

Mass effect and oedema

Deep to be periventricular and deep brain structure involvement

Caution with steroid use prior to established dx

Slit lamp exam to exclude orbital involvement

  • If involved include orbit in WBRT field

Management

Fit, young pt:

High dose MTX based induction chemo – MATRIX (MTX, cytarabine, thiotepa, rituximab)

  • If CR → consolidation with auto SCT with myeloablative chemotherapy prior
  • If PR → palliative RT WBRT 36Gy with 45Gy boost to residual disease

Unfit, elderly patient

High single agent MTX induction chemotherapy

  • If CR → consolidation with non-myeloablative chemotherapy or further MTX chemo or rdWBRT 23.4Gy/13#
  • If PR → BSC or WBRT as above
  • WBRT shown to have worse neurocognitive s/e in older

Response assess on IPCG guidelines: assesses MRI, steroid use, occular exam, CSF

Note: IT therapy has poor penetration into brain parenchyma

→ misses the dominant site of relapse therefore not used

Evidence:

  • MATRIX induction better than MTX based double agent > MTX alone – phase II
  • Consolidation AuSCT improves OS compared to consolidation non myeloablative chemo – ph III
  • Consolidation WBRT has no OS diff to AuSCT but worse PFS and neurocog tox. Hence consider rd WBRT for CR - ph II
  • Consolidation WBRT better than obs alone for PFS – phase III

Dose: 36Gy/20# + 9Gy/5# seq boost, VMAT, daily treatment no BD compensation

Pre-sim: slit lamp exam, MRI, MDT discussion, commence 4 weeks after chemo

Sim: supine, arms down, TPS for head

Fuse MRI T1 + gad and T2

Volumes:

CTV: whole brain, meninges, including crista gali, cribriform plate, excluding anterior 1/3rd orbit if uninvolved/cover posterior 2/3rd orbit, C1 and C2

PTV3600: +3mm

GTVb: residual disease

CTVb: +10mm cropped to anatomical boundaries (falx, ventricles)

PTV 45000: +3mm

Epidemiology

I: Rare

A: 20-40yo

G: F

RF

E: young, female

I: nil

I: nil

3 yr OS as per IPI score

5 yr OS (From IESLG-37):

>95% for localised

>90% for advanced

Refractory - 60-70%

Natural Hx:

Locally aggressive anterior mediastinal mass

Continuous nodal involvement

Distant spread uncommon

Treatment responsive

Natural history

Presents with rapid bulky mediastinal mass and symptoms secondary to compression

Unlikely to have nodal dx vs NSHL

SVCO in 30-50%: distended chest/neck veins, orthopneoa, stridor, SOB, hoarse voice, facial swelling, plethora, pembertons positive, headaches, confusion

Path:

  • Arises from thymic B cells
  • can resemble NSHL
  • B/g of compartmentalising sclerosis
  • IHC: CD20+, CD 19+, CD45
  • CD23 and CD30 weak +ve
  • CD15-ve

Chemo:

DA-R-EPOCH: dose adjusted ritux, etoposide, prednisone, vincristine, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin

Toxicites:

  • Rituximab: infusion reaction (fevers, bronchospasm), late neutropaenia
  • Etoposide: myelosuppression, n/v
  • Vincristine: peripheral neuropathy
  • Cyclophosphamide: haem cystitis
  • Doxorubicin: cardiac toxicity
  • Other: TLS

Management

DA-R-EPOCH x6 chemotherapy more effective than CHOP

  • If D4 → consolidation ISRT – 30Gy/15#
  • If D5 → CAR-T

If R-CHOP x6 chemo then:

  • Generally need consolidation ISRT 30Gy for D1-3 or 40Gy for D4
  • Omission of RT for D1-3 post R-CHOP is an emerging std (IESLG-37 trial, 2.5 yr f/u showed excellent PFS >95%)
  • Employ DIBH for RT

Post treatment PET 8-12 weeks post RT to assess response

Survivorship post thoracic RT:

  • Breast cancer screening – annual MRI +/- MMG/US at 25yo or 8 years post RT, whichever occurs later
  • Risk is elevated lifelong and related to dose, field size and age at exposure
  • Annual TFTs, lipid studies, HbA1c
  • Cardiac TTE/stress test with cardiologist 10 yrs post RT
  • Carotid US 10 years post

  • SLL: lymphomatous deposits → treated with palliative RT if symptomatic: 4Gy/2# is effective
  • CLL is leukemic variant → if symptomatic, cytopaenic or rapid increase in peripheral blood lymphocytosis → venetoclax +/- rituximab, ibrutinib

  • Causes hyperviscosity syndrome → plasmapheresis

Burkitt’s Lymphoma:

  • Children, very aggressive
  • Endemic: African children, EBV, rapidly progressing jaw mass, B sx, unwell child
  • Sporadic form: rapidly progressing abdominal mass, B sx, unwell child
  • t(8:14)
  • Medical emergency → chemotherapy urgently; watch for tumour lysis syndrome

Mantle cell lymphoma

  • Variable clinical course – may remain indolent for many years then suddenly progress
  • t(11:14)
  • Limited stage → 24Gy/12# RT alone is potentially curative but this presentation is rare

Advanced staged → chemotherapy, no role for RT

Tumour Lysis Syndrome

Rapid destruction of tumour resulting in release of intracellular contents into systemic circulation causing:

  • electrolyte derangement with 2 of or 25% change from baseline within 3 days: high urea, hyperkalaemia, hyperphosphatemia, hypocalcaemia [PUCK + SRC]
  • + one of the following: seizure, renal impairment, cardiac arrythmia

Risk factors:

  • Tumour bulk >10cm
  • High tumour proliferation rate
  • High WCC
  • High LDH
  • Radiosensitive subtype
  • Pre-existing renal dysfunction

Prophylaxis:

  • risk based depending: bulk of disease, LDH, sensitivity of pathology to planned treatment
  • vigorous IV hydration and allopurinol OR rasburicase commencing 24-48 hours prior to treatment
  • Treatment usually continues for 3-7 days after the high risk TLS period

Epidemiology

I: 10% of lymphoma, 70% of HL is NSHL

A: Bimodal for mixed cellularity, 15-30 for NSHL, 60+

G: F=M for NSHL, remainder M
R: Caucasian, North America

RF:

E: smoking

I: EBV – mixed cellulaity, LD

G: Fhx

I: HIV, organ transplant

German Hodgkin score

Early stage disease – fav vs unfav. Any of the following:

3 - ≥3 LN areas

B- Bulk >1/3rd thoracic diameter (at T5/6), B sx

E- Extranodal disease

E- ESR >50 if A, >30 if B

IPS score

Advanced stage

M - male
A – age ≥45
S – stage IV
H – Hb low <105
A – albumin <40
L – lymphopenia
L – leukocytosis		Each factor drops PFS by 5%, caps at 5

Chemo agents:

  • Adriamycin: cardiomyopathy, heart failure
  • Bleomycin: pneumonitis
  • Vinblastine: peripheral neuropathy
  • Dacarbazine: n/v, myelosuppression
  • BEACOPP: bleomycin, etoposide, Adriamycin, cyclophosphamide, oncovin (vincristine), procarbazine, pred

Natural history

Intermediate growth. Spreads contiguously along nodal chains with late extranodal disease.

NSHL presents with mediastinal mass

Pathology:

Classic HL:

  • Nodular sclerosing ~ 70%
  • mixed inflammatory background with broad collagen bands dividing node into nodules
  • rich in lacunar cells
  • Mixed cellularity ~ 30%
  • Male, 50+
  • HIV, EBV
  • often advanced/B sx
  • RS cells, lymphocytes, eosinophils, plasma cells; no nodular sclerosis
  • Lymphocyte predominant 5%
      • Many lymphocytes, few RS cells
  • Lymphocyte deplete <5%
  • Few lymphocytes, many RS cells
  • HIV
  • Poor prognosis

General:

  • Reed-Steinberg with owl-eye nuclei cells in variable concentration
  • Fibrotic or sclerotic background
  • IHC: CD 45-, CD30+, CD15+, EBV/LMP1

Nodular lymphocyte predominant HL:

  • M predominant, 40-50yo
  • Variant of RS cells → popcorn cells in a nodular B cell lymphocyte rich and histiocyte background
  • IHC: CD20+, CD19+, CD45+
  • managed as per indolent B cell lymphoma
  • 20-30% risk of transformation to DLBCL

Management

PET adapted approach following EORTC HD 10

Early stage favourable:

ABVD x2 → PET2

  • If D1-3:
  • ISRT 20Gy/10# OR
  • 2 additional cycles of ABVD. Option to drop bleomycin
    • inferior to ISRT for PFS by 10% and has worse cardiac toxicity for mediastinal disease (HD-10)
  • OS same for ISRT vs 2 further ABVD
  • If D4: escBEACOPPx2 → ISRT 30Gy if D1-3, biopsy + ISRT 30Gy if D4

Early stage unfavourable, non-bulky:

ABVD x2 → PET2

  • If CR: ABVD x2 + ISRT 30Gy/15#, option to drop Bleomycin to reduce lung tox
  • If PR: escBEACOPP x2 → observe if D1-3, 30Gy if D4 (HD-17)

Advanced stage or any stage bulky:

ABVD x2 → PET2

  • If CR: ABVD x 4, option to drop Bleomycin to reduce lung tox
  • If PR: escBEACOPP x4 → If D4 → ISRT if PR 36Gy

ABVD = Adriamycin, bleomycin, vinblastine, dacarbazine

  • Adriamycin + RT = cardiac risk
  • Bleomycin + RT = pneumonitis risk, hence dropped after 2 cycles if CR

Relapsed:

  • D5 at any stage → biopsy of most accessibly progressive site
  • Goal is to achieve PET response with salvage chemo
  • 2nd line chemotherapy e.g.: ICE, combination with brentuximab vedotin or Nivolumab
  • If responsive to chemo follow with myeloablation + AuSCT
  • ISRT 36Gy-40Gy can still be used for peri-transplant consolidation

Notes:

  • nivo-AVD and BV-AVD are upcoming chemotherapy regimes for HL

Outcomes:

Early stage: 10+ year OS 90%

Adv stage: 10+ year OS 80%

Mortality driven by treatment side effects rather than lymphoma

  • Second malignant: breast, thyroid, lung, sarcoma
  • Cardiovascular disease: RT + anthracycline

EBRT – advanced stage with PR

  • IHC: CD 38+, CD 138+, MUM1+ve

Radiosensitive

Management paradigm

  • Chemotherapy is backbone of treatment
  • Induction phase: induce disease remission
  • Consolidation phase: involves allogenic stem cell transplant
  • Maintenance phase
  • TKI can be used in the presence of a Philadelphia chromosome in CML
  • CAR-T cells can be used in relapsed/refractory disease
  • Observation appropriate in CLL unless active symptomatic disease

Role of radiotherapy:

  • ALL, and to a lesser extent AML, can involve CNS
  • CSI is indicated for ‘CNS3’ disease (CSF with WBC >5/µL and blasts) if no SCT planned or in relapsed/refractory CNS disease
    • 18Gy/10#
  • WBRT is used in paediatric patients instead of CSI to reduce late effects
  • TBI may be required for conditioning for SCT
  • Testicular leukaemia is a sanctuary site and if relapsed/refractory requires 24Gy/12#
  • Symptomatic splenomegaly in CML can be managed with low dose shrinking field hyperfractionated RT 5Gy/10#
  • Extramedullary myeloid sarcoma can be managed with 24-30Gy in 1.8Gy – 2Gy/#

TBI rationale:

  • Eradicate malignant cells, esp in sanctuary sites
  • Use non cross resistant treatment to eradicate chemoresistant cells
  • Create space in marrow for transplant
  • Host immunosuppression to accept transplant and reduce GVHD
