Three keys that bite

  • Ctrl+Z only reverses contour / block edits — not margins, booleans or structure deletion. Save before big operations.
  • Delete removes the selected contour on that slice; Shift+Delete removes the entire structure. Know which one your finger is on.
  • Esc cancels a half-drawn contour — use it instead of clicking out and leaving a stray open segment.

Speed set-up (do once)

  • Build personal W/L presets and a saved layout; set it as your user default.
  • Learn the guide-radius arrows ( ↑ / ↓ ) so you never leave the image to resize the Replace tool.
  • Use right-click → tool instead of travelling to the ribbon; keep one hand on the keyboard for PgUp / PgDn, Esc, Ctrl+Z.
  • Turn on Autosave Contours so a crash doesn't cost you the session.
  • Use Anatomical Groups to show/hide related structures (e.g. all H&N OARs) in one click.

Contouring toolkit: what to reach for

What each tool does and when it is the right one.

Monaco-specific gotchas and workflow

How Monaco handles structures differently from other systems, a working order for a typical case, and window/level starting points.

Monaco-specific gotchas

IssueWhat to do
Voxel-based structuresMonaco rasterises structures to voxels; a voxel is assigned to a structure once roughly half of it lies inside the contour. Thin or tiny structures (urethra, chiasm, cochlea, small mets) can lose or gain volume — check the reported volume and DVH, and contour generously-but-accurately rather than 1-voxel-thin.
Jagged external / bodyMonaco re-voxelises the CT on import, so contours follow the voxel grid and the external can look stepped (relevant for SGRT surfaces). Applying a 0 mm Auto Margin to the external is a commonly used way to smooth it.
Structure layering & overlapsWhen structures overlap, the layering order in the IMRT constraints decides which owns each voxel during optimisation. Create explicit optimisation structures (PTV minus OAR, OAR minus PTV) rather than relying on layering alone.
Contours outside the patientStructures extending beyond the external (e.g. PTV into air) need cropping or deliberate density handling — crop PTV 3–5 mm under skin for optimisation where your protocol allows.
Scan reference pointDefine and lock it once contouring is complete; moving it late creates downstream set-up errors.
Structure mismatch at planningRenaming or deleting structures after a template is loaded triggers mismatches — finalise names (TG-263 style) before the planner starts.

Efficient sequence for a typical case

  • Fuse first (MR/PET) and check the registration locally around the target, not globally.
  • External → auto-segment / EZ Sketch OARs → edit → targets last, when your eye is fresh.
  • GTV on every slice; OARs every 2–3 slices + interpolation, then verify.
  • CTV via Auto Margin from GTV, then edit against anatomical barriers using paintbrush with structure avoidance.
  • PTV and PRVs only by Auto Margin / templates — never hand-drawn.
  • Run EZClean, then a final scroll in sagittal and coronal: steps and gaps jump out.

Quality habits

  • Change W/L for each structure: soft-tissue for pelvis, lung window for airways/lung, bone for canal and sacral plexus.
  • Check superior/inferior extent on sagittal & coronal — the commonest miss in axial-only contouring.
  • Use colour-wash / transparency to spot overlaps between CTV and OARs.
  • Keep a _orig copy before major edits; it's your audit trail and undo for non-undoable steps.
  • Consistent naming and colours via structure templates help peer review and auto-planning.
  • Screen-grab with Ctrl+Alt+PrtScn for peer-review or teaching cases (de-identify first).
