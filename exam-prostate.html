Low & fav. intermediate

Epidemiology · Pathology subtypes · Management · LDR Brachytherapy (ABS, GEC-ESTRO · EBRT SABR

Clinic page: Prostate

Epidemiology

I: Most common ca in Au, 1 in 8 men, 4th cause of death

A: 60+

G: M

R: Caucasian

RF

E: chronic prostatitis, obesity, high red meat/animal fats, smoking

I: nil

G: BRCA 2 (more aggressive), fhx, Fanconi anemia

I: nil

Staging

T1: detected by needle

T2: palpable or radiological

T2a: <1/2 of one side

T2b: >1/2 of one side

T2c: both sides

T3a: EPE

T3b: SVI

T4: adj organ

N1: pelvic node

M1a: distant node (mesorectum, CI)

M1b: bone

M1c: other viscera

NCCN Risk groups

Low risk all of

  • PSA<10
  • GS 6
  • T1-2a

FIRPC all of

  • <50% bx cores PLUS:
  • PSA<20
  • GS3+4
  • T2b-c

UIRPC one of

  • PSA<20
  • GS4+3
  • T2b-c

HRPC one of

  • PSA>20
  • T3a
  • GS 8 or 4+5

VHRPC one of

  • GS 5+4
  • >4 cores of GS 8-9
  • T3b, T4

Oligometastatic

  • STOPCAP M1: ≤5 bone mets
  • STAMPEDE: non regional nodes, ≤ 3 mets with ≤1 outside the axial skeleton

Genomic scores

  • Oncotype Dx: predicts PCM, DM, chance of aggressive features on pathology after RP
  • Decipher score: allows improved risk group stratification
  • prognostic score for OS, CSS, DM.
  • Higher score correlated to shorter time on AS.
  • Validated in Decipher MA – retrospective and prospective studies
  • ArteraAI: predictive of benefit of addition of ADT to RT

Pathology subtypes

  • acinar adenocarcinoma (95%)
  • ductal adenocarcinoma (3%) – more aggressive, less PSA secretion
  • mucinous adenocarcinoma (<2%)
  • small cell carcinoma – may transform from acinar, high aggressive, no PSA secretion
  • neuroendocrine carcinoma
  • carcinosarcoma
  • squamous cell carcinoma

IHC: AMACR+, NKX3.1+, PSA+, HMWCK-, p63-

Other histology factors

  • cribriform pattern - higher DM rates (PROTECT secondary analysis)
  • extensive intraductal component – BRCA
  • both of these are at least pattern 4
  • PNI increases risk of SM+

Gleason score

Ranges from 3-5 and reflects histology

  • 3- separate infiltrating glands
  • 4- fused glands without intervening stroma but lumen remains present
  • 5- sheets of tumour cells, no glands

Biopsy

  • Each core graded separately
  • Composite score = predominant pattern + highest pattern
  • Highest score = individual core with the highest composite score (this is used for stratification)

Prostatectomy

  • Composite score = highest pattern + most predominant pattern
  • Tertiary score = highest score if present in <5% of tissue

Biopsy methods

  • TP bx – recommended, allows best sampling
  • TRUS bx – poorer sampling of anterior prostate, higher infection risk
  • Targeted bx – multiple cores taken from suspicious region
  • Saturation bx – extensive sampling
  • TURP chips – incidental

PSMA PET

ProPSMA trial (Hoffman)

  • 30% more sensitive and accurate than conventional staging
  • Led to changes in management 15% more
  • Less equivocal findings
  • Less radiation exposure

Disadv

  • Stage migration uncertainty
  • Urine obscures results
  • Some histologies have less PSMA avidity

Screening PSA

  • PSA has low specificity and sensitivity ~ 70%
  • PPV increases with ↑ PSA and velocity
  • Men>50yo or >40yo if fhx should still have
  • Normal PSA:
  • 40’s - 2.5
  • 50’s - 3.5
  • 60’s - 4.5
  • 70’s - 5.5
  • PSA ½ life: 2 days
  • False positive: recent IDC/trauma, prostatitis, BPH, post-ejaculation
  • Free/bound PSA ratio: ↓ in PCA

Active surveillance (PRIAS project guidelines)

  • LE > 10 years
  • Fit for active treatment
  • GS 3+3 or less, 3+4 in <50% ok if no cribriform/IDC
  • No cribriform pattern
  • PSA<10
  • cT2
  • PSA q6monthly, DRE, MRI annually
  • 1 further biopsy, then as indicated

Management

1. Watchful waiting

  • LE <10 years (PIVOT)

2. Active surveillance

  • Indications as outlined
  • Treat if criteria breached or PSA DT <12mo
  • Delay toxicity
  • Genomic scores help guide decision making (Decipher)
  • 15 yr PCM equivalent to active treatment, higher DM rate, 50% of patients will require treatment by 10 years (ProtecT)

3. Prostatectomy

  • Robotic, nerve-sparing
  • Significant obstructive sx
  • RP: 50% erectile dysfunction with nerve spare (PACE A)
  • ProtecT:
  • RP: Higher urinary incontinence needing pads 20% vs 10%
  • RP: worse upfront ED, converges by 10+ yrs
  • RT: ↑ voiding at 6 mo but recovered, ↑ nocturia 50% vs 30%
  • RT: worse bowel function at 6 mo, mostly recovered except bloody stools in 5% and faecal leakage 12% vs 6%

4. LDR Brachytherapy monotherapy with I-125

  • Indications: adequate urinary fx (PVR <100mL, peak flow >10mL/s), no PAI, GS3+4, T2, PSA <10, pros volume <60cc, no TURP defect (mini TURP or bladder neck incision ok), LE >10 years, fit for GA
  • No cremation within 2yrs
  • More subacute GU toxicity for up to 3mo, less erectile dysfunction and low GI toxicity (King et al, Zelefsky et al)
  • PSA bounce phenomenon
  • The combination of EBRT + LDR BT boost vs LDR BT monotherapy did not improve PFS - RTOG 0232
  • NB: HDR monotherapy not recommended (Hudson et al, SHERBERT)

5. SABR: 40Gy/5#

  • Indications: PSA 3+4, T2, low IPSS
  • Rectal spacer and fiducial prior (Hamstra et al)
    • Hamstra et al demonstrated improves late GI tox however Barrigel trial reduced G2+ acute tox but not late
  • Equiv bPFS vs 60Gy/20#, more medium term GU toxicity at year 1-2, equiv by 5 yrs (PACE B)
  • convenience

6. Standard hypofractionated RT

  • 60Gy/20# (CHHiP)
  • Optimises cancer control whilst reducing toxicity
  • TURP prior if IPSS > 14
  • less incontinence than surgery, more GI toxicity, similar ED

Focal therapy

  • HIFU, cryotherapy
  • Likely high recurrence risk up to 70%
  • NCCN does not recommend in first line, paucity of high level evidence

PIVOT

P: T1-2, PSA<50, age <75yo

O: WW vs RP

I: 20 yr OS no different in LR

PROTECT

P: prospective cohort study. ~66% low-risk, ~34% intermediate or high risk combined

I: AM (PSA monitoring only) vs RT vs RP

O: 15yr PCM no diff between arms <5%. 50% of AS had treatment by 10 yrs. DM higher in AS (10% vs 5%) & if cribriform pattern present on secondary MVA

Bloody stools, GI leakage, nocturia - ↑ RT
GU incontinence and ED - ↑ RP. 20% did not recover continence

PACE A

P: LR or FIRPC

I: RP vs SABR

O: efficacy pending

GU: 10% GU in both, 50% pad use after RP, 5% after RT

GI: 0% G2 in both, 16% mod bowel sx after RT, 0% after RP

PACE B

P: LR, FR or UIRPC, GS 4+3 excluded

I: 40Gy/36.25Gy/5# vs 60Gy/20#. No rectal spacer.

O: SABR non-inferior, 5 yr BDFS 95%, DM 1% for both

Acute G2 GI tox: 10%, G2 GU tox 25% - no diff

More cumulative late G2 GU toxicity 40% vs 30% due to 1-2 yr rise

Late cumulative GI tox no diff 20%, no G3+

CHHiP

P: LR to HRPC

I: 74Gy/37# vs 60Gy/20# vs 57Gy/19#

O: 60Gy non inferior to 78Gy. No diff in late tox. ED better with hypofrac

Hamstra et al (2017)

P: ph III RCT, IRPC having 79Gy/44#

I: spacer vs no spacer

O: reduction in late G2 GI toxicity from 6% to 0%. Bowel QoL better.

King et al

  • Systematic review and consensus statement of LDR BT use
  • LDR BT monotherapy acceptable for LR & FIRPC. Expect 8-10 bPFS >90%. GU s/e peak by 3mo, settle by 1 yr

Zelefsky et al

  • Case series reporting LDR I-125 5yr bPFS ≥90% with acceptable toxicity when implant dosimetry goals are met (see below)

Hudson et al

P: LR-IR PCA

I: HDR monotherapy 19Gy/1# vs 27Gy/2#

O: 8 yr biochemical control 60-80%. Inferior oncologic outcomes

SHERBERT pooled analysis

  • In IRPC, HDR BT vs pros SABR had more biochemical failure at 10 years – 38% vs 10%
  • No OS or DM difference
  • Acute G2 GU toxicity higher with HDR BT, late tox no diff

LDR Brachytherapy (ABS, GEC-ESTRO

LDR Brachytherapy (ABS, GEC-ESTRO guidelines)

Dose: 145Gy to prostate PTV as per ABS guidelines

Method: LDR via I-125 seeds

I-125 mean energy 28 keV, X-ray and gamma emission, 60 day half life

Pre-volume study: Urology review, uroflow assessment +/- median lobe resection, consent, empty rectum and bladder for all

Volume study: dorsolithotomy, cystoscopy + IDC insertion, TRUS with stepper unit, 5mm slices of entire prostate, contour pros, rectum, urethra on each slice

Expand prostate by 4mm except 1mm posteriorly to PTV

Plan design + seed ordering → seed QA on arrival

Implant: dorsolithotomy, IDC, TRUS with stepper unit, grid sutured to perineum, pre-loaded needles inserted to prostate via perineum using Seattle technique (ant to post) under US guidance

Post-procedure: TOV, tamsulosin 400mcg, strain urine for 2 weeks, avoid close lap contact for 1 month, day 30 CT urethrogram for post-implant dosimetry and CXR for seed migration

Post-implant dosimetry

V100% > 95% (minimum 90%)

V150% >50%

V200%<20%

  • too cold → insert more seeds
  • too hot → rectal spacer, counsel pt, can consider a miniTURP with HBO for healing

Urethra Dmax <150%

Rectum D1cc <100%

GI G1: 40%, G2 <5%

Late

GU G2: 20%, 4% urethral stricture, 20% urethritis

GI G1: 20%, G2 <10%

ED: 40%

Unfav. intermediate

Definition · EBRT · OARs (EQD2 doses)

Clinic page: Prostate

Definition

  • GS 4+3 or GS 3+4 >50% bx cores
  • PSA <20
  • cT2b-c

Mgmt

1. RP

  • More urinary incontinence
  • Worse ED if no nerve spare performed

2. Hypofractionated RT: 60Gy/20# +/- WPRT

  • Pelvic nodal irradiation is now controversial
  • Historically it was considered if ROACH score ≥ 20 [2/3 x PSA + 10 x (GS – 7)] (POP-RT, RTOG)
  • ROACH score may overestimate risk in modern era - POP-RT showed 5-10% DFS and DM benefit at 5 yrs however RTOG showed only 5% BF benefit at 10 years and was a much larger multi-centre trial
  • WPRT increases late GU toxicity by ~ 10%
  • RT has worse GI toxicity and nocturia vs RP

3. SABR: 40Gy/36.25Gy/5#

  • Now being used increasingly since WPRT is less standard
  • PACE C shows equivalent toxicities compared to 60Gy/20# though efficacy pending

4. Short term ADT

  • 4-6 months adjuvant with RT for DFS benefit and possible OS benefit (EORTC Bolla, RTOG 0815, Patel et al MA, RTOG 9910/DART)
  • Given concurrently with RT (Malone et al, Canadian)
  • S/e: hot flushes, wt gain, muscle mass loss, gynaecomastia, mood change, osteoporosis, cardiac death no difference (Nguyen MA)
  • Most will recover testosterone by 1 year post ADT
  • Direct antagonist (degarelix): less risk of urinary obstruction with flare, less cardiac toxicity (REVELUTION demonstrated less coronary plauqes), faster testosterone recovery
  • GnRH agonist: lucrin, zoladex

EORTC Bolla

  • 6mo ADT with definitive RT improved biochem and clinical DFS

RTOG 0815

  • 6mo ADT reduces 5 yr DM from 4% to 1% and improves PCM. No DM benefit in GS≤6

Patel et al MA

  • ST ADT can improve OS (5% at 10 yrs, 2% at 15 yrs), PFS and BF in IRPC → this survival is not replicated in all but 1 trial though

RTOG 9910/DART

  • Longer durations of ADT (9 or 18mo) did not improve OS or bPFS in IR

Malone et al, Canadian

  • 4 mo NA ADT + 2mo concurrent vs 6 mo concurrent ADT did not impact bPFS or OS

POP-RT

  • P: N0 with ROACH score ≥ 20, n=200. 80% PSMA PET
  • I: WPRT 50Gy/25# vs pros/SV only
  • O: 5 yr ↑ DFS 90% vs 80% & less DM 90% vs 96%. OS no diff. Late G2 GU tox 30% vs 20%, late G2 GI 5% no diff

RTOG 0924 (Roach et al, ASTRO 2025)

  • P: ROACH ≥ 15 with IRPC – fav HRPC, n=2400, no PSMA PET which may = higher risk cohort
  • I: WRPT 45Gy/25# vs pros/SV only. ADT allowed 6 or 32mo
  • O: 10 yr BF 13% vs 17%, DM and OS no diff

PACE C

  • P: IRPC & HRPC (T1c-T3a, GS ≤8 or less, PSA ≤30)
  • I: 36.25Gy/5# vs 60Gy/20# to prostate. No rectal spacer.
  • 2 yr G2+ GU toxicity 13% vs 4%, 2 yr G2+ GI tox 3% in both. G3 GU/GI tox very low for all <2%

Nguyen MA 2011

  • Systematic review of >4000 pts demonstrated no risk of cardiac death with ST or LT ADT whilst ADT improved PCM

Concurrent 6 month ADT

Comfortably full bladder, empty bowel

PTV 4400: +7mm

Daily CBCT with fiducial match and soft tissue check

High & very high

Definitions · EBRT + HDR · EBRT + DIL boost

Clinic page: Prostate

Definitions

HRPC

  • GS 8 or 4+5
  • PSA >20
  • T3a

VHRPC

  • GS 8 or 4+5 in > 4cores
  • GS 5+4
  • T3b-T4

Mgmt

1. WPRT: 45Gy/20# or 50Gy EQD2

  • if N+ then boost to 60Gy
  • RT + ADT has lower risk of DM in HRPC than RP (post-hoc analysis of 2 RCTs PUNCH & RTOG 0521)
  • No WPRT in VHPRC - PEACE 2 showed no MFS or PFS benefit

2. RT boost to primary

  • HDR brachytherapy: 15Gy/1# followed by 46Gy/23# EBRT to pelvis (Hoskin trial, Zaorsky MA)
  • DIL isotoxic boost: 65-67Gy in 20#– EQD2 90Gy (FLAME)
  • LDR brachytherapy boost not preferred due to increase in toxicity (ASCENDE-RT)

3. Long term ADT

  • 18 – 36 months (PCS IV, TROG RADAR, MARCAP MA)
  • S/e: as above + osteoporosis risk – need baseline BMD
  • Testosterone recovery takes roughly double the duration of administration (18 mo → 3 years etc)
  • if VHRPC or N+ consider adding Abiraterone for 2 yrs improves OS in addition to 3 yrs of ADT (self funded $1000 per month) (STAMPEDE)

4. Radical prostatectomy + PLND not preferred

  • High risk of micrometastatic disease (MSKCC calculators, (post-hoc analysis of 2 RCTs PUNCH & RTOG 0521)
  • High risk of requiring salvage RT, increasing toxicity

Hoskin trial

  • P: IRPC and HRPC
  • I: 55Gy/20/# vs 55Gy/20# + HDR 17Gy/2#
  • O: 12 yr RFS improved by 20% with HDR. Risk category and no ADT also ↑ relapse risk. Arms were equitoxic.

Zaorsky MA

  • Dose escalation to up to BED 200Gy associated with improved biochemical control

FLAME

  • P: IRPC & HRPC
  • I: Isotoxic DIL boost up to 95Gy EQD2 (only achieved in 20%) vs 78Gy
    O: 10 yr bDFS 85% vs 70%, OS and DMFS not diff. Equitoxic.
  • NB: hypoFLAME examining SABR 35Gy/5#+ isotoxic boost to 50Gy

ASCENDE-RT

  • P: IR-HR PCA
  • I: EBRT 78Gy vs EBRT + LDR BT boost 115Gy I-125
  • O: 15yr bPFS 85% vs 70%. No OS or DM diff. Worse G3 GU toxicity 18% vs 5%, more pad usage and catheter need

MARCAP MA

  • Addition of any ADT improves 10yr OS by 8% and MFS by 8%
  • Addition of LT ADT improves 10 yr OS by 6% and MFS by 8%
  • Prolonged NA ADT >2mo has no benefit

PCS IV

  • 36mo ADT not superior to 18mo for OS or DFS. QoL better with 18months
  • Though near significance favouring 36mo in 5 yr OS and 10 yr DFS

TROG RADAR

  • 18mo ADT superior to 6mo ADT for PCM

STAMPEDE

  • P: regional N+ patients or VHRPC (≥ 2 of: T3-4, GS 8-10, PSA ≥ 40) or recurrent high risk disease
  • I: Abiraterone for 2 years + ADT vs ADT alone vs Abi + enza + ADT
  • O: Abi improved OS by ~10% at 6 yrs. ↓DM with Abi vs ADT alone. No benefit to addition of enzalutamide

EBRT + HDR

Dose: 46Gy/23# WPRT VMAT + 15Gy/1# HDR BT with Ir-192

Concurrent ADT

Ir-192 mean energy 280 keV, 95% B-emission, 5% gamma, half life 75 days

PTV V100 >95%

HDR BT Procedure

  • Dorsolithotomy position
  • IDC inserted
  • TRUS + stepper unit
  • 5mm slices of prostate taken
  • Template sutured to perineum
  • Needles with catheters inserted to prostate such that even coverage obtained
  • Insert fiducials and rectal spacer for EBRT

Volume

  • Needles reconstructed
  • Pros CTV contoured +5mm to PTV + urethra and rectum OARs volumed
  • CTV = PTV
  • Plan created + QA completed

Treatment

  • Pt asleep in bunker, catheters connected to afterloader
  • Dummy run to check all cables
  • RT delivered via Ir-192

EBRT delivered to whole pelvis 2 weeks post HDR, CT sim 1 week post HDR for swelling reduction

OARs

  • Urethra max <120% TD
  • Urethra D0.1cc <120Gy (EQD2 including EBRT)
  • Rectum D2cc <75Gy (EQD2 including EBRT)

EBRT + DIL boost

Dose: 67Gy/60Gy/44Gy/20# VMAT, no BD compensation

Concurrent ADT

Pre sim: fiducials, rectal spacer (CI in posterior EPE), assess urinary function

Sim

  • Comfortably full bladder, empty rectum
  • Supine, arms of chest, vac bag
  • Fuse MRI T2, DCE, DWI and PSMA PET (NB cytoreductive effect of ADT may have changed tumour from PET scan)

Volumes

  • CTV pros: prostate + prox 2cm SV if uninvolved or entire SV if +
  • PTV 6000: +5mm
  • GTV DIL = PTV DIL 6700 excluding urethra PRV [optional]
  • GTVn
  • CTVn: +5mm, cropped to bone/muscle
  • PTV6000: +5mm [reduce to 5400 to meet OAR]
  • CTV elective nodes: distal CI, EI, II, ob, PS from L4/L5, cropped to bone/muscle (extending 2cm above involved node)
  • PTV 4400: +7mm

OARs

  • Urethra PRV +2mm D2% <67Gy
  • Prioritise OAR over DIL boost coverage

Oligometastatic

Definition · EBRT

Clinic page: Prostate

Definition

  • STAMPEDE: non high volume: ≥4 bone mets with 1 or more outside axial skeleton
  • STOPCAP M1: <5 bone mets

Mgmt

De novo oligometastatic PC

1. Systemic therapy

  • ADT lifelong + ARPI + pros RT
  • Degarelix to prevent bone pain from flare
  • Abiraterone in addition to ADT and RT in addition to abi + ADT both improve OS – population consisted of mainly de novo metastatic dx. OS benefit reduces with increasing number of mets in oligomet setting (STAMPEDE)
  • Analysis of low volume met population PEACE-1 found ADT + abi + pros RT improves PFS, delayed CRPC and does not worsen toxicity but does not improve OS
  • Addition of enzalutamide improves MFS in biochemically recurrent high risk PCa with PSA DT<9mo, PSA>1 (EMBARK)

2. Prostate radiotherapy

  • 36Gy/6# weekly or 55Gy/20# - improves OS by 8% at 3yrs, no OS diff between regimes (STAMPEDE, STOPCAP M1)
  • If attempting ‘cure’ with planned SABR to oligmets then consider definitive 60Gy/20# to prostate

3. Upfront docetaxol chemotherapy + ARPI + ADT

  • Improves OS, however generally reserved for young, fit with high volume metastatic dx (STAMPEDE)

4. MDT SABR/surgery

  • Delays improves PFS, castrate resistance (WOLVERINE MA)

Oligorecurrent prostate cancer

  • SABR to distant oligorecurrence if no previous treatment
  • improvement of PFS, delay castrate resistance, delay lifelong ADT and delay intermittent ADT (STOMP/ORIOLE, EXTEND)
  • Addition of ST ADT to MDT improves PFS from 15 to 30mo (RADIOSA)

High volume de novo metastatic Pca

  • STAMPEDE: lifelong ADT intensification with Abiraterone or docetaxel improves OS
  • PEACE 1: lifelong ADT + ARPI + docetaxel improves OS
  • Lu-177 PSMA improves PFS in CRMPC after docetaxel (VISION)
  • Other: PARPi, Radium 223

STAMPEDE

  • prostate RT improved 3yr OS from 75% to 85% in oligomets and non-regional nodes
  • FFS better for 55Gy/20# vs 36Gy/6#
  • 3yr OS no diff for non-oligomets ~ 50%
  • first line docetaxol improves median OS from 70 to 80mo

STOPCAP1

  • RT to primary in <5 bone mets improves 3yr OS from 70% to 77%

WOLVERINE MA

  • MA comparing MDT vs systemic treatment for mostly oligorecurrent PCA
  • The addition of MDT to SOC results in: significant improvement in PFS and castration resistance free survival.
  • OS favoured MDT but not significant

EXTEND

  • ph II trial. Indefinite ADT vs indefinite ADT + SABR to oligorecurrence (≤ 5 lesions). Pts had primary prostate RT too.
  • SABR improved PFS and allowed prolongation of intermittent ADT

STOMP/ORIOLE: ph II trial. MDT to oligorecurrence delayed time to start lifelong ADT

SABR-COMET

  • ph II. SABR to 1-5 oligomets with a controlled primary site improves PFS and OS
  • Most were lung, breast, CRC and prostate

EBRT

Dose: 36Gy/6# weekly or 55Gy/20# to prostate alone, VMAT, no BD compensation

Pre-sim: fiducials, rectal spacer (CI in posterior EPE), MRI

CTV: prostate + involved SV

PTV: +5mm margin

Palliative

Pheonix recurrence definition after

Pheonix recurrence definition after

Pheonix recurrence definition after EBRT & BT

  • PSA rise 2.0 above nadir

Mgmt

PSMA PET for all

1. Local radiorecurrent prostate cancer

  • If considering salvage therapies need bx proven confirmation of recurrent disease without metastatic disease and PSA <10, slow DT, LE >10yrs

Salvage prostatectomy – preferred by NCCN and EUA guidelines as most mature evidence

  • HIFU, cryotherapy – limited long term data
    • HIFU equivalent to salvage RP for CSS – HIFI-2
  • Salvage BT
  • All salvage treatment to be performed in experienced centres, counsel pt on higher GU toxicity

2. BCR only - palliative ADT

  • Immediate vs deferred ADT – less metastatic complications with immediate, if PSA >50 or DT <12months do immediate as these pts more likely to die from PCa (EORTC 30891 secondary analysis)
  • Continuous is recommendation (SWOG – intermittent ADT was inferior to continuous however OS similar to both)
  • Consider intermittent after 6-12 months if: poor tolerance, PSA response <4, low volume disease w/o visceral mets
    • Recommence if DT <12 months, symptomatic progression, PSA >10
    • Intermittent ADT not shown to delay castrate resistance
    • Better physical and sexual function without sig OS, PCSS or PFS difference (Magnan et al – systematic review)
  • Degarelix to avoid bone pain/symptom flare

3. Other palliative systemic treatment

  • Upfront docetaxel not shown to provide OS benefit unlike de novo metastatic PCa
  • Generally ARPI and docetaxol are reserved for castrate resistance

4. Other palliative treatment

  • RT: symptom control
  • Ensure no HUN from obstruction
  • IDC

Salvage RT

Post-op recurrence definition · EBRT Salvage RT · PTV6600: +5mm

Clinic page: Prostate

Post-op recurrence definition

  • PSA rise on 2 consecutive tests above 0.20

PSMA PET sensitivity

  • <0.2 = 30%
  • 0.5 = 50%
  • 0.5 – 1 = 80%
  • 1-2 = >90%. >50% will have DM

Mgmt

All should have PSMA PET & MRI

1. Adjuvant radiotherapy

  • Could be discussed in T4 (these patients were underrepresented in salvage trials, <5%)
  • Worse urinary function as less recovery time post-operatively

2. Early salvage radiotherapy

  • Reduces early and late GU and GI toxicity (RADICALS)
  • Allows restaging of disease
  • Allows time to assess which patients truly require RT and hence avoid unnecessary RT ~ 50% of patients will require RT when there are positive margins, ECE, T3a, GS7 (from RADICALS, RAVES, GETUG)
  • Aim to commence radiotherapy before PSA <0.5 ‘early salvage’
  • Salvage is no different to adjuvant RT in regards to bPFS and EFS (ARTISTIC MA)

3. RT dose and target

  • Prostate only RT considered if: GS 7, positive surgical margin, slowly rising PSA
  • WPRT had better FFP (SPPORT)
  • RT dose: 66Gy/33#
  • OR hypofractionated 52.5Gy/20# - hypofrac had worse acute GI tox NRG-GU003, but acute GU better in RADICALS. Late no diff in both.
  • If macroscopic local recurrence boost to 72Gy (SPIDER)
  • If no macroscopic disease, possible role for PB dose escalation to 72Gy if GS ≥8 (SAKK 09/10 - no bPFS and worse late GI tox, Peking hospital trial – bPFS if GS≥8, no diff in tox)

4. Pelvic node oligorecurrence

  • In the setting of oligorecurrent pelvic node with no prior RT, radiotherapy to the entire pelvis with boost has better MFS vs RT to 1 involved node. Prostate bed was dealers choice (PEACE V STORM)
  • Addition of short term ADT to SABR/MDT further improves PFS (RADIOSA)

5. ADT

  • Consider 6 months if high risk of micrometastatic disease or PSA >0.5 for DMFS benefit (POSEIDON MA)
  • Improves DMFS and PCSS (DADSPPORT MA)
  • Enzalutamide in addition to ADT in high risk biochemical recurrence improves OS (EMBARK)

ARTISTIC MA

  • P: MA of RADICALS, RAVES, GETUG
  • O: salvage RT vs adj RT had no diff in 5 year EFS, bFS, death or ADT use. Salvage RT can lessen toxicity of trimodality treatment
  • RADICALS showed no MFS or OS benefit to adding ST ADT

SPPORT

  • P: post-prostatectomy, PSA 0.1 – 2, GS ≤9
  • I: PB RT vs PB RT + ST ADT vs WPRT + ST ADT
  • O: 5 yr FFP 70% vs 80% vs 87%. DM 4% less with ADT. 5 yr BF less with WPRT + ADT. No increase in G2 GU/GI tox. Increase BM toxicity. Better outcomes if lower PSA ~ 0.2

DADSPPORT MA

  • P: MA of RADICALS, RTOG 9601, GETUG-16, GETUG-22, SPPORT
  • O: ADT improves PCSS and DMFS. No OS benefit.

POSEIDON MA

  • P: >6000 pts from 6 RCT
  • O: Addition of ADT to PORT did not improve OS. DMFS survival emerged with PSA >0.5. No difference between ST or LT ADT

STAMPEDE – see above HRPC

SPIDER

  • Boost to 72Gy or higher for macroscopic local recurrence improved 5 yr PFS
  • Better PFS and DMFS assoc with: ADT duration, PSA <0.5, DT > 6months pre salvage RT, GS ≤ 8 and dose escalation to ≥ 72Gy

Types of ADT

Clinic page: Prostate

Types of ADT

  • Surgical orchiectomy – lifelong castration
  • GNRH agonists: rely on negative feedback cycle to reduce production of GNRH and subsequent downstream androgens/testosterone, assoc with initial flare
    • Lucrin, Zoladex
  • GNRH antagonists: direct suppression of GNRH release, no flare, quicker testosterone recovery, less cardiac toxicity
    • Degarelix, relugolix
  • CYP17 inhibitor – prevents adrenal synthesis of androgens and downregulates androgen receptors, can cause hypoadrenalism hence given with prednisone
    • Abiraterone
    • S/e: metabolic syndrome from prednisone ( fluid retention, HTN, DM, labile BSL, weight gain, infection risk, OP), cardiac toxicity (arrythmia, CHF)
  • ARPI inhibitors – androgen receptor competitive antagonists prevent the action of testosterone & DHT
    • Enzalutamide, Apalutamide, darolutamide, bicalutamide (first gen oral)
    • Enzalutamide – cardiac events, falls, seizures, fatigue (crosses into CNS)
    • Apalutamide – rash, hypothyroidism
    • Darolutamide – fatigue
    • Bicalutamide – gynaecomastia, diarrhoea, hepatotoxicity. Relative preservation of bone strength and libido
