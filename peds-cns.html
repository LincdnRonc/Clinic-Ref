Medulloblastoma

Maximal safe resection, then craniospinal irradiation (CSI) and a boost, then chemotherapy. Use the involved-field (tumour bed) boost, not the whole posterior fossa. For children <3 y, delay CSI with chemotherapy where possible.

Risk-adapted treatment

RiskDefinitionRTOutcome
Standard (66%)M0, residual <1.5 cm², classic or desmoplastic, age ≥3 yCSI 23.4 Gy → tumour-bed boost to 54–55.8 Gy + weekly VCR → adjuvant chemo5 y OS 80–85% (ACNS 0331)

  • 18 Gy CSI is not standard for children <8 y (ACNS 0331: worse EFS). It is being tested in WNT-subgroup tumours.
  • Boost: tumour bed + 1–1.5 cm is as good as whole posterior fossa (ACNS 0331: local recurrence about 3%, distant about 10%).

Key trials

ACNS 0331 (2021)Standard risk · tumour-bed vs posterior fossa boost; age 3–7 · 18 vs 23.4 Gy CSIBed boost was non-inferior (5 y OS about 85%). Low-dose CSI was worse: 5 y EFS 83 vs 72%.
SJMB03 (2021)23.4 Gy (average) or 36–39.6 Gy (high) CSI → conformal boost → high-dose chemo5 y PFS 83% (average risk) vs 59% (high risk).
CCG 9892 (1999)23.4 Gy CSI + VCR → 55.8 Gy posterior fossa → chemo5 y PFS 80%.

Ependymoma

Ependymoma is a surgical disease: aim for gross total resection, with second-look surgery if needed. Posterior fossa tumours all get conformal RT, even after GTR. There is no CSI unless the tumour is metastatic.

Decisions

  • Infratentorial: PORT for all, including after GTR.
  • Supratentorial: PORT after STR; after GTR only if grade 3.
  • Dose: 54 Gy + 1 cm (newer protocols 0.5 cm). Boost to 59.4 Gy to residual and bed above the cord, limiting brainstem to 57.8 Gy. 59.4 Gy has been shown to be safe (Merchant).
  • Age 12–18 months: stop at 54 Gy, and omit RT after GTR (ACNS 0121). Chemo and second-look surgery are standard for infants <18 months.
  • 1q gain is a poor prognostic marker. Myxopapillary (spinal) grade 1: the role of RT is controversial.

Germ cell tumours, craniopharyngioma and paediatric glioma

Germinoma is very sensitive to chemo and RT, so doses are low. Craniopharyngioma: limited surgery plus RT gives the best control with less morbidity. DIPG: 54 Gy as soon as possible.

Intracranial germ cell tumours

  • Germinoma, chemo then CR: whole-ventricular RT 18 Gy + boost 12 Gy (total 30 Gy).
  • Germinoma, PR or limited residual: WVRT 24 Gy + boost to 36 Gy.
  • Germinoma, RT alone: WVRT 23.4–25 Gy + boost to 45–50.4 Gy. Disseminated: CSI (21–24 Gy, or 30 Gy RT alone) + boost to discrete lesions.
  • Non-germinomatous GCT: chemo × 4–6 → CSI 30–36 Gy + boost 14.4–18 Gy (to 54 Gy).

Craniopharyngioma

  • GTR alone: 70–85% DFS, at the cost of diabetes insipidus, vision loss and hypothalamic injury. STR alone: 30% DFS. STR + RT: 85–90% DFS.
  • Dose: 54 Gy conventional (50–55 Gy acceptable).
  • Image during RT: 20–30% have cyst growth within weeks, which may need drainage or replanning.

Paediatric glioma

  • Low-grade glioma: resection where possible. If unresectable and <10 y, use chemo first (carboplatin/vincristine delays RT by about 3 y; dabrafenib/trametinib for BRAF V600E). Older than 7 y: RT 50.4–54 Gy. 10 y LC about 70%.
  • DIPG: steroids and 54 Gy as soon as possible (median OS 9–11 months). Hypofractionation 39/13 is reasonable (median OS 9.6 vs 8.7 months for 54/30). H3 K27M in about 85%.
  • Re-irradiation at progression can give palliation (for example 25/10 at 16 months).

Management

Manage raised ICP

Maximal safe surgical resection

  • Post-op MRI brain within 48 hours

  • LP to test CSF pre-op if safe (risk of herniation with raised ICP) OR 2 weeks post-op
    • LP after MRI spine to avoid confounding with inflammation
  • Post-op posterior fossa syndrome in 25%: SAME (speech impairment, ataxia, mutism, emotional lability) is self-resolving over weeks-months and should not delay adjuvant Rx

High risk <3yo: risk adapted approach

Low risk (>3yo, <1.5cm2 residual, M0, WNT subgroup, classic histology)

Adults

  • Consider omission of vincristine due to greater myelosuppression risk (less bone marrow recovery in adults)

Germ cell

From the exam summary.

Epidemiology

I: rare <1% of brain cancers

A: 10-20yo

G: M

R: East Asian

RF

I: nil

A: nil

G: Rb1, Klinefelter syndrome (mediastinal GCT and CNS)

R:nil

Natural Hx

  • Arise from pineal gland or suprasellar region
  • Bifocal (both regions) is pathognomic for GCT and treated as localised dx
  • Tendency to spread through CSF → drop mets

Sx

  • Parinauds syndrome from dorsal midbrain injury: upward gaze palsy, convergence retraction nystagmus, light-near dissociation (pupil hyporeflexia)
  • Suprasellar: diabetes insipidus (↓ ADH from hypothalamus – polyuria, polydipsia, hypernatremia), hypopituitarism, bitemporal hemianopia
  • Obstructive hydrocephalus and raised ICP: papilloedema, early morning headaches, n/v

Outcomes

  • Germinoma 5 yr OS: 90%
  • NGGCT 5 yr OS: 60%+
  • NGGCT often relapses in spine

Differential diagnosis

  • Pineal: GCT, pineoblastoma, pineocytoma, teratoma, glioma
  • Suprasellar: GCT, craniopharyngioma, PitNET, teratoma

Path

Germinoma: 60%

  • Polygonal cells, clear cytoplasm, prominent nucleoli
  • Chemo and radiosensitive
  • Best prognosis
  • cKITm
  • 10-15% can have mildly raised b-HCG (syncytiotrophoblastic cells)

Non-germinoma GCT: 40%

Yolk sac tumour

  • Raised AFP, Schiller duval bodies
  • Aggressive

Choriocarcinoma

  • Cytotrophoblasts and synctiotrophoblasts
  • Very raised b-hCG
  • Aggressive

Embryonal tumour

  • Primitive epithelial tumour with necrosis, atypia, mitoses
  • Can have elevated b-hCG

Teratoma

  • Mature (benign) or immature (malignant)
  • Tissues from ectoderm, mesoderm and endoderm
  • Immature can have raised mild raised b-HCG

IHC

  • GGCT: PLAP, OCT3/4, SALL4, cKIT
  • NGGCT: OCT3/4, SALL4
  • Negative: GFAP, S100, vimentin

  • MRI brain and whole spine
  • All need CSF x 2 with cytology and tumour marker testing
    • CSF tumour markers are more sensitive than serum
  • Biopsy avoided if typical presentation – pineal/suprasellar location and tumour markers present
  • May have ‘growing teratoma’ syndrome if enlarging mass post treatment

Germinoma

1. Induction chemotherapy: etoposide/carboplatin & ifosfamide/etoposide x 4 cycles

2. Assess response on MRI +/- second look surgery if residual mass

3. Sequential WVRT 24Gy + 16Gy boost to tumour in 1.6Gy/# if no CR

  • Dose reduction if CR: 18Gy + 12Gy boost can be considered
  • Omission of tumour bed in single site disease (ie not bifocal) with CR post chemo is acceptable (European guidelines)
  • CSI 24Gy if CSF +
  • Expect cure rates >90%

Non germinoma GCT

1. Induction chemotherapy: etoposide/platinum & ifosfamide/etoposide x 4-6 cycles

2. Response assessment +/- second look surgery if residual mass

  • CR → Adj RT: CSI 36Gy + 54Gy TD boost to tumour in 1.8Gy/#
  • Omitting CSI increases spinal relapse
  • Second look surgery:
    • If no viable tumour → proceed as per CR
    • If viable tumour → escalate dose boost to 60Gy
    • If mature teratoma → resect (chemoradioresistant)

Growing teratoma syndrome

  • Growing mass with normalisation of tumour markers with mature teratoma on pathology

Mature teratoma

  • GTR → no adjuvant treatment
  • STR → aim re-resection or consider 54Gy RT

From the exam summary.

Epidemiology

I: Rare, 4% of paediatric CNS tumours, <1% of adult CNS tumours

A: Bimodal 5, 60yo

G: M

R: nil

Presentation

  • Visual field deficit – homonymous hemianopia
  • Pituitary dysfunction – diabetes insipidus, ↓ GH, ACTH, TFT, sex hormones, galactorrhea
  • Headaches, n/v

Radiology

  • Calcified mass in suprasellar region

Pathology

  • Benign tumour arising from the remnant of the craniopharyngeal duct in the suprasellar region (Rathke’s pouch)
  • WHO grade 1
  • Multiple cysts is characteristic
  • Adamantinomatous – 90%
    • Children
    • CTNNB1 mutation (B-catenin) → WnT signalling
  • Papillary – 10%
    • Adults
    • BRAF V600E mutation

Notes

  • Cystic component can swell during and post RT → monitor

Management

Benign tumour in a non-benign location

Avoid dual modality treatment – aim for either GTR or use RT

1. Maximal safe resection

  • STR alone has poor LC rates
  • Consider pre-op aspiration if large cystic component
  • Consider pre-op BRAF-MEK inhibitors to reduce tumour volume

2. Adjuvant or salvage RT or SRS

  • Indicated post STR
  • 54Gy/30#
  • SRS 10-14Gy/1# if 2mm or more from critical structures (optic pathways, pituitary)
  • STR + adj RT has similar OS and LC to GTR – 10 yr OS 95%

Late RT toxicities

  • Vision impairment
  • Hypopituitarism – reduced growth, hypothyroidism, reduced ACTH, infertility, amenorrhea, impotence
  • Moyamoya syndrome (microvascular ischaemia increasing stroke risk)
  • Cognitive decline
  • Radionecrosis
  • Stroke risk due to proximity to carotid arteries
