Overview

At a glance

DLBCL
Limited stage: R-CHOP × 4 (+ R × 2) if PET-negative (FLYER), or short chemo + ISRT. Advanced: RT consolidates bulky or PET-positive residual sites. Doses are 30–36 Gy after CR and 36–45 Gy for residual disease.
Follicular & MZL
Stage I–II indolent lymphoma can be cured with 24 Gy ISRT. For palliation, 4 Gy in 2 fractions is effective and can be repeated.
PCNSL
High-dose methotrexate-based chemo (e.g. R-MPV) first. Reduced-dose WBRT consolidation for CR; avoid 45 Gy WBRT because of delayed neurotoxicity, especially if >60 y.

Lymphoma (Lugano)

Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma

Limited stage: R-CHOP × 4 (+ R × 2) if PET-negative (FLYER), or short chemo + ISRT. Advanced: RT consolidates bulky or PET-positive residual sites. Doses are 30–36 Gy after CR and 36–45 Gy for residual disease.

When and how much (ILROG)

  • PET CR after chemo: 30–36 Gy ISRT to initially bulky (≥7.5 cm) or extranodal sites if consolidating.
  • PET-positive residual: 36–45 Gy (up to 40–55 Gy if refractory or RT alone).
  • Limited stage, PET-negative after R-CHOP × 4: chemo alone is acceptable (FLYER, 3 y PFS about 95%). PET3-positive: add ISRT 36 Gy ± 9 Gy boost (SWOG S1001).
  • Bulky (≥7.5 cm) in older patients: RT to bulk reduces relapse (UNFOLDER, RICOVER-noRTh, OPTIMAL >60).
  • Testicular: R-CHOP + intrathecal MTX + 30 Gy to the contralateral testis (IELSG-10).
  • CAR-T bridging: RT alone (20–40 Gy) gives in-field response.

Indolent lymphoma (follicular, marginal zone)

Stage I–II indolent lymphoma can be cured with 24 Gy ISRT. For palliation, 4 Gy in 2 fractions is effective and can be repeated.

RT-alone ISRT volumes for indolent lymphoma: Lymphoma ISRT contouring guide, section 2

Doses

  • Curative stage I–II: 24/12 (24–30 Gy). Larger margins if RT alone.
  • Palliative: 4/2 (FORT: CR 49% with 4 Gy vs 68% with 24 Gy). Re-treat as needed.
  • Stage I–II FL with systemic therapy: adding R-CVP to 30 Gy improved 10 y PFS 43 → 62% (TROG 99.03).
  • Grade 3 FL is treated like DLBCL.
UK FORT (2014)548 · indolent NHL · 24/12 vs 4/2ORR 91 vs 81%, CR 68 vs 49%. 24 Gy better for durable control.
TROG 99.03 ANZ (2018)150 · stage I–II FL · 30 Gy ISRT ± R-CVP10 y PFS 43 → 62% with systemic therapy.

Extranodal MALT and orbit (ILROG)

  • Gastric MALT: eradicate H. pylori first. RT for H. pylori-negative or persistent disease: 24–30 Gy in 1.5–2 Gy fractions to the whole stomach (nil by mouth for 6 h; consider DIBH).
  • Orbit/ocular adnexa: 24 Gy; 4/2 is being studied. Cataract about 33% after 2.5–6.5 Gy; retinal damage does not occur below 24 Gy.
  • Salivary: 24 Gy. Other extranodal sites 24–30 Gy, usually treating the whole organ.

Primary CNS lymphoma

High-dose methotrexate-based chemo (e.g. R-MPV) first. Reduced-dose WBRT consolidation for CR; avoid 45 Gy WBRT because of delayed neurotoxicity, especially if >60 y.

WBRT doses

  • CR after R-MPV: 23.4 Gy WBRT, then cytarabine × 2.
  • PR: ILROG 36–45 Gy WBRT (1.5–1.8 Gy) without boost; NCCN 30–36 Gy with a 45 Gy boost to residual. Example: 36/24 + SIB 43.2/24.
  • Median survival about 6 y with this approach (MSKCC).
  • Intraocular involvement: CTV includes the entire globe and optic nerves to the chiasm.

Introduction: overview

Hodgkin lymphoma summary:
-HL initially treated with RT alone with large fields (STNI)
-ABVD chemo later developed, and eventual NCIC ECOG HD6 showed chemo may have better OS than RT. But there are criticisms to the study
-As a result, RT use decreases over time some regions
-Multiple trials (H7, H8, HD8) show that RT field can be safely reduced to IFRT when chemo given
-HD10 for favorable lymphoma establishes ABVD + 20 Gy IFRT
-HD11 for unfavorable lymphoma establishes ABVD + 30 Gy IFRT
-RAPID and EORTC trials show RT still gives improvement in PFS for PET negative after chemo. EORTC H10 has 10-yr f/u with improved PFS. There was no increase in second cancer.
-HD17 for unfavorable found that no RT is noninferior to RT with BEACOPP when PET is negative after chemo.
-HD14 for unfavorable lymphoma establishes escBEACOPPx2 + ABVDx2 + IFRT 30 Gy
-Some are less inclined to use BEACOPP because of PFS benefit with added adverse effects, complexity, and no OS benefit
-Phase II NIVAHL in unfav evaluates N+AVD+ISRT

This trial is the largest to assess the benefit of RT in extranodal and bulky disease in ages 18-60 in NHL in R era. It was conducted prior to PET era. About 70-75% had PET. Whether PET was obtained or not, response was assessed by CT.

Secondary malignancy was increased in the obs arm. Salvage therapies could have increased the risk of 2nd cancer. CAR T cell has reported secondary malignancies of 4-16% (Cappell & Kochenderfer 2023). Information is not provided on what salvage therapies were given.

Management

Stage I-II: potentially curable ~ 50%

  • Definitive radiotherapy improves PFS & may improve OS – retrospective data
  • 24Gy/12# is non-inferior to 45Gy – Lowry trial
  • post-RT chemo with R-CVP improves PFS only – TROG 99.03
  • Implies FL is indolent, relapse is salvageble and does not cause immediate mortality, and has a long natural hx
  • Alternative: surveillance if Rx is morbid

Stage III-IV

  • Upfront treatment does not improve OS and is unlikely to reduce transformation risk – UK RCT, Mayo clinic and French retrospective studies
  • 15% may have spontaneous regression
  • ‘GELF’ criteria to treat:
  • Impending organ dysfunction
  • Symptomatic – B sx or spleen enlargement
  • Bulky dx >7cm or 3 LN regions >3cm
  • Cytopaenia, lymphocytosis
  • Serous effusions
  • Palliative RT: 24Gy/12# OR 4Gy/2# - FORT
  • Chemotherapy:
    • rituximab alone – low tumour burden. S/e: infuction reaction, infection risk
    • R-CHOP – young fit pts with high tumour burden
    • R-CVP (cyclophosphamide, vincristine, pred) or R-bendamustine – unfit pts with high tumour burden

Relapsed dx

  • Bx to confirm low grade dx
  • Surveillance if no GELF criteria
  • Chemotherapy – different regime to avoid cross resistance
  • Stem cell transplant
  • CAR-T cells
  • Palliative RT

Tumour Lysis Syndrome

Rapid destruction of tumour resulting in release of intracellular contents into systemic circulation causing:

  • electrolyte derangement with 2 of or 25% change from baseline within 3 days: high urea, hyperkalaemia, hyperphosphatemia, hypocalcaemia [PUCK + SRC]
  • + one of the following: seizure, renal impairment, cardiac arrythmia

Risk factors

  • Tumour bulk >10cm
  • High tumour proliferation rate
  • High WCC
  • High LDH
  • Radiosensitive subtype
  • Pre-existing renal dysfunction

Prophylaxis

  • risk based depending: bulk of disease, LDH, sensitivity of pathology to planned treatment
  • vigorous IV hydration and allopurinol OR rasburicase commencing 24-48 hours prior to treatment
  • Treatment usually continues for 3-7 days after the high risk TLS period
