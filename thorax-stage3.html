Lung (TNM 9th edition)

Unresectable stage III NSCLC

Concurrent chemoRT to 60 Gy, then a year of durvalumab (or osimertinib for EGFR-mutant disease). Do not escalate above 60–66 Gy outside trials. Decide resectability before starting.

Decisions

  • Standard: 60/30 with weekly carboplatin/paclitaxel or cisplatin/etoposide, then durvalumab for 1 y (PACIFIC). 60–63 Gy usually appropriate; up to 70 Gy may be considered (ARS 2024). Doses above 70 Gy are not appropriate.
  • EGFR-mutant: chemoRT followed by osimertinib (LAURA: median PFS 6 → 39 months).
  • Bulky disease where V20 or MLD is unacceptable: sequential chemotherapy then RT.
  • Not fit for concurrent: sequential chemo → RT ± durvalumab (PACIFIC-6, G3–4 AEs 19%). Or hypofractionated RT alone: 60/15 or 55–60/20 with IMRT.
  • Poor PS, symptomatic: palliative 30/10 or 45/15; 20/5 or 1–2 fractions also appropriate.
  • Concurrent durvalumab: no benefit (PACIFIC-2). Start durvalumab after chemoRT.
  • PCI: not standard for NSCLC. RTOG 0214 reduced brain metastasis (1 y 18 → 8%) without an OS benefit.

Resectable stage III and PORT

Perioperative chemo-immunotherapy is now standard for resectable stage II–III disease. PORT halves mediastinal relapse but does not improve DFS or OS for completely resected N2. Reserve it for R1/R2 or very high-risk N2.

PORT

  • R0 pN2: not routine. Discuss for high-risk N2 (multistation, ECE, low nodal sampling). If given: 50–54 Gy sequentially after chemotherapy.
  • R1 or ECE: 54–60 Gy. Concurrent chemoRT then chemo for a positive margin.
  • R2: 60 Gy or more, with concurrent chemotherapy.
  • pN1: PORT only if not receiving chemotherapy (ANITA).
  • Volumes: CTV = bronchial stump + involved stations + ipsilateral hilum, mediastinum ± subcarinal (follow Lung ART). PTV = CTV + 5 mm with daily CBCT (up to 1 cm otherwise).

Neoadjuvant and perioperative options

  • Chemo-immunotherapy → surgery: nivolumab (CheckMate 816), pembrolizumab (KEYNOTE-671) or durvalumab (AEGEAN), with adjuvant immunotherapy for the latter two.
  • Superior sulcus (T3–4 N0–1): chemoRT 45 Gy with cisplatin/etoposide → resection (INT 0160). 5 y OS 54% after R0, 44% overall. pCR 29%. Brain is the commonest site of failure.
  • cN2 (single station, <3 cm, lobectomy planned): may be surgical. Multistation N2, N3, ECE or progression on chemotherapy → definitive chemoRT.

Planning for conventional thoracic RT

Use 4DCT and IMRT. Involved-field only (no elective nodal RT). Watch lung V20, mean heart dose, heart V50 and oesophageal V60.

Volumes

  • GTV: primary and nodes ≥1 cm short axis or PET-avid (SUV >3). Include 2+ nodes in a high-risk station.
  • CTV: GTV + 5–8 mm, edited off bone and uninvolved structures. ESTRO-ACROP option: iGTVn + 5 mm and iGTVp + 8 mm.
  • PTV: CTV + 5 mm with ITV and daily IGRT. Without ITV: free breathing 1.5 cm craniocaudal and 1 cm axial; DIBH 1 cm craniocaudal and 0.5 cm axial.
  • Motion >7–10 mm: use active breathing control or gating. Motion <7 mm: ITV.

SCLC, dose and PCI: overview

SCLC dose summary
-Early trials show an OS benefit with RT, and this is confirmed on meta-analysis
-INT 0096 establishes 45 Gy/ 30 fx BID as standard
-Over 15 years later, CONVERT compares 45 Gy BID regimen to 60 Gy and did not show superiority. Trends in results support 45 Gy BID
-CALGB is an ongoing trial to also evaluate dose

SCLC PCI cognitive benefit summary
-There was previously a debate as to whether PCI caused cognitive decline
-Early trials were mixed on whether PCI causes neuroadverse effects (INT and UKCCR/EORTC)
-SRS trials looking at addition of WBRT (EORTC, MDACC, Alliance NCI), or WBRT vs. SRS (RTOG 1270) showed improved QOL and cognitive function without WBRT.
-See also the PCI study in NSCLC, NVALT-11/DLCRG-02, that does show cognitive impairment with PCI but similar QOL later.
-Hippocampus sparing with IMRT in 30 Gy WBRT has shown improvement in cognitive function and seems translatable to PCI.
-PCI specific IMRT trials are ongoing.

Commentary
Overview. Note that 74 Gy didn't even improve LC. In fact, LF, LRF, PFS and DM all trended to better outcome with 60 Gy. Why was 60 Gy superior? Theories include dose to heart, better immune response at 60 Gy, or variations in contouring and RT QA. Regarding RT protocol compliance, although there was slightly worse contouring in the 74 Gy arm, this was not associated with worse outcomes (see the discussion section of the 2019 publication). Per authors, RT protocol compliance was rigorous and every patient was centrally reviewed. It's hard to conceive that a better trial could have been performed. Regarding the immune system, we know that immune cells are affected at even low doses of radiation, and immunotherapy has proven how critical for NSCLC. Heart dose might be representative of dose to the immune system. Since its completion, RTOG 0617 has substantially advanced our knowledge of dosimetry and the immune system in NSCLC.

Dosimetry innovation. 0617 eventually inspired an array of dosimetric goals for heart substructures (Bergom 2021). On a later secondary analysis, atria D45%, mean lung, pericardium mean of hottest % of 55%, and ventricles MOH% 5% predicted OS. Rx dose was actually not predictive of OS, nor was IMRT (Thor 2020), differing from the original MVA analysis. The authors are careful to say that causation cannot be ascertained. IMRT gave less dose to heart and lower grade 3+ pneumonitis but no OS change. LAD V15 <10% was also related to OS (McKenzie 2022). Lung V5 not associated with grade 3+ pneumonitis; V20 was (Chun 2017). How could dose and IMRT not be directly associated with OS? Well, dose has to hit and pass through something. So the answer must be the something it's hitting and the duration, such as lung, heart, or blood pool. 74 Gy IMRT to a point would mean nothing.

Outcomes. Since this study, PACIFIC improved outcomes further to 2-yr OS 66% and 5-yr OS 43% with immunotherapy. And PACIFIC had more Stage IIIB (45%) than RTOG 0617 (~35%). RTOG 9410 had more Stage IIIB of 57%. Note stage IIIC was not an AJCC stage at the time, but included in IIIB.

Lessons. Some hold RTOG 0617 as a cautionary tale against those practicing phase II data before confirmation in phase III. Reality is more nuanced. RTOG 9410, the prior trial, resulted a 5-yr OS of a dismal 15%. The LC benefit of dose escalation was not questioned. In such situations, practicioners and patients may be more willing to take risks with phase II and prospective evidence. For example many treat oligometastasis as per phase II data.

This trial de-escalated RT, but mandated adjuvant escalated chemo in both arms based on low evidence (see CALGB 39801 and HOG trials). Since the publication of PACIFIC this practice has largely discontinued.

60 Gy/15 fx was associated with increased Grade 2 AEs, which may be impactful in the more fragile "target" patient population with medical comorbidities and poor KPS who are too deconditioned to tolerate chemotherapy in the first place. Commentary
Osimertinib is tumorostatic rather than tumorocidal. In a pre-op study, there were zero pCRs with osimertinib, and major path responses were 15% (Blakely 2024). Still osimertinib is a landmark discovery in personalized medicine, a drug that greatly extends outcomes with minimal adverse effects. There is controversy regarding whether all need the drug in the adjuvant setting, for what duration and the design of the control arm.

Role of radiation. The findings of later disease failure and lack of pCR may lend for a tandem role of radiation alongside osimertinib for recurrence, especially for brain metastasis and oligometastatic disease. The SINDAS and NROGC 002 trials showed OS benefit with RT for EGFR+ oligometastatic disease treated by earlier generation EGFR inhibitors (of note osimertinib was not included in the studies).

DFS statistics. In the DFS and CNS DFS figures there seems to be eventual merging of the curves at about 5-6 years (Willers 2023 and West 2023). This suggests that disease recurrence might eventually rapidly "catch-up" to control. The answer for some could be simply that the drug should be continued indefinitely until failure. Yet 33% discontinued the drug in the study.

Although the authors do report what they call cumulative DFSs of HRs 0.23-0.27 at "data cutoff" - when all had completed therapy - it is impossible that these could be the final DFS outcomes. They are DFSs during the time which the drug was given, not cumulative - it would be better termed as "on-trial-therapy DFS HRs". The authors wrote that because DFS was significant, no further formal statistical testing was done, and p values after are not provided. The statistical approach raises concerns that data is being withheld. The 4 year values nonetheless are excellent, and may be all that some need, especially those with limited life expectancy or who wish to avoid other types of treatment.

Design controversy. A debate centers around the control arm - the argument is that the control arm should have had 100% salvage crossover to osimertinib (similar to the adjuvant vs salvage prostate trials) when it was approved as SOC first line therapy for metastasis by the FDA on 4/18/18 (FDA and FLAURA). The trial began enrollment 10/2015 and closed 1/2020, however note also that recurrence takes time. West and Prasad comment that AztraZenica was urged to make the drug available to the control arm, but did not, even while promoting the drug as standard (West 2023, Prasad 2023). Instead only 39% in the control arm with progression crossed-over (Prasad 2020, 2023 & Olivier 2023). MRI brain and PET were not required, which passively biases the trial to occult disease, and again may overstate the benefit of the drug. West, Akhade, and Gyawali (2023) write "We submit that oncologists and patients should expect that phase III trials are conducted in a way that aspires to more than the lowest threshold of what a lenient regulatory authority will accept. Rather, the oncology community should seek that key trials enroll appropriately staged patients and ensure they receive access to the best treatments as defined by the contemporary practice in health care systems in which the experimental therapy would be marketed."

Operable stage II-III

  • No N3, non bulky <2cm single station N2, T3, select T4 where R0 feasible, adequate pulmonary reserve FEV1 and DLCO >80%
  • All should have peri-operative chemo-immunotherapy – increase pCR and OS (NADIM II, KEYNOTE 671)

  • If upstaged intra-op and no pre-op chemo-IO given, should still have adjuvant platinum doublet chemo followed by IO for DFS benefit

Inoperable stage II

Inoperable stage III
