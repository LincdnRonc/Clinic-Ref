Volumes and constraints

CT sim with 2 mm slices, IV contrast and a mouth bite. Fuse MRI and PET. Use 5 mm and 10 mm CTV expansions edited to anatomy, and a 3–5 mm PTV.

Contour step by step with the Head and neck contouring guide

Target volumes (international consensus)

  • Definitive: CTV1 = GTV + 5 mm (high dose). CTV2 = GTV + 10 mm, edited at natural barriers (intermediate dose). CTV3 = elective nodes.
  • Postoperative: CTV1 = pre-op GTV + 5 mm. CTV2 = pre-op GTV + 10 mm (R0). CTV3 = surgical bed + elective nodes. Cover the entire flap plus about 1 cm.
  • Nodes: suspicious if ≥1 cm short axis, or >3 grouped nodes of 6–8 mm. Add 5 mm for CTV (10 mm if ENE).
  • PTV: 3–5 mm.
  • Perineural spread: microscopic focal PNI → 50–60 Gy. Positive margin along a nerve → 60–66 Gy ± chemo. Gross or imaging-visible PNI → chemoRT 66–70 Gy and trace the nerve to the skull base.

Re-irradiation

Select by RPA class, time since RT and organ function. Resect if possible. SBRT suits small unresected recurrences; conventional IMRT suits larger or postoperative cases.

On UVA, ENE was the only independent risk factor for recurrence (regardless of dose). Trend to worse recurrence with ≥2 nodes, p=0.08. There was increasing recurrence risk with ≥2 risk factors (factors: oral cavity, close or positive margins, PNI, ≥2 nodes, node ≥3cm, delay >6 wks, low PS)
Commentary
This trial laid the foundation for dosing in intermediate and high risk post-op H&N cancer. There are a few important points to note in context of modern statistical approaches and staging. Observe that the trial endorses 63 Gy for ENE, not 66 Gy. Note that ENE was allowed in both groups and not randomized, and the finding that ≥63 Gy is ideal for ENE was determined by subanalysis, which was simply a review of crude recurrence rates by risk factors (Table 9). Today, a subanalysis such as MVA would have been performed with p values. Modern standards in statistical approaches in these situations are much stricter. At the time the trial was not questioned to be underpowered, but from modern standards it seems it could be. There are two randomizations of about 130 patients each.

The late f/u publication used more modern statistical approaches. ENE was analyzed as a prognostic factor in general but the exploratory analysis in the previous publication was not done to determine if ENE specifically benefited from higher dose RT.

"Low risk" and "high risk" were determined by a historical point system. The head vs. neck risks were determined separately. Cobalt era, 1.8 Gy/fx with no chemotherapy.

Immunotherapy should not be used for a borderline surgical candidate, or to convert a morbid procedure into a less radical one. What if the tumor does not become resectable or progresses and then chemoRT is needed? T4b were not eligible for the trial. About 14% may have progression with immunotherapy. If a tumor does not become resectable, chemoRT then becomes less feasible. The phase II study IMMUNEBOOST-HPV showed that after two cyles of nivolumab prior to chemoRT, 10% of patients required reduced dosing of cisplatin. The study did not meet its feasibility target.

Those p16+ have excellent response to chemoRT already, but more advancements are needed for p16 negative. Although pCRs were low in KEYNOTE 689, a CR after two cycles immunotherapy is an outstanding result and could allow for response adapted treatment to avoid surgery or chemoRT.

Recurrent

From the exam summary.

Epidemiology

  • 20% risk of second H&N primary with field change from smoking

Tumour factors

  • Second primary may respond better to re-RT than recurrence of original disease as radioresistant clones are selected in the latter
  • Higher volume = harder to achieve LC and more toxicity

Treatment factors

  • Salvage surgery such as pharyngolaryngectomy is generally associated with higher morbidity than upfront surgery – surgical planes less defined, worse lymphoedema, worse recon/flap healing, higher salivary leak and carotid blow out risk
  • Time from first course of radiation <2 years and existing organ dysfunction → worse OS (Ward et al – patient selection for re-irradiation of H&N SCC)
  • Volume of disease and proximity to OARS influences technique – VMAT vs SBRT
    • SBRT ideally for smaller volumes <25cc
  • Chemo + RT should be considered if fit enough
  • Brachytherapy ~ 20-48Gy in 3-4Gy/# BD. Dose is prescribed to periphery, centre will receive ~150%+. LC ~70%
  • Systemic therapy alone has worst outcomes

Management

Options

  • salvage surgery, definitive intent re-irradiation, palliation, systemic rx
  • Toxicity of re-RT: higher risk of osteoradionecrosis, aspiration, swallow dysfunction and PEG dependence, carotid blowout (<5% 8 months after re-RT, MacDonald 2012)
  • In IMRT era Re-RT 2 yr LC ~ 50%, G3 toxicity ~ 30%, G5 tox 3%, 2 yr OS 45% (Reirradiation with IMRT for recurrent H&N MA)

Re-irradiation doses

  • 60Gy/30# → this is almost always the desired dose, LC ~ 80%
  • 35-40Gy/5# (EQD2 of 60Gy) improved LC/OS vs < 6Gy per #
    • SBRT may have less toxicity
  • 65Gy/54# BD
  • Quadshot: 3.7Gy BD x 2 days, repeated up to 5 cycles q4weekly. 65% symptom response. 5% G3 toxicity (mostly mucositis)
