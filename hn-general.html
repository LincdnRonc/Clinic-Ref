Postoperative RT

Give PORT for intermediate-risk features. Add cisplatin for positive margins or extranodal extension. Start within 6 weeks of surgery. A total package time (surgery to end of RT) of ≥14 weeks is associated with worse survival.

Risk groups

GroupFeaturesTreatment
LowT1–2, N0–1, no PNI/LVSI (5 y LRR about 10%)Observe
IntermediateClose margin (<1–5 mm; ECOG 3311 used 3 mm), 2+ nodes, node >3 cm, PNI, T3–4, <1 mm ENE (oropharynx), level IV–V nodes. Oral tongue depth of invasion >3–4 mm (neck)PORT 60/30
HighENE and/or positive marginCisplatin chemoRT 60–66 Gy
  • Relative indications for chemotherapy: multiple positive nodes, PNI/LVSI, T4a, oral cavity primary with level IV nodes. In NCDB data, only ≥3 positive nodes benefited (HR 0.84 for 3–5; 0.65 for ≥6). A single node with ENE (pN2a, HPV−) did not benefit.
  • Cisplatin schedule: weekly 40 mg/m² is non-inferior to 100 mg/m² q3w in the postoperative setting (JCOG1008: 3 y OS 59 → 72%), with less renal and hearing toxicity.
  • Glottic larynx: PORT for pT4 (not pT3), pN2–3, LVSI, PNI, or margin <5 mm (controversial).
  • Treat primary and nodes together, except for parotid or skin primaries.

Key trials

RTOG 9501416 · 2+ nodes, ENE or positive margin · 60–66 Gy ± cisplatin 100 q3w3 y LRC 67 → 78%, DFS 36 → 47%. On long-term follow-up the benefit persisted only for ENE and/or positive margin (10 y LRC 21 → 33%).
EORTC 22931334 · pT3–4 or adverse features · 66 Gy ± cisplatin5 y LRC 69 → 82%, OS 40 → 53%. Acute G3+ toxicity 21 → 41%, no difference in late toxicity.
JCOG1008261 · high-risk PORT · 66/33 with cisplatin 100 q3w vs 40 weeklyWeekly was non-inferior (3 y OS 59 → 72%, HR 0.69). Hearing impairment 17 → 7%, renal 40 → 30%.

Definitive chemoRT and fractionation

Concurrent cisplatin adds 6.5% OS at 5 y (MACH-NC). For patients who cannot have cisplatin, use docetaxel, accelerated or hyperfractionated RT, or cetuximab. Induction chemotherapy is not standard.

  • Not fit for cisplatin: weekly docetaxel with RT (Tata: median OS 15 → 26 months). Cetuximab is inferior to cisplatin in HPV+ oropharynx (RTOG 1016, De-ESCALaTE). Accelerated (6 fractions a week, DAHANCA) or hypofractionated RT alone.
  • Hypofractionation: 44–55/20 was non-inferior to 66/33 over 5.5 weeks (6 fractions a week) in IAEA-HYPNO for LRC, with similar late G3+ toxicity (about 20%).
  • Induction: if used, give TPF (TAX 324: 5 y OS 42 → 52% vs PF). It has no proven benefit over chemoRT alone (PARADIGM, DeCIDE).

Volumes and constraints

CT sim with 2 mm slices, IV contrast and a mouth bite. Fuse MRI and PET. Use 5 mm and 10 mm CTV expansions edited to anatomy, and a 3–5 mm PTV.

Contour step by step with the Head and neck contouring protocol.

Target volumes (international consensus)

  • Definitive: CTV1 = GTV + 5 mm (high dose). CTV2 = GTV + 10 mm, edited at natural barriers (intermediate dose). CTV3 = elective nodes.
  • Postoperative: CTV1 = pre-op GTV + 5 mm. CTV2 = pre-op GTV + 10 mm (R0). CTV3 = surgical bed + elective nodes. Cover the entire flap plus about 1 cm.
  • Nodes: suspicious if ≥1 cm short axis, or >3 grouped nodes of 6–8 mm. Add 5 mm for CTV (10 mm if ENE).
  • PTV: 3–5 mm.
  • Perineural spread: microscopic focal PNI → 50–60 Gy. Positive margin along a nerve → 60–66 Gy ± chemo. Gross or imaging-visible PNI → chemoRT 66–70 Gy and trace the nerve to the skull base.

Re-irradiation

Select by RPA class, time since RT and organ function. Resect if possible. SBRT suits small unresected recurrences; conventional IMRT suits larger or postoperative cases.

Selection and dose (MIRI RPA)

RPA classDefinition2 y OS
IResected, >2 y after prior RT62%
IIUnresected >2 y, or <2 y without organ dysfunction40%
III<2 y plus feeding tube or tracheostomy dependence17%. Generally avoid curative re-RT
  • Avoid re-irradiation within 6 months. At 6–12 months, consider only in highly selected cases.
  • Doses: definitive IMRT 66 Gy; postoperative 60 Gy; SBRT about 40/5 (35–45/5; no ENI) for small tumours (≤25 cc, about 3 cm).
  • Palliative: QUAD shot 14.8/4 BID over 2 days, repeated every 1–2 months if tolerated; or 20–30/5.
  • Risks: severe G3–4 toxicity up to 34% and carotid blowout. The larynx has about 50% G3+ late toxicity.
