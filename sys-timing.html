The ESMO–ESTRO framework (2025–2026)

  • Three joint consensus papers plus a framework paper grade each drug class by irradiated area (skin, brain, head and neck, thorax, abdomen/pelvis, musculoskeletal) and RT scenario (low-dose palliative, high-dose conventional, high-dose stereotactic).
  • Each scenario gets one of three safety levels: minor or no adaptation, major adaptation (drug interruption, drug dose reduction or a major RT adaptation), or not combining.
  • How long to stop: when interrupting, about 5 elimination half-lives before RT is proposed (about 97% of drug eliminated). The framework paper calls this threshold arbitrary: for antibodies with long half-lives it is often impractical, and for PARP inhibitors sensitisation can outlast it.
  • When to restart: a secondary summary of the Annals paper gives at least 1 week after RT, later if acute toxicity persists. Verify against the paper.
  • Chemotherapy is not covered by these papers: for cytotoxics use the eviQ protocol and the concurrent chemo-RT page.

Brain SRS and systemic therapy

  • PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors: continue; do not delay either treatment.
  • T-DM1: should not be offered with intracranial stereotactic RT (Meattini 2024).
  • BRAF/MEK, EGFR and ALK inhibitors: all brain RT scenarios are graded major adaptation except low-dose palliative brain RT with EGFR inhibitors (ESMO–ESTRO).
  • Radionecrosis: dexamethasone first; bevacizumab 7.5 mg/kg q3w × 4 (or 5 mg/kg q2w) for steroid-refractory cases. Verify

Immune-related adverse events

What to recognise in an RT review clinic. Grade, hold the drug, start steroid early, call the medical oncologist.

General approach

  • Grade 1: continue with monitoring (except neurological, cardiac, some haematological).
  • Grade 2: hold immunotherapy; prednisolone 0.5–1 mg/kg/day if not settling.
  • Grade 3–4: admit; prednisolone or methylprednisolone 1–2 mg/kg/day; taper over at least 4–6 weeks; infliximab (not for hepatitis), mycophenolate or others if steroid-refractory; usually stop permanently.
  • Endocrine toxicities (thyroid, hypophysitis, adrenal, type 1 diabetes) are replaced, not reversed; immunotherapy can often continue.
  • PJP prophylaxis and bone protection for prolonged steroid. Verify

Durvalumab after chemo-RT (PACIFIC): any-grade pneumonitis about one third, grade ≥3 about 3–4%. Grade 2: hold and treat; rechallenge once on prednisolone ≤10 mg. Verify

Others to recognise

  • Colitis: diarrhoea on immunotherapy is colitis until shown otherwise; exclude infection, steroid early. Do not blame pelvic RT by default.
  • Hepatitis: rising ALT after liver SBRT on immunotherapy: consider both RILD and immune hepatitis.
  • Myocarditis: rare, high mortality; troponin and ECG for chest pain, dyspnoea or palpitations; overlap with myositis and myasthenia.
  • Hypophysitis: headache, fatigue, low sodium; check cortisol before assuming brain metastasis symptoms.
  • Skin: rash and pruritus common; bullous or mucosal involvement is urgent.
