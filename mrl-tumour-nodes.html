Involved lymph nodes

On both sequences a node is a grey oval sitting in bright fat. Morphology (round, irregular, heterogeneous, lost fatty hilum) matters more than size, and 3D Vane often shows the node–fat edge best.

Treated nodeShrinks, darker (fibrosis)Dark

Node, vessel or bowel?

  • Scroll it: a node is an oval that starts and ends within a few slices; a vessel is a tube that continues; bowel connects to more bowel.
  • On T2 nav vessels are black flow voids; nodes are grey. On 3D Vane vessels may be bright; nodes stay dark.
  • Collapsed small bowel and the left renal vein are the commonest para-aortic mimics; D3 and D4 mimic aortocaval and left para-aortic nodes.
  • Fuse PET and diagnostic imaging at planning; involved nodes are rarely more conspicuous on the daily MR than on PET.

RegionCommonly used threshold

Size is insensitive: small PET-avid or morphologically abnormal nodes count. Check thresholds against your tumour-site protocol.

Upper abdominal node stations to know

  • Hepatoduodenal ligament / porta hepatis: around the CBD (bright) and portal vein (black); common for biliary, pancreatic head and gastric cancers.
  • Gastrohepatic ligament / lesser curve: fat between the left liver lobe and stomach.
  • Coeliac and SMA origin: fat around the arteries arising from the anterior aorta.
  • Peripancreatic: along the splenic vein and pancreatic head.
  • Para-aortic, aortocaval, retrocaval: around and between aorta and IVC, from coeliac axis to bifurcation; retrocrural just above.

Nodal SABR on the MR-linac

  • Contour GTVn as the node edge in fat on the sequence where it is crispest (often 3D Vane); confirm on T2.
  • Para-aortic nodes move little with breathing but sit on the duodenum and small bowel, which move daily: these are the OARs to edit.
  • Pelvic nodes run with the iliac vessels; the bowel loops around them change most between fractions.

Upper GI and hepatobiliary tumours

Most upper GI tumours are only subtly different from their organ on T2. Look for the secondary signs (duct cut-off, wall disruption, vessel encasement) and lean on the reference GTV and fused diagnostic imaging.

Pancreatic adenocarcinoma

  • T1 (3D Vane): usually the best sequence; tumour is darker than the relatively bright normal pancreas.
  • T2: iso to slightly bright, ill-defined.
  • Secondary signs: abrupt cut-off of the bright pancreatic duct with upstream dilatation and tail atrophy; double duct sign (dilated CBD and pancreatic duct) for head tumours.
  • Vessel contact: grey soft tissue replacing the bright fat cuff around the SMA, SMV, coeliac axis or portal vein; follow each vessel in all planes.
  • Tumour vs pancreatitis or post-stent oedema is hard on any MR: anchor to the diagnostic pancreatic-protocol CT. The 1.5 T consensus protocol covers margins and constraints (see Adaptive workflow → Resources).

Cholangiocarcinoma and gallbladder

  • Hilar/extrahepatic: periductal thickening or a small mass, mildly bright on T2, at the point where bright ducts suddenly stop; upstream ducts dilated.
  • Stents (black voids) hide the duct and wall: use pre-stent imaging.
  • Intrahepatic: see Liver & upper GI → Liver lesions.
  • Gallbladder: focal or diffuse wall thickening, intermediate T2 against bright bile; check for direct liver invasion of segments IV/V.

Stomach and GOJ

  • Tumour = intermediate T2 wall thickening that breaks the normal layered wall; bright fluid distension (water before imaging) outlines it best.
  • A collapsed stomach looks thick everywhere: compare with the reference distension before calling tumour.

Adrenal and other abdominal mets

  • Adrenal met: rounded mass replacing the thin λ shape, brighter than normal adrenal on T2.
  • Liver lesions by type: see Liver & upper GI → Liver lesions.

Pelvic tumours on T2

The pelvis is where T2 defines the GTV best: tumour is usually intermediate signal breaking a known dark or bright layer.

GTV tips

  • Compare with the diagnostic MRI (DWI, multiparametric prostate) fused at planning; the daily T2 is for position and shape.
  • Bladder and rectal filling deform cervix, uterus and rectal tumours between fractions: the GTV shape, not just its position, can change.
  • Fiducials and spacers help with prostate position but not with the edge of a DIL.
