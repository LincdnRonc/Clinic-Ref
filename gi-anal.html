Anal canal SCC: treatment and doses

Definitive chemoRT with 5-FU (or capecitabine) and mitomycin, dose-painted IMRT, no planned breaks, and patience: assess response at 26 weeks.

Clinical pearls

  • Don't omit MMC (RTOG 87-04). Cisplatin is an alternative; capecitabine can replace 5-FU.
  • No induction (RTOG 98-11, ACCORD 03) and no maintenance chemo (ACT II).
  • Avoid breaks: don't extend overall time by more than ~4 days (ASTRO, conditional).
  • Wait until 26 weeks to call persistent disease: 72% of non-responders at 11 wk had cCR by 26 wk. Biopsy only if progression or persistence after 26 wk.
  • T1N0 anal margin, or superficially invasive canal cancer (≤3 mm invasion, ≤7 mm spread) with good margins (≥1 cm margin / ≥2 mm canal) and no G3/LVI/PNI: local excision alone.
  • Diversion before chemoRT if obstruction, incontinence or fistula.
  • HIV: treat with standard chemoRT if CD4 adequate; HIV raises risk 4–5×.

Chemotherapy
  • 5-FU 1000 mg/m²/d CI d1–4 and d29–32 + MMC 10 mg/m² d1 and d29 (RTOG 98-11), or single MMC 12 mg/m² d1 (ACT II, max 20 mg). Capecitabine 825 mg/m² BD on RT days is an alternative to 5-FU.
  • MMC causes more myelosuppression (G4 haematological 7 → 23% in RTOG 87-04); treatment breaks in a third with 5-FU/MMC (RTOG 98-11, 3D era).
De-escalation
  • PLATO ACT4: T1–2 (≤4 cm) N0, reduced-dose 41.4/23 gave cCR 92% with fewer breaks and less G3+ acute toxicity (46 → 35%) vs 50.4/28.

Planning

  • Supine, full bladder; frog-leg (less skin fold) or legs together for disease at the verge (auto-bolus). Consider vaginal dilator in situ and scrotal shelf. Prone for large patients to spare bowel.
  • Fuse PET-CT and MRI. Bolus only if skin involved or nodes close to skin; check dose with TLD/diodes in week 1 if disease extends beyond the verge.
  • CTVp: GTVp + 1–1.5 cm (2.5 cm in 0529), ≥3 mm into ischiorectal fossa if sphincters/levators involved. CTVn: GTVn + 1 cm. PTV ~1 cm.
  • Elective: whole anal canal, rectum, mesorectum, presacral, internal and external iliac, obturator and inguinal nodes (AGITG atlas).
  • Inguinal nodes lie anterior and anteromedial to femoral vessels, not posterior or lateral.
  • Coverage: PTV Dmin 90% for primary, 85% for ENI. 81% of plans in RTOG 0529 needed revision on central review: peer review matters.

Response, follow-up and salvage

  • Clinical exam with DRE, inguinal check ± anoscopy for ~6 mo before biopsy or salvage; MRI as an adjunct.
  • After cCR: exam q3 mo ×2 y, q6–12 mo in years 2–3, then annually to 5 y; endoscopy q6–12 mo to 2 y; CT C/A/P at least annually for 2 y.
  • ACT II: 5-y OS 46% without vs 87% with complete response at 26 wk.
  • Persistent or recurrent disease: salvage APR. Re-irradiation in selected cases.
  • Vaginal dilator from ~4–6 wk after treatment, a few minutes 2–3× weekly, for ≥9–12 mo.
