Brachytherapy basics

Dose-rate categories (ICRU 38)

CategoryDose rateExample
LDR0.4–2 Gy/hPermanent prostate seeds (much lower, decaying), eye plaques
MDR2–12 Gy/hRarely used (worse late effects than expected)
HDR>12 Gy/hIr-192 afterloader: gynae, prostate, breast, skin, oesophagus, bronchus
PDRHDR pulses, usually hourlyMimics LDR with an HDR-type source
  • Dose falls with roughly the inverse square of distance near the source: very high dose inside the implant, steep fall-off outside. Hot spots around catheters are expected and accepted.
  • HDR uses large fractions, so convert to EQD2 (α/β 10 for tumour, 3 for late OARs) when adding to EBRT.

Before an implant

  • Anaesthetic review, consent (including the possibility of no implant if anatomy is unsuitable), and a plan for analgesia and catheter care.
  • Anticoagulants and antiplatelets: stop and restart according to the local peri-procedural policy and the prescriber; most invasive implants need anticoagulants held, aspirin often continued.
  • Prophylactic antibiotics for transperineal and transrectal procedures per local protocol; bowel preparation for prostate and gynae.
  • Radiation safety: patients with permanent seeds get written instructions (close contact with pregnant women and children limited for about 2 months for I-125; condoms for the first few ejaculations).

Prostate brachytherapy

Selection

  • LDR or HDR monotherapy: low and favourable intermediate risk.
  • Brachytherapy boost with EBRT ± ADT: unfavourable intermediate and high risk. ASCENDE-RT (LDR boost) roughly halved biochemical failure compared with dose-escalated EBRT, at the cost of more late GU toxicity (strictures).
  • Relative contraindications: large gland (>60 cc, pubic arch interference), high IPSS (>15–20) or high post-void residual, recent TURP with a large defect, inflammatory bowel disease, previous pelvic RT.
  • Salvage brachytherapy for local recurrence after EBRT: selected patients, experienced centres (Reirradiation: prostate).

  • LDR post-implant (day 0–30 CT/MRI): prostate D90 ≥100% and V100 ≥90–95%; urethra D10 <150%, D30 <130%; rectum V100 <1 cc (D2cc below about 145 Gy for I-125 monotherapy).
  • HDR (GEC-ESTRO/ABS): prostate V100 ≥95%; urethra D10 <115–120%; rectum D2cc <75% of the prescription.

Gynae and other sites

Gynae

  • Cervix: image-guided adaptive brachytherapy with MRI at the time of implant; HR-CTV D90 aims and OAR D2cc limits (EMBRACE II) are on Cervix: Brachytherapy and the gynae contouring guide. Interstitial needles for parametrial or distal vaginal extension.
  • Endometrium (post-hysterectomy): vaginal vault brachytherapy, e.g. 21/3 at 0.5 cm or 30/5 at the surface (PORTEC-2 used 21/3 HDR at 5 mm), treating the upper half of the vagina.
  • Keep overall treatment time for cervix ≤50 days, including brachytherapy.

Breast

  • Multicatheter interstitial APBI (GEC-ESTRO phase III): non-inferior local control to whole-breast RT at long-term follow-up, with good cosmesis. HDR schedules such as 32/8 or 30.3/7 twice daily; NSABP B-39 used 34/10 twice daily.
  • Single-entry balloon and strut devices are an alternative where available.
  • Selection follows ASTRO/GEC-ESTRO low-risk criteria (Partial breast).

Other sites

  • Skin: surface moulds or flaps for curved sites (nose, ear, scalp) where electrons fit poorly.
  • Oesophagus and bronchus: intraluminal HDR for palliation of dysphagia or haemoptysis (e.g. single 12–16 Gy oesophageal fraction), not routinely as a boost.
  • Uveal melanoma: I-125 or Ru-106 plaques (COMS: survival equal to enucleation for medium tumours) (Uveal melanoma).
  • Keloid: post-excision HDR or electrons within 24–48 h of surgery (Benign).
