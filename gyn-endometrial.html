Endometrial: adjuvant treatment by risk

FIGO 2023 and molecular class (POLEmut, MMRd, NSMP, p53abn) now drive decisions. VBT replaces pelvic RT for most high-intermediate risk; chemoRT for stage III and serous disease.

Adjuvant treatment by stage and risk

SituationRecommendation
IA G1–2, no LVSIObservation
IA G1–2 with risk factors; IA G3; IB G1–2 without high-risk featuresVBT (PORTEC-2: equal vaginal control to pelvic RT with better QoL)
IB G1–2 with high-risk features (substantial LVSI, age ≥60, LUS involvement)Pelvic RT conditionally recommended; VBT for many
IB G3, stage IIPelvic RT ± VBT (VBT commonly added for cervical stromal involvement). VBT alone for selected node-negative, no LVSI
High-risk histology IA (serous, clear cell, carcinosarcoma)VBT ± chemo (chemo may be omitted if non-invasive/polyp-confined)
High-risk histology IB–IIPelvic RT ± VBT with chemo
III–IVA, any histologyChemoRT → carbo/paclitaxel (PORTEC-3) or chemo ×6 ± RT (GOG 258). Both sequences reasonable
Advanced / recurrent dMMRCarbo/paclitaxel + dostarlimab or pembrolizumab (RUBY, NRG-GY018)
  • Molecular (ASTRO 2022, conditional): p53abn IA–IIIC → chemo + RT; MMRd IB–IIIC2 → RT without chemo; POLEmut → RT without chemo (or de-escalate). PORTEC-4a: molecular profiling spared 46% from VBT without worse outcomes.
  • LVSI: focal (one focus, ≤2 vessels) vs substantial (multiple foci). Substantial LVSI drives pelvic and distant risk, favouring pelvic RT.
  • Nodal staging: SLN mapping preferred. ITCs = node-negative; micrometastases = node-positive. No nodal assessment + deep invasion or LVSI: restaging or pelvic RT.
  • Start VBT within ~12 wk of surgery. Use the Bayta.us nomogram for intermediate-risk counselling.

Medically inoperable

  • MRI essential. HR-CTV = whole uterus to serosa + cervix + 1–2 cm proximal vagina.
  • IA G1–2: brachytherapy alone (LC 80–90%). D90 EQD2 48–62.5 Gy; e.g. 36/6 (48 Gy), 34/4, 36.5/5, 38.4/6 (~52.5 Gy).
  • IA G3, IB–II: pelvic RT 45 Gy + BT to 65–75 Gy EQD2, e.g. 25/5 (75 Gy), 19.5/3, 17/2 (~70 Gy). After 50.4 Gy: 12/2.
  • III–IV: chemoRT + interstitial BT.
  • Not fit for RT, ER/PR positive: progestins (megestrol), tamoxifen or aromatase inhibitor; biopsy q3–6 mo.

Uterine sarcoma

  • Surgery (no lymphadenectomy needed). Low-grade ESS: hormonal therapy (progestins or aromatase inhibitor, not tamoxifen). LMS/undifferentiated: chemo (gemcitabine/docetaxel, doxorubicin).
  • EORTC 55874: pelvic RT 51/28 halved local recurrence for carcinosarcoma (47 → 24%) but not LMS (24 → 20%); no survival gain.

Planning and follow-up

Postoperative pelvic IMRT with a vaginal ITV.

Vaginal CTV, ITV and nodal borders after hysterectomy: Gynae contouring guide, section 6.

Postoperative volumes

  • Sim with full and empty bladder (vaginal ITV), vaginal marker at the apex, IV contrast.
  • Nodes: common iliac, external and internal iliac, obturator; presacral if cervical stromal involvement. Para-aortic if common iliac or para-aortic nodes involved (isolated para-aortic involvement is rare, 1–3%).
  • Vaginal CTV: upper vagina and paravaginal tissue, ~3 cm of vagina; inferior limit around the upper third of the symphysis.
  • IMRT lowers acute and late bowel toxicity (TIME-C, PARCER) and haematological toxicity; bone marrow V40 <37%, V10 <90%; bowel V40 <30%.

Follow-up and recurrence

  • Most recurrences within 2–3 y; vaginal vault is the commonest local site, and often salvageable with RT.
  • Isolated pelvic/para-aortic recurrence: about half alive at 3 y. ReRT SBRT (40–45/5) can cure some (3-y LC 61%, OS 52%; G3+ 14%).

Landmark trials

Design and population, arms, then the result.

Endometrial

PORTEC-1Phase III · n=714 IB G2–3, IC G1–2 · observation vs pelvic RT5-y LRR 14 → 4% (HIR 18 → 5%), vaginal relapse 10 → 2%; no OS gain. Grade and age drive risk.
GOG 99Phase III · intermediate risk · observation vs pelvic RT~20% relative improvement in recurrence with RT; defined HIR (age, grade, LVSI, depth).
PORTEC-2Phase III · n=427 HIR (age >60 with IA G3–IB G2) · pelvic RT vs VBT 21/35-y vaginal relapse ~1.7% both; pelvic recurrence 0.5% → 3.8%; better bowel QoL with VBT. 10-y pelvic recurrence 1% → 6%.
GOG 249Phase III · n=601 HIR stage I–II · pelvic RT vs VBT + carbo/paclitaxel ×3No RFS/OS difference (3-y OS ~90%); pelvic/para-aortic failure 4% → 9% with VBT-chemo; acute G3+ 11% → 64%.
PORTEC-3Phase III · n=660 high risk (IA G3 + LVSI, IB G3, II–III, serous/clear cell) · RT 48.6 Gy vs chemoRT + carbo/paclitaxel ×45-y OS 76 → 81%, FFS 69 → 76%; 10-y OS 67 → 74%. Stage III: 10-y OS 56 → 70%. Benefit concentrated in p53abn.
