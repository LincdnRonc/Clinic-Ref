Endometrium (FIGO 2009)

Endometrial: adjuvant treatment by risk

FIGO 2023 and molecular class (POLEmut, MMRd, NSMP, p53abn) now drive decisions. VBT replaces pelvic RT for most high-intermediate risk; chemoRT for stage III and serous disease.

Adjuvant treatment by stage and risk

SituationRecommendation
IA G1–2, no LVSIObservation
IA G1–2 with risk factors; IA G3; IB G1–2 without high-risk featuresVBT (PORTEC-2: equal vaginal control to pelvic RT with better QoL)
IB G1–2 with high-risk features (substantial LVSI, age ≥60, LUS involvement)Pelvic RT conditionally recommended; VBT for many
IB G3, stage IIPelvic RT ± VBT (VBT commonly added for cervical stromal involvement). VBT alone for selected node-negative, no LVSI
High-risk histology IA (serous, clear cell, carcinosarcoma)VBT ± chemo (chemo may be omitted if non-invasive/polyp-confined)
High-risk histology IB–IIPelvic RT ± VBT with chemo
III–IVA, any histologyChemoRT → carbo/paclitaxel (PORTEC-3) or chemo ×6 ± RT (GOG 258). Both sequences reasonable
Advanced / recurrent dMMRCarbo/paclitaxel + dostarlimab or pembrolizumab (RUBY, NRG-GY018)
  • Molecular (ASTRO 2022, conditional): p53abn IA–IIIC → chemo + RT; MMRd IB–IIIC2 → RT without chemo; POLEmut → RT without chemo (or de-escalate). PORTEC-4a: molecular profiling spared 46% from VBT without worse outcomes.
  • LVSI: focal (one focus, ≤2 vessels) vs substantial (multiple foci). Substantial LVSI drives pelvic and distant risk, favouring pelvic RT.
  • Nodal staging: SLN mapping preferred. ITCs = node-negative; micrometastases = node-positive. No nodal assessment + deep invasion or LVSI: restaging or pelvic RT.
  • Start VBT within ~12 wk of surgery. Use the Bayta.us nomogram for intermediate-risk counselling.

Medically inoperable

  • MRI essential. HR-CTV = whole uterus to serosa + cervix + 1–2 cm proximal vagina.
  • IA G1–2: brachytherapy alone (LC 80–90%). D90 EQD2 48–62.5 Gy; e.g. 36/6 (48 Gy), 34/4, 36.5/5, 38.4/6 (~52.5 Gy).
  • IA G3, IB–II: pelvic RT 45 Gy + BT to 65–75 Gy EQD2, e.g. 25/5 (75 Gy), 19.5/3, 17/2 (~70 Gy). After 50.4 Gy: 12/2.
  • III–IV: chemoRT + interstitial BT.
  • Not fit for RT, ER/PR positive: progestins (megestrol), tamoxifen or aromatase inhibitor; biopsy q3–6 mo.

Uterine sarcoma

  • Surgery (no lymphadenectomy needed). Low-grade ESS: hormonal therapy (progestins or aromatase inhibitor, not tamoxifen). LMS/undifferentiated: chemo (gemcitabine/docetaxel, doxorubicin).
  • EORTC 55874: pelvic RT 51/28 halved local recurrence for carcinosarcoma (47 → 24%) but not LMS (24 → 20%); no survival gain.

Planning and follow-up

Postoperative pelvic IMRT with a vaginal ITV.

Vaginal CTV, ITV and nodal borders after hysterectomy: Gynae contouring guide, section 6.

Postoperative volumes

  • Sim with full and empty bladder (vaginal ITV), vaginal marker at the apex, IV contrast.
  • Nodes: common iliac, external and internal iliac, obturator; presacral if cervical stromal involvement. Para-aortic if common iliac or para-aortic nodes involved (isolated para-aortic involvement is rare, 1–3%).
  • Vaginal CTV: upper vagina and paravaginal tissue, ~3 cm of vagina; inferior limit around the upper third of the symphysis.
  • IMRT lowers acute and late bowel toxicity (TIME-C, PARCER) and haematological toxicity; bone marrow V40 <37%, V10 <90%; bowel V40 <30%.

Follow-up and recurrence

  • Most recurrences within 2–3 y; vaginal vault is the commonest local site, and often salvageable with RT.
  • Isolated pelvic/para-aortic recurrence: about half alive at 3 y. ReRT SBRT (40–45/5) can cure some (3-y LC 61%, OS 52%; G3+ 14%).

Landmark trials

Endometrial

PORTEC-1 (2000)Phase III · n=714 IB G2–3, IC G1–2 · observation vs pelvic RT5-y LRR 14 → 4% (HIR 18 → 5%), vaginal relapse 10 → 2%; no OS gain. Grade and age drive risk.
GOG 99 (2004)Phase III · intermediate risk · observation vs pelvic RT~20% relative improvement in recurrence with RT; defined HIR (age, grade, LVSI, depth).
PORTEC-2 (2010)Phase III · n=427 HIR (age >60 with IA G3–IB G2) · pelvic RT vs VBT 21/35-y vaginal relapse ~1.7% both; pelvic recurrence 0.5% → 3.8%; better bowel QoL with VBT. 10-y pelvic recurrence 1% → 6%.
GOG 249 (2019)Phase III · n=601 HIR stage I–II · pelvic RT vs VBT + carbo/paclitaxel ×3No RFS/OS difference (3-y OS ~90%); pelvic/para-aortic failure 4% → 9% with VBT-chemo; acute G3+ 11% → 64%.
PORTEC-3 (2018; OS 2019)Phase III · n=660 high risk (IA G3 + LVSI, IB G3, II–III, serous/clear cell) · RT 48.6 Gy vs chemoRT + carbo/paclitaxel ×45-y OS 76 → 81%, FFS 69 → 76%; 10-y OS 67 → 74%. Stage III: 10-y OS 56 → 70%. Benefit concentrated in p53abn.
GOG 258 (2019)Phase III · n=813 stage III–IVA · chemoRT + carbo/paclitaxel ×4 vs carbo/paclitaxel ×6No RFS difference (5-y ~58%); vaginal recurrence 7 → 2%, pelvic/para-aortic 20 → 11% with chemoRT; distant 21 → 27%.
PORTEC-4a (2026)Phase III · n=564 HIR · VBT vs molecular-risk-based observation/VBT/pelvic RT46% spared VBT (favourable profile) without worse vaginal control, RFS or OS.
NRG-GY018 / RUBY (2023)Phase III · stage III–IV or recurrent · carbo/paclitaxel ± pembrolizumab or dostarlimabdMMR: 1-y PFS 38 → 74% (HR 0.30); pMMR also benefited (HR 0.54).

Uterine

From the exam summary.

Epidemiology

I: Increasing in western world, most common gynae malignancy here

A: 60

G: F

R: Caucasian

RFs

E:

Unopposed estrogen. Exogenous – tamoxifen, HRT, in utero DES. Endogenous – nulliparity, no breast feeding, early menarche, late menopause, PCOS.

Other: alcohol, obesity. Exercise, breastfeeding and AI protective.

I: nil

G: Lynch syndrome (30-50% lifetime risk, should have TV US screening from 30yo), Cowden

I: nil

FIGO 2023 stage

1A1: NA histo in polyp

1A2: NA histo <50% MMI

1B: NA histo >50% MMI

2A: cervical stromal invasion

2B: substantial LVSI = 5 or more vascular spaces on a slide

2C: aggressive histo with MMI

3A: adnexa or through serosa

3B: into parametria or vagina

3C1: pelvic node: i vs ii for micro (0.2-2mm) vs macro (>2mm) met

3C2: PA node i vs ii

4A: adjacent organ

4B: distant met

2023 vs 2009 FIGO staging differences

  • Confined to polyp is new
  • Grade, histotype, LVSI alter stage
  • Molecular classification encouraged routinely
  • Micro vs macro nodal mets distinction

F/u

  • 80% recurrences occur in first 3 years. Most are distant
  • H&P q3-6mo
  • Consider Ca-125
  • Vaginal dilator 10min 3x per week from 4-6 weeks post RT for at least 5 yrs

5 yr OS

  • Stage III 50%
  • Stage IV: 20%

Pathology

DDx

  • Benign – fibroma, cyst, hyperplasia, polyp
  • Malig – carcinoma, sarcoma, melanoma, lymphoma, choriocarcinoma

Type 1: 80%

  • 60yo
  • associated with estrogen exposure
  • natural hx is simple hyperplasia → atypical hyperplasia → carcinoma in situ → cancer
  • slower growth and spread
  • grade 1-2 endometrioid carcinoma
  • MSI in 1/3rd

Type 2: 20%

  • 65yo
  • not assoc with estrogen
  • arises from atrophy and intraepithelial carcinoma
  • grows and spreads quicker
  • grade 3 or aggressive variants
  • Ca-125 correlates well
  • rare MSI, frequent TP53m

Subtypes

  • Carcinoma
  • Non-aggressive: grade 1-2 endometriod carcinoma
  • Aggressive: grade 3 endometrioid carcinoma, serous, clear cell
  • Sarcoma: leiomyosarcoma, endometrial stromal sarcoma
  • Mixed: carcinosarcoma/MMMT
  • Other: choriocarcinoma, gestational trophoblastic disease

IHC: CK7+, CK20-, ER/PR, PAX8, Ca125

Grade: 1, 2, 3: graded on solid non-squamous component and well formed glands vs no glands <5%, 5-50%, >50%

Molecular

  • POLEmut – trumps all other mutations.
    • Downstages all except FIGO 4 → De-escalate Rx (+CTNNB1 WT) as per PORTEC 4a
    • 5%
  • NSMP – intermediate
    • 50%
  • MMRd – intermediate – poor, responds to IO
    • 30%
  • P53abn – upstages, escalate Rx
    • 15%

Other RF that escalate Rx

  • Age >60
  • Substantial LVSI = 5 or more foci

Management

Total or radical hysterectomy + BSO + SLNBx for all

  • Subtotal – leaves cervix, not oncologic procedure
  • Total hysterectomy removes cervix does not remove parametrium (Standard)
  • Modified radical removes proximal parametria, uterosacral ligaments and 1-2cm of vagina
  • Radical removes parametria to pelvic side wall, at least 2cm of uterosacral ligaments and upper 1/3rd of upper vagina → if cervical involvement
  • Consider pelvic LND if high risk
  • Peritoneal washings and omental biopsy if aggressive histology

Stage 2A-B

  • Adjuvant pelvic EBRT

Stage 2C – 4A

  • Adjuvant chemoradiotherapy (48.6Gy/1.8Gy/# + cisplatin q3weekly) + carbo AUC5/taxol 175mg/m2 3 weekly x 4cycles – PORTEC 3
  • Consider adjuvant brachytherapy boost 10Gy/2# if cervical stromal involvement (also include PS nodes in volumes), close or positive vault margin, extensive LVSI, serous, clear cell or carcinosarcoma histo

Stage 4B

  • Carboplatin/paclitaxel chemotherapy
  • Immunotherapy (RUBY trial – dostarlimab + carbo/taxol in stage III or IV improved OS in all, larger PFS benefit in MMRd)
  • Endocrine therapy – progestin and tamoxifen alternating
  • Palliative RT

Inoperable

  • Stage 1, grade 1-2, <4cm: BT alone – HR CTV D90 = 50-60Gy EQD2
    • 36Gy/6#, 40Gy/5#, 50Gy/10# HDR
    • Uses tandem and ovoid or Y ‘Rotte’ applicator which sits within uterine cornua
  • Others get EBRT + BT boost similar to cervix, aim HR CTV D90= 70-75Gy EQD2
  • 46Gy/23# + 21Gy/3#
  • All GTV D90 80Gy EQD2

PORTEC 1

P: high intermediate risk stage 1

I: adj pelvic RT

O: very low locoregional recurrence with RT 4 vs 14%, 75% were in vaginal vault, however sig bladder/bowel toxicity from EBRT

GOG 99

  • Similar to above – found most HIR pts recurred at vaginal vault, EBRT reduces recurrence but has GI/GU tox

PORTEC 2

P: high intermediate risk stage 1

I: adj VVBT vs WPRT

O: Vaginal vault recurrence <4% at 10 yrs. Pelvic relapse <2% in both. DM/OS no diff. VVBT acceptable.

GOG 249

P: Stage 1 HIR or HR

I: VVBT + carbo/taxol vs pelvic EBRT

O: more G3 tox and nodal relapse with VVBT + CT therefore EBRT preferred. OS no diff.

PORTEC 3

P: stage 2-3 endometrial ca

I: adj chemoRT (cisplatin) + adj carbo/taxol x4 vs RT alone

O: 10 yr OS better with chemoRT 75% vs 67%. Stage III, serous histology or p53 had biggest OS benefit. CRT reduced DM. More G3 toxicity with CRT.

GOG 258

P: stage 2-3 endometrial ca

I: adj chemo only (carbo/taxolx6) vs adj chemoRT (45Gy/25# + cisplat +/- VVBT → carbo/taxol)

O: No diff in OS. CRT had less vaginal recurrence 7% vs 2%, and less pelvic/PA nodal recurrence 20% vs 11%. Chemo alone trended to less DM.

PORTEC 4a

P: HIR endometrial ca

I: Observation for POLE & CTNNB1-WT, VVBT for NSMP & MMRd, escalation for TP53

O: Obs in POLE and CTNNB1 WT is acceptable, vaginal vault recurrence 4% vs 1% with BT

RUBY

P: stage III or IV or recurrent endometrial ca

I: carbo/taxol vs carbo/taxol + dostarlimab

O: 2 yr OS and PFS improved for all. Greater benefit in MMRd
