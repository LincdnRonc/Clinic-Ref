Endometrial: adjuvant treatment by risk

FIGO 2023 and molecular class (POLEmut, MMRd, NSMP, p53abn) now drive decisions. VBT replaces pelvic RT for most high-intermediate risk; chemoRT for stage III and serous disease.

  • Molecular (ASTRO 2022, conditional): p53abn IA–IIIC → chemo + RT; MMRd IB–IIIC2 → RT without chemo; POLEmut → RT without chemo (or de-escalate). PORTEC-4a: molecular profiling spared 46% from VBT without worse outcomes.
  • LVSI: focal (one focus, ≤2 vessels) vs substantial (multiple foci). Substantial LVSI drives pelvic and distant risk, favouring pelvic RT.
  • Nodal staging: SLN mapping preferred. ITCs = node-negative; micrometastases = node-positive. No nodal assessment + deep invasion or LVSI: restaging or pelvic RT.
  • Start VBT within ~12 wk of surgery. Use the Bayta.us nomogram for intermediate-risk counselling.

Medically inoperable

  • MRI essential. HR-CTV = whole uterus to serosa + cervix + 1–2 cm proximal vagina.
  • IA G1–2: brachytherapy alone (LC 80–90%). D90 EQD2 48–62.5 Gy; e.g. 36/6 (48 Gy), 34/4, 36.5/5, 38.4/6 (~52.5 Gy).
  • IA G3, IB–II: pelvic RT 45 Gy + BT to 65–75 Gy EQD2, e.g. 25/5 (75 Gy), 19.5/3, 17/2 (~70 Gy). After 50.4 Gy: 12/2.
  • III–IV: chemoRT + interstitial BT.
  • Not fit for RT, ER/PR positive: progestins (megestrol), tamoxifen or aromatase inhibitor; biopsy q3–6 mo.

Uterine sarcoma

  • Surgery (no lymphadenectomy needed). Low-grade ESS: hormonal therapy (progestins or aromatase inhibitor, not tamoxifen). LMS/undifferentiated: chemo (gemcitabine/docetaxel, doxorubicin).
  • EORTC 55874: pelvic RT 51/28 halved local recurrence for carcinosarcoma (47 → 24%) but not LMS (24 → 20%); no survival gain.

Planning and follow-up

Postoperative pelvic IMRT with a vaginal ITV.

Vaginal CTV, ITV and nodal borders after hysterectomy: Gynae contouring guide, section 6

Postoperative volumes

  • Sim with full and empty bladder (vaginal ITV), vaginal marker at the apex, IV contrast.
  • Nodes: common iliac, external and internal iliac, obturator; presacral if cervical stromal involvement. Para-aortic if common iliac or para-aortic nodes involved (isolated para-aortic involvement is rare, 1–3%).
  • Vaginal CTV: upper vagina and paravaginal tissue, ~3 cm of vagina; inferior limit around the upper third of the symphysis.
  • IMRT lowers acute and late bowel toxicity (TIME-C, PARCER) and haematological toxicity; bone marrow V40 <37%, V10 <90%; bowel V40 <30%.

Follow-up and recurrence

  • Most recurrences within 2–3 y; vaginal vault is the commonest local site, and often salvageable with RT.
  • Isolated pelvic/para-aortic recurrence: about half alive at 3 y. ReRT SBRT (40–45/5) can cure some (3-y LC 61%, OS 52%; G3+ 14%).

Commentary
PORTEC-3 is a landmark trial that revealed important information as to who benefits from chemotherapy added to WPRT, and began the transition to the molecular subtype era in post-op endometrial cancer.

POLEmut. PORTEC 4a shows that early POLEmut can be observed. PORTEC3 shows no benefit with chemo in Stage I-III. GOG258 did not test POLEmut.

NSMP. There is some trend to benefit with chemotherapy in PORTEC 3, 10-yr RFS p=0.13 and OS p=0.21 that warrants further investigation. There was RFS benefit with WPRT in GOG 258 after adjustment, though OS p=0.26. In NSMP in early stage in PORTEC 1&2, VCB is preferred.

dMMR. There appears to be no benefit in dMMR with chemotherapy or radiation (PORTEC 1-3 and GOG 258). This population may be better treated with immunotherapy (NRG GY018 & RUBY). Per both EEE and ASTRO, omission of chemotherapy can be considered in Stage I-III dMMR; these guidelines were published prior to GOG 258 molecular data.

p53abn. There is benefit from WPRT in p53 early stage (PORTEC 4a) but there seems to be no benefit in advanced stage (GOG 258). p53abn benefits from chemotherapy in PORTEC 3.

Statistics. The authors adjusted the data due to imbalances between arms. Although many may have been taught that adjustment in clinical trials means that the trial is no longer randomized, this is not the consensus among statisticians. On the contrary adjustments are endorsed to increase the power of a study (Kahan & Morris 2012).

Compare to GOG 249 which showed no benefit with chemotherapy added to RT in stage I-II (Randall 2019).

Commentary
This study is conducted by PORTEC and available internationally. The trial investigates the benefit of new agents based on molecular profiles, and makes a few changes in chemo and radiation based on prior molecular analysis. However reconsideration of chemo and radiation in some additional subgroups is still warranted.

POLEmut. Outcomes were outstanding in all arms in PORTEC 1-3 in POLEmut, and in PORTEC 4a these HIR patients were able to be observed. RAINBO doesn't randomize this question. However many clinics don't have access to POLEmut testing.

NSMP. ER is a prognostic factor in NSMP, which is why the trial is evaluating progestin. Re-evaluation of chemo should also be performed since there was no clear benefit in PORTEC 3. In GOG 258 (2024) there was RFS benefit with WPRT after adjustment, but not OS.

dMMR. NRG GY018 and RUBY already show benefit with duralumab in Stage III-IV, and the trial will evaluate if this also extends to Stage II. The trial rationally omits chemo based on no benefit in dMMR in PORTEC 3. But the benefit of WPRT should also be re-evaluated at some point since there was no benefit in GOG 258 (2024).

p53 abn. There is benefit from WPRT in p53 early stage (PORTEC 1, 2, 4a) but WPRT in advanced stage warrants re-evaluation. There was no benefit in GOG 258. p53abn benefits from chemotherapy in PORTEC 3.
