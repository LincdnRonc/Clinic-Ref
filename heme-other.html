Solitary plasmacytoma is curable with RT alone (35–50 Gy). In myeloma, use low doses for palliation; even 4 Gy relieves bone pain, though slowly.

Solitary plasmacytoma and myeloma

Solitary plasmacytoma is curable with RT alone (35–50 Gy). In myeloma, use low doses for palliation; even 4 Gy relieves bone pain, though slowly.

Total body irradiation and ALL CNS RT

Myeloablative TBI is 12–15 Gy in 6–12 fractions over 3–5 days, BID, at low dose rate with lung shielding. Reduced-intensity TBI is 2–6 Gy.

TBI

  • Myeloablative: 12–15 Gy in 6–12 fractions BID over 3–5 days.
  • Reduced intensity / non-myeloablative: 2–6 Gy in 1–4 fractions.
  • Dose rate <20 cGy/min (commonly 5–15 cGy/min) to reduce pneumonitis. Uniformity ±10% along the body axis. Partial lung blocks to a mean lung dose of 50–85% of the prescription.
  • Consent: acute parotitis about 75% within 24 h; G3+ pneumonitis about 2%; cataract about 16% (median 1–4 y).

ALL: cranial RT

  • CNS3 disease: 18/10. CNS1–2 (intermediate risk, if given): 12/8.
  • 24 Gy caused more IQ decline than 18 Gy; modern protocols keep cranial doses ≤18 Gy.

Mycosis fungoides and cutaneous lymphoma

Localised MF responds to very low doses: 8/2 gives about 90% CR. Total skin electron therapy (TSEBT) 12 Gy is now standard because it can be repeated.

Doses

  • Localised MF/CTCL: 8/2 preferred (CR 92% vs 30% with 4 Gy in 2 fractions). Thicker or larger lesions: 20–30 Gy in 2–3 Gy fractions.
  • TSEBT: 12 Gy in 1 Gy fractions over 2–3 weeks (historically 30–36 Gy). Boost shadowed areas (soles, perineum, scalp). It can be repeated.
  • Technique: 80% isodose at ≥4 mm depth and 20% isodose <20 mm (EORTC).
  • Primary cutaneous B-cell lymphoma (MZL, FCL): curative 24–30 Gy (ILROG range 20–45 Gy, bolus required); palliative 4/2.

Commentary
This was a worldwide multi-institutional study performed in the six main continents. As of this writing this is the only randomized trial evaluating TBI in ASCT. Results appear overwhelmingly positive. The incredible survival benefit in this study insists upon implementation and study in other leukemias and lymphomas as well.

In pediatrics there is concern regarding late effects of growth impairment, gonadal and cognitive function, cataracts, and secondary malignancies. The authors write that any difference in 2nd malignancy would unlikely washout this marked survival benefit.

Plasmacytoma

From the exam summary.

Epidemiology

I: <10% of all Haem cancer

A: 60

G: M

R: African

RF

E: M, increasing age

I: nil
G: AI conditions

I: nil

Generally a solitary lesion however

multiple solitary plasmacytoma lesions are possible if there is no:

  • systemic marrow involvement
  • peripheral blood FLC ratio <100
  • no other CRAB

Poor prog factors

  • Patient: age, ECOG
  • Tumour: size >5cm, minimal marrow clonal plasma cells, SBP, abnormal FLC ratio
  • Treatment: M protein spike after RT (higher MM risk)

Sites

Solitary bone plasmacytoma >50%

  • Axial and proximal long bones: VB, femur, scapula, proximal upper limb
  • 60% risk of MM at 10 years

Solitary extramedullary plasmacytoma

  • 50% in H&N airways (nasal cavity), chest, GIT, skin
  • 15% risk of MM at 10 years

Work-up

  • Myeloma screen bloods & urine
  • Lesion bx & BMBx (<10% plasma cells)
  • MRI spine & pelvis
  • FDG PET
  • Skeletal survey

Path

  • Biopsy proven clonal expansion of plasma cells
  • Negative BMBx (<10% plasma cells) and myeloma bloods
  • IHC: CD 38+, CD 138+, MUM1+ve

Management

Radiosensitive

Surgical resection/fixation

  • Instability, fracture risk with Mirels score ≥8
  • Complete resection in small accessible EMP can be curative without adj RT
  • Adjuvant radiotherapy: 40-50Gy

Curative radiotherapy – ILROG guidelines

  • <5cm: 30-40Gy/15-20#
  • ≥5cm: 40-50Gy/20-25#
  • Any size EMP gets 45Gy/25#
  • CTV: GTV + 5mm – 3cm cropped to anatomical boundaries
    • In a long bone - 3cm longitudinal along the bone
    • In a VB – 5-10mm + consider including entire VB if uncertainty regarding the extent of bone involvement
    • In soft tissue – 5-10mm isotropic expansion
    • Nasopharyngeal EMP – 5mm + entire nasopharynx
  • Post-op RT recommended for incomplete excision
  • CTV: residual disease + tumour bed + contaminated hardware + 5mm – 3cm expansion
    • Include all possible contaminated surgical hardware (ie entire intramedullary nail)
    • Spine stabilising hardware above/below the VB may not disrupt tumour therefore may not require inclusion
  • If regional N+ then include entire nodal chain (ie entire ipsilateral cervical chain)
  • No ENI routinely, though can consider where ENI does not add much toxicity or optimal imaging/assessment of nodes not available
  • Expected long term LC >90%

10 yr OS

SBP – 50-60%, influenced by progression to MM

SEP – 80%

Long term surveillance is important given risk of progression to MM

EBRT

Dose: 45Gy/25#, VMAT, daily treatment, no BD compensation

Pre-sim: discuss at haem MDT, exclude MM, MRI

Sim: supine, immobilised with vac bag, limb separated, fuse PET and MRI

Volumes

GTV

CTV: GTV + tumour bed if post-op + entire bone medullary cavity, 1-2cm radially

  • If spine then entire VB
  • If SEM then +1-2cm isotropic
  • crop to uninvolved anatomical boundaries

PTV: +5mm

Post-fixation volumes

  • Do not need to cover entire hardware if outside CTV
  • Cover entire bone cavity and any contaminated tracts

Daily CBCT with bone match

Myeloma

From the exam summary.

Epi: I: 10% of haematological cancers

A: 60yo

G: M

R: African

RF

E: obesity, plasmacytoma (B > EM), smouldering myeloma, MGUS

I: nil

I: nil

Path

  • Clonal expansion of plasma cells with ≥ 10% plasma cells in BM OR bx proven plasmacytoma AND one of:
  • S – sixty % clonal plasma cell population
  • Li – free light chain ratio >100
  • M – MRI detected lesion ≥ 5mm
  • C- hypercalcaemia [osteoclastic activity]
  • R- renal dysfunction [light chain nephropathy]
  • A- anaemia [marrow infiltration with plasma cells]
  • B – bony lesions [lytic lesion as myeloma cells release RANKL → osteoclast activation and suppress osteoblast]
  • IHC: CD38+, CD138+, MUM1 +ve, CD56, CD20- or weak focal +ve, CD19-
  • High risk cytogenetics: del17p, t(4;14), t(14;16)

Outcomes

5 yr OS

Stage I = 80%

Stage II = 60%

Stage III = 40%

R-ISS staging

Based on

  • B2 micoglobulin level, albumin, LDH, high risk chromosomal translocations

Work-up

  • PET and skeletal survey via low dose CT now
  • FDG PET: variable sensitivity
  • Bloods: FBC, EUC, CMP, albumin, SPEP, immunofixation, SFLC ratio, LDH, B2 microglobulin
  • 24 hr urine collection: UPEP, IF (Bence jones proteins)

Mgmt

  • All incurable
  • If fit: induction chemotherapy → AuSCT → maintenance lenolidamide indefinitely
  • Chemo options:
  • VRd: Lenolidamide (Revlimid) + bortezomib (Velclade) + dex
  • Frail/transplant ineligible:
    • CyBorD - less effective but replaces lenolidamide (sig myelosuppression) with cyclophosphamide
    • VRd-lite (dose adjuted)

Supportive care

  • Bisphosphonates, PRBC transfusion, IVF
  • Hyperviscosity syndrome: headaches, confusion, blurred vision, mucosal bleed → urgent plasmapheresis and hydration

Palliative RT

  • Typical lytic bone lesions
  • Mirel’s score (estimates # risk with RT) to guide prophylactic fixation if ≥8
    • Type: lytic, blastic, mixed
    • Diameter: <1/3, >2/3
    • Site: upper, lower, trochanteric
    • Pain: mild, mod, functional
  • ILROG suggests: 8Gy/1#, 20Gy/5# or 30Gy/10# based on:
  • prognosis
  • symptom urgency/cord compression
  • prior RT
  • lesion size/soft-tissue bulk
  • marrow reserve/field size
  • logistics: systemic Rx cycles, convenience

Leukaemia & TBI

From the exam summary.

Arise from clonal proliferation

Arise from clonal proliferation of hematopoietic stem cells and classified by lineage (lymphoid, myeloid) and maturity.

  • Myeloid further differentiates into: erythrocytes, granulocytes (neutrophils, eosinophils, and basophils), monocytes, macrophages, megakaryocytes (platelets)
  • Lymphoid further differentiates into: B cells, T cells, NK cells

I: EBV, HTLV

G: Fanconi anaemia, Bloom syndrome, Down syndrome

Management paradigm

  • Chemotherapy is backbone of treatment
  • Induction phase: induce disease remission
  • Consolidation phase: involves allogenic stem cell transplant
  • Maintenance phase
  • TKI can be used in the presence of a Philadelphia chromosome in CML
  • CAR-T cells can be used in relapsed/refractory disease
  • Observation appropriate in CLL unless active symptomatic disease

Role of radiotherapy

  • ALL, and to a lesser extent AML, can involve CNS
  • CSI is indicated for ‘CNS3’ disease (CSF with WBC >5/µL and blasts) if no SCT planned or in relapsed/refractory CNS disease
    • 18Gy/10#
  • WBRT is used in paediatric patients instead of CSI to reduce late effects
  • TBI may be required for conditioning for SCT
  • Testicular leukaemia is a sanctuary site and if relapsed/refractory requires 24Gy/12#
  • Symptomatic splenomegaly in CML can be managed with low dose shrinking field hyperfractionated RT 5Gy/10#
  • Extramedullary myeloid sarcoma can be managed with 24-30Gy in 1.8Gy – 2Gy/#

TBI rationale

  • Eradicate malignant cells, esp in sanctuary sites
  • Use non cross resistant treatment to eradicate chemoresistant cells
  • Create space in marrow for transplant
  • Host immunosuppression to accept transplant and reduce GVHD

TBI

Indications

  • Myeloablation prior to allo SCT where there is sanctuary site involvement (testes/CNS)
  • Myeloablation prior to allo SCT in ALL
  • Non -myeloablative: 2-4Gy/1-2# can be used in frail patients prior to allo SCT

Dose: 12Gy/6# with low dose rate, lateral opposed 6MV beam, BD, with cyclophosphamide or fludarabine chemotherapy

Dose to umbilicus

Aim to deliver 90-110% of the prescription dose to the whole body

NB mediastinum may require boost as shielding reduces dose here

Pre-sim: haem MDT, co-ordinate with SCT timings such that treatment finishes 5 days prior to SCT

Sim

Pt behind Perspex shield in a supine position in vac bag

Legs folded in such that total length ≤160cm

Arms straight by sides

Sand bags for stabilisation and rice bags/bolus for tissue compensation around head and neck

Lead blocks mounted on Perspex box to shield lungs (such that projected shape matches lungs)

Technique

6MV beam

Perspex spoiler – creates secondary electrons, increases surface dose

4m extended SSD

Gantry at 90 or 270 degrees

Rotate collimator to 45 degrees ensure pt in field

Light field to verify position

Alternate laterality each treatment, 6 hrs between fractions

Verification with TLD

OAR: MLD <8Gy

Acute toxicities

Alopecia, mucositis, parotitis, fatigue, mild skin reaction, cytopenia, n/v, diarrhoea, myelosuppression

Subacute: pneumonitis, pericarditis

Late: lung fibrosis, hypothyroidism, cataracts, renal dysfunction, cardiac toxicity, infertility, 2nd malignancy, growth/puberty impairment and CNS effects especially in children

OARs: MLD <8Gy crucial

T-cell

From the exam summary.

Epidemiology

I: rare

A: 60

G: M

R: Asia (nasal NK/T cell), Africa (MF)

RF

E: smoking, ETOH
I: EBV, HTLV-1

I: HIV, solid organ transplant

Pathology

Mature T cell IHC

  • CD2, CD3, CD5, CD7
  • CD4 or CD8

Malignant T cell IHC

  • Loss of normal T cell markers, classically CD7-

Peripheral T cell lymhoma NOS

  • Most common T-cell lymphoma, diagnosis of exclusion
  • CD5-, CD7-
  • Advanced stage at presentation common – widespread nodal dx

Mgmt of PTCL-NOS

  • CHOP-based chemo + consider autoSCT if CMR
  • RT has limited role: 30-36Gy PR, bulky, palliation

Anaplastic T cell lymphoma

  • ALK+ - better prognosis
  • CD30+

Mgmt of anaplastic Large Cell Lymphoma

  • Limited cutaneous involvement – 24Gy
  • Widespread disease: BV-CHP chemotherapy

NK/T cell lymphoma

  • CD56+, EBV+
  • Aggressive but responds to Rx
  • Commonly extranodal in nasal cavity/NPX
  • Can use Lugano staging or specialised staging for nasal cavity/NPX – CSWOG staging

Mgmt of NK/T cell lymphoma

  • Most commonly in nasal cavity
  • Usually early stage
  • Localised disease: RT 50Gy/25# + cisplatin
  • No prophylactic cervical nodal treatment routinely for nasal cavity NK/T but treat to 40Gy for Waldeyer’s ring primary
  • stage III/IV: SMILE chemotherapy

Cutaneous T cell lymphoma

Cutaneous T cell lymphoma – all cutaneous T cell lymphomas are generally indolent

Mycosis Fungoides

  • Epi: 55-60yo M, Black population
  • Presentation: chronic slowly progressive pruritic patches/plaques refractory to eczema and dermatitis treatments
  • Work-up: full skin exam, hepatosplenomegaly, bloods, bx, PET/CT
  • Expansion of skin tropic memory T cells
  • typically presents with skin involvement but can involve nodes, viscera and blood in late stage
  • CD2/3/4/5+, CD7-, CD8-, TCR gene rearrangement is supportive
  • Large cell transformation: 10-20%, higher chance with adv stage. Emergence of large atypical CD30+ cells with rapid clinical progression. Worse prognosis. Needs systemic therapy.

Sezary syndrome

  • leukaemic variant with mature T cells circulating in blood which clonally match those in the skin.
  • T4 skin involvement
  • Often has splenic, nodes, liver and BM involvement

TNM staging

Patch → plaque → tumours

T1: <10% BSA

T2: >10% BSA

T3: tumours

T4: erythroderma >80% BSA

N1-3

M0-1

Mgmt - Cutaneous T cell lymphoma

Limited stage – T1

  • Palliative RT 24Gy/12#
  • Topical therapies: UV phototherapy, steroid cream, topical imquimod, intralesional chemo injection
  • Prognosis > 20 years

Extensive skin involvement – T2+

  • TSET – 12Gy/6# - ORR 90%, CR 30%, duration of benefit ~1yr
  • Boost to tumours
  • Prognosis 15 years, 5-10 years if tumours

Disseminated disease

  • Widespread visceral and nodal involvement
  • Chemotherapy – methotrexate, multi-agent
  • Extracorporeal photopheresis for Sezary syndrome
  • Skin directed therapy for symptoms – as above, if palliative RT 4Gy/2#
  • Poor prognosis

OS

  • I: near normal LE
  • II: 5-10 yrs
  • III: 3-5 yrs
  • Sezary syndrome: 2-5 yrs, worse with significant visceral disease median OS 1-2 years

TSET

Dose: 12Gy/6# to surface, 6MeV, 2nd daily. Dose to surface to 2mm skin depth

1# is often delivered over 48 hrs (3 positions each day)

Perspex spoiler allows 90-95% skin dose

Sim: custom Pb shielding – eyes, nails, genitalia

Technique

Extended SSD 4m

Gantry rotated to 90 or 270 degrees, collimator rotated to 45 degrees

Ensure patient fits within light field

Perspex spoiler

Standing on rotating platform or in 6 stanford positions (ant, post, right ant oblique, right post oblique, left ant oblique, left post oblique)

  • If standing on platform switch arms and legs each fraction
  • If Stanford positions either 3 positions each treatment

Hand holds and feet markers for reproducibility

Electron or photon boost

Electron or photon boost 12Gy to shadowed areas or tumours

  • thick tumours, soles of feet, palms, skin folds, groin, vertex

Multiple TLD for verification, esp over affected shadow regions

Toxicities

Acute: fatigue, alopecia, dermatitis, oedema, mucositis, myelosuppression

Subacute: pneumonitis

Late: dry skin, impaired sweating, pigment change, fibrosis, nail dystrophy, lymphoedema, infertility, hypothyroidism, cataracts, 2nd malignancy
