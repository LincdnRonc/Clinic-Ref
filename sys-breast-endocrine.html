Choosing and duration

  • Postmenopausal: AI upfront, or tamoxifen 2–3 years then AI; 5 years standard; extend to 7–10 years for node-positive or higher-risk disease (MA.17R, ATLAS, aTTom for tamoxifen).
  • Premenopausal, lower risk: tamoxifen 5 years (to 10 if higher risk).
  • Premenopausal, higher risk (needed chemotherapy, age under 35, node-positive): ovarian suppression + exemestane or tamoxifen (SOFT/TEXT).
  • DCIS: tamoxifen 20 mg, or low-dose 5 mg for 3 years (TAM-01), or anastrozole if postmenopausal (IBIS-II DCIS, NSABP B-35): reduces ipsilateral and contralateral events, no survival benefit.
  • Adjuvant bisphosphonate for postmenopausal women (EBCTCG 2015): see bone agents.
  • Omitting RT: PRIME II and CALGB 9343 assumed 5 years of endocrine therapy; poor adherence removes the basis for omission. EUROPA is testing RT alone against endocrine therapy alone.

Verify

Endocrine therapy and RT

Sequencing questions that come up at the RT consent visit.

Sequencing

  • Tamoxifen and AIs can be started before, during or after RT: no consistent difference in control or toxicity. Many start after RT to separate the side-effects.
  • CDK4/6 inhibitors: adjuvant trials started them after RT. For palliative or ablative extracranial RT in metastatic disease they could be offered concurrently (Meattini 2024).
  • Tamoxifen and lung fibrosis with concurrent RT: older reports, not confirmed in randomised data.
  • Ovarian suppression injections continue through RT.

Before starting: checklist

  • Menopausal status (LH, FSH, oestradiol if under 55 or chemotherapy-induced amenorrhoea): an AI alone in a woman with ovarian function is ineffective.
  • Baseline DEXA for AI or ovarian suppression; calcium and vitamin D.
  • VTE history and planned surgery or long-haul travel for tamoxifen.
  • Interacting drugs: paroxetine, fluoxetine, bupropion with tamoxifen.
  • Contraception: tamoxifen is teratogenic and can induce ovulation.

Managing endocrine side-effects

Side-effects are the main reason for stopping early; most can be managed.

Hot flushes and night sweats

  • Lifestyle: layers, cooling, less alcohol and caffeine, weight loss, CBT.
  • Venlafaxine 37.5–75 mg/day for moderate to severe vasomotor symptoms after breast cancer (Cancer Australia guideline). RACGP: start 37.5 mg, most benefit at 75 mg, more side-effects above that. Safe with tamoxifen.
  • Paroxetine 10–20 mg/day only if not on tamoxifen (Cancer Australia guideline): paroxetine, fluoxetine and bupropion are strong CYP2D6 inhibitors and should be avoided with tamoxifen.
  • Alternatives: escitalopram or citalopram, gabapentin (about 900 mg/day, or 300 mg at night for night sweats), oxybutynin 2.5–5 mg bd, clonidine.
  • Neurokinin antagonists (fezolinetant 45 mg daily; elinzanetant studied in women on endocrine therapy, OASIS-4): check current TGA and PBS status. Verify
  • More detail on doses and cautions is on the supportive drugs page.

AI arthralgia

  • Affects up to half; peaks at 3–6 months.
  • Exercise (strongest evidence), simple analgesia, vitamin D repletion.
  • Switch to a different AI, or to tamoxifen.
  • Duloxetine 30 mg then 60 mg daily (SWOG S1202); acupuncture.

Bone health on an AI or ovarian suppression

  • DEXA at baseline and every 1–2 years; calcium, vitamin D, weight-bearing exercise.
  • Treat if T-score ≤ −2.0, or ≤ −1.5 with risk factors, or prior fragility fracture: zoledronic acid 4 mg 6-monthly, denosumab 60 mg 6-monthly, or oral bisphosphonate. Verify
  • Do not stop denosumab 60 mg without a bisphosphonate to follow: rebound vertebral fractures.

Genitourinary and sexual

  • Non-hormonal moisturisers and lubricants first.
  • Low-dose vaginal oestrogen is reasonable on tamoxifen; on an AI discuss with the medical oncologist (small systemic absorption).
  • Pelvic floor physiotherapy; vaginal DHEA or ospemifene are less established after breast cancer.

Tamoxifen-specific

  • Post-menopausal bleeding: refer for endometrial assessment. No routine ultrasound screening.
  • VTE: stop around major surgery and prolonged immobilisation (commonly 2–3 weeks before). Verify
  • Unilateral leg swelling or pleuritic pain is a DVT/PE until excluded.
