Exam summary: Larynx · Hypopharynx

Early glottic cancer

T1–2 N0 glottic cancer is treated with RT or transoral laser surgery. Use more than 2 Gy per fraction. Fields are small and nodes are not treated.

Doses

StageDose
Tis60.75/27 (2.25 Gy)
T163/28 (2.25 Gy) or 66/30 (2.2 Gy)
T265.25/29 (2.25 Gy), 74.4/62 BID, accelerated (DAHANCA) or ± chemo. Some use SIB 66/30 ± nodes to 54 Gy if subglottic extension
  • Fields: generally 5×5 or 6×6 cm opposed laterals. Superior at the thyroid notch (1 cm higher for T2). 1 cm anterior skin flash (consider 5 mm bolus). Posterior 2 cm margin.
  • Laser surgery gives similar voice quality, with less breathy voice but more hoarseness-related inconvenience with RT.

Key trials

Yamazaki180 · T1 glottic · 2 Gy vs 2.25 Gy per fraction5 y LC 77 → 92% with 2.25 Gy. More acute skin erythema (63 → 83%), equivalent late toxicity.
RTOG 9512T2 glottic · 70/35 vs 79.2/66 BID5 y LC about 70 → 78% (p=0.14, not significant).
JCOG0701T1–2 glottic · accelerated vs standard fractionationNon-inferior: 5 y OS about 91 vs 93%.

Advanced larynx: preservation

For T3 and low-volume T4 disease with a functioning larynx, concurrent cisplatin chemoRT gives the best larynx preservation. Bulky T4a (cartilage destruction or >1 cm into the base of tongue) or a non-functioning larynx → total laryngectomy and PORT.

Decisions

  • T3, functional larynx: chemoRT 70 Gy with cisplatin (RTOG 91-11). Alternative: induction TPF → RT in responders.
  • T4a: laryngectomy, unless the larynx has good function and the patient is motivated, non-smoking and supported (MDACC: 10 y LRC 58 → 72% with laryngectomy).
  • Neck: no planned neck dissection after a complete response to chemoRT (ASCO).
  • Hypopharynx: induction chemo → RT in responders (EORTC 24891) or chemoRT. CR rates fall sharply with T stage (T2 82%, T3 48%, T4 0%).

Key trials

RTOG 91-11547 · stage III–IV glottic/supraglottic · RT vs induction PF → RT vs concurrent cisplatin2 y larynx preservation 70, 75 and 88%. 10 y laryngectomy-free survival 17, 29 and 24% (both chemo arms better than RT alone).
VA Larynx332 · laryngectomy + PORT vs induction PF → RTNo OS difference, 64% larynx preservation at 2 y. Salvage laryngectomy 56% for T4 vs 29% for other T stages.
EORTC 24891202 · hypopharynx · laryngectomy vs induction PF → RT5 y OS about 30%; 22% alive with a functional larynx. About two-thirds of survivors kept their larynx.
