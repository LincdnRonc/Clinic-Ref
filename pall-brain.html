Brain metastases

SRS for 1–4 metastases (and conditionally 5–10). After resection, give cavity SRS. If WBRT is needed, use hippocampal avoidance with memantine. For poor-prognosis patients, steroids and supportive care may be as good as WBRT (QUARTZ). WBRT doses, adjuncts and toxicity timing are on WBRT principles.

SRS and cavity contouring, dose by size: Brain metastases contouring guide, section 2

Decisions (ASTRO and ASCO/SNO/ASTRO guidelines)

  • 1–4 intact metastases, good PS: SRS alone (strong). 5–10: SRS conditionally recommended.
  • Resected: cavity SRS (strong) rather than WBRT.
  • WBRT: if favourable prognosis, use HA-WBRT 30/10 + memantine (no hippocampal lesions, expected survival ≥4 months).
  • Asymptomatic, CNS-active systemic therapy available: deferring local therapy is conditionally acceptable (for example EGFR/ALK NSCLC, some melanoma).
  • Poor prognosis (KPS ≤50, or ≤70 without systemic options): best supportive care. QUARTZ showed no meaningful QALY gain from WBRT in NSCLC.
  • Steroids: dexamethasone for symptoms only (e.g. 4–8 mg; 10 mg load then 4–6 mg q6h for severe symptoms) with PPI. No routine antiepileptic prophylaxis.
  • Use the disease-specific GPA (brainmetgpa.com) for prognosis.

Toxicity and consent numbers

From the Whole-brain radiotherapy principles page.

Timeline

PhaseOnsetEffectsCourse
AcuteDuring RT to ~6 weeksFatigue; alopecia (starts ~2–3 weeks in); scalp erythema, dryness, itch; headache; nausea or vomiting; transient worsening of focal deficits from oedema; serous otitis media or otitis externa; taste change; seizuresMostly resolves within weeks. Hair regrowth begins 2–3 months after RT and may be thinner. Oedema responds to steroids.
Early-delayed (subacute)1–6 months (peak ~1–4 months)Somnolence syndrome (drowsiness, lethargy, anorexia, headache); transient memory and cognitive dip (seen at 4 months in trials); persisting fatigue; transient demyelination or pseudoprogression on MRI; Lhermitte's if upper cervical cord was in the fieldUsually reversible over weeks to months. Thought to be from transient demyelination. Steroids help somnolence.
Late>6 months to yearsProgressive cognitive decline (memory, executive function, processing speed); leukoencephalopathy and atrophy; dementia, ataxia and incontinence (NPH-like); radionecrosis; sensorineural hearing loss; cataract; hypopituitarism; vasculopathy and stroke; SMART syndrome; permanent alopecia; second tumours (meningioma, glioma)Often irreversible and progressive. Radionecrosis has a median of 1–2 years and is rare after WBRT alone (more after SRS boost or re-irradiation). Hearing loss, cataract and endocrine effects take years. Second tumours have a latency of >10 years.

Notes by phase

Acute
  • In QUARTZ, drowsiness, hair loss, nausea and dry or itchy scalp were all more frequent with WBRT. Serious adverse events were not.
  • For new headache, vomiting or deficits during RT, think of oedema: increase dexamethasone and image if it doesn't settle.
Early-delayed
  • Somnolence is more common and more marked in children. Reassure patients and families, and use steroids if needed.
  • Memory decline at ~4 months was 52% with SRS + WBRT vs 24% with SRS alone (Chang). Some recovery is possible.
Late
  • Severe dementia developed in 11% of 1-year survivors who had 3–6 Gy fractions (DeAngelis 1989). Onset was 5–36 months (median 14). It wasn't seen with ≤3 Gy per fraction. This is why long-survivors get ≤3 Gy per fraction.
  • For hearing, cochlear dose equals the prescription in conventional WBRT. The risk adds to cisplatin ototoxicity.
  • Hypopituitarism and second tumours matter mainly for long survivors (PCI, PCNSL, young patients). Check endocrine function if symptoms suggest it.

Commentary
On subanalysis, ≥5 mets did favor WBRT for OS. WBRT seems to be more of use in those with greater numbers of mets, while SRS could help preserve QOL in those with fewer lesions.

QOL metrics can be unreliable (Amdur 2019), and may especially tend to be negative in poor prognosis patients regardless of the therapy. This is imporant to note, but it should not be suggested that QOL results are to be ignored. Physician assessed symptoms might have been a useful endpoint. There is a better possibility that QALYs would be improved if SRS was used (RTOG 1270, Brown 2017). Over 50% of patients had only 1-4 mets, and 30% had 1 met! It is unclear why these were not suitable for SRS in the trial but likely due to poor KPS perceived as a contraindication or logistical issues such as setup and treatment time.

The patients were poor prognosis: median OS was 2 mos. The lack of OS benefit was not suprising, and it was not a primary endpoint. Palliative therapies, in general, and also perhaps by definition, are not expected to improve OS. On the other hand since this study we know that some patients with oligometastasis and limited brain metastasis can have more favorable prognosis. The subanalysis results showing OS benefit in ≥5 mets are actually new findings to support RT. WBRT did have neurologic death benefit in Patchell II (1998). An endpoint of neurologic death might have been useful. There is consistent LC benefit across trials.

Since the trial's design, memantine and hippocampal sparing are innovations to improve QOL. The dose used in this study, 20 Gy/5 fx, is routine in UK, Europe, Asia, Canada, and Australia. It was as effective as other regimens in RTOG 6901 & 7361 (Tsao 2012, Borgelt 1980 and 1981).

Individualized approaches are called for. Although some concerns with the trial may exist regarding the role of RT, it should not be dismissed. It is the only randomized trial evaluating WBRT vs. observation in multiple brain mets. The trial provides support for hospice in limited life expectancy.

Commentary
GammaTile is a bioresorbable collagen implant embedded with four cesium-131 seeds. Half life 9.7 days. Collagen implants are already used in neurosurgery and act as scaffold grafts for dura repair.

About five patients in each arm were found to have glioblastoma on final pathology and were excluded from analysis. In the SRS group 92% received 3-5 fractions. MRI review was central and blinded by two ACR certified neuroradiologists who had 100% agreement. Regarding logistics, the radiation oncologist no longer is required to be present during the procedure, but there is a billing code for supervision.

How could survival be so markedly improved? OS was a secondary endpoint and only evaluated because the study met predetermined thresholds. Benefits of GammaTile include improved LC, less radiation necrosis, faster time to begin systemic therapy, and better adherence. Recall that cavity LC with post-op SRS is 70-80% in RTOG 1270 & MDACC randomized trials, which allows for opportunity for improvement (Brown 2017 and Mahajan 2017). There are some differences in histologies between arms, but generally they seem balanced. It's indeed possible that improved LC and less radiation necrosis did improve survival though the magnitude of benefit seems astonishing, or in other words possibly some contribution of Type 1 error. There was higher compliance since 18% weren't able to even undergo postop SRS. Per protocol analysis was performed, and results were still in favor of GammaTile. For comparison in RTOG 1270, only 5% did not receive postop SRS, in the MDACC trial all received postop SRS, and in the JCOG study only 2% did not receive postop SRS (Kayama 2017). The ROADS population may have been higher risk, and this still supports GT's use. Explanations for nonadherence are of interest and could reveal processes that could be improved upon. Perhaps extra collagen grafting also contributed to survival, but this would be purely speculative; placebo collagen without cesium was not used in the control arm. Some results exceed conventional wisdom, but the results are persuasive and the trial was rigorous.

🔀 30 Gy and 37.5 Gy compared:
-More grade ≥3 adverse effects with 37.5 Gy
-no difference in cognitive failure (p=0.64). Some trend to benefit in cavity LC (p=0.14), cranial control (p=0.09), and OS (p=0.18) with 37.5 Gy
Commentary
The defined primary endpoints of the study were QOL and OS, but LC is shown here first due to its interest to practitioners and to make parallel to other similar studies here. If the study is powered for OS, it should also be powered for LC as a primary endpoint.

The initial report showed inferior LC with SRS. This finding was contrary to the conventional wisdom that SRS should result in superior LC due to its higher BED (But note similar LC of 70% in MDACC post-op study, as well as EORTC and Alliance looking at SRS without surgery [Chang 2009, Kocher 2011, Brown 2016]). Are these failures or necrosis? Unlike the MDACC study, RTOG 1270 did not require central review: A post-hoc central review of LF was later reported at ASTRO 2022 and showed less of a difference, LC 79% vs. 87%, p=0.099. The trending p value of 0.099, however, may not provide complete confidence for some, especially since tumors of size >3 cm still had better results with WBRT. At the very least, the trial illustrates the difficulty in assessing LF after SRS and distinguishing LF from radiation necrosis. There is no consensus or reliable method to do so. But even if SRS indeed tends to worse LF, OS is maintained and QOL is superior. Indeed, some data even suggest that radiation necrosis-like findings are correlated with improved OS, perhaps a sign of immune response (Lehrer 2022).

Results for SRS could be improved with fractionation, better conformality, protons, or higher dose. Or perhaps better criteria are needed to assess LF vs. radiation necrosis. And the elephant in the room: perhaps brain metastasis, especially larger ones, are better treated with 30-37.5 Gy focal IMRT in 10-15 fractions. Or maybe some necrosis is ideal if it represents an immune response.

This protocol provides useful doses to prescribe SRS based on size. Note 15 fx has higher BED than the 10 fx regimen.

Brain mets

From the exam summary.

Pathophysiology

  • Most common intracranial tumour
  • Tumour cells lodge in cerebral microvasculature
  • Adhesion to BBB via integrins, ICAM/VCAM
  • Disruption of BBB through MMP and VEGF secretion
  • Cells transmigrate across BBB and adapt and grow with brain parenchyma
  • Leaky tumour vessels leads to vasogenic oedema
  • Most common metastatic site is at grey-white junction due to vessel calibre change

Common primaries

  • Overall: lung, breast, melanoma, colon
  • Haemorrhagic: Melanoma, RCC, thyroid, choriocarcinoma, testicular

Raised ICP mgmt

  • Dexamethasone, BP management
  • Ventriculostomy and decompression with temporary EVD
  • VP shunt
  • Decompressive craniotomy

Radionecrosis

  • Strong association with treatment volume and dose
  • 6-18 months post RT
  • Fractionated SRS for lesions >2cm reduces risk (Minniti et al)
  • Diagnosis: surveillance MRI, MRA, MR with spectroscopy, biopsy
  • Mgmt: steroids, bevacizumab, Bosweillic acid, resection, laser interstitial thermal therapy has low evidence, HBOT has insufficient evidence (ISRS guidelines)

Intracranial activity of systemic agents

ChemotherapyLow; 10-30%
MethotrexateModerate; 30-60%
Single agent IOLow; 20-30%
Doublet agent IOModerate; 50%
EGFR TKI - OsimertinibHigh; 70-90%
Alectinib, loralatinibHigh; 70-90%
Dabrafenib/trametinibModerate; 60%
CrizotinibLow; 20-30%
HER-2 directed therapyOld agents low; 10-20%, new agents mod; 40-60%

WBRT

  • 30Gy/10#, 20Gy/5#
  • For mNSCLC pts there is likely no benefit compared to BSC in those with poor PS and limited LE (QUARTZ trial)
  • HA-WBRT and memantine can reduce neurocognitive toxicity
    • HA-WBRT associated with less cognitive decline (executive function, learning and memory) with no difference in OS vs WBRT (Brown et al)
    • Hippocampi D100 ≤9Gy, Dmax <16Gy, mets need to be ≥5mm from hippocampi
    • Memantine reduced time to cognitive decline and probability of cognitive decline. Some results did not reach significance due to patient attrition (RTOG 0614)

SRT

  • <15mm: 20Gy/1#
  • <2cm: 24Gy/3#
  • ≥ 2cm: 30Gy/5#
  • Aiming BED10 of 50Gy, fractionation trades dose intensity for brain protection
  • 1cm – 4cm is approximate size guide for standard linac
  • Subcm lesions can be treated with gamma knife, cyber knife, ZapX
  • Tumour volume more important than number
  • Reduced cognitive decline compared to WBRT (Brown et al)
  • Can be deferred if asymptomatic and being treated with EGFR TKI
  • SRS up to 20 BM: ph III RCT found SRS vs HA-WBRT in 5-20 BM had fewer pt reported symptoms and less interference in function (Aizer et al)
  • pre-op vs post-op SRS: less RN risk, smaller volume, less LM disease risk – no ph III data yet

Cavity SRT

  • <3cm: 24Gy/3#
  • ≥ 3cm: 30Gy/5#
  • Indications for resection: large ≥ 4cm, symptomatic, pathology uncertainty, oligometastatic in young fit patient
  • Surgical resection and post-op WBRT was historically shown to improve OS and LC compared with WBRT alone in patients with a single met and good PS (Patchell I & II)
  • Modern trials have shown post-op SRS provides comparable OS to post-op WBRT with less neurocognitive toxicity but higher distant brain relapse requiring MRI surveillance/salvage (Brown/NCCTG N107C)
  • Post op SRS improves LC when compared to no post-op RT (70% vs 40% - Mahajan et al MDACC)
