Rectal cancer: risk groups and treatment

MRI decides everything. Low-risk tumours may skip RT; high-risk tumours get TNT; dMMR tumours get immunotherapy first.

MRI risk: the good, the bad and the ugly

  • Rectum = ≤15 cm from anal margin (mid 5–10 cm). EMS >5 mm (T3c/d) is adverse. EMVI may matter more than nodes.
  • Pathology: CRM <1 mm and TME quality are key; ≥12 nodes.
  • Test MMR/MSI on the biopsy (ASTRO, strong).

Who needs RT (ASTRO 2024, ASCO 2024)

  • Consider omitting RT (upfront surgery or chemo-first): cT2 or cT3a/b >5 cm from verge, <4 nodes >1 cm, mrCRM ≥2 mm, no EMVI, with >20% response to chemo (PROSPECT).
  • TNT recommended for higher local risk: low cT3, mrCRM <2 mm, cT4, EMVI, lateral nodes. Chemo before or after LCCRT (strong) or SCRT → chemo (conditional).
  • Organ preservation wanted: RT as part of TNT; LCCRT followed by consolidation chemo is preferred (OPRA).
  • cT1–2N0 needing APR: neoadjuvant RT to improve sphincter preservation (conditional).
  • dMMR/MSI-H: checkpoint inhibitor first; omit RT after cCR (dostarlimab: 100% cCR in the MSKCC series).
  • RT is always preoperative rather than postoperative (strong).
  • ASCO: preferred TNT sequence is chemo after RT; LCCRT preferred over SCRT for TNT, though SCRT is a valid option.

Organ preservation and watch-and-wait

  • OPRA: chemoRT first then consolidation chemo gave 3-y organ preservation 53% vs 41% (induction first); ~27% regrowth among watch-and-wait patients, mostly salvageable.
  • Surveillance (ASTRO): first assessment with MRI, CT and endoscopy + DRE 2–3 mo after treatment; then MRI q3–6 mo for 2 y, q6–12 mo to 5 y; endoscopy + DRE q3–4 mo for 2 y then annually; CT C/A/P q6–12 mo.
  • Local excision after near-complete response: low/mid tumours ≤4 cm, 50–50.4 Gy chemoRT (conditional).
  • Near-complete response at week 14 (OPERA) had similar 5-y organ preservation to cCR, so it need not trigger surgery automatically.
  • Elderly or frail: contact X-ray or endorectal HDR boost (e.g. 39/13 EBRT → 24/3 HDR) for organ preservation is being studied.

Rectal planning

Elective volumes follow the tumour: everyone gets mesorectum, presacral, internal iliac and obturator nodes.

Volumes

  • CTVA (all): mesorectum, presacral space (8–10 mm strip), internal iliac, obturator, superior rectal nodes, to the pelvic floor and ≥2 cm below gross disease.
  • CTVB: add external iliac if anterior organ invasion (T4).
  • CTVC: add inguinal + external iliac if anal canal invaded (<4 cm from verge); inguinals to 2 cm below saphenofemoral junction.
  • Superior border: common iliac bifurcation (sacral promontory, around L5–S1).
  • Boost CTV: GTVp + 1–2.5 cm CC, 1–1.5 cm radial, with sacral hollow at those levels. Lateral nodes >7 mm: boost unless they'll be dissected.
  • Vessels + 7–10 mm for nodal CTV; CTVn = GTVn + 0.5–1.5 cm. PTV ~7 mm with IGRT.
  • Omit the anal canal for mid/high tumours. Cover the sphincter complex and ischiorectal fossa if APR planned or sphincters involved.

Simulation and bowel sparing

  • Supine with full bladder (empty, then drink ~500 mL 1 h before) on vac-lok; or prone on belly board (cuts irradiated small bowel by ~70%, ~100 cc) if canal not involved.
  • Marker at anal verge. IMRT/VMAT conditionally recommended; most useful with external iliac or inguinal coverage.
  • Small bowel V30 (individual loops) best predicts late G2+ and G3+ toxicity.
  • After APR, wire the perineal scar and treat with 1.5 cm margin.

Re-irradiation and recurrence

  • Carefully selected locally recurrent rectal cancer: reRT as neoadjuvant (to improve R0), definitive, or palliative. Needs prior dose reconstruction and cumulative EQD2 planning.
  • Pelvic SABR reRT cumulative EQD2 (α/β 3) D0.5cc: bladder ≤120 Gy, small bowel ≤98 Gy, colon/rectum ≤110 Gy, sacral nerve roots ≤60 Gy (allowing ⅓ recovery).
  • IORT can be added at resection for close or positive margins.

Colon and metastatic colorectal cancer

RT plays a small role in colon cancer but a growing one in oligometastatic disease.

Colon cancer

  • Stage III: adjuvant CAPOX 3 mo for most (IDEA), FOLFOX 6 mo for higher risk. Stage II: only for high-risk features (T4, <12 nodes, LVI/PNI, poor grade, obstruction, perforation).
  • ctDNA-guided adjuvant decisions (DYNAMIC) reduce chemo use without compromising outcomes.
  • pT4 colon (adherent/invading): adjuvant RT may help selected patients (SEER: NNT ~14).
  • TORCH-C: SCRT + CAPOX + PD-1 raised pCR in high-risk MSS colon cancer (phase II).

Metastatic colorectal cancer

  • Liver resection can be curative; perioperative FOLFOX for resectable liver mets (EORTC 40983: 3-y PFS 28 → 35%, OS same). Avoid cetuximab perioperatively (worse OS).
  • Asymptomatic primary with unresectable synchronous mets: upfront primary resection adds complications without survival gain.
  • Lung oligomets: higher BED, smaller lesions and lower burden predict better SBRT control.
  • dMMR/MSI-H: pembrolizumab beats chemo first line (KEYNOTE-177); nivo/ipi (CheckMate 8HW).
  • FOLFOXIRI + bevacizumab upfront beats sequential (TRIBE2).
  • Liver-directed: TARE adds <1 mo PFS second line (no OS); HAI pump trials ongoing.

Also counsel on low anterior resection syndrome (urgency, clustering), sexual dysfunction, infertility and premature menopause (offer fertility preservation, ovarian transposition), sacral insufficiency fractures and second cancers.

Managing bowel toxicity

  • High-dose probiotics during pelvic RT modestly reduce days with loose stools (ProRad, gynae).
  • Chronic proctitis: optimise stool; vitamins A/E/C (90 d) or metronidazole 400 mg TDS (30 d); sucralfate enema 2 g BD (77% improve two grades); APC if persistent (ulcers 26%); hydrocortisone enemas if inflammatory.
