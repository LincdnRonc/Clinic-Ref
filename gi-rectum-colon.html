Colon and rectum (AJCC 8)

Rectal cancer: risk groups and treatment

MRI decides everything. Low-risk tumours may skip RT; high-risk tumours get TNT; dMMR tumours get immunotherapy first.

  • Rectum = ≤15 cm from anal margin (mid 5–10 cm). EMS >5 mm (T3c/d) is adverse. EMVI may matter more than nodes.
  • Pathology: CRM <1 mm and TME quality are key; ≥12 nodes.
  • Test MMR/MSI on the biopsy (ASTRO, strong).

Who needs RT (ASTRO 2024, ASCO 2024)

  • Consider omitting RT (upfront surgery or chemo-first): cT2 or cT3a/b >5 cm from verge, <4 nodes >1 cm, mrCRM ≥2 mm, no EMVI, with >20% response to chemo (PROSPECT).
  • TNT recommended for higher local risk: low cT3, mrCRM <2 mm, cT4, EMVI, lateral nodes. Chemo before or after LCCRT (strong) or SCRT → chemo (conditional).
  • Organ preservation wanted: RT as part of TNT; LCCRT followed by consolidation chemo is preferred (OPRA).
  • cT1–2N0 needing APR: neoadjuvant RT to improve sphincter preservation (conditional).
  • dMMR/MSI-H: checkpoint inhibitor first; omit RT after cCR (dostarlimab: 100% cCR in the MSKCC series).
  • RT is always preoperative rather than postoperative (strong).
  • ASCO: preferred TNT sequence is chemo after RT; LCCRT preferred over SCRT for TNT, though SCRT is a valid option.

Organ preservation and watch-and-wait

  • OPRA: chemoRT first then consolidation chemo gave 3-y organ preservation 53% vs 41% (induction first); ~27% regrowth among watch-and-wait patients, mostly salvageable.
  • Surveillance (ASTRO): first assessment with MRI, CT and endoscopy + DRE 2–3 mo after treatment; then MRI q3–6 mo for 2 y, q6–12 mo to 5 y; endoscopy + DRE q3–4 mo for 2 y then annually; CT C/A/P q6–12 mo.
  • Local excision after near-complete response: low/mid tumours ≤4 cm, 50–50.4 Gy chemoRT (conditional).
  • Near-complete response at week 14 (OPERA) had similar 5-y organ preservation to cCR, so it need not trigger surgery automatically.
  • Elderly or frail: contact X-ray or endorectal HDR boost (e.g. 39/13 EBRT → 24/3 HDR) for organ preservation is being studied.

Volumes

  • CTVA (all): mesorectum, presacral space (8–10 mm strip), internal iliac, obturator, superior rectal nodes, to the pelvic floor and ≥2 cm below gross disease.
  • CTVB: add external iliac if anterior organ invasion (T4).
  • CTVC: add inguinal + external iliac if anal canal invaded (<4 cm from verge); inguinals to 2 cm below saphenofemoral junction.
  • Superior border: common iliac bifurcation (sacral promontory, around L5–S1).
  • Boost CTV: GTVp + 1–2.5 cm CC, 1–1.5 cm radial, with sacral hollow at those levels. Lateral nodes >7 mm: boost unless they'll be dissected.
  • Vessels + 7–10 mm for nodal CTV; CTVn = GTVn + 0.5–1.5 cm. PTV ~7 mm with IGRT.
  • Omit the anal canal for mid/high tumours. Cover the sphincter complex and ischiorectal fossa if APR planned or sphincters involved.

Simulation and bowel sparing

  • Supine with full bladder (empty, then drink ~500 mL 1 h before) on vac-lok; or prone on belly board (cuts irradiated small bowel by ~70%, ~100 cc) if canal not involved.
  • Marker at anal verge. IMRT/VMAT conditionally recommended; most useful with external iliac or inguinal coverage.
  • Small bowel V30 (individual loops) best predicts late G2+ and G3+ toxicity.
  • After APR, wire the perineal scar and treat with 1.5 cm margin.

Re-irradiation and recurrence

  • Carefully selected locally recurrent rectal cancer: reRT as neoadjuvant (to improve R0), definitive, or palliative. Needs prior dose reconstruction and cumulative EQD2 planning.
  • Pelvic SABR reRT cumulative EQD2 (α/β 3) D0.5cc: bladder ≤120 Gy, small bowel ≤98 Gy, colon/rectum ≤110 Gy, sacral nerve roots ≤60 Gy (allowing ⅓ recovery).
  • IORT can be added at resection for close or positive margins.

Colon and metastatic colorectal cancer

RT plays a small role in colon cancer but a growing one in oligometastatic disease.

Colon cancer

  • Stage III: adjuvant CAPOX 3 mo for most (IDEA), FOLFOX 6 mo for higher risk. Stage II: only for high-risk features (T4, <12 nodes, LVI/PNI, poor grade, obstruction, perforation).
  • ctDNA-guided adjuvant decisions (DYNAMIC) reduce chemo use without compromising outcomes.
  • pT4 colon (adherent/invading): adjuvant RT may help selected patients (SEER: NNT ~14).
  • TORCH-C: SCRT + CAPOX + PD-1 raised pCR in high-risk MSS colon cancer (phase II).

Metastatic colorectal cancer

  • Liver resection can be curative; perioperative FOLFOX for resectable liver mets (EORTC 40983: 3-y PFS 28 → 35%, OS same). Avoid cetuximab perioperatively (worse OS).
  • Asymptomatic primary with unresectable synchronous mets: upfront primary resection adds complications without survival gain.
  • Lung oligomets: higher BED, smaller lesions and lower burden predict better SBRT control.
  • dMMR/MSI-H: pembrolizumab beats chemo first line (KEYNOTE-177); nivo/ipi (CheckMate 8HW).
  • FOLFOXIRI + bevacizumab upfront beats sequential (TRIBE2).
  • Liver-directed: TARE adds <1 mo PFS second line (no OS); HAI pump trials ongoing.

Managing bowel toxicity

  • High-dose probiotics during pelvic RT modestly reduce days with loose stools (ProRad, gynae).
  • Chronic proctitis: optimise stool; vitamins A/E/C (90 d) or metronidazole 400 mg TDS (30 d); sucralfate enema 2 g BD (77% improve two grades); APC if persistent (ulcers 26%); hydrocortisone enemas if inflammatory.

Commentary
Overview. The trial supports chemotherapy→TME in those that meet PROSPECT eligibility criteria. LR rates were very low in both arms. Nearly double the amount of chemotherapy was permitted in the selective arm. Radiation may be useful for those who wish to undergo less chemotherapy. Another alternative for this population is avoidance of surgery through organ preservation with chemoradiation.

Adverse effects. Neoadjuvant QOL was reported as worse overall with FOLFOX. ChemoRT QOL showed worse acute diarrhea as well as later fatigue, sexual function, and neuropathy, though neuropathy may be secondary to how chemotherapy was administered. For adverse effects chemo has worse neuropathy during neoadjuvant treatment. Patients who do not wish to undergo chemotherapy, or are unsure if they will complete it due to its difficultly, may elect for chemoRT.

FOLFIRINOX and sequencing. There are questions regarding application of other trials. PRODIGE 23 showed OS and DM benefit with FOLFIRINOX. In that study 7-yr LR was 5% with TNT, indicating a potentially continuing role for radiation. Selective use of radiation has not yet been evaluated with FOLIRINOX (though might be a reasonable extrapolation). The two trials will have different patient populations and should not be directly compared. Sequencing trials with FOLFOX found that chemoradiation should be given first for better pCR, and is recommended by ASCO 2024 rectal guidelines (Scott). This approach is especially useful for organ preservation.

Other stages. Those ineligible in PROSPECT were T4, N2, tumors <5 cm from anal verge, ≤3 mm radial margin, and requiring APR, though some were eligible for FOWARC. If strictly following PROSPECT, these should continue to receive chemoradiation. Additionally LR was 5% with chemoRT in PRODIGE 23. A similar population of advanced tumors were enrolled in RAPIDO, which showed improved LRC in this subset with long course chemoRT compared to short course. Prior to this study, the ASTRO 2020 guidelines already conditionally recommended to omit pre-op RT for T3a/b N0 located >10 cm from anal verge with mrCRM ≥2 mm and no mrEMVI.

  • Leiomyosarcoma

  • Grade
  • Subtype
  • EMVI
  • LVSI
  • MMR
  • Response to NAT: residual tumour size, tumour bed size, presence of fibrosis, granulation tissue, hyalinization of vessels, foamy macrophages

Radiology

  • Involved MRF/CRM if ≤1mm from mesorectal margin

Anatomy

  • Rectum divided into 5cm thirds, extending upwards from top of anorectal ring
  • Anorectal ring is the internal anal sphincter and needed for anal continence, thus tumours extending below here are not candidates for LAR
  • Superior rectum has anterolateral peritoneal coverage and drains into PV via SMV
    • Superior rectum tumours have far lower local recurrence, thus treatment can be de-escalated
  • Mid third is retroperitoneal and drains into II
  • Lower third of rectum is extraperitoneal and drains into II via internal pudendal
    • Historically rectal cancer trials only included those in the lower-mid rectum

Management

Surgical resection

  • EMR: <1mm infiltration into submucosa, no LVSI, G1-2, R0
  • Total mesorectal excision: en-block resection of entire mesorectum and assoc vasculature and lymphatics to avoid tumour spill
    • Improves LC (Dutch TME trial)
  • Low anterior or ultra-low resection: sphincter preserving
  • Abdominoperineal resection: sphincter sacrifice, for advanced low rectal tumours

T1-2N0, clear CRM, mid-upper rectum

  • Surgical resection alone as above (EMR or TME)
  • Adjuvant treatment if upstaged
  • R1 or N+ disease: adjuvant pelvic RT 50Gy/25#
  • If upstaged with T3-T4, N+, LVSI, EMVI: adjuvant chemotherapy – FOLFOX or CAPOX

T2-3 N0-1: Neoadjuvant CRT

  • Short course RT vs LC CRT
    • SC RT largely reserved for those pts unfit for chemotherapy
    • No evidence for SC CRT followed by organ preservation
    • NA RT improves R0 and local control and hence preferred where more downstaging needed. No OS benefit in the TME era
  • Neoadjuvant CRT vs adj CRT: NA 50Gy/25# with conc/adj 5-FU improved LRC and sphincter preservation (German trial) and less acute and late toxicity
  • Long course NA CRT vs SC RT improves LRC, esp in low lying tumour (TROG 01.04)
  • Consider adjuvant FOLFOX or 5FU/leucovorin

RT omission

  • T2-3 N0-1, mid/upper rectal tumour
  • NA FOLFOX x6 alone if >20% MRI response and >5 cycles of chemo completed is not inferior for OS at 5 yrs however has more G3 tox (PROSPECT)
  • CAPOX chemo alone was inferior to LC CRT for LRC (CONVERT)

TNT

  • T3-T4, N2, EMVI, CRM+, threated sphincter, low lying tumour
  • Short course TNT (25Gy/5# + FOLFOX) vs LC CRT improved pCR, DM at 3 yrs but worse LRC at 5 yrs (RAPIDO)
    • Established pCR not a surrogate for LRC
  • Short course TNT had better 3 yr OS than LC-CRT in the STELLAR trial
  • Long course TNT with mFOLFIRINOXx6 q2w vs LC-CRT improved LRC, DM & OS by 6% at 5 years (PRODIGE-23)
  • TME surgery performed 6-12 weeks after TNT. >6 week interval can improve pCR but this does not translate to improved survival

TNT & watch & wait

  • Sequencing: long course CRT → consolidation FOLFOX/CAPOX x8 has superior organ preservation than vice versa - 40% vs 54% (OPRA)
  • 95% recurrence within first 2 yrs
  • OPRA trial had a median dose of 54Gy
  • Watch & wait protocol:
    • Restaging with CEA, MRI, colonoscopy + bx showing at 12 weeks showing cCR
    • DRE, CEA, MRI q3monthly for year 1 then q6monthly
    • CTCAP annually
    • Sigmoidoscopy at 6 months, colonoscopy at 1 year, then sigmoidoscopy q6monthly

Immunotherapy

  • Emerging role for NA IO in MMRd cancers with excellent 100% cCR at 6 months with neoadjuvant dostarlimab (Cercek et al, ph II)

re-RT

  • 10-15% G2 GU and GI toxicity rates

Types of polyp

Types

  • Adenomatous polyp ~ 30%, have malignant potential
    • Tubulovillous
    • Tubular
    • Villous – highest malignant potential
  • Serrated polyps (sawtooth)
    • Sessile serrated – malignant potential via MSI pathway ~ 5%
    • Traditional serrated – malignant potential
    • Hyperplastic polyps – benign ~ 60% of all
  • Inflammatory polyps – benign, assoc with IBD ~ 5%
  • Harmartomatous polyps – assoc with Peutz-Jegher and juvenile polyposis, can be malignant but low risk

Macroscopic

  • Sessile – flat and higher risk of malignancy
  • Pedunculated – on a stalk

Risk of malignancy

  • ↑size >1cm, ↑dysplasia, villous, sessile serrated lesion

Carcinoma progression pathway

MSI high – 10%

  • MMR protein deficiency → high microsatellite instability → accumulation of DNA damage → cellular mutations in TSG and POG (TGF-B, BAX) → unchecked proliferation of cells → dysplasia → carcinoma
  • Fast; 2-3 years

CpG island methylation – 30%

  • BRAF mutation → Sessile serrated lesion → CIMP develops → silencing of TSG (MLH1 hypermethylation +/- PMS2 loss ‘double somatic event’) → microsatellite instability → further mutations → dysplasia → carcinoma
  • moderate then becomes fast; 3- 5 yrs

Volgestein model – 60%

  • Stepwise accumulation of mutations
  • APC → increased B-catenin accumulation → increased WnT signalling → acts as a transcription factor → proliferation → early adenoma → activating mutation in KRAS → further mutations such as p53, SMAD2 & SMAD4 → increased growth signalling and cell survival → dysplastic polyp → carcinoma
  • Slow; 10 yrs

Path

  • Adenocarcinoma – 90%
    • Mucinous, signet ring cell, medullary, micropapillary
    • IHC: CK20, CDX2, MUC2
  • Neuroendocrine carcinoma
  • Neuroendocrine tumour
  • Lymphoma
  • GIST
  • Melanoma
  • Leiomyosarcoma

MMR

  • Proteins responsible for repair of DNA base-base mismatch or insertion/deletion loops at microsatellites
  • Syndromes: Lynch, Muir-Torre, constitutional MMRd syndrome

Molecular

  • KRAS – poor prognosis, predictive of resistance to EGFR TKI in metastatic setting (cetuximab)
  • BRAF – worst prognosis, likely minimal response to EGFR TKI as above, but can response to encorafenib + cetuximab
  • EGFR → RAS → RAF → MEK → ERK → proliferation
    • RAS mutation is downstream thus EGFR Ab does not impact. RAF inhibition causes +ve feedback → increases EGFR signalling which can be blocked with EGFR Ab.
  • MSI/MMRd: MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, PMS2
    • MSI can be tested via PCR or NGS, or MMRd via IHC
    • BRAF strongly associated with sporadic MSI high phenotype
    • good prognosis and predictive of IO response (relevant in metastatic setting)
  • HER-2

Screening

  • Std population: FOBT from 45yo – 74yo q2 yearly
    • If +ve FOBT then 1 in 26 chance of CRC
    • Colonoscopy risk: 1 in 1000 bleed, 1 in 10 000 death
  • Category 2: 1st degree relative ≥60
    • Colonoscopy q5yr from 50yo or 10 yrs younger
  • Category 3: 1st degree relative <60yo or 2 relatives with CRC with at least 1 being 1st degree
    • Colonoscopy q5yr from 40yo or 10 years prior to youngest
    • Consider aspirin prophylaxis (CAPP trial showed benefit with high dose)
  • Very elevated risk: multiple 1st degree relatives
    • Same as above
  • Lynch syndrome: annual colonoscopy from 25yo, aspirin prophylaxis
  • FAP: annual colonoscopy from 10yo, prophylactic colectomy in late teens
  • Other modalities: capsule endoscopy, CT colonography, flexi-sigmoidoscopy
  • Genetics referral for elevated risk and syndromes

Mgmt

Surgery

  • Hemicolectomy
  • Synoptic pathology: tumour size, depth, grade, LVSI, tumour budding, number of harvested nodes, positive nodes, size, ENE, margins, MMR, BRAF status
  • In MMRd neoadjuvant doublet IO had major pathologic response in 70% (NICHE)

Adjuvant systemic therapy

  • Indications: T4, N+, <12 nodes, G3, LVSI, high tumour budding, R1+
  • FOLFOX or CAPOX chemotherapy for 3-6 months
  • ctDNA can aid in estimating risk in the adjuvant setting and avoid chemotherapy
  • Adjuvant aspirin in PI3Km and exercise in all has been shown to reduce recurrence
  • MMRd/MSI high has a good prognosis and chemo can be avoided unless very high risk

Metastatic

Lt vs Rt sided ca

  • Lt sided more likely to occur through Volgestein’s model, hence have KRAS mutation
  • Rt sided more likely to be MMRd or BRAF
  • If left sided RAS WT → EGFR Ab + doublet chemo
  • Rt sided BRAFm → BRAFi + EGFRab + chemo
  • Rt sided no mutations → bev + chemo
