Hot flushes: venlafaxine and alternatives

For women on endocrine therapy or after ovarian suppression, and men on ADT.

Sources and how to use this page

Agents and doses

AgentDoseSide-effectsNotes
Venlafaxine (modified release)37.5 mg daily for 1 week, then 75 mg dailyNausea in week 1, dry mouth, constipation, raised BP, sexual dysfunction; taper to stopFirst choice with tamoxifen. Oxybutynin2.5 mg nocte or bd, up to 5 mg bdDry mouth, constipation, urinary retention, confusion in older patientsWorks in men on ADT and women on endocrine therapy.

Sourced: venlafaxine 37.5–75 mg/day and paroxetine 10–20 mg/day only when not on tamoxifen (Cancer Australia, menopausal symptoms after breast cancer); venlafaxine start 37.5 mg, gabapentin 900 mg/day, fezolinetant 45 mg (RACGP update on non-hormonal therapies). Other doses Verify.

Practical points

  • Benefit usually shows within 2–4 weeks; if none by 4 weeks, switch class.
  • Venlafaxine must be tapered; warn about nausea in the first week (take with food).
  • Menopausal hormone therapy is avoided after ER-positive breast cancer.
  • Rule out other causes if atypical: thyroid, infection, lymphoma B symptoms, opioids, steroids.

Dexamethasone and prednisolone

The doses used most in an RT clinic, and the cover that goes with them.

Dexamethasone

IndicationTypical doseNotes
Symptomatic brain metastases or oedema4–8 mg daily; 16 mg/day for severe mass effectLowest dose that controls symptoms; morning and midday dosing; taper as soon as RT response allows. Half-life: biological 36–54 h.

Prednisolone

  • Radiation pneumonitis: about 40–60 mg daily for 2 weeks, taper over 6–12 weeks; relapse on fast tapers.
  • Immune-related toxicity: see the targeted and immunotherapy page.
  • Equivalence: dexamethasone 0.75 mg ≈ prednisolone 5 mg ≈ hydrocortisone 20 mg.

Cover for anyone on steroids beyond 2–4 weeks

  • PPI if on NSAID, anticoagulant or prior ulcer.
  • Glucose monitoring; warn diabetics.
  • PJP prophylaxis (trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole 160/800 mg three times a week or half-strength daily) for prednisolone ≥20 mg/day for ≥4 weeks, and with temozolomide-RT.
  • Oral candida check; insomnia and mood (dose before 2 pm); proximal myopathy.
  • Taper, do not stop, after more than 3 weeks; sick-day rules.

Common drugs for RT side-effects

First-line choices by symptom. Doses are usual adult starting doses.

Nausea and vomiting

  • Emetic risk of RT (MASCC/ESMO): high for total body irradiation; moderate for upper abdomen and craniospinal; low for brain, head and neck, thorax, pelvis; minimal for breast and extremities.
  • Moderate risk: ondansetron 8 mg before each fraction ± dexamethasone 4 mg for the first 5 fractions.
  • Low risk: rescue metoclopramide 10 mg tds or ondansetron 4–8 mg.
  • Ondansetron constipates and prolongs QT; metoclopramide: limit duration, avoid in Parkinson disease and in bowel obstruction.

Verify

Mucositis, oesophagitis and xerostomia

  • Salt and bicarbonate mouthwash; benzydamine 15 mL qid for H&N RT up to moderate dose.
  • Analgesic ladder: paracetamol, lidocaine viscous 2% 10–15 mL before meals, mucaine, then opioid (liquid oxycodone or morphine; fentanyl patch when stable).
  • Oral candida: nystatin 1 mL qid or fluconazole 50–100 mg daily for 7–14 days.
  • Oesophagitis: PPI, sucralfate 1 g qid, lidocaine viscous; exclude candida.
  • Xerostomia: saliva substitutes, high-fluoride toothpaste, pilocarpine 5 mg tds (sweating; avoid in asthma, glaucoma).
  • Photobiomodulation for mucositis prevention where available (MASCC/ISOO).

Skin

  • Moisturise from day 1; wash normally.
  • Mometasone 0.1% daily from the start reduces dermatitis and itch in breast RT.
  • Moist desquamation: non-adherent silicone dressings; swab and treat infection.
  • Barrier films (e.g. Mepitel film, StrataXRT) by local protocol.

Bowel and rectum

  • Diarrhoea: loperamide 4 mg then 2 mg after each loose stool (max 16 mg/day); low-fibre advice; codeine second line; exclude infection and, on immunotherapy, colitis.
  • Proctitis, acute: steroid or mesalazine suppositories have little evidence; sucralfate enemas or sitz baths; stool softening.
  • Late rectal bleeding: sucralfate enema 2 g bd, argon plasma coagulation, formalin, hyperbaric oxygen.
  • Bile acid malabsorption after pelvic RT: cholestyramine 4 g daily–bd.

Bladder and prostate

  • Obstructive or irritative symptoms: tamsulosin 400 µg daily (start before brachytherapy or SBRT if IPSS high); postural hypotension, retrograde ejaculation.
  • Urgency: mirabegron 25–50 mg, or solifenacin 5–10 mg once obstruction excluded.
  • Dysuria: exclude UTI; urinary alkaliniser.
  • Late haematuria: cystoscopy; hyperbaric oxygen for radiation cystitis.

Sexual and pelvic health

  • Erectile dysfunction after prostate RT: tadalafil 5 mg daily or 10–20 mg on demand; sildenafil 50–100 mg. Not with nitrates.
  • Vaginal stenosis: dilators from 2–4 weeks after RT, 2–3 times a week; vaginal moisturiser; topical oestrogen for cervical, endometrial (discuss) and anal cancer patients.
  • Premature menopause after pelvic RT: MHT is appropriate after cervical SCC and most non-hormone-dependent cancers until the natural age of menopause.

Late effects: fibrosis, necrosis, cognition

  • Pentoxifylline 400 mg bd–tds + vitamin E 1000 IU daily for fibrosis and osteoradionecrosis (PENTO; add clodronate in PENTOCLO): at least 6 months.
  • Memantine with whole-brain RT: 5 mg daily, increasing weekly to 10 mg bd, for 24 weeks (RTOG 0614).
  • Brain radionecrosis: dexamethasone; bevacizumab if refractory.
  • Lhermitte sign: self-limiting; reassure.
  • Neuropathic pain or plexopathy: pregabalin 75 mg bd or gabapentin; duloxetine; amitriptyline 10–25 mg nocte.
