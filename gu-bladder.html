Bladder (AJCC 8)

Bladder: trimodality therapy

Trimodality therapy is a recommended alternative to cystectomy for selected cT2–4a N0 (ASTRO 2026, strong). RT alone is not curative.

Volumes and contouring step by step: Bladder contouring guide (FROGG 2020 doses, margins and constraints in sections 8–9).

Trimodality therapy: who

  • Best candidates: solitary tumour <5–7 cm, predominant urothelial histology, maximal TURBT, no extensive CIS, no hydronephrosis, good bladder function, cN0, will comply with cystoscopic surveillance.
  • Poor features (cT3–4a, hydronephrosis, CIS) are not absolute contraindications; cCR may be <50% with hydronephrosis.
  • cN1: TMT or cystectomy after neoadjuvant/induction systemic therapy. cN2–3 responding: consider consolidative local therapy.
  • Higher distant risk (cT3–4, N+): neoadjuvant / induction systemic therapy.
  • Outcomes: ~70% CR and keep bladder; 5-y OS ~50–57%; mostly distant failure. No RCT vs cystectomy (SPARE couldn't accrue).
  • Incomplete TURBT: pooled RTOG cCR still ~50%.

Bladder planning

  • Sim and treat with an empty bladder; start within ~8 wk of TURBT. IMRT/VMAT with daily CBCT; adaptive / plan-of-the-day if coverage is unreliable.
  • CTV: bladder + 6 mm without extravesical extension, 1 cm with it (Jenkins). Include prostate (males) and proximal urethra if bladder neck/base, T4 or prostatic urethra involved (22% stromal involvement at cystoprostatectomy).
  • BC2001 PTV: outer bladder wall + 1.5 cm; extravesical disease + 2 cm. Even with CBCT a 1.5 cm margin covers >90% of fractions.
  • BC2001 treated a "mini-pelvis" without intentional nodes, with <10% pelvic nodal failure.

Post-cystectomy RT, NMIBC and systemic therapy

Post-cystectomy RT

  • Indications: (y)pT3–4, (y)pN+ or positive margins (urothelial, conditional); pure SCC with (y)pT3–4/N+. Neobladder is not a contraindication.
  • BART: 50.4/28 stoma-sparing pelvic IMRT improved locoregional control, essentially eliminated isolated pelvic failures; OS NS.
  • Dose 44–50.4/22–28 to bed + pelvic nodes; SIB up to 56 Gy to a positive margin; gross residual up to 64 Gy.
  • Start within 2–3 mo of cystectomy (≤4 mo OK for neobladder continence) or within 8 wk of adjuvant chemo. If on adjuvant IO, give RT before or during it.
  • Omit the cystectomy bed if neobladder and margin negative (failures are mostly pelvic sidewall).
  • 2-y local failure: pT3N0 12% · pT3N1 or pT4N0 33% · pT4N1 72% (Reddy).

NMIBC basics

  • Everyone gets TURBT. Low-grade Ta, solitary, <3 cm, no CIS: observe (cytology/cystoscopy q3m ×2 y, q6m ×2 y, then annually).
  • High risk (high grade, T1, CIS, multifocal, ≥3 cm, LVI): intravesical BCG (weekly ×6, 3–4 wk after TURBT) or MMC.
  • High-grade T1 recurrence after adequate intravesical therapy, unfit/declining cystectomy: TMT or trial (ASTRO conditional).
  • ~10% of high-risk NMIBC behave lethally; BCG vs cystectomy has never been settled.

Systemic therapy

  • Cystectomy path: NAC Gem/Cis ×4 → RC (pT0 ~15% → ~38% with NAC). No carboplatin NAC; go to surgery.
  • NIAGARA: durvalumab + Gem/Cis → RC → adjuvant durvalumab: EFS and OS benefit.
  • KEYNOTE-B15/EV-304: perioperative EV + pembrolizumab beat neoadjuvant Gem/Cis (EFS, OS, pCR).
  • Cisplatin-ineligible (VOLGA): perioperative durvalumab + EV around RC is FDA-approved (Nov 2026; not yet confirmed for Australia). Relevant when discussing TMT vs cystectomy in this group (check).
  • IMvigor011: ctDNA-positive after RC → atezolizumab improved DFS/OS; ctDNA-negative did well on surveillance.
  • Metastatic: EV + pembrolizumab first line (EV-302 OS 16→32 mo). Maintenance avelumab after platinum response (JAVELIN Bladder 100). Erdafitinib for FGFR3.

Toxicity and follow-up

  • Acute: frequency, urgency, haematuria, spasm, bowel upset. Send urine culture for suspected UTI (leucocyte esterase often false positive during RT).
  • Late after TMT (pooled RTOG): G3 7% (GU 5.7%, GI 1.9%); no cystectomies for toxicity. Late: contracted bladder, haemorrhagic cystitis, stricture.
  • After TMT: TUR/biopsy at first assessment, cystoscopy + cytology q3–4m ×2 y, then q6m to 5 y, lifelong annual cystoscopy; CT chest/abdo/pelvis q3–6m ×2 y then annually ≥5 y (IBCG).
  • Cystectomy follow-up: retained urethra needs surveillance. 35% develop CKD 3.

Bladder

From the exam summary.

Epidemiology

I: Common, 4th most common in M, 8th in F

A: 60yo

G: M

R: Western countries

RF

E: smoking, ETOH, chronic UTI, stones, neurogenic bladder, previous alkylating chemotherapy (cyclophosphamide), working – aromatic amines, paints, hair dyes, prv pelvic RT, prv upper renal tract ca (40% risk)

I: Schistosoma parasite (endemic in Egypt) – causes SCC

G: nil

I: nil

Staging

Tis: carcinoma in situ (flat)

Ta: pre-invasive papillary neoplasm

T1: no muscle invasion

T2: muscle invasive

T3: perivesical soft tissue

T4: adjacent organ invasion

N1: single pelvic node

N2: multiple pelvic node

M1: DM

Outcomes

5 yr OS

  • Localised node negative: 55%
  • Node positive: 30%

Distant relapse: 20-30%

Node relapse 10-15%

  • Worse for variant histology

5 yr bladder preservation: 80%

F/u

  • q3monthly surveillance cystoscopy for 2 yrs, then q6mo for year 3-5 then annual
  • q6monthly CTCAP

Pre-invasive path

Tis: flat, carcinoma in situ

  • low, intermediate, high grade

Ta: papillary neoplasm

  • papillary urothelial neoplasm of low malignant potential
  • low grade papillary neoplasm
  • high grade papillary neoplasm

Invasive

Urothelial cell carcinoma: classified by flat or papillary.

  • Flat is always high grade
  • Subtypes: micropapillary, sarcomatoid, nested, plasmacytoid

Variant

  • SCC 3-5%
  • AdenoCa
  • Sarcoma: RMS
  • Small cell NEC – RT + cis/etop
  • Melanoma
  • Lymphoma

IHC: Uroplakin, GATA3, CK7, CK20, AE1/AE3

Note: core biopsy must sample detrusor muscle

  • Exception is cancer in diverticulum which lacks muscle wall

Multiple GU cancer theories

Monocloncal seeding

  • malignant clone travels through GU tract and implants into another site resulting in a secondary primary cancer

Field cancerization

  • Epigenetic changes due to exposure to a common carcinogen lead to accumulation to mutations, dysplasia and malignancy

Mgmt – pre-invasive & T1N0M0

1. Cystoscopy + maximal TURBT

  • Repeat if R1 as BCG only effective for eliminating microscopic disease

2. Intravesical MMC or BCG

  • commence by 2 mo post op to avoid macro regrowth
  • Low risk: Ta, low grade, unifocal, <3cm
  • Intermediate: not low or high risk
  • High risk: T1, G3, CIS, multifocal/diffuse, recurrent, large >3cm

Induction

  • ALL: weekly BCG for 6 weeks
  • Low risk can have MMC

Maintenance

  • Check cystoscopy
  • Low risk: TURBT +/-MMC
  • Intermediate risk: intravesical BCG at month 3, 6, 12
  • High risk: if non response → cystectomy. If good response then maintenance BCG q6mo for 3 yrs

50% or more LR by 10 years for high risk

  • Rechallenge BCG if >6mo
  • cystectomy if <6mo
  • or adj RT

BCG toxicity: systemic TB (if bladder injury to ongoing haematuria due to systemic seeding), irritative bladder sx, bladder stricture

Mgmt – T2NanyM0

1.Cystoscopy + maximal TURBT +/- HUN management for all

Cystectomy

NA chemotherapy if for cystectomy OR N+

  • ddMVAC: methotrexate, vinblastine, Adriamycin, cyclophosphamide
    • 5% OS benefit, may be up to 15% for higher risk
  • Cis/gem/Durvalumab + adj durva for 1 yr vs cis/gem had 8% 5yr OS benefit and 20% EFS – NIAGARA (on PBS now)
  • Enfortumab vedotin + pembro showing promising pCR rates ~60%

Cystectomy + PLND with at least 10 LN

  • Indications: young, fit, N-ve, non TCC histology
  • males: removal of prostate, SV, distal vas deferens, proximal urethra
  • females: removal of uterus, anterior vaginal wall, all urethra
  • all: need urinary diversion through neo-bladder using ileum or cutaneous stoma via ileal conduit
    • Neo-bladder is continent, ileal conduit/stoma can be continent or non-continent
  • G3 toxicity rate of 20%, G5 2% in one series (Hirobi et al), QoL is poor

Adjuvant nivolumab (CHECKMATE 274 – improved DFS)

  • if ypT2+ or pN+ or pT3+

Adjuvant radiotherapy: 50.4Gy/28# (BART trial – improved LRC and maybe OS without increased tox)

  • if SM+ve, pT3-4, N+, ≤10 nodes dissected
  • Pre-IO era, no data integrating IO and RT
  • CTV: cystectomy bed + pelvic nodes, spare stoma. Boost SM+ve

TMT

  • Equivalent survival outcomes - Zlotta et al. Better QoL.
  • Selective bladder preservation:
  • functional bladder
  • willing to attend surveillance cystoscopies
  • unifocal tumour without extensive CIS
  • non-variant histology
  • maximal TURBT completed
  • treated HUN
  • 55Gy/20# preferred (pooled analysis of BC2001 & BCON)
  • 64Gy/32# with concurrent MMC/5FU (BC2001 trial)
  • Concurrent weekly cisplatin is alternative (TROG 97.01 & 99.06 analysis)
  • Dose escalation to 70Gy and adaptive RT not shown to be superior (RAIDER)
  • Carbogen + nicotinamide if unfit for chemo (BCON)
  • if N-ve not role for ENI (Tunio et al)
  • if N+ then boost involved nodes to 60Gy and ENI 50Gy ENI EQD2
  • 20% need salvage cystectomy, more morbid than upfront surg

Palliative RT

  • 21Gy/3# 2nd daily non-inferior to 35Gy/10# for sx relief (MRC trial)
  • 70% haematuria relief at 3 mo

Zlotta et al

  • Retrospective review with matched cohort analysis showed TMT is comparable to cystectomy for 5 year CSS, DFS and beneficial for OS 66% vs 73%

BC2001

P: MIBC, unresectable

I: 64Gy/32# or 55Gy/20# +/- MMC/5FU

whole bladder RT vs modified bladder RT (80% dose to whole bladder, 100% to GTV)

No ENI, 30% had NA CT

O: CRT vs RT:

  • 5 yr OS 48% vs 35%
  • Invasive bladder recurrence 11% vs 19%
  • Nodal relapse 5% vs 7%
  • 5 yr LRC 60% vs 50%
  • Cystectomy 14% vs 22%
  • G3/4 tox: 8% vs 16%
  • Benefit persists at 10 yrs
  • Modified bladder RT non-inferior

BCON and BC2001 pooled analysis

  • 55Gy/20# had better LRC and isotoxic, hypothesised due to less repopulation time

Tunio et al

  • in N-ve ENI did not improve DFS, bladder preservation or OS

RAIDER

  • adaptive plan of the day RT is feasible but did not reduce toxicity
  • 74Gy vs 60Gy DE has numerical but non significant benefit in LRC

BCON

P: MIBC receiving radical RT

I: radical RT +/- carbogen and nicotinamide

O: 3 yrs OS 45% vs 50% however no sig difference at 10 yrs ~ 30%. Tumour necrosis predictive for greater benefit

BART (ph III RCT)

P: High risk MIBC – any of: T3-4, N+, SM+, ≤10 nodes dissected

I: Post-op pelvic RT 50.4Gy/28# vs no RT.

Chemo allowed in neoadj or adj setting. No IO.

O: 10% reduction in LRR at 2 yrs. 2 yr OS 70% vs 57%. No G3 tox.

CHECKMATE 274

  • Addition of adj nivo to cis/gem chemo improved mOS from 50 to 70 months
  • 10% DFS benefit, 6-8% OS benefit in interim analysis

NIAGRA

Neoadjuvant gem/cis/durva compared to gem/cis had 8% 5 yr OS benefit.

NB the VESPER/GETUG trial has previously compared ddMVAC to cis/gem and found ddMVAC to have superior OS but more tox
