The positive sentinel node

For cN0 disease with 1–2 positive sentinel nodes, completion ALND does not improve control. Either treat with breast tangents (Z-11) or replace ALND with axillary RT (AMAROS). Each option roughly halves lymphoedema compared with ALND.

Decisions

  • With 1–2 positive SLNs, about 30% have further positive axillary nodes, yet regional control is not improved by ALND (Z-11, AMAROS).
  • Micrometastases or ITC: breast RT alone; consider high tangents (IBCSG 23-01, NCCN).
  • 1–2 macrometastases, BCS: no ALND. Choose between whole breast with or without high tangents and full RNI (axilla and SCF), based on risk.
  • Mastectomy with SLN macrometastasis: do ALND if the PMRT recommendation is uncertain.
  • Lymphoedema to quote: about 15% with ALND vs 5% with SLNB (roughly double these by patient report). AMAROS at 5 y: 25% with ALND vs 12% with axillary RT.

AMAROS1,425 · cN0 SLN+ · ALND vs axillary RT (axilla + SCF, no IMN)10 y axillary recurrence <2% in both arms. Lymphoedema about halved with RT. Mastectomy patients were included (17%).
IBCSG 23-01934 · ≤2 SLN micrometastases · ALND vs none10 y DFS 77 → 75% (no benefit from ALND). Recurrence in the undissected axilla <1%. Late lymphoedema 4 → 13% with ALND.
SINODAR-ONE889 · cT1–2 · 1–2 macrometastatic SLNs · BCS or mastectomy · ALND vs noneNo difference in survival or recurrence. Lymphoedema 2 → 13% with ALND.

Regional nodal irradiation

RNI after BCS or mastectomy lowers locoregional and distant recurrence by about 3–5%, improving DFS, with a small breast cancer mortality benefit. The cost is about 1% more pneumonitis and 4% more lymphoedema.

Who gets nodal RT (NCCN)

  • ≥4 positive nodes: RNI including IMN (category 1).
  • 1–3 positive nodes: strongly consider RNI, especially if the tumour is central or medial.
  • pN0: RNI if central or medial, ≥2 cm and other risk factors (young age, LVSI). MA.20 high-risk node-negative: cT3, or cT2 with <10 nodes removed and G3, ER− or LVSI.
  • cN+ before neoadjuvant chemotherapy: RNI. In NSABP B-51 (cN1 → ypN0), omitting RNI did not increase recurrence over 5 y. Discuss omission for ypN0 after cN1.
  • IMN: benefit is greatest for N+ medial or central tumours and for ≥4 nodes in any location (DBCG-IMN). If the decision is borderline, consider treating only the first two intercostal spaces to spare the heart.
  • Aim to keep 10 y LRR below 5%. In EORTC 22922, a 3% breast cancer mortality benefit was seen with 10 y LRR of 5.5%.

Post-mastectomy RT

PMRT reduces LRR by 19%, which translates into a 9% reduction in breast cancer mortality (EBCTCG 2014). This includes women with only 1–3 positive nodes. It is recommended for pN+ and pT4 disease. For pN1 disease with favourable features, omission can be discussed.

Indications (ASTRO 2025)

StageRecommendation
pT1–2 N0Not recommended. May benefit if there are multiple adverse features: TNBC, G3, LVSI, young age, central or medial tumour
pT3 N0Conditionally recommended. May omit PMRT or treat chest wall alone if low–intermediate grade, HR+/HER2−, postmenopausal, no LVSI and low 21-gene recurrence score
pN+ (including 1–3 nodes)Recommended. Omission may be appropriate for pN1mic or low-burden pN1a after ALND with the same favourable features
Any pT4Recommended even without other risk factors
After neoadjuvant chemotherapyStrongly consider for clinical stage III or any residual disease. Local recurrence is <10% after total pCR and 10–20% with residual disease (Mamounas)
  • Volumes: chest wall + SCF/axillary apex + IMN in most cases.
  • Chest wall boost only for close (≤2 mm) or positive margins (NSABP B-51), to 12–14 Gy. Also used for inflammatory cancer.
  • Bolus: use throughout for T4b–d. Not needed routinely otherwise (ESTRO Delphi consensus). Bolus increases skin toxicity.

Key trials

Danish 82b and 82c1,708 premenopausal and 1,375 postmenopausal · high-risk · CMF or tamoxifen ± PMRT (tangents, SCF, IMN)82b: 10 y LRF 32 → 9%, 10 y OS 45 → 54%. LRF fell from about one third to <10% in both trials. Critics point to inadequate axillary dissection.
SUPREMO1,600 · T1–2 N1 or T2 N0 high-risk · ± chest wall RT10 y chest wall recurrence 2.5 → 1.1%. 10 y OS about 82%.
HYPO-PMRT / RT CHARMPMRT with reconstruction · conventional vs hypofractionationRT CHARM 2 y reconstruction complications about 12 → 14% (non-inferior). 3 y LRR 2.3 → 1.5%. Hypofractionation is acceptable with reconstruction.

Reconstruction and PMRT

  • Major reconstruction complications about 41% overall with PMRT in one series. Reconstruction failure about 10%.
  • Irradiating the tissue expander has more complications than irradiating the final implant. Removal 40% vs 32% in one series. Capsular contracture rates vary between series.
  • Autologous reconstruction is more often used when PMRT is planned.
