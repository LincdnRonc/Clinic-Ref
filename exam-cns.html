Grade 1–3 glioma

Epidemiology · WHO classification 2021 · Management · EBRT · OARs

Clinic page: Glioma (adult)

Epidemiology

I: rare, GBM > oligo > astro

A: low grade – 40, high grade - 60

G: M

R: Western world (↑ access to imaging)

RF

E: Fhx, prior cranial RT

I: nil

G: NF-1 (optic glioma, astrocytoma), Turcot’s syndrome (MMR GBM), NF2, Li Fraumeni (astrocytoma), VHL (haemangioblastoma),

I: nil

Staging

  • Does not spread outside CNS, no staging applies

DDx

Benign:

  • Abscess, haemorrhage, sarcoidosis, MS, epidermoid cyst, aneurysm, pituitary adenoma

Low grade/ high grade/malignant:

  • Extra-axial: meningioma, schwannoma, LM disease, NPC, esthesioneuroblastoma
  • Posterior fossa: ependymoma, medulloblastoma, pilocytic astrocytoma
  • Intra-axial: glioma, craniopharyngioma, germ cell tumour, PiTNET, pineoblastoma, PCNSL, metastases
  • Haemorrhagic mets: melanoma, RCC, follicular thyroid cancer, choriocarcinoma

Background

  • Gliomas are derived from neuroglional cells (predominantly oligodendrocytes and astrocytes)
  • Grade represents infiltration with G1 being no infiltration
  • LGG typically supratentorial

MRI appearance:

  • 20% enhance
  • T1 hypointense/isointense
  • T2/FLAIR hyperintense, diffuse change
  • Calcifications in oligo

Natural Hx:

  • G1 – GTR is curable
  • G2 – long natural hx but typically recur post surgery
    • At recurrence 50-70% can transform to higher grade

Outcomes:

  • mOS oligodendroglioma G2 15-20 years, G3 10-15 years
  • mOS astrocytoma G2 7-10 years, G3 5-7 years, G4 3-5 years

Diffuse glioma, adult type

  • Oligodendroglioma, IDHm G2-3
  • GBM, IDHwt G4

Diffuse glioma, paediatric type

  • Low grade or high grade, IDHwt

Circumscribed astrocytic glioma

  • Pilocytic astrocytoma, IDHwt G1
  • Pleomorphic xanthoastrocytoma, G1

  • Ganglioglioma, G1

Grade determined by:

C: cellularity

A – atypia

M - mitoses

E – endovascular proliferation

N – necrosis

& molecular features

Oligiodendr-EGG-glioma

  • Chicken wire vasculature
  • Fried egg appearance
  • Calcifications

Astrocytoma:

  • Pleomorphic cells in a fibrillary glial cell background
  • Can have anaplasia

Pilocytic Astrocytoma:

  • G1, well circumscribed, often cystic, posterior fossa in children
  • KIAA1549-BRAFm common

GBM, IDHwt:

  • Pseudopalisading necrosis
  • Microvascular proliferation (MVP)
  • MGMT methylation predicts poor response to TMZ as it antagonizes the alkylating effect of TMZ

WHO molecular classification (2021):

1. IDH mutant

2. ATRX loss, TP53 loss → astro

3. 1p/19q co-deletion → oligo

4. CDKN2A/B HD → G3/G4 astro

5. Histology – MVP, PPN → G4 astro

1. IDH WT

2. H3 mutation (K27, G34) → paediatric

3. H3 WT → adult

4. TERTp mut, +7/-10, EGFRamp → GBM

5. Histology → GBM

IDH significant:

More often evolves from lower-grade glioma

Better prognosis than IDH-wildtype at same histological grade

IHC:

  • S100, GFAP, olig2

PCV vs TMZ:

  • TMZ is daily tablet
  • TMZ s/e: n/v, fatigue
  • PCV - procarbazine, lomustine are tablets, vincristine is IV
  • PCV has more significant myelosuppression, n/v and fatigue and neuropathy from vincristine

Management

1. Maximal safe resection for all

2. Post-op MRI within 48 hours

  • Later MRI results in obscuring of residual tumour due to blood products

3. Adjuvant treatment guided by grade

Grade 1:

  • Observe regardless of resection extent
  • Salvage surgery or radiotherapy at symptomatic progression
  • RT: 50.4Gy/28#

Grade 2:

  • Adjuvant Rx vs salvage Rx
  • These pts have survivals 10+years, therefore neurocognitive toxicity and 2nd malignancy is important
  • However analysis of neurocognitive function in these trials shows stable neurocognition after RT whilst tumour progression was associated with greater short term decline
  • 50% will progress at 5 years so close surveillance crucial
  • Adjuvant radiotherapy vs salvage RT improves seizure free survival and PFS from 3.5yrs to 5 years (Non Believer’s trial)
  • Adjuvant treatment if meeting 3 Pignatti criteria:
    • S – size ≥ 6cm
    • A – age ≥ 40
    • T – tumour crossing midline
    • A - astrocytoma
    • N – neuro deficits pre-op
  • RT: 54Gy/30#
    • Dose escalation does not improve outcomes (Believer’s trial)
  • Adjuvant sequential PCV chemotherapy improves median OS (8 vs 13yrs), particularly in oligo (RTOG 9802 phase III)
  • IDHm inhibitors alone improve PFS from 11 to 28mo and delayed ‘next treatment intervention’ by 50% in pts with residual or recurrent dx & halves seizures. No OS benefit. (INDIGO trial)
    • Ie used to delay RT
    • s/e of Vorasidenib: transaminitis, n/v, fatigue, diarrhea, headache

Grade 3:

  • Adjuvant RT 59.4Gy/33# and sequential chemotherapy for all
  • Addition of PCV improves OS for all, oligo particularly benefits from PCV (EORTC 26951, RTOG 9402)
  • G3 astrocytoma has OS benefit from adjuvant TMZ, not concurrent (CATNON)
  • Omission of RT and treatment with TMZ has worse PFS - Codel
  • Sequential PCV vs concurrent/adj TMZ in G3 astro - Codel trial ongoing
  • Chemo vs RT sequencing does not impact outcomes – NOA-04

NB: most trials pre-dated molecular classification, anaplastic = G3

Non Believers trial

P: resected G1-2 glioma

I: immediate RT vs observation and RT at progression

O: median OS 7 years in both, PFS 5 vs 3.5 years, seizures 40% vs 25%

RTOG 9802 phase III

P: resected G2 glioma

I: adj RT vs RT and seq PCV

O: PCV improved OS 8 yrs vs 13 yrs

EORTC 26951

P: Resected glioma

I: RT vs RT +PCVx6 cycles

O: PCV improves OS for all (3.5 vs 2.6 yrs), more benefit in oligo

RTOG 9402

P: Resected G3 glioma

I: PCV followed by RT vs RT alone 59.4Gy/33#

O: Improved OS for oligodendroglioma only – median OS 15 vs 7 yrs

CATNON

P: Resected G3 astrocytoma

I: RT + concurrent TMZ vs RT + sequential TMZ vs RT + concurrent and adj vs RT alone

O: Adj TMZ had superior OS to concurrent TMZ (7 vs 4 yrs). Further results pending.

CODEL

P: Resected G3 oligodendrogliomas

I: RT + seq PCV vs RT + conc and seq TMZ vs TMZ alone

O: TMZ alone has inferior OS. Further results pending

NOA-04

P: Resected G3 glioma

I: RT followed by CHT or CHT followed by RT

O: no diff in time to failure, OS or PFS.

Identified IDHm as strongest prognostic factor

Found PCV benefit greater in oligo

EBRT

Dose: 54Gy/30# for G2, 59.4Gy/33# for G3. VMAT, daily treatment, no BD compensation

Organise med onc review for sequential chemotherapy (temozolomide for 12 months for G3 Astro, PCV for 9-12 months for G2 any and G3 oligo)

Pre-sim: MDT discussion, fertility sparing, baseline pituitary panel bloods (FSH, LH, ACTH, early morning cortisol, TFT, IGF-1, prolactin), ensure scar healed, neurocognitive testing, audiometry, vision assessment

Sim: Supine, arms down, TPS shell for head, fuse pre-op and post-op MRI – T1 + gad and T2/flair

Volumes:

GTV: surgical cavity + entire T2/flair change + enhancement on T1 + gad

CTV: +10mm for G2, +15mm for G3. Crop to ventricles, falx, bone

PTV: +3mm

Daily CBCT with bone match

OARs

Globe: Dmax <50Gy, mean <35Gy

Lacrimal: mean <25Gy

Lens: Dmax <6Gy, accept up to 10Gy

Optic nerve & chiasm: Dmax<54Gy

Brainstem: Dmax <54Gy

Brain: ALARA

Pituitary: mean <45Gy

Hippocampus: try to spare contralateral

Parotid: mean <26Gy

Cochlea: mean <45Gy

Toxicities

Acute: fatigue, alopecia, dermatitis, headaches, n/v, seizure, dry eye, worsening of pre-existing neurology

Subacute: somnolence syndrome

Late: chronic fatigue, headaches, neurocognitive decline, radionecrosis, neurological deficit, hypopituitarism, cataracts, vision loss, 2nd malignancy

Acute: BBB disruption, inflammation, oedema

Subacute: reversible demyelination and oligodendrocyte injury

Late: endothelial injury, ischemia, glial stem cell death, irreversible demyelination and injury of white matter, necrosis

Grade 4 glioma

Clinic page: Glioma (adult)

GBM IDHm and G4 astrocytoma IDHwt

Epi:

  • GBM makes up 50% of brain primary tumours

MRI features:

  • Diffuse, infiltrative borders
  • Contrast enhancing
  • T1 hypointense, T2/flair hyperintense
  • 20% multifocal, majority supratentorial
  • Central necrosis, surrounding oedema, midline shift

RANO criteria – FUNN25

  • Used to determine true progression
  • Baseline MRI 4 weeks post CRT for comparison
  • F: T2/Flair change progression despite steroids
  • U: Definite growth of Unmeasurable lesions
  • N: New lesions
  • N: Neurology not explained by other reason
  • 25: Increase in perpendicular dimensions by ≥25%

Radionecrosis is not self-limiting and occurs later (months – years), pseudoprogression is limiting and occurs earlier (weeks – early months).

Both are low on MRI spec and perfusion imaging.

Use MRI with DWI and perfusion study, MR spectroscopy (choline uptake), FET/FDOPA PET imaging to help distinguish

Outcomes

  • mOS 12-18mo

Management

1. Maximal safe resection for all

  • Lower residual volume is associated with better outcomes – RANO group

2. Post-op MRI within 48 hours

3. Adjuvant treatment by fitness

Young, fit patient

  • STUPP protocol: 60Gy/30# with 75mg/m2 7 days/week then adj TMZ 150mg/m2 5 days per month for 6 months
    • TMZ improved mOS from 12 to 15mo. 2 yr OS 30%
    • MGMT promoter hypermethylation is prognostic and predictive of response to TMZ, in methylated improves mOS from 15 to 22mo
  • DE is superior to RT alone if not given with TMZ
  • PFS benefit with dose escalation using protons – NRG-BN001

Elderly >70yo, unfit patient

  • 40Gy/15# was not different to 60Gy/30# when delivered without chemo – Canadian trial Roa et al
    • 45% died by 6mo, more patients completed 40Gy/15#
  • 25Gy/5# RT alone was non-inferior to 40Gy/15# RT alone in old/frail pts ECOG >/=2 – IAEA Roa et al
    • Median OS 7 mo
  • Addition of concurrent and adjuvant TMZ remains beneficial with 40Gy/15# - mOS 9 vs 7 months – EORTC Perry et al
  • TMZ alone is non-inferior to 60Gy/30# RT alone, especially if MGMT methylated, and may in fact improve mOS - NOA-08, Nordic trial

Tumour treating fields:

  • Alternating electric field designed to disrupt the polarisation within a cell that occurs during mitoses (spindle formation) thereby prohibiting cell division
  • Cap with electrodes worn over shaved head for 18hrs per day continuously for as long as tolerated
  • Causes skin dermatitis
  • Only other treatment shown to improve mOS – 20 months vs 16 months – Stupp et al
  • Can be used with maintenance TMZ

IORT/ dose-escalation:

  • 30Gy intra-op tile BT in addition to std chemoRT improves OS in resectable newly diagnosed GBM (ROADS, ph III)

Mgmt - recurrence

  • Re-resection is associated with favourable outcomes if post-op volumes of <1cm3 achievable – RANO group
  • Re-irradiation with 35Gy/10# is safe and feasible, ideally >6mo interval
  • Bevacizumab improves PFS when given with or without RT. Given until toxicity or progression.
  • Bev + RT > bev alone for PFS
    • Bev can cause pseudoresponse on MRI by reducing tumour enhancement so important to assess on T2/flair
  • Lomustine
  • Clinical trial
  • Survival 6-12mo

EBRT
Dose: 60Gy/30#, VMAT, daily treatment with BD compensation. Concurrent TMZ 75mg/m2 daily

  • ESTRO-ACROP uses 1 dose level, RTOG uses 2 dose levels 46Gy to T2/Flair, 60Gy to cavity with 2cm CTV margin for both
  • Low level evidence suggests TTT over 7 weeks worsens outcomes

Pre-sim: scar healed, discuss at MDT, aim to commence 2-4 weeks post-op

Sim: supine, arms down, TPS for head, fuse pre and post op MRI T1 + gad and T2/Flair

Volumes (ESTRO-ACROP):

GTV: residual tumour as seen on T1+gad enhancement or T2/flair change suspicious for tumour rather than oedema + entire surgical cavity

CTV: GTV + 1.5cm, cropped to bone and falx if uninvolved + T2/flair change that is not clearly oedema

  • 1.5cm used as 80% of recurrences occur within 2cm from primary

PTV: +5mm

Daily CBCT with bone match

OARS (EQD2):

Brainstem:

Max dose - can accept up to 59Gy to small volumes <10cc

PRV Dmax <60Gy

Optic nerve and chiasm:

Dmax up to 60Gy has up to 7% risk of radiation induced optic neuropathy

Dmax >60Gy has up to 20% risk of RION

PRV Dmax <60Gy

Meningioma

Epi: I: rare, 1% of population · Pathology · Mgmt – WHO G1 · EBRT · Grade 3 · ESTRO-ACROP guidelines of BoS tumours

Clinic page: Benign and functional CNS

Epi: I: rare, 1% of population

A: 65yo

G: F>M 2:1, growth during pregnancy

R: nil

RF:

E: prior cranial RT, obesity, breast ca assoc

I: nil

G: NF2, gorlin syndrome, MEN1, female

I: nil

MRI:

  • Low grade is well encapsulated
  • High grade has brain invasion, bone invasion, oedema
  • Dural tail
  • Hyperostosis (esp sphenoid wing)
  • T1 isointense, T2/flair hyperintense
  • Homogenous contrast enhancement

Other notes:

  • DOTATATE PET can help distinguish between mets, lymphoma and meningioma when MRI equivocal

5 yr recurrence risk after GTR w/o RT

  • Grade 1: 10%
  • Grade 2: 30%
  • Grade 3: 60%
  • Recurrent disease and STR are independent poor prognostic factors (RTOG 0539)

10 yr PFS/OS (RTOG 0539):

  • Low risk (G1): 85%/95%
  • Intermediate risk (G2 GTR or recurrent G1 with adj RT): 70%/85%
  • High risk (G3, STR or recurrent G2 with adj RT): 40%/50%

Pathology

Grade 1:

  • Secretory, fibroblastic, psammomatous, meningothelial
  • Cellular whorls, psammoma bodies, calcifications
  • Mitoses <4 per 10 hpf, no grade 2 criteria

Grade 2:

  • Diagnoses needs 1 of:
  • Clear cell or chordoid histo
  • Mitoses 4-19 per 10 hpf
  • Unequivocal brain invasion
  • ≥ 3 atypical features: hypercellular, high N:C ratio, prominent nucleoli, sheet like growth, spontaneous necrosis (HNNSS)

Grade 3:

  • Anaplastic
  • Micro: multifocal necrosis, loss of usual growth pattern
  • Diagnosis needs 1 of:
  • Mitoses ≥ 20 per 10 hpf
  • OR TERTp mut or CDKN2A/B HD
  • Or frank anaplasia: carcinomatous, melanomatous, sarcomatous

IHC: Vimentin, EMA, SSTR2A, PR, AR

Molecular: NF2 loss

Simpson’s grading

0: GTR with resection of dural tail and bone plus stripping of 2-4cm dura

1: GTR with resection of dural tail

2: GTR with coagulation of dural attachment

3: GTR but dural tail left

4: STR

5: Biopsy

Mgmt – WHO G1

Asymptomatic, no high grade features– surveillance

Symptomatic or high grade features → maximal safe resection

  • brain or bone invasion
  • oedema
  • near critical structure – cavernous sinus, optic pathway, brainstem
  • growth >2mm per year
  • >3cm size
  • Recurrent

If GTR → observation

  • PFS 90% at 10 yrs (RTOG 0539)

If STR → Salvage RT 50.4Gy/28#

  • LR 30% at 10 years (RTOG 0539)
  • RT reduces PFS, no OS benefit, therefore either acceptable – Souver et al

WHO G2

Maximal safe resection

  • If GTR → surveillance
    • ROAM trial ongoing regarding adjuvant RT
  • If STR → adjuvant RT 54Gy/30# to reduce LRR – Lee et al

WHO G3

Maximal safe resection

All get adjuvant RT: 60Gy/30#

  • Reduces LRR from 50% to 8% - Cho et al
  • may improve OS – Cleveland data
  • Consider DE to 66Gy for 5 yr PFS benefit of 25% but no OS (includes recurrent grade 2 and G2 STR) – FIRESTORM MA

Inoperable:

By location (BOS invasion):

  • cavernous sinus
  • optic nerve or chiasm meningioma
  • petroclival
  • foramen magnum invasion

By growth pattern:

  • Diffuse / en plaque meningioma – sheet like growth along dura, often involves sphenoid wing and medial structures (orbit, cavernous sinus) or skull base
    • Difficult to achieve meaningful resection

Definitive RT:

  • 60Gy/30# for G2-3, 54Gy/30# for G1
  • SRS 12-14Gy if <3cm and >3mm from critical OARs, 25Gy/5#

Recurrence post RT

  • Observation if appears low grade and asymptomatic
  • Resection
  • Re-irradiation: 12-14Gy/1# SRS if away from critical structures and <3cm
  • Lutetium

EBRT

Dose: G1: 50.4Gy/28#, G2 54-60Gy/30#, G3 60Gy/54Gy/30#

Pre-sim: scar healed, aim to commence 4-6 weeks post-op, fertility sparing, neurocognitive testing, audiometry, vision assessment, pituitary panel

Sim: supine, arms down, TPS head, fuse pre and post-op MRI +/- DOTATATE PET

Volumes:

Grade 1:

GTV: residual tumour

  • Do not include oedema, T2/flair changes

CTV: not required in G1 meningioma, can consider 3mm margin if rapid growing tumour

PTV: +3mm

Grade 2:

GTV: residual tumour + surgical cavity

  • Do not include oedema, T2/flair changes

CTV: +10mm, limit expansion into brain if no brain invasion

PTV 5400: +5mm

Grade 3

GTV: residual tumour + surgical cavity

  • Include suspicious T2/flair changes

CTV1/6000: 10mm

CTV2/54000: +20mm

  • Crop CTV to uninvolved tissue

PTV6000 & 5400: +5mm for both

OARs:

Globe Mean dose <30Gy, Dmax <50Gy

Lens Dmax <6Gy

Optic nerve/chiasm Dmax <54Gy

Lacrimal gland Dmean <30Gy

Pituitary Dmean <45Gy

Brainstem Dmax <54Gy

Cord Dmax <45Gy

Cochlea Dmean <45Gy

Notes:

  • Optic nerve, chiasm and brainstem PRV should be created
  • Gaze fixation required for optic nerve sheath meningioma to reduce movement
  • Consider DOTATATE PET after resection to distinguish tumour from post-op change
  • From ESTRO guidelines:
  • Only include clearly abnormal/thickened dural tail that is clearly invaded
  • For en-plaque meningioma all dural thickening is covered hyperostotic bone should be included
  • Craniotomy flap/drill holes are included if adjacent/involved/violated barrier, not routinely

Germ cell

Epidemiology · Differential diagnosis · Management · EBRT - NGGCT · EBRT – GGCT

Clinic page: Paediatric CNS

Epidemiology

I: rare <1% of brain cancers

A: 10-20yo

G: M

R: East Asian

RF:

I: nil

A: nil

G: Rb1, Klinefelter syndrome (mediastinal GCT and CNS)

R:nil

Natural Hx:

  • Arise from pineal gland or suprasellar region
  • Bifocal (both regions) is pathognomic for GCT and treated as localised dx
  • Tendency to spread through CSF → drop mets

Sx:

  • Parinauds syndrome from dorsal midbrain injury: upward gaze palsy, convergence retraction nystagmus, light-near dissociation (pupil hyporeflexia)
  • Suprasellar: diabetes insipidus (↓ ADH from hypothalamus – polyuria, polydipsia, hypernatremia), hypopituitarism, bitemporal hemianopia
  • Obstructive hydrocephalus and raised ICP: papilloedema, early morning headaches, n/v

Outcomes:

  • Germinoma 5 yr OS: 90%
  • NGGCT 5 yr OS: 60%+
  • NGGCT often relapses in spine

Differential diagnosis

  • Pineal: GCT, pineoblastoma, pineocytoma, teratoma, glioma
  • Suprasellar: GCT, craniopharyngioma, PitNET, teratoma

Path:

Germinoma: 60%

  • Polygonal cells, clear cytoplasm, prominent nucleoli
  • Chemo and radiosensitive
  • Best prognosis
  • cKITm
  • 10-15% can have mildly raised b-HCG (syncytiotrophoblastic cells)

Non-germinoma GCT: 40%

Yolk sac tumour

  • Raised AFP, Schiller duval bodies
  • Aggressive

Choriocarcinoma

  • Cytotrophoblasts and synctiotrophoblasts
  • Very raised b-hCG
  • Aggressive

Embryonal tumour

  • Primitive epithelial tumour with necrosis, atypia, mitoses
  • Can have elevated b-hCG

Teratoma

  • Mature (benign) or immature (malignant)
  • Tissues from ectoderm, mesoderm and endoderm
  • Immature can have raised mild raised b-HCG

IHC:

  • GGCT: PLAP, OCT3/4, SALL4, cKIT
  • NGGCT: OCT3/4, SALL4
  • Negative: GFAP, S100, vimentin

Management

  • MRI brain and whole spine
  • All need CSF x 2 with cytology and tumour marker testing
    • CSF tumour markers are more sensitive than serum
  • Biopsy avoided if typical presentation – pineal/suprasellar location and tumour markers present
  • May have ‘growing teratoma’ syndrome if enlarging mass post treatment

Germinoma:

1. Induction chemotherapy: etoposide/carboplatin & ifosfamide/etoposide x 4 cycles

2. Assess response on MRI +/- second look surgery if residual mass

3. Sequential WVRT 24Gy + 16Gy boost to tumour in 1.6Gy/# if no CR

  • Dose reduction if CR: 18Gy + 12Gy boost can be considered
  • Omission of tumour bed in single site disease (ie not bifocal) with CR post chemo is acceptable (European guidelines)
  • CSI 24Gy if CSF +
  • Expect cure rates >90%

Non germinoma GCT:

1. Induction chemotherapy: etoposide/platinum & ifosfamide/etoposide x 4-6 cycles

2. Response assessment +/- second look surgery if residual mass

  • CR → Adj RT: CSI 36Gy + 54Gy TD boost to tumour in 1.8Gy/#
  • Omitting CSI increases spinal relapse
  • Second look surgery:
    • If no viable tumour → proceed as per CR
    • If viable tumour → escalate dose boost to 60Gy
    • If mature teratoma → resect (chemoradioresistant)

Growing teratoma syndrome:

  • Growing mass with normalisation of tumour markers with mature teratoma on pathology

Mature teratoma:

  • GTR → no adjuvant treatment
  • STR → aim re-resection or consider 54Gy RT

EBRT - NGGCT

Dose: 36Gy/20# CSI + 18Gy/10# sequential tumour boost, VMAT, daily treatment, no BD compensation

Pre-sim: MDT discussion, fertility sparing, pituitary panel (FSH, LH, IGF-1, prolactin, TFT, ACTH, early morning cortisol), tumour markers, baseline neurocognitive, audiometry, visual testing

Aim to commence 4 weeks post chemo.

Sim: supine, arms down, TPS head shell and vac bag, fuse pre and post chemo MRI

Volumes

CSI:

CTV: entire brain including posterior 2/3rs of orbit, crista gali, cribriform plate + entire spinal canal + proximal 1cm exit nerve roots to S2 (filum terminale)

PTV 3000: +5mm

Tumour boost:

GTV: pre-chemo disease extent, cropped to uninvolved normal structures

CTV: +10mm

PTV 5000: +3mm

Field based technique:

Opposed lateral whole brain field, shielding as above + oropharynx. Inferior down to C4.

2x PA fields for whole spine from C4 to S2 with collimator rotation and couch kick to match divergence of brain field. Junction of fields lies in VB anterior to cord.

Feather junction by 5mm every 5 #

Arises from ependymal cells lining ventricles and central canal

MRI:

-T1 hypointense, heterogenous contrast enhancement

-T2 hyperintense

Outcomes:

5 yr OS:

- 80% with GTR + adj RT in low grade

- 60% with STR + adj RT in G2

- 50% G3

- 30% with STR alone

Molecular classification

Supratentorial:

  • ZFTA (RELA) fusion – unfavourable. 10 yr 60%.
  • YAP1 mutation – favourable. 10 yr 95%

Posterior Fossa – mostly kids

  • Group A- unfavourable, infants. 10 yr OS 60%
  • Group B- intermediate. 10 yr 90%.

These 2 typically occur in kids <10yo

Spinal – mostly adults

  • SP-EPN (NF-2) – favourable, 10 yr 95%
  • SP-MYCN amp – very unfavourable, 10 yr 40%

These typically occur in adults

WHO Grading:

  • Grade 1 ~ 15%: subependymal
  • 4th ventricle
  • Adults > kids
  • Grade 2 ~ 70%: classic, myxopapillary
    • PF and spinal cord
    • Kids > adults
  • Grade 3 ~ 15%: anaplastic
    • Intracranial
    • Kids > adults

Extent of resection is more important prognostic factor than grade

Age <3yo is a poor prognostic factor

Histology: perivascular and true ependymal rosettes

IHC: GFAP, EMA dot like pattern

Management

1. Maximal safe resection

Most important prognostic factor

All need LP 2-3 weeks post-op to assess for CSF dissemination

  • If CSF +ve or radiological CSF spread → CSI

Grade 1:

GTR → observe

STR → salvage RT:

  • 45Gy/25# to cord or 50.4Gy/28# to cauda equina or 54Gy/30# to intracranial

Grade 2:

Intracranial → observe or adj RT: STR 59.4Gy/33#, GTR observe

  • Consider delaying RT in children <3yrs with chemotherapy (cyclophosphamide, vincristine, cisplatin, etoposide) though this may compromise survival

Spinal → STR → adj RT: 45Gy/25# to cord or 50.4Gy/28# cauda equina

Grade 3

Intracranial → adj RT for all: 60Gy/30#

Spinal → adj RT for all: 45Gy/25# to cord or 50.4Gy/28# to cauda equina

If CSF dissemination:

  • 36Gy CSI if >3yo
  • Boost gross disease to 45Gy
  • CSF dissemination risk is lower than medulloblastoma

EBRT – spinal cord

Dose: 45Gy/25# daily, VMAT, no BD compensation

PTV: +5mm

Epidemiology

I: rare

A: 60

G: F
R: nil

RF:

E: prv RT

I: nil

G: NF2 – bilateral, younger ~30, NF1

I: nil

Koos grade:

1: within internal auditory canal

2: outside of auditory canal

3: contacting brainstem

4: compressing brainstem

Pathology

  • Benign condition
  • Proliferation of schwann cells of vestibular portion of CN VIII
  • Antoni A: hypercellular spindle cells in fasicles, verocay bodies
  • Antoni B: hypocellular with schwann cells in myxoid stroma
  • IHC: S100, SOX10

Presentation:

  • CP angle tumour
  • Unilateral vestibulocochlear nerve dysfunction – hearing loss, tinnitus, ataxia, vertigo
    • Gradual over years
  • Brainstem compression
    • Lower CN dysfunction – facial numbness, weakness dysarthria, dysphagia, hoarse voice, aspiration
  • Ataxia, raised ICP

Serviceable hearing

  • Formal audiometry to test dB level heard and speech discrimination
  • Graded on Gardener-Robertson grade

Management

Grade 1-2 asymptomatic:

  • Observe if small, asymptomatic with q6-12 monthly MRI and audiometry
  • If growing >2mm/year or reduction in audiometry then → maximal safe resection or SRS
  • If serviceable hearing then surveillance preferred to avoid treatment related hearing loss

Koos Grade 1-2:

  • Serviceable hearing → delay treatment
  • Unserviceable hearing → treatment will not reverse hearing loss, but still treat if other symptoms
    • Surgery preferred for young pts to avoid late tox
  • 13Gy/1# for <3cm

Koos G3-4:

  • Treat with maximal safe resection (preferred)
  • Alternative: fractionated RT (45Gy/25# or 50.4Gy/28#) to preserve brainstem function or 25Gy/5# fractionated SRS
  • Less response to RT than surgery

Bilateral:

  • Treat 1 at a time
  • bevacizumab – reduces volume by up to 50%

Outcomes:

RT:

  • Hearing preservation 50-70% at 5 yrs, declines over time
  • Other CN preservation >90%
  • Surgery hearing preservation variable 40-70%
  • 40% will shrink, 50% remain stable, up to 30% pseudoprogression (6-36mo), <10% progression

Dose: 13Gy/1# for small lesion <3cm and Koos grade 1-2, or 25Gy/5# for small lesion abutting brainstem

50.4Gy/28# for large lesion >3cm or Koos grade 3-4

PTV D100 >95%

Supine, arms down by side, thermoplastic head shell

Clinic page: Benign and functional CNS

Epidemiology

I: 10% of intracranial tumours

A: 30-60yo

G: Prolactinoma – F, others M

R: nil

RF:

E: prior cranial RT

I: nil

G: MEN1, AIP mutation

I: nil

Size:

Picoadenoma <3mm

Microadenoma <10mm

Macroadenoma >10mm

Giant >40mm

Sx:

  • Bitemporal hemianopia, headaches
  • Functioning → hormonal sx

Stalk effect:

  • Pituitary stalk compression → interrupts hypothalamic dopamine delivery to lactotrophs → causing mild–moderate hyperprolactinaemia
  • Helps distinguish a non-functioning macroadenoma with stalk effect from a true prolactinoma, where prolactin is much higher
  • Other: DI from lack of ADH

Classification

Functioning ~ 65%

  • Prolactinoma most common – galactorrhea, amenorrhea, infertlity
  • Somatotroph (GH, IGF-1) – acromegaly, macroglossia, OSA, HTN, DM
  • Corticotroph – Cushing disease, abnormal dex suppression test, high 24hr urinary cortisol
  • Thyrotroph

Non-functioning ~35%

  • Mass effect, hypopituitarism

Pathology

IHC: synaptophysin, chromogranin, Tpit Aggressive features:

  • increasing Ki67 >3%
  • mitotic activity
  • invasion to cavernous sinus or bone
  • rapid growth

Pituitary carcinoma – rare

  • defined by CSF or systemic mets

IHC: T-pit, pit1

KNOSP grading:

-cavernous sinus invasion, used to determine resectability

Management

All:

  • formal visual field testing
  • baseline pituitary panel: prolactin, IGF-1, dex suppression test for ACTH, early morning cortisol, 24 hour urinary cortisol, TFT, FSH/LH

Prolactinoma: dopamine agonists (cabergoline) are able to reduce functioning and shrink the tumour over weeks-short months

All other functioning – resect or RT

If ongoing functional:

  • ACTH: ketaconazole (high) or hydrocortisone (low)

Surgery: trans-sphenoidal resection

RT:

  • Indications:
    • Symptomatic gross residual disease post-op
    • Symptomatic recurrent disease
    • Symptomatic inoperable tumour (KNOSP grade)
    • Cavernous sinus residual
    • Aggressive pathology/high Ki67
    • compression of CN/optics that is unresectable
  • SRS: 18-20Gy/1# if small, >3mm from optic structures
  • 50.4Gy-54Gy/28-30# for others. Higher dose for hypersecreting
  • GTV → +5mm to CTV if aggressive tumour → +3mm to PTV
  • Late tox:
    • Can cause hypopituitarism in 30-50%, increases over time
    • Optic neuropathy
    • Neurocognitive effects
    • 2nd malignancy
  • >90% LC with RT, some may shrink over years, hormonal normalisation can take up to 5 years
