Oesophagus

Adenocarcinoma has moved towards perioperative FLOT (ESOPEC); CROSS remains the standard for SCC and for patients unfit for FLOT. Use 50 Gy for definitive chemoRT: dose escalation has not helped.

  • Siewert: I = 1–5 cm above GOJ; II = 1 cm above to 2 cm below; III = 2–5 cm below (treated as gastric).
  • Node risk: T1a 7% · T1b (submucosa) ~20% · T2 ~40%. No serosa, so early spread.
  • AJCC 8: location by epicentre; coeliac nodes are regional for lower/GOJ tumours.
  • 5-y OS: stage I 70–80%, roughly halving with each stage; pCR at any stage behaves like stage I.

Work-up

  • Gastroscopy + biopsy; EUS ± FNA for T/N (T ~90%, N 80–85% sensitivity).
  • CT chest/abdomen ± pelvis, then PET-CT (finds M1 in ~15%; N sensitivity ~60%).
  • Bronchoscopy if at or above carina (exclude TOF). Staging laparoscopy for GOJ/Siewert II–III may upstage 10–15%.
  • Biomarkers: HER2 (10% oesophageal, up to 33% GOJ), PD-L1 CPS, MMR/MSI.
  • Dietitian early; consider feeding tube if dysphagia and weight loss. Smoking cessation.

Organ preservation

  • SANO: cCR after 41.4/23 nCRT → active surveillance non-inferior to surgery (2-y OS 74% vs 71%). About half avoided surgery; durable cCR in only ~10% overall.
  • Response assessment at 6 and 12 wk with endoscopy + bite-on-bite biopsies, EUS-FNA, PET-CT.
  • SCC is more radiosensitive (CROSS pCR 49% SCC vs 23% AC), so organ preservation is more plausible.
  • FFCD 9102: in responders, more chemoRT was as good as surgery for OS with lower 3-mo mortality (9% → 1%), at the cost of more local failure and stents.

Palliation of dysphagia

  • RT or chemoRT preferred over stent for durable relief: 70–90% respond, 50–70% stay dysphagia-free until death; transient worsening in ~30%.
  • 20/5 vs 30/10 + 12/1 BT: persistent/recurrent dysphagia 64% → 42%; consider higher dose if longer life expectancy.
  • Brachytherapy gives more durable relief and fewer complications than stenting; BT meta: G3–4 fistula 8%, stenosis 12%.
  • ROCS: adding RT to stent did not reduce dysphagia deterioration (~47%) but delayed bleeding.
  • Stent in situ before radical treatment: consider induction chemo then remove the stent (stent + RT morbidity).

Gastric

Perioperative FLOT is the standard; RT is now for incomplete surgery, margin-positive disease, patients unfit for perioperative chemo, and palliation (ASTRO 2025).

Doses and volumes

  • Pre- or postop: 45/25 (strong). Boost 5.4–9 Gy for R2/gross residual. Definitive: 45/25 CTV + 54–59.4 Gy GTV.
  • Preop CTV: whole stomach, perigastric extension, ≥1 cm proximal oesophagus (more for cardia/GOJ) and regional nodes (coeliac, common hepatic, splenic, left gastric, hepatoduodenal, peripancreatic, SMA, regional para-aortic).
  • Postop CTV: tumour bed, anastomoses, duodenal stump, 2 cm beyond margins, regional nodes. Include gastric remnant only if margin positive. Proximal third: add paraoesophageal and hepatogastric; middle/distal: supra- and infrapyloric.
  • Coverage: preop PTV D95 ≥95%, D2cc ≤107%; postop PTV D100 ≥95%.
  • Fast 2 h before sim and treatment. 4DCT/motion management with IMRT; daily IGRT.
  • D2 dissection: recurrences still locoregional as often as distant; >¾ of regional relapses outside the D2 field (stations 16a2, 8, 12, 16b1, 9).

Systemic therapy pointers

  • FLOT ×4 → surgery → FLOT ×4 (5-FU 2.6 g/m² CI, leucovorin, oxaliplatin, docetaxel).
  • Metastatic HER2-negative, CPS ≥5: nivolumab + chemo. HER2+: trastuzumab + pembrolizumab + chemo; trastuzumab deruxtecan after progression. Second line: ramucirumab + paclitaxel.
  • Test HER2 (~20% gastric, higher in intestinal type), PD-L1, MMR.

Oesophagus planning

Simulation and IGRT

  • Fast ≥2 h (empty stomach), arms up on wingboard/vac-lok, IV contrast, 4DCT.
  • Daily CBCT (or kV daily + CBCT days 1–3 then weekly). For GOJ, match on the gastrohepatic region.
  • Motion (fiducial study): CC peak-to-peak ~9 mm; required PTV with CTV-based match ~7 mm LR, 8 mm AP, 11 mm CC.

Target volumes

  • CTVp: GTVp + 3–4 cm CC (≤2 cm into involved stomach), 1 cm radial (0.5 cm into heart/liver). Submucosal spread: 3 cm covers 94% of SCC; AC spreads further distally (5 cm covers 94%).
  • CTVn: GTVn + 0.5–1.5 cm.
  • PTV: CTV + 0.5–1.0 cm.
  • ENI by site: cervical → SCV ± higher neck; upper third → bilateral SCV + mediastinal; middle → paraoesophageal + mediastinal; distal/GOJ → paraoesophageal, lesser curve, coeliac; consider splenic for Siewert II–III.
  • PROTECT (involved-field) approach: GTVp + 3 cm CC with node stations along that length, GTVn + 1 cm, no extensive elective nodes.
  • Optional cone-down for small, non-operative tumours: 45/25 elective (4 cm CC) and 50/25 primary (2 cm CC) as SIB, then 54–60 Gy sequential to 1 cm.

Constraints (conventional)

OrganGoal
LungV20 <20%, V30 <15%, V10 <40%, V5 <50–60%, MLD <15 Gy
HeartV30 <20–30%, mean <20 Gy where possible (15–26 Gy used); V40 <30%, V50 <20%

Each 10% rise in heart V30 ≈ 24% higher cardiac event risk. Protons cut heart V5 87→42% and 2-y cardiac events 18→11% in one series. 3D vs IMRT: IMRT lowers heart high dose but spreads low lung dose.

Follow-up and failure patterns

  • H&P q3–6 mo ×2 y, q6–12 mo for 3 y, then annually. If organ-preserving: gastroscopy q3–6 mo ×2 y, q6 mo in year 3.
  • Most relapses within 2 y. After definitive 50.4 Gy (MDACC), local failure 50%, 90% within GTV; risks: T3+, >8 cm, poor PET response.
  • After CROSS, only ~1 in 8 fail locoregionally; distant failure dominates.
  • Strictures 12–30% after RT; balloon dilatation works in 80–90%.

Landmark trials

Oesophagus

RTOG 8501 (1992; 5 y 1999)Phase III · n=129, T1–3 N0–1, 86% SCC · 64 Gy RT alone vs 50 Gy + cisplatin/5-FU5-y OS 0 → 27%; MS 9 → 14 mo. RT alone is palliative. Nearly half had G3 toxicity with chemoRT.
INT 0123 (2002)Phase III · n=236, 85% SCC · 50.4 vs 64.8 Gy with cisplatin/5-FUNo gain from 64.8 Gy (2-y OS ~35%, LRF ~50%); treatment deaths 2 → 10%.
ARTDECO (2021)Phase III · n=260, 60% SCC · 50.4/28 ± SIB to 61.6 Gy, carbo/paclitaxel3-y local PFS 71 → 73% (NS); OS ~40%; G5 5 → 10%.
CONCORDE / PRODIGE 26 (ASTRO 2021)Phase II/III · n=217 · FOLFOX + 50 vs 66 GyNo OS benefit from dose escalation.
CROSS (2012; 10 y 2021)Phase III · n=363 T1N1 or T2–3N0–1, 75% AC · 41.4/23 + carbo/paclitaxel → surgery vs surgeryR0 69 → 92%; pCR 29% (SCC 49%, AC 23%). MS 24 → 49 mo; 10-y OS 25 → 38%. SCC MS 21 → 82 mo.
NEOCRTEC5010 (2018; long-term 2021)Phase III · n=451 SCC, N+ or T4N0 · nCRT → surgery vs surgerypCR 43%; MS 66 → 100 mo; 4-y recurrence 46 → 34%.
ESOPEC (2025)Phase III · n=438 resectable oesophageal/GOJ AC (~80% cT3–4, ~80% N+) · CROSS vs perioperative FLOT ×4 + ×4MS 37 → 66 mo (HR 0.70); 3-y OS 51 → 57%, 5-y 39 → 51%; 3-y PFS 35 → 52%. Only 68% completed CROSS in full.
NEO-AEGIS (2023)Phase III · n=377 AC · CROSS vs perioperative MAGIC/FLOT (only 27 got FLOT)No OS difference; closed early for futility.
SANO (2025)Phase III NI · n=309 with cCR after nCRT · active surveillance vs surgery2-y OS 74% vs 71% (non-inferior); about half avoided surgery.
CheckMate 577 (2021)Phase III · n=794 R0 with residual disease after nCRT (70% AC) · ± nivolumab 1 yMedian DFS 11 → 22 mo (HR 0.69); weaker in AC (HR 0.75) than SCC (HR 0.61).
FFCD 9102 (2007)Phase III · n=444 T3N0–1, 90% SCC · responders to chemoRT → surgery vs more chemoRTSimilar OS; 3-mo mortality 9% (surgery) vs 1%; 2-y local failure 34% vs 43%; stent need 5% vs 32%.
ESO-Shanghai 1 (2019; 10 y ASTRO 2026)Phase III · ESCC definitive 61.2/34 · cisplatin/5-FU vs paclitaxel/5-FUEquivalent at 10 y; choose by toxicity. Locoregional-only failure 27%.
ROCS (2021)Phase III · n=220 incurable, stent ± palliative RTRT did not reduce dysphagia deterioration (~47%) but delayed bleeding.
KEYNOTE-975 (ESMO 2026)Phase III · n=703 unresectable oesophageal/GOJ · definitive chemoRT (50–60 Gy) ± pembrolizumabCPS ≥10: median EFS 14.7 vs 15.2 mo (HR 0.97); OS HR 1.00. No benefit from adding pembrolizumab. (check)
Stahl (2005)Phase III · n=172 locally advanced SCC · induction chemo → chemoRT 40 Gy + surgery vs chemoRT ≥65 Gy aloneOS equivalent; 2-y local PFS 41 → 64% with surgery; treatment-related mortality 3.5 → 12.8%. (check)
JCOG0508 (2019)Phase II single arm · n=176 cT1b N0 SCC · endoscopic resection, then observation or chemoRT 41.4 Gy by pathology3-y OS about 91% in pT1b margin-negative patients given prophylactic chemoRT. (check)
TROG 03.01 (2018)Phase III · n=220 advanced, dysphagia · RT 35/15 or 30/10 ± cisplatin–5FUDysphagia response 35 → 45% (NS); G3–4 toxicity 16 → 36%; MS about 6.7 vs 6.9 mo. RT alone is the palliative standard. (check)
SIREC (2004)Phase III · n=209 inoperable, dysphagia · brachytherapy 12 Gy ×1 vs metal stentStent relieved dysphagia faster; brachytherapy gave better long-term relief and fewer complications (about 13 vs 25%). (check)
KEYNOTE-590 (2021)Phase III · n=749 advanced oesophageal/GOJ, first line · cisplatin–5FU ± pembrolizumabMS 9.8 → 12.4 mo (HR 0.73); SCC CPS ≥10 8.8 → 13.9 mo. (check)

Oesophagus

From the exam summary.

Epidemiology

I: SCC ↓, more common globally

adenoCa ↑, more common in Western world

A: 65

G: M

R: SCC oesophageal ca Asian belt – Turkey, China, central Asia, Mongolia. Adenoca - Western

RF

SCC

  • A -achalasia (x30 risk)
  • B- betel nut chewing, bad diet
  • C - cigarette smoking
  • D - drinking scalding hot liquids
  • E - ETOH
  • F- Familial:
  • Tylosis (palmoplantar hyperkeratosis), Plummer-vinson, Fanconi anaemia

AdenoCa

  • GORD
  • Obesity
  • Barrett’s oesophagus
  • Smoking (weaker assoc than SCC)
  • Familial Barrett’s syndrome

Staging

T1a: into lamina propria or into muscularis mucosa

T1b: into submucosa

T2: into muscularis propria

T3: into adventitia

T4: into adjacent structures

N1: ≤ 2 locoregional

N2: 3-6 nodes

N3: 7+ nodes

M1: DM

  • SCF included as regional however in a distant oesophageal often treated as M1
  • PA nodes beyond celiac axis are treated as M1

Siewert classification for GOJ tumours

1: -5 to -1cm from cardia

2: -1 to +2cm from cardia

3: +2 to +5cm from cardia

Anatomy

  • 40cm long, divided into thirds
  • 15-20cm: cervical
  • 20-30cm: upper & mid
  • 30-40cm: distal

Path

SCC

  • Exophytic, flat or ulcerated
  • More infiltrative and circumferential
  • Keratin pearls, whorls, intercellular bridges
  • Basaloid – aggressive
  • Spindle, verrucous – less aggressive
  • p40, p63, AE1/AE3, CK5/6

AdenoCa

  • B/g of Barrett’s oesophagus, goblet cells, gland formation, mucin production
  • CK7, AE1/AE3, MUC 1/2 Alcian blue

Barrett’s oesophagus

  • Chronic irritation of oesophageal lining (reflux) → Intestinal metaplasia from squamous cells to columnar cells extending from the Z line → progress to dysplasia → carcinoma
  • 0.5% annual ca risk if metaplasia
  • 5-20% annual ca risk of dysplasia

Response to NA Rx

  • Becker-Mandard grading system – lower score = better response
  • Prescence of fibrosis, necrosis, acellular tissue, granulation inflammatory response
  • Residual invasive or in-situ disease

Pre-treatment considerations

  • Dietitian, SP review
  • Definitive feeding strategy: NGT, JEJ tube, RIG/PEG
  • Gastrostomy avoided if surgery planned
  • Pulmonary function testing

Management

T1aN0

  • Endoscopic mucosal resection, endoscopic submucosal dissection
  • Minimally invasive procedure
  • If upstaged: T1b, poorly differentiated, LVSI then requires adjuvant chemoRT: 50Gy/25# with concurrent carboplatin/paclitaxel

T1b-T2N0

  • Upfront esophagectomy + locoregional nodal dissection
  • Adjuvant chemoRT 50Gy/25# with carboplatin/paclitaxel if: T3, LVSI, R1, N+

Surgical approaches

  • Stomach is used as conduit in all
  • Trans-thoracic approach
  • Ivor Lewis – anastomosis distally in lower thorax
  • Mckeown – anastomosis more proximally in cervical region
  • Trans-hiatal approach
  • High morbidity and mortality ~ 10%
  • Minimally invasive and robotic approaches have been shown to reduce morbidity
  • CI to surgery: T4b (heart, great vessels, surrounding organs), bulky multistation LN, medically unfit

Mid-distal SCC

  • CROSS protocol: 41.4Gy/23# with concurrent carboplatin/paclitaxel followed by oesophagectomy 6-8 weeks post
    • NA CRT Improves median OS from 24 to 48 months, SCC>adenoCa
  • Definitive chemoradiotherapy: 50Gy/25# with cis/5FU in week 1 and week 5 (RTOG 8501)
  • If unfit for chemo 50Gy/25# + 14Gy/7# tumour boost RT alone (RTOG 8501)
  • No role for DE (ART-DECO, Intergroup, <5% LRR from CROSS)
  • No role for ENI (Lyu et al – ENI increases pneumonitis and oesophagitis without PFS or OS benefit in thoracic SCC)

Mid-distal AdenoCa

  • Peri-operative q2weekly x4 FLOT (5FU, leucovorin, oxali, docetaxol) chemotherapy (ESOPEC, improves median OS from 37 to 66 months)
  • If unfit for FLOT → CROSS protocol as above
    • pCR for adenoCa 20% vs 40% for SCC
  • FLOT chemotherapy more toxic than CROSS protocol
  • The addition of Durvalumab to FLOT improves EFS (MATTERHORN)

Cervical oesophageal ca

  • <5cm from cricopharyngeus
  • Often managed as a H&N treatment: 66Gy/33# with carboplatin and paclitaxel or 70Gy/35# with cisplatin
  • If treating as H&N then cover elective nodal regions: level 3/4, SCF to 56Gy
  • Surgical resection involves cervical oesophagectomy + laryngopharyngectomy
  • Significant morbidity and mortality

Immunotherapy

  • Adjuvant Nivolumab for 12mo if no pCR improves DFS for SCC and adenoCa (CHECKMATE 577)
  • Can be considered in all MMRd cases as per NCCN

Organ Preservation

  • Close surveillance with salvage oesphagectomy
  • Consider DE to 54Gy/27# - no high level evidence
  • Emerging management option from SANO ph II trial however:
    • Needs cCR on PET and endoscopy 12 weeks post treatment
    • Short term 2 year OS non-inferior
    • 50% need oesophagectomy at 6 mo
    • Trend towards increase DM with surveillance
  • Ongoing trials – SANO 3, ESOTRATE

Metastatic

  • Carboplatin, paclitaxel
  • Test for HER-2, PDL1 and MMR status to add in targeted and IO options
  • Her2 therapy
  • Immunotherapy, especially in MMRd

Palliative RT

  • ROCS trial shows no benefit in post stent RT (20Gy/5# or 30Gy/10#) for dysphagia in regards to symptom deterioration, stent complication or OS. RT reduced bleeding.

CROSS

P: T1-3N0M0 oesophageal ca, 75% adenoCa

I: oesophagectomy vs NA CRT → oesophagectomy

O: pCR 50% for SCC, 25% for adenoCa

Median OS 24 → 50mo.

10 yrs OS 38% vs 25%

Bigger median OS difference for SCC than adenoCa

Most recurrences were distant, <5% LRR

ART DECO/ Intergroup

  • DE to 64Gy with cis/5FU (Int) or 61Gy with carbo/taxol (ART) in definitive setting demonstrated no benefit in LRR (regardless of histo) or survival and more tox in Int

RTOG 8501

P: inoperable T1-3N0-1

I: 64Gy/32# RT alone vs 50Gy/25# with cisplatin and 5FU

O: 5 yr OS 0% vs 25%. 1/3rd will have LRR

ESOPEC

P: adenoCa, including GOJ

I: peri-operative FLOT chemo vs CROSS protocol

O: median OS 66 mo vs 37mo. G3 toxicity higher in peri-op FLOT, esp haematologic. Arms were not well balanced.

CHECKMATE 577

P: no pCR after CROSS

I: adjuvant nivolumab for 12 mo vs obs

O: DFS improved from 11 to 22.4 months regardless of histology and PDL1. Numerical but no sig OS benefit

SANO (ph II)

P: cCR at 3 months post CROSS

O: 2 yr OS no different ~ 70%, 50% need salvage surgery by 6 months, trend towards increase DM with surveillance

Epidemiology

I: common

A: 70

G: M

R: Asian

RF

E: high salt or smoked foods, low fruit/veg intake, smoking, ETOH, GORD, obesity, chronic gastritis, NSAIDs

I: H.pylori, EBV

G: GAPPS, Peutz-Jager syndrome, Lynch, hereditary diffuse gastric cancer, FAP, Li Fraumeni, pernicious anemia

I: nil

Staging

T1: into submucosa

T2: into muscularis propria

T3: into serosa

T4: into subserosa (visceral peritoneum) into adj structures

N1: 1-2 nodes

N2: 3-6 nodes

N3: 7+

M1: DM

T2+ should have staging abdo laparoscopy as FDG PET insensitive for this

Tumour marker: Ca19.9

Differential diagnosis

Benign: cyst, gastritis, polyp, ulcer

Malignant: gastric carcinoma, MALT, GIST, mets (luminal breast ca), melanoma, sarcoma

Path

Intestinal type – 60%

  • Adenocarcinoma
  • Correa cascade: stepwise progression from chronic H.pylori gastritis to intestinal metaplasia, dysplasia then adenoCa
  • Intermediate prognosis
  • CK20, CDX2 +ve, HER2 +ve

Diffuse gastric type – 30%

  • Signet ring cells, infiltrative and diffuse growth, linitis plastica
  • Assoc with CDH1 syndrome
  • More advanced and aggressive at diagnosis
  • CK7+ve, CK20 -ve, CDX2 -ve, loss of E-cadherin on IHC

Lymphoepithelial type – 10%

  • Assoc with EBV, lymphocytic infiltrate around tumour
  • Best prognosis
  • EBER ISH, LMP IHC
  • High PDL1 expression

IHC: CK7+, AE1/AE3, HER2, MMR

CK20-

Natural History

  • Early lymphatic spread due to rich lymphatics in submucosa
  • Diffuse type has early haemtogenous spread
  • Transcolemic spread leading to peritoneal carcinomatosis and Krukenburg tumours
  • Nodal skip metastases to Virchows node – indicates advanced disease

Management

Early T1aN0

EMR

  • T1a (through lamina propria but not into submucosa) N0, intestinal type, well diff, ≤2cm, no LVSI

T1b+ Nany M0

Gastrectomy with D2 lymph node dissection

  • Partial gastrectomy for small distal tumours
  • Total gastrectomy for diffuse, large or proximal tumours, commonly with RouX-en-Y reconstructions
  • D2 with ≥ 15 nodes: celiac, splenic, CHA, left gastric artery

Peri-operative systemic therapy – T2+ or N+

  • MAGIC found OS improved with ECF peri-op chemo vs gastrectomy upfront
  • Peri-op FLOT improves 5 yr OS from 36% to 45% compared with peri-op ECF chemotherapy (FLOT 4)
  • No benefit with addition of adjuvant RT in peri-op ECC chemo (CRITICS)

Neoadjuvant RT

  • No OS benefit shown in TOPGEAR with addition of NA 45Gy/25# to peri-operative FLOT
    • Did improve pCR from 8% to 17% and downstaging

Adjuvant chemotherapy and radiotherapy

  • CRT after R0 gastrectomy was historically shown to improve OS, and LRC. 3 yr OS 50%. (INT0116 trial, McDonald et al)
  • No benefit in diffuse type
  • These pts did not receive D2 LND
  • May still be relevant after D0/D1 LND
  • Capecitabine/oxaliplatin after D2 gastrectomy improves OS and DFS. 5 yr OS OS 78% (Classic trial)
  • Adj 45Gy/25# in addition to cape/cis chemotherapy improved DFS in patients who did not have NA chemotherapy in a subgroup analysis - node positive and intestinal subtype (ARTIST 1)
  • RT in addition to SOX chemotherapy better than S1 chemo (ARTIST 2)
  • Overall 45Gy/25# in addition to chemotherapy may improve DFS and can be considered in:
    • R1, N+, D0/1

Definitive chemoradiation

  • No high level evidence
  • Extrapolating from other data – 45Gy/25# or 50.4-54Gy in 28-30# reasonable
  • Concurrent cisplatin, capecitabine, 5FU
  • Definitive chemo: CAPOX, FOLFOX
  • Ph 2 data shows promising outcomes of adjuvant IO

Metastatic

  • FOLFOX
  • CAPOX
  • HER2 directed therapy
  • IO if MMRd or high PDL1

EBRT

Dose: 45Gy/25#, daily, VMAT, with concurrent capecitabine

  • Boost to 50.4Gy if R1

Pre-sim: scars healed, discuss at MDT, aim to commence 4-6 weeks post-op, optimise nutrition

Sim: NBM 4 hours, supine, arms about head, vac bag immobilisation, EEBH, oral contrast

Volumes

CTV: gastrectomy bed, tumour bed, and entire D2 nodal region

  • If proximal – include GOJ
  • If distal – include duodenum

PTV + 8mm margin

OARs

duodenum, small bowel, pancreas, liver, kidneys, lung, heart

GIST

From the exam summary.

Epidemiology

I: Rare

A: 70

G: M

R: Chinese

RF

G: hereditary GIST syndrome, Carneys triad (pulmonary chondroma, extra-adrenal paraganglioma), NF1

Risk stratification

Tumour size >5cm

Location: gastric > small bowel

MR: mitotic rate <5 or >5 per 5mm2

R1/R2 resection

Tumour rupture intra-op

  • Arises from interstitial cells of Cajal in muscularis propria
  • Haematogenous spread can occur, lymphatic spread rare

Pathology

  • Spindle – 80%
    • Fascicles/whorls of uniform spindle cells
    • Better prognosis
    • cKITm common
  • Epitheliod – 10%
    • Round/polygonal epithelial cells in sheets
    • Worse prognosis
    • associated with PDGFRA mutation
  • Mixed – 15%
    • Intermediate prognosis

IHC: CD117, DOG1, CD34

Gastric GIST better than SB GIST

Genetic

  • cKIT mutation (80%) – sensitive to TKI (Imatinib)
  • PDGFRA D842V – often confers resistance to Imatinib, sensitive to avapritinib

Management

Resectable

  • En-bloc resection. No LND routinely.
  • Adjuvant TKI for 3 years if large size >5cm, high mitotic rate or tumour rupture with appropriate mutation
  • No lymph node dissection required in cN0. Lymphatic spread is rare

Unresectable/borderline resectable

  • Neoadjuvant TKI → surgery

Metastatic

  • Imatinib 1st line

GIST is radioresistant, therefore limited role for RT
