Oesophagus

Adenocarcinoma has moved towards perioperative FLOT (ESOPEC); CROSS remains the standard for SCC and for patients unfit for FLOT. Use 50 Gy for definitive chemoRT: dose escalation has not helped.

  • Siewert: I = 1–5 cm above GOJ; II = 1 cm above to 2 cm below; III = 2–5 cm below (treated as gastric).
  • Node risk: T1a 7% · T1b (submucosa) ~20% · T2 ~40%. No serosa, so early spread.
  • AJCC 8: location by epicentre; coeliac nodes are regional for lower/GOJ tumours.
  • 5-y OS: stage I 70–80%, roughly halving with each stage; pCR at any stage behaves like stage I.

Work-up

  • Gastroscopy + biopsy; EUS ± FNA for T/N (T ~90%, N 80–85% sensitivity).
  • CT chest/abdomen ± pelvis, then PET-CT (finds M1 in ~15%; N sensitivity ~60%).
  • Bronchoscopy if at or above carina (exclude TOF). Staging laparoscopy for GOJ/Siewert II–III may upstage 10–15%.
  • Biomarkers: HER2 (10% oesophageal, up to 33% GOJ), PD-L1 CPS, MMR/MSI.
  • Dietitian early; consider feeding tube if dysphagia and weight loss. Smoking cessation.

Organ preservation

  • SANO: cCR after 41.4/23 nCRT → active surveillance non-inferior to surgery (2-y OS 74% vs 71%). About half avoided surgery; durable cCR in only ~10% overall.
  • Response assessment at 6 and 12 wk with endoscopy + bite-on-bite biopsies, EUS-FNA, PET-CT.
  • SCC is more radiosensitive (CROSS pCR 49% SCC vs 23% AC), so organ preservation is more plausible.
  • FFCD 9102: in responders, more chemoRT was as good as surgery for OS with lower 3-mo mortality (9% → 1%), at the cost of more local failure and stents.

Palliation of dysphagia

  • RT or chemoRT preferred over stent for durable relief: 70–90% respond, 50–70% stay dysphagia-free until death; transient worsening in ~30%.
  • 20/5 vs 30/10 + 12/1 BT: persistent/recurrent dysphagia 64% → 42%; consider higher dose if longer life expectancy.
  • Brachytherapy gives more durable relief and fewer complications than stenting; BT meta: G3–4 fistula 8%, stenosis 12%.
  • ROCS: adding RT to stent did not reduce dysphagia deterioration (~47%) but delayed bleeding.
  • Stent in situ before radical treatment: consider induction chemo then remove the stent (stent + RT morbidity).

Gastric

Perioperative FLOT is the standard; RT is now for incomplete surgery, margin-positive disease, patients unfit for perioperative chemo, and palliation (ASTRO 2025).

Doses and volumes

  • Pre- or postop: 45/25 (strong). Boost 5.4–9 Gy for R2/gross residual. Definitive: 45/25 CTV + 54–59.4 Gy GTV.
  • Preop CTV: whole stomach, perigastric extension, ≥1 cm proximal oesophagus (more for cardia/GOJ) and regional nodes (coeliac, common hepatic, splenic, left gastric, hepatoduodenal, peripancreatic, SMA, regional para-aortic).
  • Postop CTV: tumour bed, anastomoses, duodenal stump, 2 cm beyond margins, regional nodes. Include gastric remnant only if margin positive. Proximal third: add paraoesophageal and hepatogastric; middle/distal: supra- and infrapyloric.
  • Coverage: preop PTV D95 ≥95%, D2cc ≤107%; postop PTV D100 ≥95%.
  • Fast 2 h before sim and treatment. 4DCT/motion management with IMRT; daily IGRT.
  • D2 dissection: recurrences still locoregional as often as distant; >¾ of regional relapses outside the D2 field (stations 16a2, 8, 12, 16b1, 9).

Systemic therapy pointers

  • FLOT ×4 → surgery → FLOT ×4 (5-FU 2.6 g/m² CI, leucovorin, oxaliplatin, docetaxel).
  • Metastatic HER2-negative, CPS ≥5: nivolumab + chemo. HER2+: trastuzumab + pembrolizumab + chemo; trastuzumab deruxtecan after progression. Second line: ramucirumab + paclitaxel.
  • Test HER2 (~20% gastric, higher in intestinal type), PD-L1, MMR.

Oesophagus planning

Simulation and IGRT

  • Fast ≥2 h (empty stomach), arms up on wingboard/vac-lok, IV contrast, 4DCT.
  • Daily CBCT (or kV daily + CBCT days 1–3 then weekly). For GOJ, match on the gastrohepatic region.
  • Motion (fiducial study): CC peak-to-peak ~9 mm; required PTV with CTV-based match ~7 mm LR, 8 mm AP, 11 mm CC.

Target volumes

  • CTVp: GTVp + 3–4 cm CC (≤2 cm into involved stomach), 1 cm radial (0.5 cm into heart/liver). Submucosal spread: 3 cm covers 94% of SCC; AC spreads further distally (5 cm covers 94%).
  • CTVn: GTVn + 0.5–1.5 cm.
  • PTV: CTV + 0.5–1.0 cm.
  • ENI by site: cervical → SCV ± higher neck; upper third → bilateral SCV + mediastinal; middle → paraoesophageal + mediastinal; distal/GOJ → paraoesophageal, lesser curve, coeliac; consider splenic for Siewert II–III.
  • PROTECT (involved-field) approach: GTVp + 3 cm CC with node stations along that length, GTVn + 1 cm, no extensive elective nodes.
  • Optional cone-down for small, non-operative tumours: 45/25 elective (4 cm CC) and 50/25 primary (2 cm CC) as SIB, then 54–60 Gy sequential to 1 cm.

Follow-up and failure patterns

  • H&P q3–6 mo ×2 y, q6–12 mo for 3 y, then annually. If organ-preserving: gastroscopy q3–6 mo ×2 y, q6 mo in year 3.
  • Most relapses within 2 y. After definitive 50.4 Gy (MDACC), local failure 50%, 90% within GTV; risks: T3+, >8 cm, poor PET response.
  • After CROSS, only ~1 in 8 fail locoregionally; distant failure dominates.
  • Strictures 12–30% after RT; balloon dilatation works in 80–90%.

Commentary
ESOPEC and NeoAEGIS were pre-op trials for adenocarcinoma only. Results in ESOPEC show an impressive OS benefit with FLOT in esophageal cancer, an entity with few advancements over years other than nivolumab for those with partial response. ChemoRT did not improve LRR or pCR in ESOPEC. ChemoRT did have better pCR in NeoAEGIS. Neither trial investigated radiation with FLOT or nivolumab (Checkmate 577, Kelly 2021).

Fojo et al (2025) explain a statistical bias called skimming the median that overestimates survival gains by not taking into account the full dataset, and provide ESOPEC as one of their teaching examples. 3-yr OS of 57% and pCR of 4-17% is certainly short of reliable cure, and there is the potential that radiation and nivolumab could add benefit to FLOT, if they can be tolerated. The FLOT authors write that nivolumab was less effective in adenocarcinoma in Checkman577, supporting FLOT in adenocarcinoma, but in the Fig 2 forest plot the confidence intervals of squamous cell carcinoma and adenocarcinoma plainly overlap, and the subanalysis is not specifically powered. Oesophagus

From the exam summary.

Epidemiology

I: SCC ↓, more common globally

adenoCa ↑, more common in Western world

A: 65

G: M

R: SCC oesophageal ca Asian belt – Turkey, China, central Asia, Mongolia. Adenoca - Western

RF

SCC

  • A -achalasia (x30 risk)
  • B- betel nut chewing, bad diet
  • C - cigarette smoking
  • D - drinking scalding hot liquids
  • E - ETOH
  • F- Familial:
  • Tylosis (palmoplantar hyperkeratosis), Plummer-vinson, Fanconi anaemia

AdenoCa

  • GORD
  • Obesity
  • Barrett’s oesophagus
  • Smoking (weaker assoc than SCC)
  • Familial Barrett’s syndrome

Staging

T1a: into lamina propria or into muscularis mucosa

T1b: into submucosa

T2: into muscularis propria

T3: into adventitia

T4: into adjacent structures

N1: ≤ 2 locoregional

N2: 3-6 nodes

N3: 7+ nodes

M1: DM

  • SCF included as regional however in a distant oesophageal often treated as M1
  • PA nodes beyond celiac axis are treated as M1

Siewert classification for GOJ tumours

1: -5 to -1cm from cardia

2: -1 to +2cm from cardia

3: +2 to +5cm from cardia

Anatomy

  • 40cm long, divided into thirds
  • 15-20cm: cervical
  • 20-30cm: upper & mid
  • 30-40cm: distal

Path

SCC

  • Exophytic, flat or ulcerated
  • More infiltrative and circumferential
  • Keratin pearls, whorls, intercellular bridges
  • Basaloid – aggressive
  • Spindle, verrucous – less aggressive
  • p40, p63, AE1/AE3, CK5/6

AdenoCa

  • B/g of Barrett’s oesophagus, goblet cells, gland formation, mucin production
  • CK7, AE1/AE3, MUC 1/2 Alcian blue

Barrett’s oesophagus

  • Chronic irritation of oesophageal lining (reflux) → Intestinal metaplasia from squamous cells to columnar cells extending from the Z line → progress to dysplasia → carcinoma
  • 0.5% annual ca risk if metaplasia
  • 5-20% annual ca risk of dysplasia

Response to NA Rx

  • Becker-Mandard grading system – lower score = better response
  • Prescence of fibrosis, necrosis, acellular tissue, granulation inflammatory response
  • Residual invasive or in-situ disease

Pre-treatment considerations

  • Dietitian, SP review
  • Definitive feeding strategy: NGT, JEJ tube, RIG/PEG
  • Gastrostomy avoided if surgery planned
  • Pulmonary function testing

Management

T1aN0

  • Endoscopic mucosal resection, endoscopic submucosal dissection
  • Minimally invasive procedure
  • If upstaged: T1b, poorly differentiated, LVSI then requires adjuvant chemoRT: 50Gy/25# with concurrent carboplatin/paclitaxel

T1b-T2N0

  • Upfront esophagectomy + locoregional nodal dissection
  • Adjuvant chemoRT 50Gy/25# with carboplatin/paclitaxel if: T3, LVSI, R1, N+

Surgical approaches

  • Stomach is used as conduit in all
  • Trans-thoracic approach
  • Ivor Lewis – anastomosis distally in lower thorax
  • Mckeown – anastomosis more proximally in cervical region
  • Trans-hiatal approach
  • High morbidity and mortality ~ 10%
  • Minimally invasive and robotic approaches have been shown to reduce morbidity
  • CI to surgery: T4b (heart, great vessels, surrounding organs), bulky multistation LN, medically unfit

Mid-distal SCC

  • CROSS protocol: 41.4Gy/23# with concurrent carboplatin/paclitaxel followed by oesophagectomy 6-8 weeks post
    • NA CRT Improves median OS from 24 to 48 months, SCC>adenoCa
  • Definitive chemoradiotherapy: 50Gy/25# with cis/5FU in week 1 and week 5 (RTOG 8501)
  • If unfit for chemo 50Gy/25# + 14Gy/7# tumour boost RT alone (RTOG 8501)
  • No role for DE (ART-DECO, Intergroup, <5% LRR from CROSS)
  • No role for ENI (Lyu et al – ENI increases pneumonitis and oesophagitis without PFS or OS benefit in thoracic SCC)

Mid-distal AdenoCa

  • Peri-operative q2weekly x4 FLOT (5FU, leucovorin, oxali, docetaxol) chemotherapy (ESOPEC, improves median OS from 37 to 66 months)
  • If unfit for FLOT → CROSS protocol as above
    • pCR for adenoCa 20% vs 40% for SCC
  • FLOT chemotherapy more toxic than CROSS protocol
  • The addition of Durvalumab to FLOT improves EFS (MATTERHORN)

Cervical oesophageal ca

  • <5cm from cricopharyngeus
  • Often managed as a H&N treatment: 66Gy/33# with carboplatin and paclitaxel or 70Gy/35# with cisplatin
  • If treating as H&N then cover elective nodal regions: level 3/4, SCF to 56Gy
  • Surgical resection involves cervical oesophagectomy + laryngopharyngectomy
  • Significant morbidity and mortality

Immunotherapy

  • Adjuvant Nivolumab for 12mo if no pCR improves DFS for SCC and adenoCa (CHECKMATE 577)
  • Can be considered in all MMRd cases as per NCCN

Organ Preservation

  • Close surveillance with salvage oesphagectomy
  • Consider DE to 54Gy/27# - no high level evidence
  • Emerging management option from SANO ph II trial however:
    • Needs cCR on PET and endoscopy 12 weeks post treatment
    • Short term 2 year OS non-inferior
    • 50% need oesophagectomy at 6 mo
    • Trend towards increase DM with surveillance
  • Ongoing trials – SANO 3, ESOTRATE

Metastatic

  • Carboplatin, paclitaxel
  • Test for HER-2, PDL1 and MMR status to add in targeted and IO options
  • Her2 therapy
  • Immunotherapy, especially in MMRd

Palliative RT

  • ROCS trial shows no benefit in post stent RT (20Gy/5# or 30Gy/10#) for dysphagia in regards to symptom deterioration, stent complication or OS. RT reduced bleeding.
