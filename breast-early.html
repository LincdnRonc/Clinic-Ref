DCIS

After BCS, whole-breast RT roughly halves ipsilateral recurrence, and about half of recurrences are invasive. It does not improve survival. Omitting RT is reasonable for small, low–intermediate grade, screen-detected DCIS with wide margins.

Decisions after BCS

  • Default: WBRT 40/15 or 42.5/16, hypofractionated (ASTRO 2018).
  • Boost: if ≤50 y, G3 or margins ≤2 mm (ASTRO). TROG 07.01 boost 16/8 improved 5 y freedom from local recurrence by 4.4% (93 → 97%), with slightly more grade 2 toxicity.
  • Consider omitting boost: >50 y, screen-detected, ≤2.5 cm, G1–2 and margins ≥3 mm.
  • Consider omitting RT: RTOG 9804-type DCIS (screen-detected, G1–2, <2.5 cm, margins ≥3 mm). RT still lowers recurrence (12 y IBTR 11 → 3%), so discuss the absolute numbers. The untreated risk is about 1% per year.
  • Endocrine therapy: tamoxifen lowers IBTR and contralateral cancer (B-24, UK/ANZ) but not mortality.
  • Margins: 2 mm is adequate for DCIS treated with WBRT (SSO/ASTRO/ASCO).

Whole-breast RT and boost

Hypofractionation is standard for whole-breast RT. 26/5 is supported for whole-breast RT and is being used more widely. Base the boost on age, grade and margin.

Tumour-bed boost (ASTRO 2018)

  • Invasive cancer: boost if ≤50 y, or 51–70 y with G3 or positive margin. Consider omitting if >70 y, HR+, G1–2 and margins ≥2 mm.
  • Dose: 10/4–5 standard. 14–16/7–8 or 12.5/5 for young age or close margins. Sequential boost recommended; SIB is supported by RTOG 1005 (40–48/15).
  • Benefit: about 3–4% absolute (about 40% relative) reduction in local failure. In EORTC 22881 the absolute benefit was largest in women ≤40 y.
  • Cost: 10 y severe fibrosis 1.6 → 4.4% (EORTC 22881).
EORTC 228815,318 · BCS + ALND · 50 Gy ± 16 Gy boost10 y IBTR 10 → 6%. 10 y OS about 82% in both arms.
Lyon1,024 · ≤3 cm · 50 Gy ± 10 Gy electron boost5 y local failure 4.5 → 3.6%. G1–2 telangiectasia 6 → 12%, with no difference in self-reported cosmesis.

Partial-breast irradiation (APBI and PBI)

For low-risk disease, partial-breast RT gives local control comparable to whole-breast RT. 30/5 every other day (Florence) or 26/5 or 40/15 to the partial breast (IMPORT LOW) are the external-beam regimens to use. Avoid 38.5/10 twice daily.

Who is suitable (ESTRO-ACROP consensus)

  • Consensus criteria: age ≥50 y, luminal-like, ≤3 cm, G1–2, non-lobular, no LVSI, unifocal. G1–2 DCIS also qualifies if ≤2.5 cm with margins ≥3 mm.
  • No consensus was reached on margin (no ink on tumour), ITC-only nodes or prior systemic therapy.
  • In the GEC-ESTRO ASTRO consensus analysis, 5 y IBTR was 1.5% for suitable, 4.4% for cautionary and 8.8% for unsuitable patients.
  • Avoid 38.5/10 BID 3D-CRT (RAPID). It gave worse late cosmesis.

Omitting RT in older, low-risk patients

For older patients with small, ER+, node-negative tumours taking endocrine therapy, omitting RT increases local recurrence (about 1% per year) without changing survival. Endocrine therapy adherence is the key question.

Who to offer omission

  • ≥65–70 y, T1 (up to 3 cm in PRIME II), pN0, ER+, HER2−, G1–2, clear margins, willing to take 5 y of endocrine therapy.
  • LUMINA (Luminal A by Ki-67 ≤13.25%, ≥55 y) and EXPERT (PAM50 low risk) use biomarkers to identify patients who can omit RT.
  • If endocrine therapy is unlikely to be taken or tolerated, discuss RT (for example PBI or 26/5) as the alternative.
