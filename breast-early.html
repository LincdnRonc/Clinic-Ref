Breast (AJCC 8 anatomic stage)

DCIS

After BCS, whole-breast RT roughly halves ipsilateral recurrence, and about half of recurrences are invasive. It does not improve survival. Omitting RT is reasonable for small, low–intermediate grade, screen-detected DCIS with wide margins.

Decisions after BCS

  • Default: WBRT 40/15 or 42.5/16, hypofractionated (ASTRO 2018).
  • Boost: if ≤50 y, G3 or margins ≤2 mm (ASTRO). TROG 07.01 boost 16/8 improved 5 y freedom from local recurrence by 4.4% (93 → 97%), with slightly more grade 2 toxicity.
  • Consider omitting boost: >50 y, screen-detected, ≤2.5 cm, G1–2 and margins ≥3 mm.
  • Consider omitting RT: RTOG 9804-type DCIS (screen-detected, G1–2, <2.5 cm, margins ≥3 mm). RT still lowers recurrence (12 y IBTR 11 → 3%), so discuss the absolute numbers. The untreated risk is about 1% per year.
  • Endocrine therapy: tamoxifen lowers IBTR and contralateral cancer (B-24, UK/ANZ) but not mortality.
  • Margins: 2 mm is adequate for DCIS treated with WBRT (SSO/ASTRO/ASCO).

Whole-breast RT and boost

Hypofractionation is standard for whole-breast RT. 26/5 is supported for whole-breast RT and is being used more widely. Base the boost on age, grade and margin.

Tumour-bed boost (ASTRO 2018)

  • Invasive cancer: boost if ≤50 y, or 51–70 y with G3 or positive margin. Consider omitting if >70 y, HR+, G1–2 and margins ≥2 mm.
  • Dose: 10/4–5 standard. 14–16/7–8 or 12.5/5 for young age or close margins. Sequential boost recommended; SIB is supported by RTOG 1005 (40–48/15).
  • Benefit: about 3–4% absolute (about 40% relative) reduction in local failure. In EORTC 22881 the absolute benefit was largest in women ≤40 y.
  • Cost: 10 y severe fibrosis 1.6 → 4.4% (EORTC 22881).

Partial-breast irradiation (APBI and PBI)

For low-risk disease, partial-breast RT gives local control comparable to whole-breast RT. 30/5 every other day (Florence) or 26/5 or 40/15 to the partial breast (IMPORT LOW) are the external-beam regimens to use. Avoid 38.5/10 twice daily.

Who is suitable (ESTRO-ACROP consensus)

  • Consensus criteria: age ≥50 y, luminal-like, ≤3 cm, G1–2, non-lobular, no LVSI, unifocal. G1–2 DCIS also qualifies if ≤2.5 cm with margins ≥3 mm.
  • No consensus was reached on margin (no ink on tumour), ITC-only nodes or prior systemic therapy.
  • In the GEC-ESTRO ASTRO consensus analysis, 5 y IBTR was 1.5% for suitable, 4.4% for cautionary and 8.8% for unsuitable patients.
  • Avoid 38.5/10 BID 3D-CRT (RAPID). It gave worse late cosmesis.

Omitting RT in older, low-risk patients

For older patients with small, ER+, node-negative tumours taking endocrine therapy, omitting RT increases local recurrence (about 1% per year) without changing survival. Endocrine therapy adherence is the key question.

Who to offer omission

  • ≥65–70 y, T1 (up to 3 cm in PRIME II), pN0, ER+, HER2−, G1–2, clear margins, willing to take 5 y of endocrine therapy.
  • LUMINA (Luminal A by Ki-67 ≤13.25%, ≥55 y) and EXPERT (PAM50 low risk) use biomarkers to identify patients who can omit RT.
  • If endocrine therapy is unlikely to be taken or tolerated, discuss RT (for example PBI or 26/5) as the alternative.

Key trials

CALGB 9343 (2004; 10 y 2013)636 · ≥70 y · stage I ER+ · tamoxifen ± RT10 y local recurrence 10 → 2%. No difference in OS, breast cancer survival or mastectomy-free survival.
PRIME II (2015; 10 y 2023)1,326 · ≥65 y · <3 cm · pN0 · HR+ · endocrine ± WBRT10 y IBTR 10 → 1% (5 y 4.1 → 1.3%). No OS difference. The authors say the results apply mainly to G1–2 ER+ tumours.
LUMINA (2023)501 · ≥55 y · T1N0 Luminal A (Ki-67 ≤13.25%) · endocrine therapy, no RT5 y IBTR 2.3% (1.4% at 55–64 y, 2.8% at ≥65 y). 5 y OS 97%.
EXPERT ANZ (results pending)1,167 · ≥50 y · Luminal A, PAM50 risk of recurrence <60 · ± WBRTPhase III trial of omitting RT. Check for results before quoting.

With reconstruction

  • Major reconstruction complications are common with PMRT (about 40% in one series). Reconstruction failure is about 10%.
  • Irradiating a tissue expander carries more risk than irradiating the permanent implant.
  • Infection 6 → 13% and skin necrosis 3 → 8% with PMRT in one implant series.

Staging

Tis

Nx

Mx

DDx for breast lump

Benign: mastitis, galactocele, fibroma, lipoma, fat necrosis, seroma, abscess, cyst, phyllodes tumour

Malignant: DCIS, IDC, ILC, IBC, secretory carcinoma, sarcoma, lymphoma, skin cancer/melanoma

Path

  • Graded based on nuclear pleomorphism, nuclear size, mitoses, necrosis, growth pattern

Growth patterns (C2PMS)

  • Comedo
  • Cribriform
  • Solid
  • Papillary
  • Micropapillary

Adverse features

  • Comedo pattern
  • Necrosis

Paget’s disease

  • Intraepidermal adenoCa with eczema of nipple due to epidermotropic spread.
  • Underlying DCIS more common than underlying invasive breast carcinoma

Phyllodes tumour

  • Biphasic epithelial and stromal tumour, leaf-like architecture.
  • Benign, borderline, malignant
  • Stromal features determine malignancy – cellularity, atypia, mitoses, stromal overgrowth, margins
  • Borders depend on nature
  • Malignant has preferential haematogenous spread over lymphatic
  • Axilla surgery not routine unless clinically suspicious nodes

LCIS

  • Classic: single discohesive invasive cell within lobules
  • Pleomorphic: pre-invasive cells have more atypical larger appearance with necrosis or calcifications
  • Florid: expansion of acini with pre-invasive cells, mass forming
  • Pleomorphic and florid should be completely excised with 2mm margin + adj RT
  • Increased risk for bilateral breast ca, 1-2% annual cancer risk

Screening

  • Screening MMG/US 2nd yearly from 50-74yo

Management

1. WLE +/- SLNBx if suspicious for foci of IDC

  • Obtain 2mm margins or re-excise

2. Whole breast adjuvant radiotherapy

  • Reduce LRR by 1/2 (EBCTCG MA, sweDCIS, MSKCC calculator)
  • 1% chance per year of recurrence up to 20yrs, half will be invasive (ECOG, EBCTCG MA)
  • 40Gy/15# (UK START B)
  • 26Gy/5# - 1% higher late tissue effect, apply more caution to large breasts due to increase separation and heterogeneity and age <50yo (FAST-FORWARD)
  • Boost for non-low risk DCIS: age<40 OR age>40 with: size >15mm, radial margin <10mm, IG or HG, comedo pattern, central necrosis, multifocal (TROG BIG)
  • DCISionRT genetic score – retrospectively validated in sweDCIS
  • Cardiac risk minimal (Danish cohort trials)

3. Partial breast radiotherapy

  • <20mm, unifocal, low-intermediate grade, margins >2mm, no BRCA, visible seroma, age >50
  • Conditional factors (should not have multiple): high grade, HER2 +ve in EBC, close margin <2mm, size 20-30mm
  • 40Gy/15# PBI or 40Gy/36Gy/15# reduced dose WBRT - IMPORT LOW
  • APBI 30Gy/5# - Florence trial
  • Multicatheter HDR brachytherapy (GEC-ESTRO ph 3 RCT)
  • Single channel HDR brachytherapy (Mammosite trial)
  • Brachytherapy on trial (ABPI – HEARTBEAT)
  • Intra-op 20Gy/1# - TARGIT

4. Endocrine therapy

  • not routine in AU however, tamoxifen shown to reduce IBTR, esp if not giving RT
  • Baby tamoxifen: 5mg for 3 years reduces recurrence risk by 50% at 10 yrs with lower toxicity than full dose tamoxifen (TAM-01)

EBRT

Dose: 48Gy/40Gy/15# OR 26Gy/5# using hybrid IMRT

Pre-sim

  • Scars healed
  • No fluctuating seroma
  • Commence within 8 weeks post-op

Sim

  • Supine, arms up, immobilised in half vacbag
  • Consider breast board for high lying breast
  • DIBH for left side

  • Boost PTV: +5mm
  • Breast CTV (ESTRO): Inferior clavicle head, between medial mammary vessels and lateral thoracic vessels, 1cm below inframammary fold

  • PTV: +10mm

Daily kV with bony match

  • Subacute: pneumonitis
  • Late: pain, fibrosis, induration, lymphoedema, pigmentation change, pulmonary fibrosis, rib fracture, minimal cardiac risk, second malignancy

Biological subtypes

Luminal A

  • ER/PR +, HER2-, Ki67<14% , G1-2
  • 50% of breast cancer

  • Older post-menopausal women
  • Less response to chemotherapy
  • Late relapse, good prognosis

  • ER/PR +, HER2 +/-, Ki67 ≥14%, G1-3
  • 20% of breast cancer
  • More aggressive than luminal A
  • Younger women
  • Responds to chemotherapy
  • Intermediate prognosis

HER2 enriched

  • 15% of breast cancer

Management

1. Surgery

  • WLE + adj RT = mastectomy (Milan, NSABP B-06)
  • Most N0 require SLNBx
  • Omission of SLNB if favourable EBC in pts >65 is an emerging option (SOUND, INSEMNA, ASTRO guidelines)
  • Reconstruction immediate or post RT (RT charm, FABREC)
  • If cN0 but SLN+ → ALND can be omitted if ≤2 nodes

2. Adjuvant chemotherapy / HER2

  • Fit, young pts with: luminal B, node positive, HER2 enriched
  • Doxorubicin/cyclophosphamide 3 weekly x4 + paclitaxel weekly x12
  • Docetaxel/cyclophosphamide 3 weekly x4
  • dual HER2 therapy: pertuzumab, trastuzumab

3. Adjuvant radiotherapy

  • 40Gy/15# or 26Gy/5#. WBI or PBI.
  • 48Gy SIB boost if: age <50, SM close, LVSI, grade 3 (EORTC, Import High)
  • RNI: axilla/SCF/IMC if 1-2 SLN+ instead of axillary dissection
  • High tangents if N1mi
    • Z11, SENOMAC, AMAROS show that in cT1-2 SLN 1-2 cN0 pts there is equiv long term OS with RT vs ALND. Axilla recurrence <3%. LE 12 vs 25%. 30% chance of further node +ve with ALND
  • RNI + ALND if <10 nodes dissected or >50% nodes +ve on dissection
    • RNI improves BCM/DFS in addition to ALND (MA 20, EBCTCG MA)
    • Concern for increased toxicity with IMC however with more modern cardiac sparing techniques this is improving

4. Omission of RT

  • T1N0, luminal A, age ≥70yo
  • Increases risk of LRR by 9% but not survival
  • LRR higher than above if ER+ low <50% (19% vs 0%), or ET non-adherence (PRIME II)
  • HrQoL worse with ET vs RT (EUROPA, final results pending)

5. Adjuvant endocrine therapy for 5 years

  • Tamoxifen if pre-menopausal
  • Aromatase inhibitor if post-menopausal

6. Bisphosphonates

  • Small OS benefit in post-menopausal women
  • Reduces skeletal events

RNI volumes

Level 1: inferior and lateral to pec minor. Bound by lat dorsi, pec major and chest wall until pec major inserts to chest wall

Level 2: posterior to pec minor. Between axillary vessels crossing medial and lateral edge of pec minor

Level 3: superior and medial to pec minor. Superior – pec minor inserts to coracoid. Inferior- axillary vessels cross medial pec minor. Posterior and medial – chest wall and thoracic inlet

SCF: bottom of cricoid to bottom of clavicle head. Bound by rib/clavicle, SCM, thyroid, medial edge ICA, pleura, anterior scalene.

IMC: 5mm lateral margin around IMC vessels from top of 1st rib to top of 4th rib

High tangents: tangent extends 1cm from humeral head to cover axilla level I-II

Late Toxicity pathogenesis

Central pathway

  • stem cell depletion, fibroblast activation, vascular injury
  • Radiation → DNA damage + ROS → endothelial dysfunction → chronic inflammation (TGF-B) → fibroblast activation → fibrosis + microvascular loss → hypoxia → perpetuates fibrosis
  • Other cytokines: TNF-a, IL-1, IL-6, PDGF

Cardiac

  • Dose volume relationship – aim MHD <2Gy and LAD dose Dmean <10Gy, Dmax <15Gy
  • Endothelial injury → pro-thrombotic state → release TGF-B + microvascular damage and ischaemia → accelerated atherosclerosis → CAD
  • TGF-B → activation of fibroblasts to myofibroblasts → collagen deposition and ECM remodelling → constrictive pericarditis, constrictive cardiomyopathy, diastolic dysfunction, valvular disease, arrythmia
  • Depletion of myocytes further reduces regenerative ability
  • Synergy between RT and traditional CV RFs

Skin

  • Consequential late effects from basal keratinocyte depletion, failed repopulation, barrier dysfunction +/- infection → chronic ulcer Chronic inflammation → TGF-B → activation of fibroblasts → collagen deposition, fibrosis and induration
  • Fibrosis and endothelial cell damage → hypoxia → compensatory superficial telangiectasia
  • Melanocyte stimulation or apoptosis → pigmentation change
