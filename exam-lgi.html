Rectum

Epidemiology · Pathology · Management · EBRT · GTVn: involved node

Clinic page: Rectum and colon

Epidemiology

I: Increasing in youth, 2nd most common, 5th leading cause of death

A: 60

G: M

R: Caucasian

RF

E: obesity, low fruit/veg intake, high red meat, smoking, ETOH, IBD

I: nil

G: Lynch, FAP, MutYH (polyposis), Cowden, Peutz-Jegher, juvenile polyposis (SMAD4)

I: HIV, solid organ transplant

Staging

T1: into submucosa

T2: into muscularis propria

T3: into pericolorectal tissue/subserosa

T4a: into visceral peritoneum/serosa (upper/mid rectum)

T4b: adjacent organs (lower rectum)

N1: 1-3 nodes

N1c: separate tumour deposit in perirectal tissue

N2: 4+ nodes

M1: distant node or met

Consider PET in suspected metastatic disease (as per NCCN)

Tumour budding: single cells or clusters at the leading invasive edge – thought to represent epithelial mesenchymal transition (metastatic cascade)

Pathology

  • Adenocarcinoma – 90%
    • Mucinous – high signal/hyperintense on MRI
    • Signet ring cell - aggressive
    • Medullary carcinoma
    • All assoc with MSI, favourable
    • IHC: CK20, CEA, MUC1/2, CDX2
  • Prominent desmoplasia
  • Squamous cell carcinoma – rare
  • Leiomyosarcoma
  • NET/NEC/carcinoid tumour

Micro: glands, mucin, nuclear atypia, desmoplastic reaction

Molecular: BRAF, RAS, HER2, MMR, PI3K-A

TILs: tumour infiltrating lymphocytes are a good prognostic factor

TRG (tumour regression grade)

  • Post NA Rx grade
  • 0-3 indicated complete to little regression
  • Does not account for residual node

Synoptic path report

  • Grade
  • Subtype
  • EMVI
  • LVSI
  • MMR
  • Response to NAT: residual tumour size, tumour bed size, presence of fibrosis, granulation tissue, hyalinization of vessels, foamy macrophages

Radiology

  • Involved MRF/CRM if ≤1mm from mesorectal margin

Anatomy

  • Rectum divided into 5cm thirds, extending upwards from top of anorectal ring
  • Anorectal ring is the internal anal sphincter and needed for anal continence, thus tumours extending below here are not candidates for LAR
  • Superior rectum has anterolateral peritoneal coverage and drains into PV via SMV
    • Superior rectum tumours have far lower local recurrence, thus treatment can be de-escalated
  • Mid third is retroperitoneal and drains into II
  • Lower third of rectum is extraperitoneal and drains into II via internal pudendal
    • Historically rectal cancer trials only included those in the lower-mid rectum

Management

Surgical resection

  • EMR: <1mm infiltration into submucosa, no LVSI, G1-2, R0
  • Total mesorectal excision: en-block resection of entire mesorectum and assoc vasculature and lymphatics to avoid tumour spill
    • Improves LC (Dutch TME trial)
  • Low anterior or ultra-low resection: sphincter preserving
  • Abdominoperineal resection: sphincter sacrifice, for advanced low rectal tumours

T1-2N0, clear CRM, mid-upper rectum

  • Surgical resection alone as above (EMR or TME)
  • Adjuvant treatment if upstaged
  • R1 or N+ disease: adjuvant pelvic RT 50Gy/25#
  • If upstaged with T3-T4, N+, LVSI, EMVI: adjuvant chemotherapy – FOLFOX or CAPOX

T2-3 N0-1: Neoadjuvant CRT

  • Short course RT vs LC CRT
    • SC RT largely reserved for those pts unfit for chemotherapy
    • No evidence for SC CRT followed by organ preservation
    • NA RT improves R0 and local control and hence preferred where more downstaging needed. No OS benefit in the TME era
  • Neoadjuvant CRT vs adj CRT: NA 50Gy/25# with conc/adj 5-FU improved LRC and sphincter preservation (German trial) and less acute and late toxicity
  • Long course NA CRT vs SC RT improves LRC, esp in low lying tumour (TROG 01.04)
  • Consider adjuvant FOLFOX or 5FU/leucovorin

RT omission

  • T2-3 N0-1, mid/upper rectal tumour
  • NA FOLFOX x6 alone if >20% MRI response and >5 cycles of chemo completed is not inferior for OS at 5 yrs however has more G3 tox (PROSPECT)
  • CAPOX chemo alone was inferior to LC CRT for LRC (CONVERT)

TNT

  • T3-T4, N2, EMVI, CRM+, threated sphincter, low lying tumour
  • Short course TNT (25Gy/5# + FOLFOX) vs LC CRT improved pCR, DM at 3 yrs but worse LRC at 5 yrs (RAPIDO)
    • Established pCR not a surrogate for LRC
  • Short course TNT had better 3 yr OS than LC-CRT in the STELLAR trial
  • Long course TNT with mFOLFIRINOXx6 q2w vs LC-CRT improved LRC, DM & OS by 6% at 5 years (PRODIGE-23)
  • TME surgery performed 6-12 weeks after TNT. >6 week interval can improve pCR but this does not translate to improved survival

TNT & watch & wait

  • Sequencing: long course CRT → consolidation FOLFOX/CAPOX x8 has superior organ preservation than vice versa - 40% vs 54% (OPRA)
  • 95% recurrence within first 2 yrs
  • OPRA trial had a median dose of 54Gy
  • Watch & wait protocol:
    • Restaging with CEA, MRI, colonoscopy + bx showing at 12 weeks showing cCR
    • DRE, CEA, MRI q3monthly for year 1 then q6monthly
    • CTCAP annually
    • Sigmoidoscopy at 6 months, colonoscopy at 1 year, then sigmoidoscopy q6monthly

Immunotherapy

  • Emerging role for NA IO in MMRd cancers with excellent 100% cCR at 6 months with neoadjuvant dostarlimab (Cercek et al, ph II)

re-RT

  • 10-15% G2 GU and GI toxicity rates
  • Variety of doses but can consider 30 in 1.8Gy/# or 1.5Gy/# BD, eg 39Gy/26# BD +/- capecitabine (ESTRO guidelines)
  • Consider SBRT if <5cm, discrete and outside lumen
  • Cumulative OARs: Bladder <80Gy

German CAO/ARO

P: T3-4, N0-1

I: 50Gy/25# with conc/adj 5FU delivered neoadj vs adj with TME

O: NA CRT + chemo improved sphincter preservation and LRR 3% vs 7%

Potential for overtreatment as surgical pathology unknown in NA group

TROG 01.04

P: T3 N0-2

I: 25Gy/5# → TME → 6 mo 5FU/leucovorin vs 50Gy/25# + 5FU → TME → 4 months 5FU/leucovorin

O: LC-CRT improved LRR, especially in low lying tumours 0% vs 12%

PROSPECT

P: T2-3, N0-1

I: 50.4Gy/28# + capecitabine → TME and adj FOLFOX x6 vs FOLFOX → restaging MRI → TME if >20% response and more than 5 cycles chemo → adj FOLFOX x6

If inadequate response pts proceeded with LC CRT

O: 90% were able to omit RT. Omission of RT did not result in worse outcomes however worse G3 toxicity with chemo only

RAPIDO

P: T4 Nany, more low lying tumours

I: 25Gy/5# + FOLFOX TNT → TME vs NA 50.4Gy/28# + cape → TME

O: pCR, 3 yr disease control better with SC TNT however 5 year LRC worse with SC TNT

PRODIGE-23

P: T3-4, Nany

I: 50Gy/25# + cape → TME → adj FOLFOX 6 mo vs FOLFIRINOX + 50Gy/25# + cape → TME → adj chemo x3 mo

O: At 5 yrs LC TNT improved OS from 76% to 82% and MFS from 65% to 73%

OPRA

P: T3-T4 Nany

I: LC CRT + consolidation FOLFOX vs induction FOLFOX + LC CRT → restaging at 6-12 weeks → W&W is cCR or nCR vs TME

Median RT dose 54Gy

O: 5 yr organ preservation 39% for induction at 54% for consolidation. 95% of recurrences occurred within first 2 years

Organ preservation rates higher if cCR vs nCR

5 yr DMFS and OS no diff between arms

EBRT

Dose: 50Gy/25# daily treatment, VMAT, no BD compensation, concurrent 825mg/m2 capecitabine PO BD on radiation days

  • If using 5FU this is a continuous pump infusion via PICC 7 days/week

Pre-sim: fertility sparing discussions, defunctioning colostomy, review MRI, assess & document baseline GI and GU function

Sim: full bladder, empty bowel, supine, arms on chest, vac bag, fuse MRI and PET

Volumes

GTV

CTV: GTV + 1cm + mesorectum and presacral space at all axial slices

  • Include pre-induction chemo extent of disease in CTV

ITV: + 1cm on anterior mesorectum

PTV5000: +10mm

  • If T4 invading into anterior structure, include entire organ in CTV
  • If invading into prostate/vagina/bladder → include EI nodes.
  • Consider inguinal nodes in lower vagina involvement or extension below dentate line anal canal
  • Extend 2cm proximal to involved node

GTVn: involved node

CTVn: +7mm

PTVn: +5mm

CTV elective nodes: internal iliac, obturator, presacral, mesorectum

ITV: +1cm on anterior mesorectum

PTV4500: +10mm

Daily CBCT with bone match and soft tissue check

OARs

Bladder & fem heads: V40Gy <40, max dose <50Gy

Small bowel: max <50Gy, V45 <195cc

Acute proctitis

  • cryptitis and crypt abscesses, surface ulceration, oedema, hyperaemia, inflammatory cell infiltrate

Chronic proctitis

  • vascular injury (obliterative endarteritis, hyalinization, telangiectasia), submucosal fibrosis, ischaemic mucosal change, crypt distortion

Mgmt

  • dietitian review, proctosedyl (gel or suppository), Suculfrate enema, gastro rv, scope + APC for bleeding

Colon

Epi/RF: same as rectal · Molecular

Clinic page: Rectum and colon

Epi/RF: same as rectal

Types of polyp

Types

  • Adenomatous polyp ~ 30%, have malignant potential
    • Tubulovillous
    • Tubular
    • Villous – highest malignant potential
  • Serrated polyps (sawtooth)
    • Sessile serrated – malignant potential via MSI pathway ~ 5%
    • Traditional serrated – malignant potential
    • Hyperplastic polyps – benign ~ 60% of all
  • Inflammatory polyps – benign, assoc with IBD ~ 5%
  • Harmartomatous polyps – assoc with Peutz-Jegher and juvenile polyposis, can be malignant but low risk

Macroscopic

  • Sessile – flat and higher risk of malignancy
  • Pedunculated – on a stalk

Risk of malignancy

  • ↑size >1cm, ↑dysplasia, villous, sessile serrated lesion

Carcinoma progression pathway

MSI high – 10%

  • MMR protein deficiency → high microsatellite instability → accumulation of DNA damage → cellular mutations in TSG and POG (TGF-B, BAX) → unchecked proliferation of cells → dysplasia → carcinoma
  • Fast; 2-3 years

CpG island methylation – 30%

  • BRAF mutation → Sessile serrated lesion → CIMP develops → silencing of TSG (MLH1 hypermethylation +/- PMS2 loss ‘double somatic event’) → microsatellite instability → further mutations → dysplasia → carcinoma
  • moderate then becomes fast; 3- 5 yrs

Volgestein model – 60%

  • Stepwise accumulation of mutations
  • APC → increased B-catenin accumulation → increased WnT signalling → acts as a transcription factor → proliferation → early adenoma → activating mutation in KRAS → further mutations such as p53, SMAD2 & SMAD4 → increased growth signalling and cell survival → dysplastic polyp → carcinoma
  • Slow; 10 yrs

Path

  • Adenocarcinoma – 90%
    • Mucinous, signet ring cell, medullary, micropapillary
    • IHC: CK20, CDX2, MUC2
  • Neuroendocrine carcinoma
  • Neuroendocrine tumour
  • Lymphoma
  • GIST
  • Melanoma
  • Leiomyosarcoma

MMR

  • Proteins responsible for repair of DNA base-base mismatch or insertion/deletion loops at microsatellites
  • Syndromes: Lynch, Muir-Torre, constitutional MMRd syndrome

Molecular

  • KRAS – poor prognosis, predictive of resistance to EGFR TKI in metastatic setting (cetuximab)
  • BRAF – worst prognosis, likely minimal response to EGFR TKI as above, but can response to encorafenib + cetuximab
  • EGFR → RAS → RAF → MEK → ERK → proliferation
    • RAS mutation is downstream thus EGFR Ab does not impact. RAF inhibition causes +ve feedback → increases EGFR signalling which can be blocked with EGFR Ab.
  • MSI/MMRd: MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, PMS2
    • MSI can be tested via PCR or NGS, or MMRd via IHC
    • BRAF strongly associated with sporadic MSI high phenotype
    • good prognosis and predictive of IO response (relevant in metastatic setting)
  • HER-2

Screening

  • Std population: FOBT from 45yo – 74yo q2 yearly
    • If +ve FOBT then 1 in 26 chance of CRC
    • Colonoscopy risk: 1 in 1000 bleed, 1 in 10 000 death
  • Category 2: 1st degree relative ≥60
    • Colonoscopy q5yr from 50yo or 10 yrs younger
  • Category 3: 1st degree relative <60yo or 2 relatives with CRC with at least 1 being 1st degree
    • Colonoscopy q5yr from 40yo or 10 years prior to youngest
    • Consider aspirin prophylaxis (CAPP trial showed benefit with high dose)
  • Very elevated risk: multiple 1st degree relatives
    • Same as above
  • Lynch syndrome: annual colonoscopy from 25yo, aspirin prophylaxis
  • FAP: annual colonoscopy from 10yo, prophylactic colectomy in late teens
  • Other modalities: capsule endoscopy, CT colonography, flexi-sigmoidoscopy
  • Genetics referral for elevated risk and syndromes

Mgmt

Surgery

  • Hemicolectomy
  • Synoptic pathology: tumour size, depth, grade, LVSI, tumour budding, number of harvested nodes, positive nodes, size, ENE, margins, MMR, BRAF status
  • In MMRd neoadjuvant doublet IO had major pathologic response in 70% (NICHE)

Adjuvant systemic therapy

  • Indications: T4, N+, <12 nodes, G3, LVSI, high tumour budding, R1+
  • FOLFOX or CAPOX chemotherapy for 3-6 months
  • ctDNA can aid in estimating risk in the adjuvant setting and avoid chemotherapy
  • Adjuvant aspirin in PI3Km and exercise in all has been shown to reduce recurrence
  • MMRd/MSI high has a good prognosis and chemo can be avoided unless very high risk

Metastatic

Lt vs Rt sided ca

  • Lt sided more likely to occur through Volgestein’s model, hence have KRAS mutation
  • Rt sided more likely to be MMRd or BRAF
  • If left sided RAS WT → EGFR Ab + doublet chemo
  • Rt sided BRAFm → BRAFi + EGFRab + chemo
  • Rt sided no mutations → bev + chemo

Anal

Epidemiology · Differential diagnosis · Management · EBRT Dose: 54Gy/45Gy/30# OR · Volumes

Clinic page: Anal canal

Epidemiology

I: Rare

A: 60

G: F>M

R: Developed countries

Epi

E: smoking, ETOH, multiple sexual partners, sexual activity from young age, receptive anal intercourse, AIN, lichen sclerosis

I: HPV

G: nil

I: HIV, solid organ transplant

Staging

T1: <2cm

T2: 2-5cm

T3: >5cm

T4: into adj organs

N1: N+

M1: non regional node, DM

Differential diagnosis

  • Benign: AIN, polyp, condyloma/wart (HPV 6 & 11 → minimal malignant potential), haemorrhoid

Pre-invasive

  • LSIL -> most regress
  • HSIL -> <0.5% annual risk of SCC, 1% annual risk of SCC for immunocompromised

Path

  • SCC – 75%
    • Warty, verrucous, basaloid
    • Keratinised SCC – anal margin cancer
    • IHC: CK5/6, p16, p40
  • AdenoCa – 15%
    • Anal adenoCa has epicentre <2cm from dentate line
    • Unfavourable
  • Neuroendocrine carcinoma
  • Melanoma
  • Lymphoma
  • Sarcoma

Anatomy

  • 5cm length
  • Anal margin cancers arise from the anal verge or perianal region and behave more indolently/as a skin cancer
  • Above dentate line tumours drain via superior rectal vein → portal drainage
  • Below dentate line tumours drain via inferior rectal vein → systemic drainage

5 yr colostomy free outcomes

  • CRT with MMC/5FU: 70%
  • CRT with 5FU/cisplatin: 70%, less myelosuppression, more n/v

Management

AIN

  • Local excision
  • Topical therapy: imiquimod, 5FU, laser ablation
  • Risk of progression to SCC depends on size
  • ~0.2% annual risk for HIV +ve, 0.02% annual risk for HIV-ve

T1N0 anal margin cancers

  • Discuss with surgeon to differentiate between true anal margin (hair bearing skin) vs anal mucosal ca
  • If margin → resect and follow principles below

T1N0 <1cm

  • Endoscopic resection if R0 resection with sphincter preservation achievable
  • If upstaged:
  • LVSI, poorly differentiated, >3mm DOI, >7mm wide, close SM (<2mm margin for canal or 1cm for margin) → adj CRT

Organ preserving

  • T1N0: 50.4Gy/42Gy/28# with MMC and 5FU
  • T2-4 Nany: 54Gy/45Gy/30# with MMC and 5FU
  • Nigro regime demonstrated excellent rates of pCR with NA CRT 30Gy prior to resection

Chemotherapy

  • ACT 1 demonstrated addition of MMC/5FU improves LRC
  • RTOG 8704 demonstrated omission of MMC results in inferior LRC
  • RTOG 9811 demonstrated MMC/5FU vs 5FU/cis resulted in superior OS, CFS and DFS
  • ACCORD trial demonstrated no benefit with induction chemo or RT boost though there was trend to DFS benefit
  • ACT 2 demonstrated MMC/5FU no different to 5FU/cisplatin, maintenance chemo does not have benefit, and tumours may take 6 months to regress. More haem toxicity with MMC/5FU

De-escalation

  • PLATO (ongoing):
    • ACT 3 ongoing – reduced dose post-op treatment for T1N0 anal margin with <1mm SM
    • ACT 4: reduced dose RT 41.4Gy/23# for T1-2N0 anal – 3 yr outcomes show equiv 3 LRC (88% vs 84%) and less toxicity
      • Abstract only
    • ACT 5: dose escalation (59Gy/62Gy) for locally adv T2N+ or T3+Nany – 6 month CR on MRI or DRE did not show benefit
      • ASCO abstract only. 3 yr outcomes pending.

Organ sacrifice

  • If non-functional anus which is unsalvageable → APR

Metastatic: 1st line is carboplatin/paclitaxel

Nigro (1974)

  • NA CRT with 30Gy + 5FU/MMC resulted in pCR for first few pts

Leichman et al (1985)

  • 5 yrs CFS was 60% with NA CRT

ACT I (1996)

P: T2+, Nany, Many

I: RT vs CRT with MMC/5FU

O: Chemo improved LRC (relative reduction in LR by 50%) and CSS

RTOG 8704

  • Omission of MMC chemotherapy resulted in worse local control

ACT II

P: Any M0

I: CRT with 5FU + MMC vs cisplatin AND maintenance vs no maintenance chemo

O: MMC and cisplatin were no different. Maintenance had no benefit. 70% of pts without CR at 3 months will have CR by 6 months.

NB MMC has more haematological toxicity than cisplatin

EBRT Dose: 54Gy/45Gy/30# OR

EBRT
Dose: 54Gy/45Gy/30# OR 50.4Gy/42Gy/28# daily, VMAT, BD compensation with concurrent IV MMC on D1 and IV 5FU in week 1 and week 5

Pre-sim: defunctioning colostomy, bloods, manage HIV with ID, assess & document baseline GU and GI function

Sim: empty rectum, full bladder, supine, arms on chest, frog leg, anal marker, consider peri-anal and groin 1cm bolus, fuse PET and MRI

Volumes (UK ACT guidelines)

GTVp

CTV: GTVp + 10-15mm craniocaudal + entire anal canal including wall

PTV5400: +10mm

  • 10mm CTV expansion for T1-2 tumour, 15mm CTV expansion for T3-4
  • IRF only if direct involvement

GTVn

CTVn: +7mm

PTV5400 (if >3cm) PTV5040 (if <3cm): +7mm

CTV elective nodes: CTV 5400 + inguinal, EI, II, obturator, presacral, mesorectum

ITV: +1cm to anterior mesorectum

PTV4500: +7mm

Volumes

  • IRF only included if direct involvement
  • Early stage:
    • Lower 5cm of mesorectum if N-, otherwise entire mesorectum
    • In T1N0 limit cranial border of elective nodes to caudal edge of SI joint
  • In others sup border is L5/S1 (CI bifurcation)

Notes

  • OTT <7 weeks
    • ACT II post-hoc analysis showed omission of week 5 chemo had worse LRFS and OTT >42 days had worse PFS and OS
  • 5FU/cis can be alternate chemo or 5FU/cape alone if unfit for MMC

OARs

Bladder V40Gy <35-50%, V45Gy <50%

Rectum V40Gy <50-60%, V45Gy <50%

Fem heads V40Gy <35%, Dmax <50Gy

Marrow V40Gy <37%

Toxicities

RTOG 0529

  • IMRT has comparable efficacy with 15% less G3+ toxicity than 3D/2D techniques
