Extremity and trunk soft tissue sarcoma

For grade 2–3 tumours, preoperative 50/25 then limb-sparing surgery 3–6 weeks later. Pre-op RT gives the same control as post-op RT, with less fibrosis and oedema but twice the wound complications. Grade 1 tumours with clear margins need surgery alone.

Decisions

  • Grade 1: wide excision alone (LR <15%). RT for positive margin, recurrence, or where salvage surgery would not be possible.
  • Grade 2–3: pre-op 50/25 → surgery. Hypofractionated 42.75/15 (HYPORT-STS) or 5-fraction schedules (UCLA, Poland) are options.
  • Post-op boost after pre-op RT: not proven to help (no randomised trial). Consider 16–18 Gy for R1 and 20–26 Gy for R2.
  • Post-op RT (if not given pre-op): 50 Gy then cone-down to 60–66 Gy; ≥64 Gy for positive margins. Indicated for margin <1 cm; consider for >10 cm.
  • Chemo: doxorubicin + ifosfamide for selected high-risk patients (meta-analysis OS OR 0.56).

Volumes and limits

  • Pre-op (RTOG 0630): CTV50 = GTV (including suspicious T2 oedema) + 3 cm longitudinal and 1.5 cm radial (G2–3 and ≥8 cm); + 2 cm and 1 cm for G1 or <8 cm. Edit at bone and fascia. PTV 5 mm with daily IGRT (1–1.5 cm without).
  • Post-op: CTV50 = bed + scar + pre-op GTV + 4–5 cm longitudinal and 1.5–2 cm radial (VORTEX tested 5 cm/2 cm → 66 Gy at 2 cm). Boost: pre-op GTV + 2 cm longitudinal and 1.5 cm radial.
  • Spare a 1.5–2 cm strip of limb circumference; never treat the entire circumference to >50 Gy.
  • Bone (fracture risk): V40 <64%, mean <37 Gy, max <59 Gy. Skin/subcutaneous V20 <50% and bone V50 <50% (RTOG 0630).

Retroperitoneal sarcoma

Complete en bloc surgery at a sarcoma centre is the key. STRASS showed no overall benefit from pre-op RT, but there may be a benefit in liposarcoma. Post-op RT is not recommended.

Pre-op RT if used

  • Dose: 45–50 Gy in 1.8–2 Gy fractions (STRASS: 50.4/28). Optional IORT boost 10–15/1.
  • CTV: GTV + 1.5 cm, cropped at bone and retroperitoneal compartment. PTV = ITV + 5 mm with IGRT (9–12 mm without).
  • OARs: stomach and duodenum V45 ≤100%, V50 <50%, max 56 Gy. Bowel bag V15 ≤830 cc, V45 <195 cc (possibly too conservative). Usually one kidney is in the field; document function of the other.
STRASS (EORTC 62092) (2020)266 · 75% liposarcoma · ± 50.4/28 → en bloc surgeryNo overall benefit in abdominal RFS (3 y about 60%). Liposarcoma subgroup: 3 y abdominal RFS 60 → 72% (HR 0.64).

Chordoma, chondrosarcoma, desmoid and Kaposi sarcoma

Chordoma and chondrosarcoma need high doses (≥70 Gy, usually protons). Desmoid tumours are observed first; RT 50–56 Gy for progressive unresectable disease.

Chordoma and chondrosarcoma

  • Chordoma: 72–76 Gy RBE (5 y LC 60–80%). CTV1 54 Gy RBE = pre-op tumour + 1–2 cm.
  • Chondrosarcoma: 70–72 Gy RBE. R1/R2 or inoperable ≥70 Gy.
  • Osteosarcoma: RT if inoperable (70 Gy) or after R1/R2 resection (64–68 Gy).

Desmoid tumour

  • Active surveillance first. Surgery first-line only for abdominal wall tumours.
  • RT: 50–56 Gy for progressive unresectable disease (3 y LC about 80% with 56 Gy); post-op 50 Gy only in selected cases. R1 alone does not mandate RT.

Kaposi sarcoma

  • HIV-associated: start antiretroviral therapy first.
  • RT: 8–12/1 for palliation, or 30/15. Total skin electrons 4 Gy weekly × 6–8 for widespread disease.

Commentary
STRASS was an impressive undertaking for an uncommon malignancy. Concerns about the trial center around its choice of endpoints and radiation planning. The authors are commended for their extensive reporting and appendix, though a few more points of information could be useful. Abd RFS is a broad composite endpoint and is not necessarily validated - its components are enlightening. The sub-endpoint of LR/DM progression during/after RT of 14% seems to be the culprit that led to no difference in abd RFS (supp p6-7), and this was mostly due to LR "during" RT. Are these tumors actually truly recurring during RT? This metric was determined by CT performed 2 weeks after RT. This is too early for response assessment; there is a possibility of treatment effect rather than LR. And 100% of those declared to have LR "during" RT underwent complete resection regardless. It is not reported what subtypes had so-called LR during RT. An important sub-endpoint, LR following R0/R1, does appear significantly in favor of RT and is implied as so in the text (p6). Interesting endpoints would have been LC alone and path response, but these were not reported. Recall that the purpose here is control, not operability - the tumors were already operable upfront, not borderline.

The sensitivity analyses in the paper could be expanded. There may be some benefit with RT in specific subtypes and not in others: on subanalysis of 3-yr abd RFS in LPS (fig 3), the curves seem to separate, but CI crosses to 1.02, p not provided. Some have commented that the K-M curves should have been truncated (Gillespie 2021) and that if done so a p value may have been significant and CI may not have crossed 1 (Scarbrough 2021). Results may also change with longer f/u. Although not specifically powered for this, the exploratory information is valuable for futher investigation. See also the STREXIT propensity analysis here, which showed improved abd RFS in well-differentiated liposarcoma and grade 1-2 dedifferentiated liposarcoma.

Radiation planning. 95% had IMRT. PTVs seem excessive at 9-12 mm. Those with poor RT compliance had worse outcomes. RT compliance was defined as PTV coverage, target delineation, dose received, and treatment time. Poor RT compliance could indicate that the trial could have been better designed, such as with narrower PTV margins and more achievable OAR constraints, that rad oncs were overly cautious in their planning, that RT is less feasible for larger tumors, and/or that better techniques are needed such as protons and arcs. Larger and more invasive tumors can lead to worse dosimetry and OS as well - especially if the trial is not designed optimally - a self-fulfilling prophecy. Poor QA is sometimes a feature of a trial and honest reporting, other times a bug.
Citation
Bonvalot et al, Lancet Oncol, 2020
Haas et al, Cancer, 2022

Management

  • mark skin entry site

1. Surgical resection

Amputation

  • if extent of tumour prevents limb preservation
  • involvement of major NVB
  • dysfunctional limb which is unsalvageable
  • Compromised normal tissue – PVD, T2DM
  • Contraindication to RT

WLE

  • Others need RT to reduce risk of LRR <15% (Rosenberg trial, NCI by Yang et al, and MSKCC trial using brachytherapy)
  • Restrospective data suggests 10% 3 yr OS benefit for high grade (SEER data)
  • Consider SLN in SCARE subtypes

The wound healing toxicity is less so in the upper limb sarcoma

Pre-op hypofractionation

  • Hypofractionation 42.75Gy/15# is safe with late toxicity rates <5% and 4 yr LC 93% (HYPORT-STS, ph II)
  • Ultra-hypofrac pre-op RT with 30Gy/5# has shown favourable toxicity with lower late tox between 4-12%, acute wound tox 30%. 5 yr LC 86% (ASTRO 2024 – Nikitas et al, ph II)
  • Ultra hypofractionation may be assoc with higher amputation rates, fracture rate and worse late wound complication

Other RT

  • Pre-op modified Eilber protocol: 30Gy/20# BD + infusional doxorubicin 72 hrs prior
  • Post-op HDR BT also improves LC 46Gy over 5 days (Pisters et al)

3. Systemic therapy

Chemotherapy: ifosfamide, doxorubicin

  • NCCN and ESMO suggest considering on case by case scenario, beware toxicity preventing definitive mgmt in neoadjuvant setting
  • Conflicting data regarding OS benefit (SMAC MA)
    • Improved recurrence free survival
    • OS outcomes not statistically significant, absolute 4% gain in 10 yrs
    • Later analysis showed ifosfamide regimes were significant for OS improvement
  • Certain subtypes exhibit chemosensitivity: synovial sarcoma, uterine LMS, myxoid liposarcoma
  • Sarculator & MSKCC nomogram to aid decision making

Immunotherapy

  • Peri-op pembrolizumab in G3 UPS or LPS improved DFS by 15% but increased G3 tox- SARC032

Chemotherapy to downstage

Special cases

Myxoid liposarcoma: NA 36Gy/18# provides 100% LC at 2 years, 97% LC at 5 yrs (Doremy trial, ph II)

Path: bland spindle cells, abundant myxoid stroma, variable round cell component, chicken wire capillaries

RPS

  • Most commonly liposarcoma
  • LR after GTR is common
  • RT may improve ARFS in well diff and G1-2 de-diff liposarcoma (STREXIT and STRASS pooled analysis)
  • RT should be given in NA setting as 60Gy to abdomen unachievable due to OARs and bowel filling tumour bed
  • STRASS II trial examining pre-op chemo vs surgery alone pending
  • CDK4i reduced mortality and improved PFS in recurrent liposarcoma. No increased G3 tox. (SARC041, ph III)

STREXIT

Retrospective propensity matched cohort analysis of pre-op RT for RPS. Pooled analysis of STRASS and STREXIT demonstrated ARFS benefit. Benefit more pronounced in WD and G1-2 DD liposarcoma rather than G3 DD liposarcoma

EBRT

Dose

  • Neoadjuvant: 50Gy/25#, daily treatment, VMAT technique
  • Adjuvant: 2 dose levels 50Gy/25# + sequential boost to 60Gy for R0, 66Gy for R1

Pre-sim: ensure scars healed, bx completed by sarcoma surgeon, refer to high volume treatment centre

Sim

  • Supine, limb immobilised and isolated, reduce skin folds– vertically raising leg, frog leg
  • vac bag or TPS immobilisation
  • wire scar
  • 1cm bolus if skin/surgical scar involvement (spare strip of skin to preserve dermal lymphatics)
  • Fuse T1 & T2 MRI and PET

Volumes (as per Hass review – red journal)

Neoadjuvant

  • GTV – on T1 post gad MRI
  • CTV 5000: +3cm longitudinally along muscle fibres/tissue plane, +1.5cm radially clipped to muscle compartment and uninvolved bone
    • Peritumoral oedema should be assessed on T2/STIR MRI and manually included
    • In subcutaneous sarcoma without tissue planes 3cm isometric margin
  • PTV 5000: + 7mm

Adjuvant

Uses sequential cone down technique 50Gy → 60Gy or 66Gy

  • CTV 6600: R1 area + 1.5cm anatomical expansion clipped to uninvolved anatomical boundaries (bone, muscle compartments)
  • PTV: +7mm

Tumour bed

  • CTV 6000: tumour bed + 2cm longitudinally, +1.5cm radially, clipped as above
  • PTV 6000: +7mm

CTV 5000: tumour bed + 4cm longitudinally along muscle fibres, +1.5cm radially clipped to bone and uninvolved muscle compartments + surgical clips, operative bed, seroma, drain sites (omitted in wide R0 low risk scenario or where inclusion would cause significant toxicity)

  • PTV 5000: +7mm

Special cases: RPS

  • DTPA, fertility sparing
  • 4DCT or EEBH or bladder/bowel prep
  • NA RT: 50.4Gy/28#
  • RPS: CTV expansion: 15mm, cropped to bone, major vessels, solid organs, expand to include entire retroperitoneal fat compartment, areas difficult to resect and at high risk of local recurrence
  • 5mm PTV expansion

Verification

  • Daily CBCT with bone match

OARS

  • Pre-op RT major risk is wound breakdown. This can be mitigated by contracting volume to 3mm below skin and avoiding bolus IF no skin involvement
  • Spare 2cm strip of skin to preserve dermal lymphatics is historical, now use V20Gy<100% of limb circumference
  • Fem head/humeral head: V50Gy<5%, V45Gy<50%
  • Joint: V50Gy <50% to reduce joint contracture
  • Bone: V40Gy <64%, mean <37Gy, max <60Gy.
    • If not met consider prophylactic fixation due to # risk

Toxicities

  • Acute: dermatitis, limb pain, fatigue
  • Late: fibrosis, lymphoedema, joint stiffness, reduced ROM, neuropathic pain, fracture, non-healing wound

Kaposi

From the exam summary.

Epidemiology

I: uncommon

A: 60

G: M

R: Mediterranean, Eastern European, African

RF

E: Mediterranean

I: HIV, HHV-8

G:

I: Immunosuppression

Staging

Patch → plaque → nodule

Patch

  • flat, red or violaceous
  • Spindle cell proliferation through dermis, complex vascular channels, extravasated red blood cells

Plaque

  • Thickened nodule
  • spindle cell proliferation into subcutis
  • Hyaline globules

Nodule

  • More mitoses and pleomorphism
  • Lumens become large blood filled spaces

Types

1. Classic

  • Mediterranean & Eastern European
  • Older males
  • Indolent
  • Affects lower limb

2. Endemic

  • African
  • Children and adults
  • Aggressive in children
  • Lower limb but earlier nodal and distant spread, esp in kids

3. Epidemic: HIV assoc

  • Aggressive biology
  • Presents with disseminated disease
  • Responsive to antiretroviral Rx

4. Immunosuppressed

  • Aggressive biology
  • Presents with disseminated disease

Pathology

  • IHC: HHV8 (LANA-1), CD34, CD31, vimentin, D240

Management

Localised disease

  • ‘Curable’ but high chance of recurrence elsewhere
  • Resection if amenable site
  • Definitive radiotherapy: highly radiosensitive: 30Gy/15#, 20Gy/5#
  • Intralesional chemotherapy injection
  • Topical imiquimod, retinoid cream
  • Cryotherapy

Widespread

  • Palliative radiotherapy to lesion: 4-8Gy/1#
  • TSET if significant skin involvement: 12Gy/6#
  • Systemic therapy: reversal of immunosuppression may cause spontaneous regression, ART optimisation for HIV related, doxorubicin chemotherapy, etoposide, paclitaxel

RT notes

  • Orthovoltage vs MV depends on thickness, if >2cm preference for MV with 1cm bolus
  • Use generous radial margins 2-3cm
  • >90% LC rates
  • 50% of tumours will respond but remain pigmented
  • Distant recurrence frequent in localised disease

Outcomes

  • OS 5+ years with DM if treated
  • Limited disease, classic type: survival near normal LE
  • Visceral involvement with irreversible immunosuppression, untreated HIV: 1-2 years
