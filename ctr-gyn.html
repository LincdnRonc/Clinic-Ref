Intact cervix EBRT (Lim 2011; Fields 2025 update)

Contour on a CT fused with a T2 MRI in treatment position. The uterus moves a lot with bladder and rectal filling, so build an ITV from full- and empty-bladder scans or use a generous margin.

Full protocol with SSWAHS structure definitions, EMBRACE II ITV and nodal SIB, border tables for every nodal region and constraints: Gynaecological contouring protocol.

CTV components

ComponentDefinition
GTVPrimary on T2 MRI (and PET); involved nodes on PET / CT.
CTV primaryGTV + whole cervix + whole uterus.
ParametriaLaterally to the pelvic side wall (obturator internus / bony wall); anterior: posterior bladder wall or posterior border of the external iliac vessels; posterior: uterosacral ligaments / mesorectal fascia; superior: top of the fallopian tube / broad ligament; inferior: urogenital diaphragm.
VaginaUpper half if no vaginal involvement; upper two-thirds if upper vagina involved; whole vagina with extensive involvement (plus inguinal nodes when the lower third is involved).
NodesCommon iliac, external iliac, internal iliac, obturator and presacral (S1–S3); para-aortic when indicated. Vessels + 7 mm, excluding bone, muscle and bowel. Ovaries if at risk.

Lim 2011 suggested an ITV / PTV margin of 15–20 mm for the uterus and cervix and smaller margins for the nodes. Fields 2025 updates the volumes for the image-guided era: check its tables for the current numbers.

Post-hysterectomy and vulva

After hysterectomy the vaginal cuff moves with the bladder and rectum: simulate full and empty bladder and build an ITV for the cuff (Small 2021).

Post-op vaginal CTV borders and ITV, vaginal cancer and vulva (incl. inguinal radial margins): protocol sections 6–9.

Post-op endometrial / cervix (Small 2008; 2021 update)

  • Vaginal CTV: vaginal cuff and paravaginal soft tissue, with the proximal vagina (about 3 cm of vagina, or a third of its length); internal target volume from full- and empty-bladder scans.
  • Nodal CTV: common, external and internal iliac, obturator; presacral for cervical cancer (or endometrial with cervical stromal involvement). Vessels + 7 mm.
  • Superior nodal limit: about 7 mm below the L4–5 interspace (aortic bifurcation) unless para-aortic RT is indicated.

Vulva (NRG, Gaffney 2016)

  • Primary CTV: GTV + margin (about 1 cm, excluding bone), or the operative bed when post-op; include adjacent vagina, urethra or anus when involved.
  • Nodes: inguinofemoral (around the femoral vessels to the top of the lesser trochanter), external and internal iliac, obturator; common iliac with positive pelvic nodes.
  • Bolus over the vulva and superficial inguinal nodes when the skin is at risk.

Image-guided brachytherapy targets (GEC-ESTRO, EMBRACE II)

Contour on the MRI with the applicator in place. Use the diagnostic MRI and the clinical drawing (at diagnosis and at brachytherapy) side by side.

Brachytherapy workflow, targets and planning aims: protocol section 5.

Targets

VolumeDefinition
GTV-T resResidual tumour at brachytherapy: high-signal T2 mass plus palpable / visible residual.
CTV-T HRWhole cervix + GTV-T res + residual pathological tissue ("grey zones") in parametria, vagina, uterus, bladder or rectum.
CTV-T IRCTV-T HR + margin that accounts for the initial tumour extent (EMBRACE II gives the standard margins); includes the initial GTV extent.

EMBRACE II planning aims include CTV-T HR D90 of 90–95 Gy EQD2 (at least 85), with OAR D2cc limits for bladder, rectum, sigmoid and bowel; see the Gynae cervix page.

When MRI is not available at brachytherapy

Use Viswanathan 2014 (CT vs MR consensus) or Ohno 2017: CT overestimates the lateral width of the HR-CTV, so use the clinical exam and the pre-brachytherapy MRI to limit it.
