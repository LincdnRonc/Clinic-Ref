Meningioma

Observe small asymptomatic grade 1 tumours. After gross total resection, grade 1 needs no RT; grade 2 is now favoured for RT after ROAM; grade 3 always gets RT. SRS suits small grade 1 tumours.

Meningioma volumes by risk group: Primary CNS contouring guide, section 4.

Decisions (EANO, RTOG 0539)

  • Incidental, asymptomatic, <3 cm: MRI surveillance.
  • Grade 1, GTR: no RT. Grade 1, STR: observe or RT/SRS. Treat if growth would threaten vision or a later SRS option.
  • Grade 1 small, primary or residual: SRS 12.5–16 Gy or 25/5.
  • Grade 2, GTR: adjuvant 60/30 (ROAM) or 54/30 (RTOG 0539) improves DFS; no OS benefit shown yet.
  • Grade 2 STR, recurrent grade 2, or any grade 3: 60/30.
  • Volumes: at least 54 Gy with 1 cm margins historically; newer protocols use 0.5 cm for grade 2 after GTR. TERT mutation predicts aggressive behaviour regardless of grade.

Vestibular schwannoma

Observation, surgery and SRS are all valid. SRS gives >95% control with <1% facial palsy. Hearing preservation declines over time; quote about 50% at 5 y.

Doses and outcomes

  • SRS: 11–14 Gy to the margin (ISRS), most often 12–13 Gy (Gamma Knife at the 50% isodose). Targets up to about 3 cm.
  • Fractionated SRS: 18–21/3 or 25/5 for larger tumours (3–4 cm) or near the brainstem. Conventional 50.4/28–54/30 is also an option.
  • PTV margin 0–2 mm (0 mm preferred). Keep the cochlea mean <4 Gy where possible.
  • Consent: 10 y local control about 97%. Facial neuropathy <1%; trigeminal symptoms about 3%. Serviceable hearing at 5 y about 41–79% (falls with time). Transient enlargement in about 14%. Malignant transformation 0.3%.
  • Full page: Vestibular schwannoma (decision by Koos grade, work-up, SRS planning, consent numbers, V-REX and ACOUNEU).

Pituitary adenoma

Transsphenoidal surgery comes first; prolactinomas are treated medically. RT is for large residual or recurrent tumours. Hormonal normalisation is slow and incomplete.

Doses

TechniqueNon-functioningSecreting
Single-fraction SRS (<2–2.5 cm, away from chiasm)13–16 Gy16–25 Gy
Fractionated SRS21/3 or 25/5
Conventional45–50.4/25–28; 54/30 for aggressive tumours or carcinoma
  • CTV margin 2–3 mm for invasive or aggressive tumours.
  • Local control after RT >90% for non-functioning tumours. Hormone normalisation in 50–60% of secreting tumours, over years.
  • Hypopituitarism is the main late effect (GH deficiency from as low as 18 Gy). Monitor pituitary function long term.

Optic apparatus tolerance

  • Conventional: 50 Gy carries near-zero risk of optic neuropathy; risk exceeds 3% beyond 55 Gy. Optic neuropathy about 3% at 55 Gy and 7% at 60 Gy Dmax.
  • SRS point maxima for about 1% risk: 10 Gy × 1 (12 Gy considered aggressive), 20 Gy/3, 25 Gy/5.
  • More conservative thresholds: <8 Gy/1, 15.3 Gy/3, 23 Gy/5.

AVM and trigeminal neuralgia

SRS obliterates most small AVMs over 1–3 years; bleeding risk persists until then. SRS for trigeminal neuralgia is for poor surgical candidates; pain recurs in many.

AVM

  • Untreated unruptured AVMs bleed 2–4% per year. A first bleed carries a 5–10% risk of death.
  • SRS controls 81–90% of lesions <3 cm. 2 y obliteration 50–70% for lesions ≥2 cm and 90–100% for <2 cm.
  • Dose–response: 16, 18, 20 Gy give obliteration rates of about 70, 80 and 90%.
  • Spetzler-Martin grade 4–5: obliteration about one-third of the time; consider staged SRS.

Trigeminal neuralgia

  • Microvascular decompression is first-line for fit patients (about 70% long-term pain control).
  • SRS: 70–90 Gy to the 100% isodose with a 4 mm shot, at the root entry zone or retrogasserian target.
  • About 90% initial pain control, but 43% recur. Only about 30% remain controlled at 10 y; retreatment helps.

Exam notes

AS's Phase 2 summary, as written and not source-checked. Where it disagrees with this page, go by this page.

Meningioma

From the exam summary.

Epi: I: rare, 1% of population

A: 65yo

G: F>M 2:1, growth during pregnancy

R: nil

RF

E: prior cranial RT, obesity, breast ca assoc

I: nil

G: NF2, gorlin syndrome, MEN1, female

I: nil

MRI

  • Low grade is well encapsulated
  • High grade has brain invasion, bone invasion, oedema
  • Dural tail
  • Hyperostosis (esp sphenoid wing)
  • T1 isointense, T2/flair hyperintense
  • Homogenous contrast enhancement

Other notes

  • DOTATATE PET can help distinguish between mets, lymphoma and meningioma when MRI equivocal

5 yr recurrence risk after GTR w/o RT

  • Grade 1: 10%
  • Grade 2: 30%
  • Grade 3: 60%
  • Recurrent disease and STR are independent poor prognostic factors (RTOG 0539)

10 yr PFS/OS (RTOG 0539)

  • Low risk (G1): 85%/95%
  • Intermediate risk (G2 GTR or recurrent G1 with adj RT): 70%/85%
  • High risk (G3, STR or recurrent G2 with adj RT): 40%/50%

Pathology

Grade 1

  • Secretory, fibroblastic, psammomatous, meningothelial
  • Cellular whorls, psammoma bodies, calcifications
  • Mitoses <4 per 10 hpf, no grade 2 criteria

Grade 2

  • Diagnoses needs 1 of:
  • Clear cell or chordoid histo
  • Mitoses 4-19 per 10 hpf
  • Unequivocal brain invasion
  • ≥ 3 atypical features: hypercellular, high N:C ratio, prominent nucleoli, sheet like growth, spontaneous necrosis (HNNSS)

Grade 3

  • Anaplastic
  • Micro: multifocal necrosis, loss of usual growth pattern
  • Diagnosis needs 1 of:
  • Mitoses ≥ 20 per 10 hpf
  • OR TERTp mut or CDKN2A/B HD
  • Or frank anaplasia: carcinomatous, melanomatous, sarcomatous

IHC: Vimentin, EMA, SSTR2A, PR, AR

Molecular: NF2 loss

Simpson’s grading

0: GTR with resection of dural tail and bone plus stripping of 2-4cm dura

1: GTR with resection of dural tail

2: GTR with coagulation of dural attachment

3: GTR but dural tail left

4: STR

5: Biopsy

Mgmt – WHO G1

Asymptomatic, no high grade features– surveillance

Symptomatic or high grade features → maximal safe resection

  • brain or bone invasion
  • oedema
  • near critical structure – cavernous sinus, optic pathway, brainstem
  • growth >2mm per year
  • >3cm size
  • Recurrent

If GTR → observation

  • PFS 90% at 10 yrs (RTOG 0539)

If STR → Salvage RT 50.4Gy/28#

  • LR 30% at 10 years (RTOG 0539)
  • RT reduces PFS, no OS benefit, therefore either acceptable – Souver et al

WHO G2

Maximal safe resection

  • If GTR → surveillance
    • ROAM trial ongoing regarding adjuvant RT
  • If STR → adjuvant RT 54Gy/30# to reduce LRR – Lee et al

WHO G3

Maximal safe resection

All get adjuvant RT: 60Gy/30#

  • Reduces LRR from 50% to 8% - Cho et al
  • may improve OS – Cleveland data
  • Consider DE to 66Gy for 5 yr PFS benefit of 25% but no OS (includes recurrent grade 2 and G2 STR) – FIRESTORM MA

Inoperable

By location (BOS invasion)

  • cavernous sinus
  • optic nerve or chiasm meningioma
  • petroclival
  • foramen magnum invasion

By growth pattern

  • Diffuse / en plaque meningioma – sheet like growth along dura, often involves sphenoid wing and medial structures (orbit, cavernous sinus) or skull base
    • Difficult to achieve meaningful resection

Definitive RT

  • 60Gy/30# for G2-3, 54Gy/30# for G1
  • SRS 12-14Gy if <3cm and >3mm from critical OARs, 25Gy/5#

Recurrence post RT

  • Observation if appears low grade and asymptomatic
  • Resection
  • Re-irradiation: 12-14Gy/1# SRS if away from critical structures and <3cm
  • Lutetium

EBRT

Dose: G1: 50.4Gy/28#, G2 54-60Gy/30#, G3 60Gy/54Gy/30#

Pre-sim: scar healed, aim to commence 4-6 weeks post-op, fertility sparing, neurocognitive testing, audiometry, vision assessment, pituitary panel

Sim: supine, arms down, TPS head, fuse pre and post-op MRI +/- DOTATATE PET

Volumes

Grade 1

GTV: residual tumour

  • Do not include oedema, T2/flair changes

CTV: not required in G1 meningioma, can consider 3mm margin if rapid growing tumour

PTV: +3mm

Grade 2

GTV: residual tumour + surgical cavity

  • Do not include oedema, T2/flair changes

CTV: +10mm, limit expansion into brain if no brain invasion

PTV 5400: +5mm

Grade 3

GTV: residual tumour + surgical cavity

  • Include suspicious T2/flair changes

CTV1/6000: 10mm

CTV2/54000: +20mm

  • Crop CTV to uninvolved tissue

PTV6000 & 5400: +5mm for both

  • Optic nerve, chiasm and brainstem PRV should be created
  • Gaze fixation required for optic nerve sheath meningioma to reduce movement
  • Consider DOTATATE PET after resection to distinguish tumour from post-op change

  • Only include clearly abnormal/thickened dural tail that is clearly invaded
  • For en-plaque meningioma all dural thickening is covered hyperostotic bone should be included

PitNET

From the exam summary.

Epidemiology

I: 10% of intracranial tumours

A: 30-60yo

G: Prolactinoma – F, others M

R: nil

RF

E: prior cranial RT

I: nil

G: MEN1, AIP mutation

I: nil

Size

Picoadenoma <3mm

Microadenoma <10mm

Macroadenoma >10mm

Giant >40mm

Sx

  • Bitemporal hemianopia, headaches
  • Functioning → hormonal sx

Stalk effect

  • Pituitary stalk compression → interrupts hypothalamic dopamine delivery to lactotrophs → causing mild–moderate hyperprolactinaemia
  • Helps distinguish a non-functioning macroadenoma with stalk effect from a true prolactinoma, where prolactin is much higher
  • Other: DI from lack of ADH

Classification

Functioning ~ 65%

  • Prolactinoma most common – galactorrhea, amenorrhea, infertlity
  • Somatotroph (GH, IGF-1) – acromegaly, macroglossia, OSA, HTN, DM
  • Corticotroph – Cushing disease, abnormal dex suppression test, high 24hr urinary cortisol
  • Thyrotroph

Non-functioning ~35%

  • Mass effect, hypopituitarism

Pathology

IHC: synaptophysin, chromogranin, Tpit Aggressive features

  • increasing Ki67 >3%
  • mitotic activity
  • invasion to cavernous sinus or bone
  • rapid growth

Pituitary carcinoma – rare

  • defined by CSF or systemic mets

IHC: T-pit, pit1

KNOSP grading

  • cavernous sinus invasion, used to determine resectability

Management

All

  • formal visual field testing
  • baseline pituitary panel: prolactin, IGF-1, dex suppression test for ACTH, early morning cortisol, 24 hour urinary cortisol, TFT, FSH/LH

Prolactinoma: dopamine agonists (cabergoline) are able to reduce functioning and shrink the tumour over weeks-short months

All other functioning – resect or RT

If ongoing functional

  • ACTH: ketaconazole (high) or hydrocortisone (low)

Surgery: trans-sphenoidal resection

RT

  • Indications:
    • Symptomatic gross residual disease post-op
    • Symptomatic recurrent disease
    • Symptomatic inoperable tumour (KNOSP grade)
    • Cavernous sinus residual
    • Aggressive pathology/high Ki67
    • compression of CN/optics that is unresectable
  • SRS: 18-20Gy/1# if small, >3mm from optic structures
  • 50.4Gy-54Gy/28-30# for others. Higher dose for hypersecreting
  • GTV → +5mm to CTV if aggressive tumour → +3mm to PTV
  • Late tox:
    • Can cause hypopituitarism in 30-50%, increases over time
    • Optic neuropathy
    • Neurocognitive effects
    • 2nd malignancy
  • >90% LC with RT, some may shrink over years, hormonal normalisation can take up to 5 years

AVM

From the exam summary.

Epidemiology

I: Rare

A: 20-40y

G: M

R: nil

RF

E: prior haemorrhage ↑ re-bleed risk

I:

G: Hereditary haemorrhagic telangectasia (HHT)

I:

Sx

  • Intracranial – most commonly presents with haemorrhage – neurology, seizure
  • Spinal – gradual progressive myelopathy

Risk

  • 2-3% annual bleeding risk
  • Risk increases if:
  • Exclusive deep venous drainage
  • Prior bleed (4-5%)

Pathophysiology

  1. Congenital abnormal connection between artery and vein without intervening capillary bed
  2. High pressure flow → tension on vessel walls
  3. Exclusive deep venous outflow increases haemorrhage risk further

RT

  • Gradual obliteration of nidus:
  • Vessel thickening
  • Progressive thrombosis
  • Endothelial cell damage
  • Response period of up to 2-3 yrs
  • Haemorrhage risk remains until obliteration

Speltzer-Martin supplemental grading

  • Predicts surgical morbidity/mortality
  • Age, size, eloquent vs non-eloquent area, superficial vs deep venous drainage, prior bleed, compaction of nidus
  • Score of ≥6 is high risk → surgery not recommended

Management

Speltzer-Martin <6

  • Surgery
  • Offers immediate cure

Inoperable, <3.5cm

  • SRS: 16Gy-20Gy/1#
  • 16Gy for >3cm, 20Gy for <3cm
  • 25Gy/5# for large/close to OAR
  • 70-80% obliteration rate. For larger ones, success rate drops from 30-70%
  • Can take 3-5 years for obliteration

Speltzer-Martin grade VI+

  • Conservative/palliative approach

Endovascular embolization

  • Adjunct to surgery or SRS to reduce nidus flow and size

SRS

Dose: 20Gy/1#, SRS as per ICRU 91

Pre-sim: MRI, discuss at MDT with Nsx, high volume centre

Sim: supine, arms down, TPS for head, fuse MRI OR on treat on MR linac

Volumes: Nidus

Toxicities

Acute: oedema, headaches, seizure

Late: radiation necrosis, neurological deficit, cyst formation
