Meningioma

Observe small asymptomatic grade 1 tumours. After gross total resection, grade 1 needs no RT; grade 2 is now favoured for RT after ROAM; grade 3 always gets RT. SRS suits small grade 1 tumours.

Meningioma volumes by risk group: CNS contouring protocol, section 4.

Decisions (EANO, RTOG 0539)

  • Incidental, asymptomatic, <3 cm: MRI surveillance.
  • Grade 1, GTR: no RT. Grade 1, STR: observe or RT/SRS. Treat if growth would threaten vision or a later SRS option.
  • Grade 1 small, primary or residual: SRS 12.5–16 Gy or 25/5.
  • Grade 2, GTR: adjuvant 60/30 (ROAM) or 54/30 (RTOG 0539) improves DFS; no OS benefit shown yet.
  • Grade 2 STR, recurrent grade 2, or any grade 3: 60/30.
  • Volumes: at least 54 Gy with 1 cm margins historically; newer protocols use 0.5 cm for grade 2 after GTR. TERT mutation predicts aggressive behaviour regardless of grade.

Vestibular schwannoma

Observation, surgery and SRS are all valid. SRS gives >95% control with <1% facial palsy. Hearing preservation declines over time; quote about 50% at 5 y.

Doses and outcomes

  • SRS: 11–14 Gy to the margin (ISRS), most often 12–13 Gy (Gamma Knife at the 50% isodose). Targets up to about 3 cm.
  • Fractionated SRS: 18–21/3 or 25/5 for larger tumours (3–4 cm) or near the brainstem. Conventional 50.4/28–54/30 is also an option.
  • PTV margin 0–2 mm (0 mm preferred). Keep the cochlea mean <4 Gy where possible.
  • Consent: 10 y local control about 97%. Facial neuropathy <1%; trigeminal symptoms about 3%. Serviceable hearing at 5 y about 41–79% (falls with time). Transient enlargement in about 14%. Malignant transformation 0.3%.
  • Full page: Vestibular schwannoma (decision by Koos grade, work-up, SRS planning, consent numbers, V-REX and ACOUNEU).

Pituitary adenoma

Transsphenoidal surgery comes first; prolactinomas are treated medically. RT is for large residual or recurrent tumours. Hormonal normalisation is slow and incomplete.

  • CTV margin 2–3 mm for invasive or aggressive tumours.
  • Local control after RT >90% for non-functioning tumours. Hormone normalisation in 50–60% of secreting tumours, over years.
  • Hypopituitarism is the main late effect (GH deficiency from as low as 18 Gy). Monitor pituitary function long term.

Optic apparatus tolerance

  • Conventional: 50 Gy carries near-zero risk of optic neuropathy; risk exceeds 3% beyond 55 Gy. Optic neuropathy about 3% at 55 Gy and 7% at 60 Gy Dmax.
  • SRS point maxima for about 1% risk: 10 Gy × 1 (12 Gy considered aggressive), 20 Gy/3, 25 Gy/5.
  • More conservative thresholds: <8 Gy/1, 15.3 Gy/3, 23 Gy/5.

AVM and trigeminal neuralgia

SRS obliterates most small AVMs over 1–3 years; bleeding risk persists until then. SRS for trigeminal neuralgia is for poor surgical candidates; pain recurs in many.

AVM

  • Untreated unruptured AVMs bleed 2–4% per year. A first bleed carries a 5–10% risk of death.
  • SRS controls 81–90% of lesions <3 cm. 2 y obliteration 50–70% for lesions ≥2 cm and 90–100% for <2 cm.
  • Dose–response: 16, 18, 20 Gy give obliteration rates of about 70, 80 and 90%.
  • Spetzler-Martin grade 4–5: obliteration about one-third of the time; consider staged SRS.

Trigeminal neuralgia

  • Microvascular decompression is first-line for fit patients (about 70% long-term pain control).
  • SRS: 70–90 Gy to the 100% isodose with a 4 mm shot, at the root entry zone or retrogasserian target.
  • About 90% initial pain control, but 43% recur. Only about 30% remain controlled at 10 y; retreatment helps.
