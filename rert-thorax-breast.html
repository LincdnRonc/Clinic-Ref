High-dose thoracic reirradiation for NSCLC

ESTRO 2026 guideline, ARS appropriate use criteria and the 2021 Delphi.

Selection and work-up (ESTRO 2026)

  • Curative intent: ECOG 0–1, life expectancy ≥6 months, ≥6 months since the prior thoracic course.
  • Widespread metastases exclude curative reRT; limited metastases by oligometastatic principles.
  • Interstitial lung disease: highly selected cases only. Prior or persistent grade ≥3 toxicity: weigh critically.
  • Larger and ultracentral tumours carry more toxicity.
  • Work-up: FDG-PET/CT, contrast CT chest, MRI brain; pathological confirmation when feasible; lung function tests. No absolute lung function cut-off (Rulach 2021).
  • Palliative reirradiation is generally safe for symptom control.

Alternatives66 Gy/33, 60 Gy/15, twice-daily or CHART-type schedules; 60 Gy/15 needs particular care
  • Aim for at least 50 Gy EQD2 to the target; reduce PTV coverage if an OAR limit would be exceeded.
  • PTV margin ≤5 mm; no isotropic GTV-to-CTV margin; no elective nodal irradiation.
  • VMAT or IMRT/SBRT with type B or C algorithms; daily online volumetric imaging.
  • Cumulative assessment in EQD2; rigid then deformable registration; visual check of the fusion is mandatory.

Derived largely from conventionally fractionated reirradiation series. Dose-limiting toxicities: acute oesophagitis and pneumonitis; late pulmonary, cardiac, oesophageal and brachial plexus injury; fatal haemorrhage and airway necrosis from vessel and bronchial maximum doses.

Systemic therapy, toxicity and follow-up

  • Concurrent chemotherapy may improve control but increases severe toxicity, especially oesophagitis; best kept for locoregional nodal recurrence.
  • Immunotherapy: sequential, preferably in a trial. Osimertinib with thoracic RT carries a higher pneumonitis risk (see targeted therapy and RT).
  • Quoted risk from the literature: grade 3 pneumonitis 5–23%; grade 5 toxicity up to 20% depending on technique and location (Rulach 2021).
  • Delphi limits (Rulach 2021): cord 60 Gy desirable, 67.5 Gy maximum if the first course gave ≤50 Gy; brachial plexus 80 Gy, 95 Gy if more than 2 years apart; aorta 115 Gy; pulmonary artery 110 Gy; heart and skin as low as achievable. Four sets are compared on the limits page.
  • Follow-up: review at 4–6 weeks; CT chest every 3–4 months for 2 years, then 6-monthly to 5 years.

Sources: Andratschke N et al. Radiother Oncol 2026;222:111707; Simone CB et al. ARS AUC executive summary; Rulach R et al. Adv Radiat Oncol 2021;6:100653.

Breast and chest wall

Second breast conservation with partial-breast reirradiation, and chest wall recurrence.

Second breast conservation: NRG/RTOG 1014

  • Eligibility: in-breast recurrence >1 year after whole-breast RT, <3 cm, unifocal on MRI, no skin involvement, excised with negative margins, clinically node negative.
  • 45 Gy in 30 fractions of 1.5 Gy twice daily over 15 days, 3D-conformal partial breast; CTV = cavity + 1.5 cm; start within 9 weeks of surgery.
  • 58 patients, median follow-up 5.5 years: 5-year in-breast recurrence 5%; mastectomy 10%.
  • Late treatment-related grade 3 events 7% (fibrosis, breast atrophy and pain, infection); grade 2 in 26%.

Brachytherapy and European data

  • GEC-ESTRO multicentre analysis of lumpectomy plus accelerated partial-breast reirradiation (Hannoun-Levi 2023): 5-year second local relapse 4%; distant metastasis 7%. The TAM score (time interval, APBI risk group, molecular subtype) was prognostic.
  • Matched comparison with salvage mastectomy: no difference in survival or third breast events (Hannoun-Levi 2021).
  • Third in-breast event about 10% after second conservation with reirradiation, similar to mastectomy, against about 20% after lumpectomy alone (Montagne 2020 review).
  • Multicatheter interstitial HDR is the largest experience; doses such as 32 Gy in 8 or 34 Gy in 10 fractions twice daily are used. Verify

Chest wall and regional recurrence

  • Combine surgery, reirradiation and systemic therapy where possible; reirradiation should be strongly considered for high risk of second recurrence or unresectable disease (DEGRO, Harms 2016).
  • Partial chest wall or scar reRT of 45 Gy in 1.8 Gy fractions gave high local control with low skin toxicity in a German series.
  • Hyperthermia with reirradiation is used in specialist centres for unresectable chest wall disease (large Dutch and German series). Not widely available in Australia. Verify
  • Limits to respect: skin and ribs, brachial plexus (see limits), heart and lung for left-sided fields.

Sources: Arthur DW et al. JAMA Oncol 2020;6:75–82; Arthur DW et al. IJROBP 2017 (toxicity); Hannoun-Levi JM et al. IJROBP 2023 and 2021; Montagne L et al. 2020; Harms W et al. Strahlenther Onkol 2016.
