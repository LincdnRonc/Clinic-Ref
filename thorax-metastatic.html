Metastatic NSCLC: where radiotherapy fits

"1–5 metastases" is not a disease state. Decide by driver status, the systemic therapy backbone, how the disease is behaving and the intent of RT: comprehensive ablation of limited disease, selective ablation to keep an effective drug going, or palliation.

Roles for radiotherapy

  • Consolidation of oligometastatic disease (all sites ± primary): strongest case is EGFR-mutant disease responding to a TKI (SINDAS, NROG-002, NORTHSTAR). See Driver-positive.
  • Driver-negative, immunotherapy era: be selective. NRG-LU002 and LONESTAR showed no benefit from routine consolidation. See Driver-negative.
  • Oligoprogression: SBRT to the progressing sites while continuing the current drug, to delay a systemic switch (CURB: benefit in NSCLC). See Oligoprogression.
  • Palliation: thoracic symptoms, bone, brain. Fractionation by performance status. See Palliative.
  • Brain metastases: not counted as oligometastases; managed separately (SRS, surgery, WBRT). Treated brain metastases were allowed on LU002. See Brain metastases.

Definitions and sequencing

  • Stage first: PET/CT and MRI brain. Tissue NGS (± ctDNA for speed) and PD-L1 for everyone.
  • Synchronous oligometastatic (EORTC consensus, Dingemans 2019): ≤5 metastases in ≤3 organs after PET/CT and MRI brain.
  • ARS appropriate use (2021): oligometastatic ≤3 deposits (not counting the primary), de novo or after 2–3 months of first-line therapy, all amenable to RT. Oligoprogression ≤3 discrete progressing sites on systemic therapy. Consider 4–5 sites case by case.
  • ASTRO/ESTRO guideline (2023): definitive local therapy only if technically feasible and safe to all sites, ≤5 distinct sites (most trial patients had 1–2). For synchronous disease, give ≥3 months of systemic therapy before definitive local therapy; treat symptomatic sites (e.g. brain) up front. Restage after 2–3 months (CT chest/abdomen and MRI brain).
  • RT vs surgery: favour RT when several organ systems are involved or breaks from systemic therapy must be minimised; favour surgery when tissue is needed for molecular testing and disease is in one organ.
  • M1a with a contralateral solitary nodule: may be treated as two primary lung cancers (NCCN).
  • Complete consolidation matters: patients who had every site treated did better on NORTHSTAR and BrightStar. LU002 and LONESTAR did not require comprehensive treatment.

Prognosis and selection

  • Ashworth RPA (2014, 757 patients, 1–5 metastases treated with local therapy): 5 y OS 52% (metachronous) → 29% (synchronous, N0) → 12% (synchronous, N+). Median OS 26 months overall.
  • Favourable factors: adenocarcinoma, T1a primary, less nodal burden, solitary metastasis, ECOG 0–1, metachronous presentation, a driver mutation, undetectable ctDNA before RT.
  • Patterns of failure: after first-line systemic therapy, first extracranial progression involves known sites in most patients (Rusthoven 2009: about 90% local ± distant, only 9% distant alone), the rationale for consolidation.
  • ctDNA, CTCs and radiomics may eventually separate true oligometastatic disease from occult polymetastatic disease, but remain trial tools.

Systemic backbone (for context)

  • General rule: targeted therapy > immunotherapy > chemotherapy. Single-agent ICI should not be given first line for EGFR-mutant disease.
  • EGFR exon 19 del or L858R: osimertinib + platinum/pemetrexed (FLAURA2) or amivantamab + lazertinib; osimertinib alone when combination therapy is not pursued (ASCO 2026).
  • ALK: lorlatinib preferred; alectinib, brigatinib or ensartinib if lorlatinib unsuitable.
  • No driver, PD-L1 ≥50%: pembrolizumab alone (KEYNOTE-024) or chemo-immunotherapy.
  • No driver, PD-L1 <50%: carboplatin/pemetrexed + pembrolizumab for non-squamous (KEYNOTE-189); carboplatin/paclitaxel + pembrolizumab for squamous (KEYNOTE-407). Pemetrexed and bevacizumab are contraindicated in squamous.
  • Source guidance is US (ASCO). Check eviQ and PBS availability.

EGFR and ALK: consolidative RT with a TKI

The cleanest modern case for local consolidation. Patients live for years on a TKI and tend to progress first in sites of known disease, so RT works as TKI preservation. Two phase III trials (first-generation TKIs) and NORTHSTAR (osimertinib era) show a PFS gain; osimertinib-era OS data are immature.

Practical approach (EGFR-mutant)

  • Who: synchronous EGFR exon 19 del or L858R with residual disease after induction TKI. Strongest evidence for ≤5 metastases; NORTHSTAR showed PFS benefit even with polymetastatic disease at baseline when residual sites could be treated.
  • When: after 6–12 weeks of osimertinib (NORTHSTAR) or about 3 months of TKI with stable or responding disease (Peng; ASTRO/ESTRO "test of time"). SINDAS treated up front.
  • What: aim for all residual sites including the primary and involved nodes. In non-randomised subgroups, RT to the primary was associated with the longest PFS (Peng; Zhang 2026), but do not use this to choose targets.
  • Dose: SINDAS 25–40/5 to all sites (mostly 30/5); NORTHSTAR primary/nodes mostly 45–60/15; NROG-002 60/30 to primary and involved nodes. See Doses & planning.
  • TKI during RT: Pneumonitis severe pneumonitis was higher with concurrent than sequential TKI–thoracic RT (4.9% vs 0.4%). SINDAS held the TKI 1 week before, during and 1 week after RT; NORTHSTAR let 77% continue osimertinib at physician discretion without excess pneumonitis. ESMO–ESTRO 2026 advises against combining osimertinib with conventional or stereotactic thoracic RT. See systemic therapy timing.
  • Plan OAR-first: lung, oesophagus and proximal bronchial tree constraints take priority over coverage.

Brain metastases on a TKI

  • Small, asymptomatic: osimertinib first is reasonable with close MRI and SRS for non-responders or progression. OCEAN (2021): about 70% of RT-naive brain metastases responded to osimertinib; brain PFS 32 months (exon 19) vs 8 months (L858R).
  • Upfront SRS or surgery: large (≥1 cm), symptomatic, critical location, or long expected survival. TURBO-NSCLC (2024, retrospective, EGFR/ALK): TKI + SRS improved time to CNS progression (HR 0.63) and local CNS control (HR 0.30), with the benefit in metastases ≥1 cm; OS similar.
  • Most brain RT with EGFR and ALK inhibitors is graded "major adaptation" by ESMO–ESTRO. See Brain metastases.

ALK-positive

  • BrightStar (phase I/II, brigatinib + local consolidation): 82% polymetastatic at entry. Median PFS 66 months, 5 y PFS 51%. Complete consolidation, ctDNA-negative baseline and lower post-induction volume (<55 cc) predicted most benefit; number of metastases at baseline did not.
  • Oligoprogression on an ALK TKI: SBRT and continuing the drug extended time on crizotinib (10 → 28 months, Gan 2014).

Driver-negative NSCLC: consolidation in the immunotherapy era

The phase II trials that launched consolidation (Gomez, Iyengar, SABR-COMET) were pre-immunotherapy. Randomised data on chemo-immunotherapy or dual checkpoint backbones (NRG-LU002, LONESTAR) are negative. Offer consolidation selectively, ideally on a trial.

Who to consider

  • Best candidates: low-volume, genuinely oligometastatic disease that has not progressed on 3+ months of first-line therapy, small primary (T1–2), limited nodes, good PS, and all sites treatable. "Passes the stress test" (Gomez).
  • Treat everything: partial consolidation and moderate primary doses were common in the negative trials (LU002 treated the primary in only 31%, often 45/15).
  • Primary dose: Gomez and NORTHSTAR favoured higher primary doses (45–60/15 or full chemoRT). CSWOG (ASTRO 2023, EGFR wild-type, ≤3 metastatic organs): 63 Gy vs 45 Gy to the primary improved 5 y OS 11 → 41%.
  • Toxicity: G3+ pneumonitis 1 → 10% with LCT on LU002, driven by treatment of primary and nodes rather than SBRT to the primary.
  • With immunotherapy: PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors can generally continue through SBRT. Do not give SBRT on the same day as ipilimumab–nivolumab; pause at least 1 week either side (OligoCare). See systemic therapy timing.
  • Ongoing: PRIME-LUNG (upfront SBRT to the primary before cycle 1–2), TOURIST/PRINCE (36/12 or 39/13 thoracic RT with systemic therapy), SARON (UK), SABR-COMET-3 and -10.

Oligoprogression

More compelling than blanket consolidation, especially when SBRT can keep a patient on an effective drug. CURB showed a large PFS gain in NSCLC; STOP was negative but hard to interpret.

Approach

  • Definition: ≤3 (ARS) to ≤5 discrete progressing sites with the rest of the disease controlled.
  • Restage first: PET/CT and MRI brain. Biopsy where it changes treatment (resistance mutation, small cell transformation in EGFR-mutant disease).
  • Treat: SBRT to all progressing sites and continue the current systemic therapy. Use site-specific SABR doses; CURB often used 27–30/3.
  • On a TKI: SBRT may lengthen time on the drug (HALT eligibility: ≤5 lesions, ≤7 cm). Hold or continue the TKI around RT per systemic therapy timing.
  • Ongoing: CURB-2 (phase III, oligoprogression on first-line ICI: switch systemic therapy vs SBRT to all sites), HALT (UK, EGFR/ALK).

Palliative thoracic and polymetastatic RT

Choose fractionation by performance status. Higher-dose courses (30/10 or more) buy modest survival and symptom gains at the cost of more oesophagitis. No concurrent chemotherapy with palliative thoracic RT in stage IV.

  • 17/2: keep spinal cord <11 Gy.
  • Stage III unsuitable for curative treatment, PS 0–2, life expectancy ≥3 months: platinum doublet concurrently with moderately hypofractionated thoracic RT (20/5, 30/10 or 42/15) (ASTRO 2018). Not recommended in stage IV.
  • Central tumours: oesophageal-sparing IMRT (oesophagus max ≤80% of prescription) cut symptomatic oesophagitis 24 → 2%, most with 30/10 (PROACTIVE).
  • Endobronchial brachytherapy: not routine first-line; reasonable for obstruction or collapse, or haemoptysis after EBRT failure.
  • Repeated Quad Shot: 14.8/4 BID over 2 days, q21–28 days for up to 3 cycles, concurrent with systemic therapy, was safe with no systemic delays (PROMISE 006, 2026).

What to expect

  • Symptoms improve after a median of about 7 days.
  • Haemoptysis improves in 70–92%; cough and dyspnoea in about 50%.
  • About 2/3 complete 5-fraction courses and 1/3 complete 10-fraction courses (Fraser 2019).
  • ICU patients: about 30% extubated, about 25% discharged home (Louie 2013).

Consolidation doses and planning

Aim for durable local control (85% at 2 years). With systemic therapy, BED10 50–75 Gy gives acceptable control; SBRT alone does best above BED10 75 Gy. Constraints come first.

Planning

  • Imaging: PET/CT and MRI brain within the restaging window. 4DCT or MR-cine with motion management; inverse planning with IGRT (ASTRO/ESTRO 2023).
  • Targets: GTV is residual disease after systemic therapy. Contour per the thorax contouring guide.
  • Several thoracic targets: sum dose across all thoracic courses (lung V20, MLD, oesophagus). Oesophageal sparing with IMRT for central disease.
  • Prior thoracic RT: see thoracic reirradiation.
  • Systemic therapy timing: see continue, adapt or hold (osimertinib and thoracic RT; ipilimumab–nivolumab and SBRT).

Toxicity and consent numbers

Pneumonitis is the main risk of thoracic consolidation, higher with a concurrent TKI and with treatment of the primary plus nodes. Quote the trial closest to the plan.

PD-L1, EGFR and ALK before consolidation. PCI is not standard (RTOG 0214).

Decision
