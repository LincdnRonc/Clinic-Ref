Prostate: localised

Risk group → management → dose. ASTRO 2026 now prefers moderate hypofractionation over conventional fractionation for every risk group.

Treat if life expectancy
  • Very low risk: only if ≥20 y (average age ~62). Otherwise observe.
  • Low / intermediate: ≥10 y (average age ~77). <10 y: observation, PSA q6m, palliative ADT for symptoms or PSA >100.
  • High: ≥5 y (average age ~87).
  • Actuarial life expectancy: add 50% for top health quartile, subtract 50% for bottom quartile.

Work-up and imaging

  • Pre-biopsy MRI; biparametric MRI may be non-inferior when quality is good. Transperineal biopsy favoured (anterior tumours, lower infection). Targeted + perilesional cores.
  • PI-RADS: PZ is read on DWI/ADC, TZ on T2. Score 4 = <1.5 cm; 5 = ≥1.5 cm or definite EPE. DWI 5 in TZ upgrades 3→4. Wait 6–8 wk after biopsy (haemorrhage = T1 bright).
  • MRI-T3 counts: if DRE says cT2 but MRI shows clear ECE, treat as T3 and exclude from AS (APCCC).
  • PSA density <0.09 ≈ 4% csPCa; 0.15 is the usual threshold. Repeat PSA 3–10 before acting.
  • PSMA PET preferred staging for high risk and above (proPSMA accuracy 92% vs 65% CT + bone scan). 3–5% of cancers are PSMA-negative.
  • Caution Don't upstage a PSMA-avid bone focus without a CT correlate, especially with 18F-PSMA-1007 (false positives), if it changes intent from curative to palliative.
  • 5-ARI (finasteride, dutasteride) halves PSA; double it (×2.5 if on >2.5 y).
  • Genomics: Decipher has the highest level of evidence; MMAI (ArteraAI) outperforms NCCN groups for DM risk. Low-Decipher IR patients (44% in RTOG 0815) gain almost nothing from 6 mo ADT.

Active surveillance
  • Schedule: PSA ≥q6m, MRI ≥annually, risk-adapted re-biopsy (confirmatory at 6–12 mo). Treat for upgrading/progression or preference, not PSA kinetics alone.
  • Favourable GG2 for AS (EAU 2026): PSA <10, cT2a, <10% pattern 4, low MRI volume, ≤3 GG2 cores, ≤50% per core.
  • Exclude: GG3, cribriform / intraductal, EPE, LVI, small-cell or sarcomatoid.
  • ProtecT: ~55% leave active monitoring by 10 y; 15-y CSS ~97% in all arms.
Focal / ablative
  • HIFU, cryo, TULSA, IRE: trials or registries only (EAU strong). Retreatment common (UK HEAT/ICE 10-y repeat ablation 34%; focal HIFU 7-y freedom from whole-gland salvage ~65–68%).

Planning pearls

  • SV: LR none · IR proximal 1–2 cm · HR ≥2 cm · SVI whole SV. SVI risk LR 1% vs HR 27%; 2.0–2.5 cm covers ~60% of SV.
  • EPE is posterolateral along the NVB, mean 2.4 mm, 90th percentile 5 mm. Posterior CTV "horns" are acceptable.
  • PTV: MHF 4–6 mm; SBRT 2–5 mm (isometric 5 mm used in PACE-B and GU005); fiducials 5–6 mm (3–5 mm posterior); no fiducials 8–10 mm.
  • Bladder: drink ~500–700 mL, scan 30 min later. Empty-bladder SBRT was feasible in 85% (EMPTY-RT, phase II).
  • Spacer: consider (conditional); avoid with posterior ECE. HA spacer with 60/20 eliminated late G2+ GI at 3 y (≈10% without).
  • Coverage: 95% PTV / 99% CTV ≥100%; hot spots 7–10% and cold 5–7% acceptable. Keep the 50% isodose inside the rectal contour on each slice.
  • Same-day fiducial insertion and sim is feasible if margins allow for small migration. No evidence for antiflatulents.
  • Spare the penile bulb (ED risk, especially younger men).

Brachytherapy: selection and plan checks

  • Absolute CI: high operative risk, no rectum, LE <10 y, metastases, ataxia-telangiectasia.
  • Relative CI: IPSS >15–18, prior pelvic RT, TURP (suburethral necrosis; keep TURP defect <110%), large median lobe, >60 cc, IBD (needs >6 mo asymptomatic off treatment).
  • Retention risk: IPSS >18, PVR >100 mL, median lobe. Prophylactic α-blocker doesn't prevent retention but speeds IPSS recovery.
  • 3 mo neoadjuvant ADT shrinks gland 25–40% (no further shrinkage after 6 mo).
  • HDR appears safe with IPSS ≥15 (scores back below baseline by 2 y).

ASCENDE-RT: LDR boost ~halved biochemical failure vs 78 Gy (10-y PSA-PFS ~70→85%) but no MFS/OS gain; late G3 GU 8→18% (half resolve after TURP). GETUG P05 (IR): BT boost no better than EBRT boost at 5 y and more GU toxicity.

Counselling numbers

  • ProtecT 15 y (AM vs RP vs RT+3–6 mo ADT): CSS ~97% all arms; metastases 9% vs ~5%; clinical progression 26% vs 11%. NNT 27 (RP) / 33 (RT) to prevent one man with mets.
  • ≥1 pad/day at 6 y: RP 17% · AM 8% · RT 4%. Rule of thumb: 1 in 8 long-term pad use after RP; 1 in 8 bowel bother after RT.
  • Erections firm enough for sex at 6 y (67% at baseline): AM 30% · RT 27% · RP 17%.
  • Bowel at 6 y: bloody stools RT 5.6% vs ~1%; loose stools 15.5% vs ~12–13%.
  • About half of potent men get new ED after RP, a little under half after EBRT; PDE5 inhibitor or Trimix restores function in ~3/4.
  • PACE-A: SBRT preserved continence and sexual function vs RP through 5 y.
  • Acute toxicity peaks ~3–4 wk with SBRT/hypofx vs ~6–8 wk with conventional.
  • Typical late G3: EBRT GU ≤2–5%, GI ≤1%; LDR late G3 GU up to 19% (ASCENDE).

Prostate: post-op and relapse

Early salvage has replaced routine adjuvant RT. ADT with SRT is decided by PSA level, grade and biomarkers.

PSA definitions

  • After RP: BCR = PSA ≥0.2, confirmed ≥0.2 (AUA/ASTRO); NCCN: detectable with ≥2 rises. PSA half-life 2–3 d, so it should be undetectable by ~2 wk.
  • Persistent PSA (5–20% of RPs) → ~74% progress. Get PSMA PET; benefit of SRT not proven by RCT. Consider aggressive multimodality if high PSA, GG4+, pT3b.
  • After RT (Phoenix): nadir + 2 ng/mL. Nadir takes ~1.5 y after EBRT, longer after BT; median nadir ~0.5 (CF, MHF, LDR) but higher after SBRT (~1.1) and HDR (~0.9).
  • Bounce: rise <2 ng/mL in 10–40%, median 12–18 mo (true failure ~36 mo). Recheck in 3–6 mo. More common if age <65, higher implant dose, no ADT.
  • 3-mo PSA ≥0.5 after RT + ADT predicts worse OS (Bryant). STAMPEDE: PSA ≤0.2 at 24 wk is strongly prognostic but doesn't cancel disease burden.
  • EAU low-risk BCR (monitoring reasonable): after RP PSA-DT >1 y and GG1–3; after RT interval >18 mo and GG1–3.

Adjuvant vs early salvage RT

  • RADICALS-RT, RAVES TROG, GETUG-AFU 17 (ARTISTIC meta): no EFS gain from ART; early SRT spares ~half of men from RT with less GU toxicity. RADICALS 10-y FFDM 90→93% (NS).
  • Still consider ART once continent: GG4–5 and pT3–4 (± R1) (EAU 2026); pN+ especially ≥4 nodes. These groups were under-represented in the trials.
  • SRT timing: after 2 consecutive rises; don't wait for a threshold (EAU). Most effective at low PSA.
  • Restage with PSMA PET (APCCC: consensus at PSA >0.2). If PSMA negative, still offer SRT ± hormones.
  • Sexual function can take 1–2 y to recover; ART traditionally at 4–6 mo after urinary recovery.

Pelvic nodes with SRT: SPPORT (bed 64.8–70.2 ± STAD ± 45 Gy pelvis) — STAD + pelvic nodes gave the best freedom from progression. Reasonable without a known nidus of disease.

Salvage RT: dose and volumes

  • 64–72 Gy conventional (≥64 minimum): 64.8–70.2 if SM–, 68.4–72 if SM+/R2. 66/33 RADICALS; 52.5/20 also used.
  • SAKK 09/10: 70 vs 64 Gy — no better biochemical control, more late G2 GI.
  • Hypofx: 62.5/25 (NRG-GU003 HYPORT) — PROs non-inferior, but physician-reported G3 GU rose 3→13% at 5 y. 65/26 (SHARE) similar control.
  • Bed SBRT (StereoBed 32/5, POPART 32.5/5): trials only; a third had meaningful QoL decline.
  • Nodes: 45 Gy (SPPORT). Gross recurrence: boost.
  • Atlases: MRI recurrences sit at VUA 53%, bladder neck 29%, retrovesical 18%. ESTRO and PERYTON cover best; EORTC under-covers VUA/bladder neck.
  • MRI or urethrogram to find the anastomosis (urethrogram places it more superiorly).

High-risk BCR, M0 on conventional imaging

  • EMBARK: PSA-DT ≤9 mo → enzalutamide + ADT. 8-y OS 79% vs 70% (HR 0.60); enza monotherapy didn't improve OS.
  • Otherwise don't routinely start ADT (avoids creating nmCRPC). After RT failure ADT can wait until PSA-DT <12 mo.
  • Intermittent ADT is an option for high-risk BCR (ASCO).

Local recurrence after RT

  • Needs biopsy confirmation, local MRI and PSMA PET to exclude distant disease first.
  • Options at experienced centres: salvage RP, LDR/HDR, SBRT. 5-y RFS ~50–60% for all (MASTER meta); severe GU >21% after RP/whole-gland HIFU vs 4–8% with re-irradiation.
  • SBRT re-irradiation: pooled 2-y LC ~90%, late G3–4 GU ~3% (SPARE meta; heterogeneous).
  • HIFU/cryo: trials or registries.

Oligorecurrence and MDT

  • STOMP / ORIOLE: MDT improves ADT-free survival and PFS; total consolidation of PSMA lesions ~5× better DMFS. ~Half still progress by 2–3 y.
  • EAU 2026: MDT within trials / prospective cohorts; PFS benefit but no OS data.
  • Pelvic nodal relapse: salvage pelvic RT with boosts is safe; ~half disease-free at 3 y (OLIGOPELVIS). OLIGOPELVIS-2 efficacy pending.
  • LUNAR: 2 cycles 177Lu-PSMA before SBRT prolonged PFS. DART: 24 wk darolutamide + SBRT delayed progression only while on drug.
  • Node-only recurrence still generally needs systemic therapy.

Prostate: metastatic

Intensify systemic therapy for every fit mHSPC patient; add prostate RT when volume is low.

Poor prognosis flags (APCCC): double-negative or neuroendocrine features, progression discordant with PSA, multiple liver mets, ≤6 mo response to ADT + ARPI/docetaxel, exclusively visceral mets.

Prostate RT in low-volume M1

  • STAMPEDE arm H: low burden 3-y OS 73→81% (HR 0.68). Regimens 55/20 daily or 36/6 weekly (daily had better FFS). No benefit in high volume.
  • PEACE-1: 74/37 (~25% had nodes) alongside ADT ± docetaxel ± abiraterone.
  • STOPCAP meta: <5 bone mets → 3-y OS 70→77%. HORRAD (all-comers) negative.
  • EAU: strongly offer prostate RT up to EQD2 72 Gy for CHAARTED low volume; no prostatectomy outside trials.
  • Post hoc: benefit holds for ≤3 bone mets ± non-regional nodes regardless of axial location, if no visceral mets.

mHSPC systemic therapy

  • No ADT monotherapy for fit men. ADT + abiraterone, apalutamide or enzalutamide (strong); darolutamide doublet weak (ARANOTE rPFS HR 0.54, OS NS).
  • Triplet (ADT + docetaxel + abi [PEACE-1] or daro [ARASENS]): benefit most in high volume. Docetaxel only as part of a triplet (EAU).
  • ENZAMET / TITAN: little added benefit of the ARPI when docetaxel was already given.
  • Docetaxel 75 mg/m² q3w ×6; abiraterone 1000 mg + prednisolone 5 mg.
  • Test early for HRR/BRCA (AMPLITUDE niraparib + abi rPFS HR 0.52 for BRCA, more G3–4 AE) and PTEN (capivasertib).
  • PSMAddition: 6 cycles 177Lu-PSMA + ADT/ARPI improved rPFS; OS immature.
  • Orchiectomy is acceptable where resources are limited.

Oligometastatic disease: MDT

  • APCCC: RT favoured over surgery for retroperitoneal nodes.
  • EXTEND and RADIOSA (SBRT + 6 mo ADT: clinical PFS 15→32 mo) show PFS benefit; no OS evidence.
  • iADT ± SBRT to all PSMA sites: 3-y biochemical progression 60%→21% (randomised feasibility).
  • After ADT + prostate RT, ~3/4 of progressions involve the index mets, supporting their ablation.
  • START-MET (MDT on top of ADT + ARPI) is the right modern question; results pending.

nmCRPC and mCRPC

  • nmCRPC, PSA-DT ≤10 mo: apalutamide, enzalutamide or darolutamide (~2-y MFS gain, OS benefit). Keep castrate indefinitely.
  • Sequencing: avoid ARPI → ARPI switch. Docetaxel after ADT + ARPI; cabazitaxel after docetaxel + ARPI (CARD).
  • PARP inhibitors for BRCA/ATM (PROfound: olaparib OS benefit after ARPI).
  • 177Lu-PSMA-617 after ARPI + taxane (VISION OS benefit), q6w ×4–6, PSMA PET selection; exclude FDG+/PSMA– discordant disease (TheraP). SUVmean ≥10 predicts response. PSMAfore: pre-taxane rPFS 11.6 vs 5.6 mo.
  • Radium-223: bone-predominant, no visceral. PEACE-3: enza + Ra-223 rPFS 16.4→19.4 mo (HR 0.69); final OS 32.6→38.2 mo (HR 0.76), with mandatory bone protection (1-y fractures 15.6% → 2.6%). Don't combine with abiraterone (ERA-223 fractures).
  • IO for dMMR/MSI-high or TMB ≥10. Docetaxel rechallenge if last dose >3 y ago. Regular imaging regardless of PSA.
  • Unfit for docetaxel: severe liver impairment, ECOG 3, G2+ sensory neuropathy, platelets <50 or neutrophils <1.
  • Oligoprogression: ARTO (abi ± SBRT to all sites, low-volume non-visceral) improved PFS and OS. TRAP: SBRT 30/5 to ≤2 progressing sites on ARPI, mPFS 6.4 mo.

Concurrent + adjuvant sequencing beats neoadjuvant-only for prostate-only RT. Be wary of adding ADT in UIR men with a recent MI.

Agents and doses

  • GnRH agonists: goserelin 3.6 mg q28d / 10.8 mg q12w; leuprorelin 7.5 mg monthly, 22.5 mg 3-monthly, 45 mg 6-monthly; triptorelin 3.75 / 11.25 / 22.5 mg (4/12/24-weekly).
  • Flare cover: bicalutamide 50 mg daily started 1 wk–30 d before the agonist.
  • GnRH antagonists (no flare, faster recovery): degarelix; relugolix oral (HERO: half the MACE of leuprorelin).
  • ARPIs: enzalutamide 160 mg; apalutamide 240 mg; darolutamide (lowest toxicity profile); abiraterone 1000 mg + prednisolone 5 mg. Always check drug interactions.
  • Bicalutamide monotherapy: gynaecomastia/breast tenderness ~50%; stop if LFTs >2× ULN.

Monitoring and bone health

  • Baseline LFTs, FBC; baseline DEXA then annually. Calcium 1200 mg + vitamin D 1000 IU daily.
  • High fracture risk (FRAX): denosumab (check Ca²⁺), zoledronic acid (check Cr) or alendronate. Dental review before osteoclast-targeted therapy.
  • Side effects to counsel: libido/ED, hot flushes, fatigue, muscle loss/fat gain, anaemia, metabolic syndrome, gynaecomastia. Even <1 y ADT affects QoL at 2 y.
  • STAMPEDE linkage: adding prostate RT to ADT did not increase MACE; prior CVD, age and hypertension did.

Gynaecomastia and hot flushes

  • Prevention must start before hormones. Tamoxifen 20 mg daily is most effective (≈95% RRR) and also treats established symptoms; more VTE/CV and other side effects.
  • Prophylactic breast RT: 12/2, 12/3 or 9–10/1, 9–12 MeV electrons, 7 cm circle around the nipple, prescribed to 90%. Cuts gynaecomastia (~75–80%) but not breast pain much.
  • Hot flushes: venlafaxine 75 mg daily (~50% severity reduction); oxybutynin 5 mg BD (Alliance A222001); magnesium oxide 400–800 mg daily (diarrhoea).

Consent: second cancer after prostate RT

  • VA cohort (~150,000 men): potentially in-field second cancer 3.7% with RT vs 2.5% without. Bladder 1.8 vs 1.4%, rectal 0.4 vs 0.3%, leukaemia 0.7 vs 0.5%.
  • Relative risk climbs with time: HR 1.24 at 1–5 y, 1.50 at 5–10 y, 1.59 at 10–15 y. Matters more in younger men with long life expectancy.

Managing urinary symptoms

  • Frequency/obstruction: tamsulosin 0.4 mg daily.
  • Dysuria: phenazopyridine 200 mg (source lists QID; turns urine orange, false-positive nitrites).
  • Urgency/spasm: oxybutynin 5–10 mg daily.
  • Exclude UTI with culture, not dipstick alone (leucocyte esterase often false positive during RT).

Managing radiation proctitis

Bleeding-predominant is the most common type and usually settles with time.

  1. Optimise bowel habit and stool consistency.
  2. Start vitamins A/E/C (A 10,000 IU, E 400 IU TDS, C 500 mg TDS; 90-day course) or metronidazole 400 mg TDS for 30 d.
  3. If bleeding affects QoL: sucralfate enema 2 g BD for 30 d (77% improve by two grades); consider holding anticoagulation.
  4. Persistent: APC (treatment of choice; rectal ulcers in 26%), formalin, or hyperbaric oxygen.

Inflammation-predominant: loperamide, fibre/bulking agents, rectal steroids (hydrocortisone 90 mg BD may beat betamethasone 5 mg BD).

Sexual function and fertility

  • About half of potent men get new ED after RP, a little under half after EBRT; PDE5 inhibitor or Trimix restores function in ~3/4. Sildenafil improves RT-related ED in ~60–70%.
  • Azoospermia from 0.5–2 Gy to the testis; testosterone production impaired at ~14 Gy.
  • Penile bulb dose predicts ED (HYPO-RT-PC), especially in younger men.
  • UTSW neurovascular-sparing SBRT: no sexual benefit (≈80% kept erections in both arms with spacer and no ADT).
