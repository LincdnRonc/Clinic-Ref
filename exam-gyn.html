Cervix

Epidemiology · HPV · Management · EBRT · BT

Clinic page: Cervix

Epidemiology

I: most common gynae cancer in developing world

A: 40

G: F

R: developing countries – asia, Africa

RFs

E: smoking, ETOH, in utero DES (clear cell adenoCa at ~20yo), sexual RFs (multiple partners), multiparity, low SES

I: HPV 18 (adenoCa), 16 (SCC), 31,33, 45 (SCC)

G:

I: HIV, solid organ transplant (q3yr screen)

Smoking role

  • causes local cervical immune suppression
  • carcinogen exposure in cervical mucus & DNA damage
  • impaired HPV clearance

FIGO staging

1A1: <3mm DOI

1A2: 3-5mm DOI

1B: >5mm DOI; B1 <2cm, B2 2-4cm, B3 >4cm

2A: upper 2/3 of vagina; A1 <4cm, A2 >4cm

2B: to parametria

3A: to lower 1/3 of vagina

3B: to pelvic side wall or HUN

3C1: pelvic node

3C2: PA node

4A: invades adjacent organs

4B: distant met

Rule of 10 & 15 LN +ve

Stage 1B=15% pelvic, 10% PA

Stage 2=30% pelvic, 20% PA

Stage 3 = 45% pelvic, 30% PA

F/u

  • MRI and PET at 3 months
  • H&P q3-6mo
  • Annual cytology
  • Vaginal dilator use 10min, 3x per week for 2-5 years independent of sexual activity
  • EMBRACE analysis showed combination of both had best outcomes for vaginal stenosis)

Stage III - IVA

  • 5 year OS 70-80%
  • Pelvic control 85-90%

HPV

  • E7 degrades Rb, releasing E2F
  • E6 promotes p53 degradation

Pathology

Pre-invasive

  • LSIL – mild dysplasia and koilocytosis, most spontaneously regress
  • HSIL – mod-severe dysplasia. 5-30% chance of progression over decades

Invasive

  • SCC (80%) – verrucous (very well differentiated, radioresistant), warty (both less likely to spread), basaloid, papillary, keratinising, non-keratinising
  • Adenocarcinoma (15%) – present with barrel cervix. Villoglandular (better prog), serous, clear cell, mucinous (all worse prog)
  • Adenosquamous (<5%)
  • Neuroendocrine – small cell
  • Melanoma
  • Sarcoma
  • Lymphoma

IHC: CK5/6, p40, p63, p16

Adenoca: CK7, CEA, p16, variable ER/PR

Screening

  • CST q5y from 25yo if any sexual contact
  • CST – tests for presence of HPV.
  • If 16 or 18 > LBC + colposcopy
  • If other HPV> LBC

LBC: LSIL or HSIL

  • LSIL > repeat in 1 year
  • HSIL > colposcopy

Colposcopy: visual assessment of TZ as most cancer arises here + LLETZ to excise the TZ/bx. Then annual HPV CST until 2 negative

Gardasil vaccine – year 7 students

Management

Stage 1A – 1B1

  • FS: CKC for 1A OR radical trachelectomy with cerclage for 1B
  • Pelvic node sampling for 1B
  • Non FS: TAH (simple hysterectomy – parametria and vagina left) + BSO
  • radiotherapy alone (EBRT or BT) is a non-surgical option

Stage 1B2 & small 2A

  • Radical hysterectomy (removes parametria and top of vagina) + BSO + SLNBx
    • Minimally invasive surgery has inferior OS to open surgery (LACC trial)
  • No role for NA CT prior to surgery to downstage (EORTC, TATA)
  • OR Radiotherapy +/- concurrent cisplatin – similar OS to surg – Landoni trial
  • Only operate if confident of R0 as trimodality Rx has worst toxicity (GU, GI, lymphedema) – Landoni trial
  • AdenoCa has better outcomes with surgery – Landoni trial

Adj pelvic RT (GOG 92)

  • Sedlis 2 of 3: LVSI, mid or deep third stromal invasion, size >5cm
  • GOG score > 120
  • GOG 92: RT improved PFS

Adj chemoRT (GOG 109)

  • Peters: Parametria, Positive margin, Pelvic node +
  • GOG 109: cisplatin improved OS by 10% and PFS
  • GOG 120 – concurrent cisplatin based chemotherapy has best survival outcomes and least tox vs hydroxyurea and cis/hydroxy/5FU

Stage 1B3, 2B – 4A

  • Definitive chemoradiation + BT boost
  • Chemo improves 5 yr OS by 8% in stage 3 – TATA memorial, GOG 123
  • Consider neoadjuvant carbo/taxol chemotherapy for 6 weeks in stage 2B – 3 – INTERLACE improves OS by 8%
  • No benefit to adjuvant carbo/taxol – OUTBACK showed no OS benefit and increased toxicity
  • Consider concurrent and adjuvant Pembro with CRT in stage 3-4– KEYNOTE A18 improved OS by 8% (compassionate access)

Stage 4B

  • Doublet chemotherapy > single agent chemo
  • + Pembro improves OS
  • + Bevacizumab improves OS – GOG 240. Use depends on bleeding, fistula and thrombosis risk

Recurrence

  • If no pelvic RT – RT to entire pelvis and boost recurrence
  • If bulky >4cm consider resection then RT
  • If prior RT then pelvic exenteration IF no distant disease and fit
  • Re-irradiation to focal pelvis can be considered for palliation

INTERLACE

P: stage 2B - 3 cervix ca

I: NA carbo/taxol weekly for 6 weeks > definitive chemoRT

O: Improved 5 yr OS from 72% to 80%.

Controversies

2 prv RCT showed no benefit, likely due to failure to control interval between NACT and definitive Rx leading to repopulation.

In interlace 80% of pts started definitive chemoRT within 1 week of completing NA CT

INTERLACE uses old RT technique without MRI planning and had low median RT dose 80Gy EQD2

KEYNOTE A18

P: stage 3-4 cervix ca

I: chemoRT +/- concurrent and adj pembro for 24 months

O: 3 yr PFS improved from 57 to 70%, 3 yr OS 75% vs 83%

EBRT

Dose: 45Gy/25# to pelvis + conc weekly IV cisplatin 40mg/m2

OTT <49days

Pre-sim

  • Attend EUA, refer to GEC-ESTRO diagram
  • Check bloods. PRBC so Hb >100.
  • Empty rectum
  • Pregnancy test, egg freezing

Sim

  • Supine, arms on chest, vac bag
  • Place vaginal marker dipped in contrast
  • Empty and full bladder scans
  • Fuse MRI and PET

Volumes

  • GTVp
  • CTVp: GTV + 1cm + entire uterus, upper 1/2 vagina (more if involved), parametria + uterosacral ligament for T3/4
  • ITV: CTVp in full and empty bladder scans
  • PTVp: CTVp + 5mm
  • PTVn = CTVn + 7mm. SIB to 55Gy EQD2 inside pelvis or 57.5Gy EQD2 outside pelvis
  • CTVelectivenodes as per EMBRACE II
  • mini pelvis: EI, II, PS, ob – if N0
  • large pelvis: CI, EI, II, PS, ob.
  • Extended pelvis: large pelvis + PA to L1/L2 interspace – if ≥3 nodes or CI node
  • Retro-EMBRACE identified these 2 factors as high risk for PA nodal relapse
  • extend 3cm above highest node
  • If lower 1/3 vagina → cover inguinal nodes

Late Tox

  • Skin fibrosis, telangiectasia, lymphedema
  • Pelvic fracture, osteoporosis
  • Bladder: chronic cystitis (frequency, urgency, nocturia), haematuria
  • Bowel: chronic proctitis, bloody stool, looser stool, frequency, chronic enteritis, bowel adhesions, obstruction
  • Vaginal/sexual: stenosis, shortening, painful intercourse/examinations, bleeding, ulceration, infertility, premature menopause
  • Other: fistula particularly rectovaginal or vesicovaginal
  • Second malignancy within RT field

BT

  • BT improves OS in context of definitive Rx

Dose: 28Gy/4# using Ir-192, 48 hrs apart

Aiming EQD2 90Gy to HR CTV D90, EQD2 60Gy to LR CTV D90

D90% of HR-CTV ≥ 100% TD

Pre-sim

  • MRI in last week of EBRT
  • Bloods: check neuts and plts given recent chemo
  • Transfuse for Hb >100
  • Empty rectum
  • Ensure total treatment time <49 – 56 days

OT

  • GA, dorsolithotomy position
  • IDC inserted and filled with 7mL of contrast
  • PV and speculum exam to assess clinical response
  • Dilate os and sound uterus to assess length
  • Insert tandem + ovoid. If >5mm thick then insert interstitial needles. Needles limited to 20% of total dose delivery (otherwise risk weak central dose, bleeding risk, greater toxicity)
  • Secure with vaginal packing/straps
  • Rectal tube inserted
  • PCA and patient woken
  • IV buscopan then CT + MRI sim performed
  • Prophylactic abx (metronidazole, cefazolin) + mechanical & chemical VTE prophylaxis

Volumes – as per GEC ESTRO

  • GTVbrachy: residual visible and palpable disease
  • CTV HR: GTV + entire cervix
  • CTV LR: CTV HR + 10mm.

Delivery: connect to HDR. Patient admitted overnight, repeat 2nd fraction next day

MRI repeated prior to 2nd week of treatment

EBRT OARs

Bladder: V40 <40%, V45 <15%, Rectum, V40 <50-60%, fem heads V40 <40%, V45 <25%, kidney mean <15Gy, marrow V10 <90%. Dmax <TD in all.

BT OARS

D2cc <65Gy for rectum/sigmoid up to 75Gy hard max, 80Gy bladder uninvolved, 90Gy bladder involved

Uterine

Epidemiology · Pathology · Management · EBRT Dose: 46Gy/23# with · VVBT

Clinic page: Endometrial

Epidemiology

I: Increasing in western world, most common gynae malignancy here

A: 60

G: F

R: Caucasian

RFs

E:

Unopposed estrogen. Exogenous – tamoxifen, HRT, in utero DES. Endogenous – nulliparity, no breast feeding, early menarche, late menopause, PCOS.

Other: alcohol, obesity. Exercise, breastfeeding and AI protective.

I: nil

G: Lynch syndrome (30-50% lifetime risk, should have TV US screening from 30yo), Cowden

I: nil

FIGO 2023 stage

1A1: NA histo in polyp

1A2: NA histo <50% MMI

1B: NA histo >50% MMI

2A: cervical stromal invasion

2B: substantial LVSI = 5 or more vascular spaces on a slide

2C: aggressive histo with MMI

3A: adnexa or through serosa

3B: into parametria or vagina

3C1: pelvic node: i vs ii for micro (0.2-2mm) vs macro (>2mm) met

3C2: PA node i vs ii

4A: adjacent organ

4B: distant met

2023 vs 2009 FIGO staging differences

  • Confined to polyp is new
  • Grade, histotype, LVSI alter stage
  • Molecular classification encouraged routinely
  • Micro vs macro nodal mets distinction

F/u

  • 80% recurrences occur in first 3 years. Most are distant
  • H&P q3-6mo
  • Consider Ca-125
  • Vaginal dilator 10min 3x per week from 4-6 weeks post RT for at least 5 yrs

5 yr OS

  • Stage III 50%
  • Stage IV: 20%

Pathology

DDx

  • Benign – fibroma, cyst, hyperplasia, polyp
  • Malig – carcinoma, sarcoma, melanoma, lymphoma, choriocarcinoma

Type 1: 80%

  • 60yo
  • associated with estrogen exposure
  • natural hx is simple hyperplasia → atypical hyperplasia → carcinoma in situ → cancer
  • slower growth and spread
  • grade 1-2 endometrioid carcinoma
  • MSI in 1/3rd

Type 2: 20%

  • 65yo
  • not assoc with estrogen
  • arises from atrophy and intraepithelial carcinoma
  • grows and spreads quicker
  • grade 3 or aggressive variants
  • Ca-125 correlates well
  • rare MSI, frequent TP53m

Subtypes

  • Carcinoma
  • Non-aggressive: grade 1-2 endometriod carcinoma
  • Aggressive: grade 3 endometrioid carcinoma, serous, clear cell
  • Sarcoma: leiomyosarcoma, endometrial stromal sarcoma
  • Mixed: carcinosarcoma/MMMT
  • Other: choriocarcinoma, gestational trophoblastic disease

IHC: CK7+, CK20-, ER/PR, PAX8, Ca125

Grade: 1, 2, 3: graded on solid non-squamous component and well formed glands vs no glands <5%, 5-50%, >50%

Molecular

  • POLEmut – trumps all other mutations.
    • Downstages all except FIGO 4 → De-escalate Rx (+CTNNB1 WT) as per PORTEC 4a
    • 5%
  • NSMP – intermediate
    • 50%
  • MMRd – intermediate – poor, responds to IO
    • 30%
  • P53abn – upstages, escalate Rx
    • 15%

Other RF that escalate Rx

  • Age >60
  • Substantial LVSI = 5 or more foci

Management

Total or radical hysterectomy + BSO + SLNBx for all

  • Subtotal – leaves cervix, not oncologic procedure
  • Total hysterectomy removes cervix does not remove parametrium (Standard)
  • Modified radical removes proximal parametria, uterosacral ligaments and 1-2cm of vagina
  • Radical removes parametria to pelvic side wall, at least 2cm of uterosacral ligaments and upper 1/3rd of upper vagina → if cervical involvement
  • Consider pelvic LND if high risk
  • Peritoneal washings and omental biopsy if aggressive histology

Notes

  • Age >60, aggressive histology, p53 and substantial LVSI – escalate treatment
  • POLEmut or CTNBB1 WT – observation – PORTEC 4a
  • If aggressive histology in otherwise low risk consider addition of chemo to VVBT

Stage 2A-B

  • Adjuvant pelvic EBRT

Stage 2C – 4A

  • Adjuvant chemoradiotherapy (48.6Gy/1.8Gy/# + cisplatin q3weekly) + carbo AUC5/taxol 175mg/m2 3 weekly x 4cycles – PORTEC 3
  • Consider adjuvant brachytherapy boost 10Gy/2# if cervical stromal involvement (also include PS nodes in volumes), close or positive vault margin, extensive LVSI, serous, clear cell or carcinosarcoma histo

Stage 4B

  • Carboplatin/paclitaxel chemotherapy
  • Immunotherapy (RUBY trial – dostarlimab + carbo/taxol in stage III or IV improved OS in all, larger PFS benefit in MMRd)
  • Endocrine therapy – progestin and tamoxifen alternating
  • Palliative RT

Inoperable

  • Stage 1, grade 1-2, <4cm: BT alone – HR CTV D90 = 50-60Gy EQD2
    • 36Gy/6#, 40Gy/5#, 50Gy/10# HDR
    • Uses tandem and ovoid or Y ‘Rotte’ applicator which sits within uterine cornua
  • Others get EBRT + BT boost similar to cervix, aim HR CTV D90= 70-75Gy EQD2
  • 46Gy/23# + 21Gy/3#
  • All GTV D90 80Gy EQD2

PORTEC 1

P: high intermediate risk stage 1

I: adj pelvic RT

O: very low locoregional recurrence with RT 4 vs 14%, 75% were in vaginal vault, however sig bladder/bowel toxicity from EBRT

GOG 99

  • Similar to above – found most HIR pts recurred at vaginal vault, EBRT reduces recurrence but has GI/GU tox

PORTEC 2

P: high intermediate risk stage 1

I: adj VVBT vs WPRT

O: Vaginal vault recurrence <4% at 10 yrs. Pelvic relapse <2% in both. DM/OS no diff. VVBT acceptable.

GOG 249

P: Stage 1 HIR or HR

I: VVBT + carbo/taxol vs pelvic EBRT

O: more G3 tox and nodal relapse with VVBT + CT therefore EBRT preferred. OS no diff.

PORTEC 3

P: stage 2-3 endometrial ca

I: adj chemoRT (cisplatin) + adj carbo/taxol x4 vs RT alone

O: 10 yr OS better with chemoRT 75% vs 67%. Stage III, serous histology or p53 had biggest OS benefit. CRT reduced DM. More G3 toxicity with CRT.

GOG 258

P: stage 2-3 endometrial ca

I: adj chemo only (carbo/taxolx6) vs adj chemoRT (45Gy/25# + cisplat +/- VVBT → carbo/taxol)

O: No diff in OS. CRT had less vaginal recurrence 7% vs 2%, and less pelvic/PA nodal recurrence 20% vs 11%. Chemo alone trended to less DM.

PORTEC 4a

P: HIR endometrial ca

I: Observation for POLE & CTNNB1-WT, VVBT for NSMP & MMRd, escalation for TP53

O: Obs in POLE and CTNNB1 WT is acceptable, vaginal vault recurrence 4% vs 1% with BT

RUBY

P: stage III or IV or recurrent endometrial ca

I: carbo/taxol vs carbo/taxol + dostarlimab

O: 2 yr OS and PFS improved for all. Greater benefit in MMRd

Vulva

Epidemiology · Differential diagnosis · Work-up · EBRT · Neoadjuvant dose

Clinic page: Vulva and vagina

Epidemiology

I: uncommon

A: 60

G: F

R: Caucasian

RF

E: smoking, alcohol, lichen sclerosis, VIN, sexual RFs, genital warts, chronic vaginitis

I: HPV (1/3rd of cases)

G: nil

I: HIV, solid organ transplant

Staging

TNM

  • T1: a <2cm stromal invasion ≤1mm; b >2cm, stromal invasion >1mm
  • T2: into distal 1/3 vagina, urethra, anal canal
  • T3: into prox 2/3 vagina, urethra, bladder, rectum
  • T4: fixed to bone
  • N1: inguinofemoral node
  • N2: fixed node
  • M1: pelvic node / DM

FIGO

  • IA = ≤2 cm and DOI ≤1 mm
  • IB = >2 cm or DOI >1 mm confined vulva/perineum
  • II = adjacent perineal structures
  • III = inguinofemoral nodes
  • IVA = upper urethra, vagina, bladder, rectum, pelvic bone or fixed/ulcerated nodes
  • IVB = distant mets including pelvic nodes

Node drainage

  • Nodal mets correlate to tumour thickness
  • 1mm = <5%
  • 2mm = 10%
  • 3mm = 20%
  • 4mm-5mm = 30%
  • >5mm = 50%
  • LN is most important prognostic factor in localised vulva ca – correlated to DM and survival
  • Pattern: superficial IF → deep IF → EI pelvic node
  • If clitoral +ve → drain directly to deep IF or pelvic nodes

Follow-up

  • Close surveillance until skin healed with NS/RO
  • PET/MRI at 3 months
  • q3mo review for 2 years, then q6mo. Alternate with gynae-onc
  • If evidence of tumour progression → refer for surgical excision

Differential diagnosis

Benign – wart, lichen sclerosis, VIN, cyst, lipoma, abscess

Malignant - extramammary pagets dx, SCC, adenoCa (bartholins glands), melanoma, lymphoma, sarcoma, BCC

Path

  • SCC (90%) – verrucous, basaloid, papillary, keratinising
  • Melanoma (5%)
  • AdenoCa (<5%) – Bartholin’s gland – CK7, CEA, MUC1 +ve
  • Adenoid cystic carcinoma – CD117, CEA +ve
  • Sarcoma

IHC: CK5/6, p16, p63, p40

Growth patterns

  • Spray = finger-like tumour projections deeper than main tumour → higher local recurrence
  • diffuse = infiltrative growth with desmoplastic stroma → higher nodal positivity

Pre-invasive

Lichen sclerosis

  • chronic inflammatory process with lymphocytic cell infiltrate, oedema and fibrosis of dermis and overlying atrophic epidermis.
  • 5% lifetime risk of SCC

uVIN (2004)/HSIL (2014, current)

  • assoc with HPV 16, younger women.
  • Multifocal, white, red
  • <10% risk of progression to basaloid or warty SCC

dVIN

  • assoc with lichen sclerosis and TP53m.
  • Unifocal, white
  • 30-50% risk of progression to G1-2 keratinising SCC, can be rapid

Mgmt: Imquimod, excision, laser therapy, topical 5FU

Vulvar Paget

  • intraepithelial adenocarcinoma with large pale Paget cells
  • IHC: CK7, CAM5.2/AE1/3, EMA, MUC1, CEA
  • Assoc with synchronous, breast, rectum, bladder carcinoma

Palliative

  • Don’t frog leg
  • Treat through dressings
  • En face electron with markup
  • IDC for comfort/ease

Work-up

H+P, ensure assessment of cervix with CST and vagina due to share HPV field effect and risk of synchronous primaries.

Biopsy + mapping biopsy if diffuse growth

FDG PET

EUA –cystoscopy, colposcopy, proctoscopy if concern for involvement

Mgmt

Node and primary managed as separate entities

Surgery is cornerstone

Operable N0M0

  • Modified or radical vulvectomy + nodal assessment >1mm thickness or stromal invasion
  • WLE may be appropriate for select T1/ stage 1 cases
  • Reconstructive options can be considered post-treatment

Upfront SLNBx vs IF LND

  • SLNBx preferred if
      • tumour <4 cm
      • radiologically node-negative
      • DOI >1 mm
      • no prior vulvar surgery disrupting lymphatics
  • Unilateral node assessment only if lesion >2cm from midline, small, unifocal, N0

Adj RT to primary

  • SM <8mm (Heaps data is controversial, some data suggests <3mm for SCC)
  • DOI >5mm
  • Diffuse or spray growth patterns
  • LVSI
  • Large tumour >4cm
  • 50Gy/25# if R0
  • 60-66Gy/30# if SM+ or close
  • Positive margin should have re-resection if cosmetic and functional impact is acceptable

SLN +ve

  • If SLN macromet (>2mm) → inguinofemoral lymph node dissection → adj RT if ENE or ≥ 2 nodes – GROINS VII & GOG 37
    • If only 1 macromet SLN consider RT if large primary, large node, inadequate dissection, LVSI
    • Adj RT field: include pelvic node if multiple positive groin nodes, ENE, large/deep femoral nodes
    • Can omit negative groin if one side has -ve SLN. Generally bilateral pelvis is treated
  • If SLN micromet (<2mm) → inguinal node + distal half of ipsilateral EI RT to 50Gy
  • Consider adjuvant chemotherapy for all N+
  • If treating LN: should treat pelvis bilaterally and consider treating primary and mons pubis

Operable cN+

  • Radical vulvectomy + inguinofemoral LND
  • If fixed node then debulking rather than LND preferred to reduce morbidity
  • Adjuvant CRT if fixed/matted node upfront
  • Upfront DE CRT if ENE or ≥ 2 nodes – GROINS VIII ongoing

Inoperable (T3)

  • If nodes operable → LND
  • Neoadjuvant CRT 50Gy/25# with cisplatin → reassess for surgery

GROINSS-VI

Observation study, early stage cN0 with SLN -ve → observation alone → low nodal relapse rate

GROINSS VII

P: Early stage cN0 with SLN +ve

I: inguinofemoral LND vs inguinal and pelvic RT

O: nodal recurrence worse if >2mm LN without dissection, therefore surgery required

Criticised as RT dose was low – 45Gy-50Gy

GOG 37

P: vulva cancer, inguinofemoral dissection with pN+

I: ipsilateral pelvic LND OR 45-50Gy RT to bilateral groin and pelvis

O: Adj RT to groin and pelvis improved long term OS from 54% to 68% if 2 or more nodes, ENE, fixed matted node

GOG 279

P: inoperable vulva ca

I: NA RT 64Gy/50Gy/32# with cis/gem

O: pCR 73% however G3 tox >85%

GROINS VII

Ongoing trial investigating whether DE RT 56Gy + cisplatin eliminates need for inguinofem LND in SLN macromet >2mm or ENE

EBRT

Vulva R0 post-op: 50Gy/25#

Positive margins: 66Gy/33#

Elective nodes or low risk post-op nodal bed: 50Gy EQD2

Post-op high risk nodal bed (macro, multiple, ENE): 54-60Gy

Consider concurrent weekly cisplatin for N+ or gross disease

Pre-sim

  • surgical wounds healed
  • med onc review for concurrent chemo in N+

Sim

  • Empty rectum, full bladder
  • Supine, arms on chest, frog leg [for tissue separation and vulva exposure], vac bag
  • consider 1cm bolus over groin and vulva [may require treatment break or removal if severe reaction]
  • wire scars + 3cm radial margin on skin
  • Fuse MRI/PET

PTVp 5000: +7mm

CTV involved node bed 5400: entire nodal bed including clips, seroma

PTV involved node bed 5400: +7mm

CTV elective nodes: involved node bed + next echelon (EI, ob & II

  • If treating primary + nodes then cover subcut dermal lymphatics in mons bridge

PTV elective nodes 5000: +7mm

NB if treating groin after SLNB+ for micromet then can treat ipsilateral inguinofemoral node and distal half of external iliac to 50Gy as per GROINSS V II
