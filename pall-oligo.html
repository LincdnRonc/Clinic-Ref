Oligometastatic disease: SABR

For controlled primaries with up to 5 metastases, SABR doubled long-term survival in SABR-COMET, with a 5% risk of treatment-related death. Benefit depends on histology and how the disease is behaving.

Bone and soft tissue SABR volumes and constraints: Spine and bone SBRT protocol, sections 6 and 10

Decisions

  • Selection: controlled primary, 1–5 metastases, good PS, and lesions that can be treated safely.
  • Oligoprogression: SABR to progressing sites while continuing systemic therapy (CURB: benefit in NSCLC but not breast).
  • Dose (OligoCare cohort): median 5 fractions of about 10 Gy. Median BED10 was lowest for prostate (64 Gy) and highest for CRC (126 Gy). Use site-specific SABR doses (see Thorax, GI and Spine SBRT pages).
  • Not every trial was positive: NRG-LU002 (NSCLC, SABR + maintenance) did not improve PFS.

Key trials

SABR-COMET (2019)Phase II · 1–5 metastases · SOC ± SABRMedian OS 28 → 50 months; 8 y OS 14 → 27%. Median PFS 6 → 12 months. G2+ toxicity 9 → 29%; treatment-related death 5% (3 patients).
CURB (2024)Oligoprogressive NSCLC or breast · SOC ± SBRT to all progressing sitesNSCLC: median PFS 9 → 44 weeks. No benefit in breast cancer.
NRG-LU002 (ASCO 2024)NSCLC after first-line therapy · maintenance ± local therapy to ≤3 metastasesNo benefit: 2 y PFS about 36 vs 40%, 2 y OS about 58 vs 54%.
EXTEND (prostate basket) (2023)Oligometastatic prostate (≤5 mets) on HT · intermittent HT ± MDT to all sitesPFS HR 0.25 (median 15.8 mo → not reached); eugonadal PFS HR 0.32. Lets patients stay off ADT longer. No OS data. See prostate.
CORE (ESTRO 2023)Breast, prostate, NSCLC · SOC ± SABRMedian PFS about 20 → 25 months (HR 0.79, not significant).

NSCLC, breast, prostate 1

SABR-COMET (2019; 2022)RT in oligometastatic · n=99also in Key trialsSBRT in oligometastatic disease of varying histologies improved PFS and OS as per phase II statistical design. Results are maintainted long term.

Overall results, including power, applicability by # mets and reproducibility in individual histologies, are to be validated in Phase III trials.
Details
Patients
1-5 oligometastases (93% were 1-3)

any histology, controlled primary tumor from definitive treatment (such as chemoRT)

(most were lung, breast, colorectal, prostate. Misc was about 1/3)
Arms
→controlled primary tumor from definitive treatment (such as chemoRT)→

Phase II

➜ SBRT and palliative standard of care (+salvage SBRT allowed)
vs.
➜ palliative standard of care

Doses: SBRT to 30-60 Gy/ 3-8 fx or single fx of 16-24 Gy for brain, spine

In this phase II trial, p<0.005 was prespecified as definitive. A p of <0.05 requires validation in phase III.
Results
Median OS 50 vs. 28 mos
5-yr OS 42% vs. 18%, p=0.006
8-yr OS 27% vs. 14%
grade 5 toxicity 4.5% (n=3) vs. none

Median PFS 12 vs. 6.0 mos
8-yr PFS 21% vs. 0, p=0.001
5-yr LC 63% vs. 46%

11/25 patients in the SBRT arm alive beyond 5 years had no progression

5/25 had salvage SBRT at some point after randomization

Grade 4 adverse effects 0
On MVA, histology did not impact findings
Commentary
Note that PFS was definitive (p<0.005), and OS was nearly definitive per the statistical design. Results should eventually be evaluated in individual histologies. The median PFS of 6 mos and LC of 63% suggests that the improvement in OS was in part a result of continuing salvage SBRT and potentially reduced future spread due to lower tumor burden, and possible immune response. There were more prostate cancer patients in the SBRT arm (21% vs. 6%). A post-hoc sensitivity analysis of other histologies showed consistent results. Although there were perhaps more favorable histologies in the SBRT arm, there were also some typically more aggressive histologies that did well. Note also that results are similar in the IAEA and Oligomez trials. Also note remarkable changes in OS with moderate LC of 63%. LC was different per met location: adrenal 100%, bone 72%, lung 51%, liver 50%, possibly due to low power in subanalysis, or other factors. The results should eventually be confirmed in phase III trials in individual histologies. Consider also that the majority of oncologic drugs approved by the FDA are approved based on PFS, but this remains controversial and OS is preferred. There were treatment deaths of 4.5% in this study, which is high, however there were no treatment deaths in the OliGomez and IAEA studies.
Citation
Palma et al, Lancet, 2019
Palma et al, JCO, 2020
Harrow et al, IJROBP, 2022

Exam notes

Liver mets

From the exam summary.

Pathophysiology

  • PV drains majority of blood supply from GIT, thus seeding to liver is common
  • Favourable microenvironment rich in nutrients for cellular growth and pro-inflammatory cells (Kpuffer cells)
  • Liver is constantly exposed to GIT antigens resulting in immune tolerance and easier immune evasion

See HCC for imaging

Fractionated palliative RT

  • Diffuse multifocal disease, unsuitable for focal therapies
  • whole liver or partial liver can be treated for pain, bloating, jaundice, deranged LFTs secondary to liver mets
  • 10Gy/2# consecutive days, 8Gy/1#, 21Gy/7#
  • eVIQ suggest generous margins – 1.5-2cm for CTV & PTV if no 4DCT
  • Up to 70% pain response with whole liver single fraction RT

SABR

  • Ideally 1-3 mets, combined diameter ≤10cm with adequate liver function (CP B7 or less if cirrhotic)
  • ≥5mm margin from GI organs
  • Consider other focal therapies with more established outcomes in fit patients: resection, MWA/RFA
  • 3# regime preferable (42Gy/3#, 45Gy/3# - 3rd daily)
  • Alternatives: 50Gy/5# as per HCC
  • HyTEC analysis showed promising outcomes with SBRT: 1yr LC 90%, 3yr LC 75%, BED >100Gy assoc with better LC
  • There is established dose response with higher dose per fraction increasing LC (e.g. 60Gy/5# at 100% 2yr LC vs 30Gy/5# had 50% 2 yr LC – Rule et al)

SABR

Dose: 54Gy/3# 3rd daily, as per ICRU 91, MV, FFF

  • Drop dose by 3Gy if not meeting OARs

Pre-sim: fiducial marker insertion 1 week prior, cease systemic treatment at least 1 week prior, DTPA scan if near kidney, NBM 3 hrs

Sim: Supine, arms above head, vac bag, 4DCT or EEBH, triple phase contrast, fuse MRI, anti-emetic pre-med

Volumes

iGTV

PTV: +5mm expansion

  • If using breath hold scan consider anisotropic margin with 8mm sup-inf

CTV: optional 5mm margin if lesion is poorly defined

OARs

MLD <15Gy

See HCC

Coverage

iGTV D100 > 100%

PTV D95 >100

Dmax 120-150%

Daily CBCT with fiducial match

Consider intrafraction tracking

SVCO

From the exam summary.

Common malignant causes

  • SCLC
  • Lung SCC
  • PMBCL
  • Hodgkins lymphoma
  • Germ cell tumour

Symptoms

  • Red flags: confusion, reduced GCS, stridor
  • Headaches, dizziness, facial plethora, facial and upper limb swelling, distended neck and chest veins, pembertons positive, SOB, dysphonia

Ix

  • CT neck and chest + IV contrast
  • Avoid IV access into upper limbs

Mgmt

  • Position upright
  • Supplemental O2
  • Anxiolytics
  • Dexamethasone in haematological malignancy
  • Urgent chemotherapy for SCLC
  • IR for endovascular stenting of SVC
  • Radiotherapy: 20Gy/5# - 30Gy/10#
