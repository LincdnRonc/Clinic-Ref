Wilms

Epidemiology · DDx renal tumour in child · Management · EBRT – flank RT · EBRT – WAI

Clinic page: Paediatric solid tumours

Epidemiology

I: Rare, 6% of paediatric cancer

A: 4yo, younger if genetic

G: F

R: African

RF

E: parent occupation exposure - maternal hair dye, paternal welder

I: nil

G (10%): WT-1 – WAGR syndrome, Denys-drash. WT-2 – Beckwith-Wioedemann (Big tongue, body (hemihyperplasia), organs)

  • Genetic syndromes assoc with bilateral and younger age

I: nil

Staging (COG, post-op)

I: confined to kidney

II: through renal capsule or involving renal vessels. Resectable

III: Abdominal spread

  • S – tumour spill
  • L – lymph node positive
  • U - unresectable
  • R – rupture
  • P – peritoneal seeding
  • P – piecemeal resection
  • P – positive margins
  • Bx – biopsy

IV: DM

V: bilateral wilms

Risk Stratification for FH

Very low: stage I

Low: stage II

Standard: stage III

Higher: stage IV

Bilateral staged by each tumour

LOH of 1p16q upstages low and std risk

Work-up

  • Do not biopsy
  • Urinary catecholamines to exclude neuroblastoma

R – resect tumour first (COG)

E – evaluate histology for anaplasia
N – no time to waste (adj Rx must start by day 10 to reduce abdo recurrence)
A – all get adj chemo except <2 yrs <550g tumour

L – local RT

Poor prognostic factors

  • Older age
  • LOH 1p16q
  • Blastemal predominant histo after chemo
  • Poor response to chemo
  • p53m
  • UH
  • Higher stage
  • Not lung only mets
  • Delayed RT start date >10-14days post-op

DDx renal tumour in child

  • Benign: cyst, haemangioma, collection
  • Malignant: Wilms, neuroblastoma, clear cell sarcoma of kidney (CCSK), rhabdoid tumour (RTK)

Path

  • Arises from embryonic renal tissue
  • Triphasic tumour: epithelial, stromal, blastemal cells
  • IHC: EMA, vimentin, WT-1, PAX-8, CD56
  • Unfavourable histology: diffuse anaplasia
    • Anaplasia is all 3 of: nuclear hyperchromatism, enlargement and mitoses,
  • Genetic: WT-1, WT-2, LOH 1p16q, 1q gain, p53 (unfavourable)
    • 1p16q LOH has ↑ relapse and death risk and upstages pt

  • III: 70-80%

Management

COG: surgery → adjuvant therapy

SIOP: neoadjuvant chemotherapy → surgery

Favourable histology

Stage I

  • Radical nephroureterectomy + locoregional lymph node sampling
    • At least 5 nodes from next 3 echelons
    • Rt sided: hilar, peri-caval, aorto-caval
    • Lt sided: hilar, para-aortic, aorto-caval
  • Observation if: <550g tumour, <2yrs
  • Others: adjuvant chemotherapy: EE4A (vincristine, dactinomycin) for 4 months

Stage II

  • Radical nephroureterectomy + LN sampling
  • Adjuvant chemotherapy: EE4A for 4 months
  • Chemo for all stages should commence 1-2 weeks post-op

Stage III

  • Radical nephroureterectomy + LN sampling/dissection
  • Consider neoadjuvant chemotherapy to improve resectability
  • Adjuvant chemotherapy: DD4A (above + doxorubicin) for 6 months
  • Adjuvant radiotherapy by day 10 post-op:
    • Flank RT: 10.8Gy in 1.8Gy/# (6#)
    • If SPAR criteria met (spillage, peritoneal seeding, ascites, pre-op rupture) then WAI with 10.5Gy in 1.5Gy/# (7#)
    • If gross residual disease then double the dose and deliver as a sequential boost to residual dx
    • If N+ then include PA nodal strip
    • Abdominal recurrence reduces if RT is commenced within 10 days post-op
    • Dactinomycin and doxorubicin withheld during RT

Stage IV

  • Radical nephroureterectomy + LN sampling/dissection
  • Consider neoadjuvant chemotherapy to improve resectability
  • Adjuvant DD4A chemotherapy for 6 months
  • Adjuvant radiotherapy as above
  • Radiotherapy to sites of metastatic disease if residual disease post-chemotherapy or if 1p16q LOH or UH:
    • Whole lung RT: 12Gy/8#
    • Whole liver RT: 19.8Gy/11#
    • Bone RT: 25.2Gy/14#

Notes

  • 1p16q LOH upstages disease and treatment in stage II & III
  • Bilateral WT is treated with neoadjuvant chemotherapy → surgery

Unfavourable Histology (= diffuse anaplasia)

  • Chemotherapy is upgraded due to chemo resistance
  • Focal anaplasia in stage II & III: DD4A
  • Diffuse anaplasia or any stage IV: UH1 or UH2 (vincristine, doxorubicin, cyclophosphamide, carboplatin, etoposide +/- irinotecan)
  • Radiotherapy: all receive radiotherapy. Dose increased by 9Gy in either 1.8Gy/# for flank or 1.5Gy/# for WAI

EBRT – flank RT

Dose: 10.8Gy/6#, daily treatment, AP/PA, with concurrent vincristine chemotherapy (hold dactinomycin and doxorubicin)

  • +10.8Gy/6# seq boost if gross residual disease
  • +9Gy if UH

Pre-sim: anaesthetics consult, discuss at MDT, ensure scars healed, consent with parents, age appropriate fertility sparing , assess renal function, aim to commence by day 10 post-op, pre-med with antiemetic and PPI

Sim: supine, arms above head, vac bag, NBM 3 hours, clamshell testes, fuse pre-op MRI/CT

GTV: pre-op disease + kidney

CTV: +1cm + surgical bed, tumour bed, adjacent vertebral body

PTV: +1cm

  • Medial – 1cm across VB, shield contralateral kidney
  • Lateral – 2cm flash

If nodal involvement then include entire PA chain in flank field:

  • Superior – 1cm above diaphragm crus, shield heart
  • Inferior – L5

Boost (gross residual disease)

  • GTV
  • CTV + 1cm
  • PTV + 1cm

OARs

contralateral kidney Dmean <5Gy (ALARA)

VB evenly covered

Liver, lung heart: ALARA

EBRT – WAI

Dose: 10.5Gy/7#, daily treatment, AP/PA, with concurrent chemotherapy except dactinomycin and doxorubicin

  • +10.5Gy/7# seq boost if gross residual disease
  • +9Gy if UH

Pre-sim: same

Sim: same

Field borders

  • Superior – 1cm above diaphragm crus, shield out lung and heart which lies 1cm above diaphragm dome
  • Lateral – 1-2cm flash
  • Inferior –obturator foramen, shield femoral head

Toxicities

  • Acute: fatigue, n/v, enteritis, dysphagia, dyspepsia, cystitis
  • Subacute: RILD if in field, pneumonitis
  • Late: reduced truncal height, scoliosis, bone fracture, bowel obstruction/stricture/adhesions, HTN from renal artery fibrosis, CKD, infertility (F>M), uterine insufficiency/pre-term birth, cardiac disease (doxorubicin and MHD), functional asplenia, diabetes, second malignancy (most commonly GI and breast)
  • WLI: Diffuse interstitial pneumonitis, cardiac disease, 15% risk of breast cancer by 40yo, soft tissue/breast hypoplasia, hypothyroidism

Neuroblastoma

Epidemiology · Presentation · Management

Clinic page: Paediatric solid tumours

Epidemiology

I: 10% of paediatric cancers, most common extracranial solid tumour, most commonly adrenal

A: 18mo

G: M

R: Caucasian

RF

E: maternal opioid, ETOH, diuretic use

I: nil

G (1%): Beckwith-Weidemann, NF1, familial neuroblastoma (ALK mutation)

I: nil

INRG Risk Stratification

Very low

  • <12 mo
  • Favourable biology (SHIMADA and no MYCN amp)

Low

  • localised tumour to one body compartment without vital structure involvement (stage I)
  • Favourable biology

Intermediate

  • Locoregional tumour with imaging defined RF (infiltration of adjuvant organs, nerves, major vessels, crossing midline etc) – stage II or III
  • Favourable biology
  • OR MS

High

  • DM
  • MYCN amplification
  • Some high risk biology (cytogenetics – near diploidy)

INRGSS staging

  • L1: Localised, no image-defined risk factors (IDRFs)
  • L2: Locoregional, IDRFs present (vessel encasement, organ invasion)
  • M: Distant metastases
  • MS: <18 months, mets to skin/liver/bone marrow only

Presentation

  • Adrenal medulla, posterior mediastinal or retroperitoneal paraspinal mass
  • Urinary/serum catecholamines (HVA, VMA) cause unwell child, diaphoresis, tachycardia
  • Opsoclonus-myoclonus syndrome (dancing eyes)
  • VIP syndrome (diarrhoea and hypokalemia)

Work-up

  • I-123 MIBG scan is gold standard (also used for well diff phaeochromocytoma)
  • If not MIBG avid then FDG PET

Differentiation spectrum

  • Neuroblastoma (malignant, least differentiated)
  • Ganglioneuroblastoma (intermediate)
  • Ganglioneuroma (benign, most differentiated)

Background

  • Arises from neural crest cells of sympathetic nervous system
  • from adrenal gland

Path

  • Neuroendocrine tumour: small round blue cells, Homer-Wright rosettes, mitoses, necrosis
  • IHC: synaptophysin, chromogranin A, NSE, S100, PHOX2B, NB84
  • Genetic: MYCN amplification, 1p & 11q deletion are worse prognosis
  • Near diploidy is unfavourable, triploid is favourable

SHIMADA criteria: favourable vs unfavourable histology

  • S – stromal rich vs poor
  • A – age <1yo vs >5yo
  • D – neuroblastic cell differentiation
  • M – mitoses-karyorrhexis index
  • And
  • N – nodularity – yes vs no

Management

Low risk

  • Resection and observation
  • Optional adjuvant chemotherapy if residual disease with unfavourable factors

Intermediate risk

  • Upfront resection if GTR possible, otherwise
  • Neoadjuvant chemotherapy → relook surgery and resection → adjuvant chemotherapy

High risk

  • Induction: Multi-agent NA chemotherapy
  • Resection
  • Consolidation:
    • Myeloablative chemo and AuSCT
    • Adjuvant radiotherapy to pre-chemo tumour and sites of residual disease: 21.6Gy/12# (no boost for residual disease now – ANBL0532)
      • GTV=post-induction chemo
      • CTV = +1cm, anatomically confined
      • Treat up to 5 metastatic MIBG avid sites – 21.6Gy/12#
  • Maintenance: systemic therapy with retinoid and IO (dinutuximab)

MS

  • Observation for spontaneous resolution which occurs in 2/3rds

Notes

  • Chemotherapy duration and regime depends on fav vs unfav histology with no universal protocol
  • Common agents: cyclophosphamide, cisplatin, doxorubicin, etoposide, topotecan, vincristine

5 yr OS outcomes

  • Low risk: >95%
  • Intermediate: 90-95%
  • High risk: 50%
  • MS: 90%+

Poor prognostic factors

  • Older age >18mo
  • MYCN amp
  • INSG stage
  • 1p & 11q deletion
  • Near diploidy
  • SHIMADA criteria

Ewing

Epidemiology · Pathology · Management · EBRT: Definitive RT · Boost

Clinic page: Paediatric solid tumours

Epidemiology

I: second most common paediatric bone tumour after osteosarcoma

A: 15yo

G: M

R: Caucasian

RF

E: nil

I: nil

G: EWSR1 gene translocation

I: nil

Natural Hx

  • 50% axial skeleton – pelvis most common
    • Pelvic primary more likely to have DM
  • 50% appendicular skeleton – femur most common
  • 30% will have DM at presentation – lung, bone
  • Lymphatic spread rare

5 yr OS outcomes

  • Locoregional disease: 80%
  • Metastatic disease: 30%
  • Late relapse possible – need long term f/u with CXR

Pathology

  • Small round blue cell tumour
  • Subtypes: typical or atypical (lobular, alveolar, organoid)
  • IHC: CD99, PAS, vimentin, B2-microglobulin, FLI1, NSE
  • High risk cytogenetics: translocation of EWSR1 gene - t(11:22) or t(21:22)
  • Peripheral PNET is Ewings of soft tissue – paraspinal > CW > extremity
  • Askin tumour is PNET of CW, has Homer-Wright rosettes

Presentation

  • Painful, swelling, loss of function, fracture
  • Diaphysis of long bones
  • Can be lytic (70%) or sclerotic (30%)
  • Onion skin, moth eaten
    • Onion skin = destruction of cortical bone, periosteal elevation, reactive new bone formation
    • Sunburst can occur but more typically osteosarcoma

Poor prognostic factors

  • Non lung only mets
  • <90% tumour necrosis post chemo
  • Pelvic/axial skeleton primary
  • Older age
  • Delayed local therapy >4 months post diagnosis
  • High disease volume
  • Primary >8cm
  • Raised LDH

Management

Very chemo and radiosensitive

Systemic disease, distant relapse is predominant failure pattern

1. Induction chemotherapy: interval compressed VDC/IE chemotherapy q2 weekly x6 cycles

  • vincristine, doxorubicin, cyclophosphamide, ifosfamide, etoposide

2. Definitive local therapy

  • Surgical resection preferred where clear margins achievable without significant functional deficit ‘expendable bones’ – scapula, certain ribs, iliac wing, pubic bone, fibula, metatarsal, metacarpal
    • Pre-op RT can be given to downstage tumour: 36Gy/18#
    • Post-op RT:
      • R0: no tumour on ink = no RT
      • R1 or <90% tumour necrosis or tumour spill: 45Gy/25# + 5.4Gy/3#
      • R2: 45Gy/25# + 10.8Gy/6#
  • Definitive RT: 45Gy/25# + 10.8Gy/6#
  • For sequential boost – cone down to post induction chemo soft tissue extent but include original pre-chemo bone disease extent

3. Consolidation chemotherapy: VDC/IE q2 weekly

  • Start concurrently with radiotherapy, hold doxorubicin
  • Total NA + adj should be 28 weeks chemotherapy

4. Metastasis directed treatment

  • lung mets at diagnosis: WLI 15Gy/10# with sequential boost to

residual disease to TD of 45Gy or 40Gy/5# SABR

    • If <6yo WLI is 12Gy/8#
  • Bone mets: 45 – 55.8Gy or SABR

L - Linked with RT (concurrent, hold doxorubicin)

L - Lung RT if mets at dx or bone sabr

4 – 14 cycles of chemo in total

I - investigate for late lung mets

N - no staging (localised vs DM)

E - EWSR - FLI1

V - VDC

I - IE - alternating

L - local Rx

EBRT: Definitive RT

Dose: 45Gy/25# + 10.8Gy/6# seq boost, daily treatment, VMAT, with concurrent VC/IE chemotherapy

Pre-sim: MDT discussion, consent with parents, age appropriate fertility sparing, obtain post chemo MRI

Sim: supine, empty rectum, full bladder, separate limb, immobilise with vag bag, clamshell for testes, tape penis at midline, fuse MRI and PET

Volumes

Pre-chemo GTV

CTV: +2cm, cropped to uninvolved anatomical boundaries

PTV4500: +1cm

Boost

GTV: Post-chemo GTV for soft tissue, pre-chemo for bony extent

CTV: +2cm

PTV5580: +1cm

Ensure VB and growth plates are completely covered volumes to avoid asymmetrical growth

Toxicities

Chemo: infertility, cardiomyopathy, haemorrhagic cystitis, renal toxicity, secondary AML

RT: site dependent – skin change, joint stiffness, lymphoedema, fracture risk, second malignancy (osteosarcoma most common)

RMS

Epidemiology · Pathology · Management · Unresectable rhabdomyosarcoma · Sim

Clinic page: Paediatric solid tumours

Epidemiology

I: most common STS in paediatrics

A: 1-5yo

G: M

R: nil

RF

E: nil

I: nil

G: Beckwith-Wioedemann, NF-1, Noonan syndrome, Costello syndrome, Li-Fraumeni

I: nil

IRSG groups (surgical staging)

I: localised, R0 resection

II: localised, GTR but R1 or regional nodal involvement

III: partially resected (biopsy or debulking) with gross residual disease

IV: DM

Sites

  • Can arise anywhere
  • Favourable sites:
    • B – billiary
    • O - orbit
    • N – non parameningeal H&N
    • G – GU non bladder/prostate
  • Unfavourable: all other – bladder, prostate, liver, PM H&N (Infratemporal fossa, Mastoid, Middle ear, Nasopharynx, Paranasal sinuses, Pterygopalatine fossa, Parapharyngeal)

I Make Money Nuc-ing Petite Paediatric Pts

10 yr OS outcomes

  • Low risk: >90%
  • Intermediate risk: 70%
  • Metastatic: 30%

OS for localised/DM

Fusion +ve

  • 65%, 20%

Fusion -ve

  • 90%, 60%

Pathology

  • Arises from mesenchymal cells committed to skeletal/striated lineage
  • Small round blue cells
  • IHC: vimentin, desmin, myogenin, myoD1

Histological subtypes

  • Embryonal variants:
    • Botryoid (grape cluster): excellent prognosis
    • Spindle cell/sclerosing: excellent prognosis
  • Embryonal: good prognosis
  • Alveolar: poor prognosis, adolescent
  • Pleomorphic: poor prognosis, adults

Molecular

  • PAX3/7-FOXO1 fusions are poor prognostic factor and override histological based prognosis
    • 80% alveolar RMS is fusion +ve & embryonal is fusion -ve
  • MYC amplification is poor prognosis
  • TP53 worse prognosis

Natural hx

  • Spreads along fascial planes over large distances
  • Extremity primary has high nodal risk
  • Lung is most common met site

Poor prognostic factors

  • FOX01 fusion trumps histological subtype
  • Degree of resection

Oberlin RF

  • Age <1yo or >10yo
  • ≥3 DM sites
  • Bone or marrow involvement
  • Unfavourable site

Management

All patients require chemotherapy: VAC (vincristine, acintomycin, cyclophosphamide) & BMBx

Low risk

Embryonal, stage I (favourable site), group I

  • Resection → VAC 24 weeks

Embryonal, stage I (favourable site) group II-III OR stage 2-3 (unfavourable site with node positive or large tumour >5cm), group I-II

  • Resection → VAC 24 weeks → adj RT at week 13
  • RT for R1: 36Gy/20#, N1 41.4Gy/23#

Intermediate risk

Embryonal stage 2-3, group III, alveolar/FOX01 fusion + group I-III

  • Resection → VAC 42 weeks adjuvant chemotherapy → adjuvant radiotherapy week 13
    • R0 fusion +ve, any R1: 36Gy/20#
    • any N+: 41.4Gy/23# (treat ISRT with 3cm craniocaudal margin)
    • R2: 50.4Gy/36/28#
  • If unresectable: Neoadjuvant chemotherapy → relook surgery at 4 weeks à
    • Resection → VAC to complete 42 weeks → adjuvant RT as above at week 13
    • Or definitive RT: 50.4Gy/36Gy → Adjuvant VAC to complete 42 weeks
    • RT preferred for organ sparing of GU RMS

High risk

Group IV

  • VAC chemotherapy for 54 weeks
  • RT at week 20 to primary and residual mets
  • WLI: 15Gy/10#
  • SABR to other mets

Notes

  • Consider brachytherapy for unresectable sites such as GU
  • Acintomycin held during RT
  • Upfront RT for threatened organ dysfunction – cord compromise or vision impairment

RT Technique

  • GTV: all disease prior to any therapy
  • CTV: +1 cm or 2cm longitudinal/1cm radial in limb
  • PTV 4140 or 5040: +5mm

Unresectable rhabdomyosarcoma

Dose: 50.4Gy/28# delivered as per ICRU 83 with a VMAT technique using 6MV photons

Daily treatment

Concurrent vincristine and cyclophosphamide (actinomycin held)

Goals

95% of PTV to receive 95% of dose

98% of GTV to receive 95% of dose

Dmax <107% within GTV

Pre-sim: fertility sparing if appropriate, coordinate with MDT such that RT commences at week 13, anaesthetics consult for sedation

Sim

Child sedated

supine, arms on chest, immobilised in a vac bag

clamshell testes

fuse pre-chemo MRI/PET

Volumes

GTV = pre-chemo extent of disease, respecting anatomical barriers (ie uninvolved bladder/bowel)

CTV = 1cm margin, cropped to anatomical boundaries

PTV = 1cm margin

Daily CBCT with bone match and soft tissue check

Medulloblastoma

Risk factors · Pathology · Management · EBRT · Toxicities

Clinic page: Paediatric CNS

Risk factors

I: most common paediatric CNS tumour, 20% of all CNS tumours in kids

A: 5-7yo, 20-25yo

G: M

R: nil

Epi

E: nil

I: nil

G: Gorlin syndrome (PTCH1), Turcot’s syndrome, Li Fraumeni, NF-1

I: nil

Staging (Chang system)

T1: <3cm

T2: >3cm

T3: into CSF or brainstem

T4: out of brain

M0 – no mets

M1 – CSF +ve

M2 – gross intracranial deposits

M3 – gross spinal subarachnoid LP deposits

M4 – outside CNS

Anatomy

  • Arise from cerebellum, most are midline at the vermis → raised ICP/hydrocephalus
    • headaches, morning emesis, papilloedema, diplopia due to CN VI palsy
  • Cerebellar hemisphere presentation assoc with desmoplastic histology in older age >10yo

Figure in the original document (not reproduced here).

Pathology

  • Small round blue cells, neuroendocrine
  • Homer-wright rosettes
  • IHC: synaptophysin, chromogranin, NSE

Histological

  • Classic – 80%, intermediate prog
  • Desmoplastic/nodular – good prog
  • Extensively nodular - excellent prog
  • Large cell anaplastic – 5%, poor prog

Molecular

  • SHH
    • SHH-TP53wt - intermediate
    • Desmoplastic/nodular
    • Infants and adults
    • 5 yr OS 80%
    • SHH-TP53m – poor prognosis
  • WnT – ‘wonderful’
    • Older classic
    • Children
    • 5 yr OS 100%
  • Group 3 – ‘threatening’
    • Large cell/anaplastic
    • Classic
    • Assoc with C-MYC amp
    • children
    • 5 yr OS 40%
  • Group 4 (non-WNT, non-SHH) – intermediate
    • Classic
    • Older children, most common
    • 5 yr OS 75%

By frequency: 4S3W

5 yr OS outcomes

  • Low risk: >90%
  • Std risk: 60-80%
  • High risk 60% or less
  • Very high risk (M+ group 3): <50%

Management

Manage raised ICP

  • Steroids, hyperosmolar IVF, anxiolytics, antiemetic, analgesia, elevate head
  • EVD or VP shunt insertion
  • Ventriculostomy
  • Decompressive craniotomy

Maximal safe surgical resection

  • Pre-op MRI brain and spine
  • Post-op MRI brain within 48 hours
    • Inflamed meninges and blood products confound post-op change and tumour after 72 hrs
  • LP to test CSF pre-op if safe (risk of herniation with raised ICP) OR 2 weeks post-op
    • LP after MRI spine to avoid confounding with inflammation
  • Post-op posterior fossa syndrome in 25%: SAME (speech impairment, ataxia, mutism, emotional lability) is self-resolving over weeks-months and should not delay adjuvant Rx

Std risk (66% of pts): M0, not large cell anaplastic, no group 3, no MYC, no SHH TP53m, <1.5cm2 residual disease, >3yo

  • Adjuvant CSI + TB boost: 23.4Gy/13# CSI + 30.6Gy/17# tumour bed boost = 54Gy/30#
  • Aim to commence 4 weeks post op
  • Reduced dose CSI does not compromise survival and reduces tox (Packer et al, ph III)
  • Concurrent vincristine
  • Adjuvant post RT chemotherapy: AABAABAAB
    • cycle A: lomustine, cisplatin, vincristine
    • cycle B: cyclophosphamide, vincristine

High risk (33% of pts): M+, large cell anaplastic, >1.5cm2, MYCamp:

  • Adj CSI + TB boost: 36Gy/20# CSI + 18Gy/10# tumour bed boost = 54Gy/30#
  • Boost M+ sites to 50.4Gy intracranially or 45Gy if spinal
  • Concurrent vincristine
  • Adjuvant chemotherapy: 6 cycles of cisplatin, cyclophosphamide, vincristine

High risk <3yo: risk adapted approach

  • Induction CHT → maximal safe resection → consolidation CHT → second look surgery with salvage focal RT or CSI to avoid neurocognitive impairment
  • Consider proton therapy

Low risk (>3yo, <1.5cm2 residual, M0, WNT subgroup, classic histology)

  • Ongoing St Jude investigation for de-escalation to 18Gy CSI

Adults

  • Consider omission of vincristine due to greater myelosuppression risk (less bone marrow recovery in adults)
  • 36Gy CSI

EBRT

Dose: 23.4Gy/13# + 30.6Gy/17# seq boost, VMAT, daily, with concurrent weekly vincristine

Pre-sim: surgical scars healed, neurocognitive testing, audiometry testing, baseline pituitary panel, age appropriate fertility counselling, clamshell testes

Sim: supine, arms down, empty bladder, neck extended, knee block to reduce lordosis, TPS head shell, fuse pre-op and post-op MRI

RT technique

VMAT: 2 arcs with 2-3 isocentres (1 for cranial, 1-2 for spine)

Volumes

CTV

  • Entire brain including crista gali, cribriform plate, optic nerve, BoS CN exit foramina, 0.5cm below foramen magnum
  • Entire spinal canal from foramen magnum to filum terminale (S3)
  • 1cm of exiting lateral nerve roots between VB
  • Cover entire VB for skeletally immature patient to avoid lordosis

PTV2340: +0.5cm for brain, +1cm for spine

CTV boost: residual disease + tumour bed + surgical cavity + 1cm, cropped to tentorium cerebri and bone

PTV 5400: +3mm intracranial or +10mm spinal

Daily kV with bone match and cranial and spinal isocentres for CSI, daily CBCT with bone match for boost

See CNS GCT for field based CSI technique

There is evidence that proton therapy reduces exit dose through structures anterior to VB and dose to temporal lobe

Toxicities

Acute: fatigue, n/v, headaches, seizures, oesophagitis, enteritis, hydrocephalus

Subacute: pneumonitis, pericarditis

Late

  • Chronic fatigue
  • Neurocognitive decline: ‘I’M ABLE’
    • IQ, Memory, Attention, Behaviour, Learning, Executive dysfx
  • Hypopituitarism – delayed growth, precocious or late puberty, gonadal dysfunction
  • Hearing impairment
  • Brainstem injury: ataxia, weakness
  • Radiation necrosis
  • Hypothyroidism
  • Stroke risk
  • Cataracts
  • Reduced truncal height, scolioisis/kyphosis
  • Pulmonary fibrosis
  • Cardiac disease
  • Bowel strictures, adhesions, obstruction
  • CKD (mild)
  • Infertility
  • Second malignancy – breast, GI, CNS
  • Depression

Refer to PENTEC dose constraints guideline

Craniopharyngioma

Epidemiology · Pathology · Management

Clinic page: Paediatric CNS

Epidemiology

I: Rare, 4% of paediatric CNS tumours, <1% of adult CNS tumours

A: Bimodal 5, 60yo

G: M

R: nil

Presentation

  • Visual field deficit – homonymous hemianopia
  • Pituitary dysfunction – diabetes insipidus, ↓ GH, ACTH, TFT, sex hormones, galactorrhea
  • Headaches, n/v

Radiology

  • Calcified mass in suprasellar region

Pathology

  • Benign tumour arising from the remnant of the craniopharyngeal duct in the suprasellar region (Rathke’s pouch)
  • WHO grade 1
  • Multiple cysts is characteristic
  • Adamantinomatous – 90%
    • Children
    • CTNNB1 mutation (B-catenin) → WnT signalling
  • Papillary – 10%
    • Adults
    • BRAF V600E mutation

Notes

  • Cystic component can swell during and post RT → monitor

Management

Benign tumour in a non-benign location

Avoid dual modality treatment – aim for either GTR or use RT

1. Maximal safe resection

  • STR alone has poor LC rates
  • Consider pre-op aspiration if large cystic component
  • Consider pre-op BRAF-MEK inhibitors to reduce tumour volume

2. Adjuvant or salvage RT or SRS

  • Indicated post STR
  • 54Gy/30#
  • SRS 10-14Gy/1# if 2mm or more from critical structures (optic pathways, pituitary)
  • STR + adj RT has similar OS and LC to GTR – 10 yr OS 95%

Late RT toxicities

  • Vision impairment
  • Hypopituitarism – reduced growth, hypothyroidism, reduced ACTH, infertility, amenorrhea, impotence
  • Moyamoya syndrome (microvascular ischaemia increasing stroke risk)
  • Cognitive decline
  • Radionecrosis
  • Stroke risk due to proximity to carotid arteries

S Sight

  • Psychological support:
    • Grief, anxiety, depression and adjustment syndrome can significantly impact day to day function in a paediatric patient and specialist counselling services should be sought/offered to all patients and carers
  • Endocrine:
    • Chemotherapy (alkylator) and radiotherapy can impact pituitary function, pancreatic, thyroid and gonadal function.
    • Hormones often require monitoring and replacing to optimise cardiometabolic health, growth, puberty and fertility. Refer early to Endocrinologist
  • Cardiometabolic health:
    • Shared care with primary physician, education for patient, carer and GP
    • organ specific toxicities to be screened - ie thorax: carotid US/TTE 10 years post, neck: TFT screening annually, abdo: HTN, diabetes
    • Chemotherapy related – anthracycline
  • Audiology / Vision / Dental:
    • Cranial radiotherapy in particular can cause hearing impairment, tinnitus, vision impairment, retinopathy, dental caries, xerostomia
    • Link in with audiology services, optometrist or specialist ophthalmologist and dentists can assist with monitoring and early intervention /prevention
  • Body image
    • Reduced growth and hypogonadism and abnormal puberty can lead to anxiety and depression stemming from body image concerns
    • It is important to educate patients and carers on expectations regarding this, and screen for this with appropriate referral to counselling
  • Fertility & sexual dysfunction
    • Sexual dysfunction due to pelvic/abdominal/cranial radiotherapy or secondary hypogonadism can occur commonly
    • Again age appropriate education and open discussions and sexual counselling should be undertaken
    • Regarding fertility if fertility preservation was completed referral to IVF clinics may be needed to facilitate this. These patients are likely to be higher risk pregnancies and therefore obstetrician input should be sought early on.
  • Vocational counselling and schooling
    • Involve education officer
    • Discussions with schools/work places regarding return to work/school plans
    • Learning assistance with individualised learning plans/classes to make up for missed learning/change in function
    • Vocational counselling particularly important in the context of a disability from treatment
    • Carer and patient education to set expectations
  • Lifelong cancer surveillance
    • Ongoing imaging for some, education about red flag symptoms and regular follow-up with oncologist
  • Second malignancy screening
    • Dose and site dependent screening
    • eg Breast cancer - MMG/US/MRI 8 years post or at age 25, screening colonoscopies 10 years post or at 30-35yo
    • Chemotherapy related – platinum, alkylator, anthracycline
  • Transition to adulthood/survivorship planning
    • Provide written documentation of exposure specific late effects to carer/patient at start of survivorship f/u
    • Autonomy over own health
    • Education on importance of surveillance protocols and what requires ongoing surveillance
    • Healthy habits: exercise, weight maintenance, diet, don't smoke, safe ETOH amounts
  • Neurocognitive:
    • expectations:
    • neuropsychologist/psychologist
    • special education
  • Physical:
    • Rehab, exercise physiologist
    • OT, aids if growth asymmetry
