Extremity soft tissue sarcoma, pre-op (RTOG, Wang 2011)

Contour on the planning CT fused with the diagnostic MRI (T1 post-contrast for the GTV, T2 for oedema). Sarcoma spreads along the long axis of the muscle compartment, so the margin is long and narrow.

Volumes

VolumeDefinition
GTVGross tumour on T1 post-contrast MRI.
CTVGTV + 3 cm longitudinally and 1.5 cm radially, plus suspicious peritumoral oedema on T2. Edited so it does not cross intact fascia or uninvolved bone, and stops at the skin surface.
PTVTypically 5 mm with daily IGRT (RTOG 0630).

Spare a strip of skin and soft tissue along the limb circumference (lymphoedema), joints, and about half the bone circumference where possible.

Post-op

CTV covers the tumour bed, surgical scar and drain sites with a longer margin than pre-op (commonly 4 cm longitudinal, 1.5 cm radial for the first phase), followed by a boost to the bed. Fuse the pre-op MRI to place the original tumour.

Retroperitoneal sarcoma, pre-op (Baldini 2015)

Since STRASS, pre-op RT is selective (e.g. liposarcoma at high risk of local recurrence). The posterior margin against the psoas and abdominal wall is where the surgeon has least room.

Volumes

VolumeDefinition
GTV / IGTVTumour on contrast CT / MRI; 4D-CT for motion (IGTV).
CTVGTV (or IGTV) + 1.5 cm, edited: no expansion into intact bone or the retroperitoneal compartment boundaries it cannot cross, small or no expansion into bowel, liver and kidney, and stop short of the skin.
HR CTV (optional)The surface against the posterior abdominal wall / psoas / vertebral body that the surgeon expects to be at risk, agreed with the surgeon, for a simultaneous boost.
PTVInstitutional (commonly 5 mm with IGRT).

Exact editing distances (bowel, liver, kidney) are tabulated in Baldini 2015; check them.
