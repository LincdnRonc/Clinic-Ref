Lung (TNM 9th edition)

Limited-stage SCLC

Start chest RT with cycle 1 or 2 of cisplatin/etoposide. Use 45/30 BID or 60–70 Gy daily, then durvalumab (ADRIATIC). For T1–2 N0, SABR or surgery followed by chemotherapy is an option.

Decisions

  • Chemo: cisplatin 75 d1 + etoposide 100 d1–3 q3w × 4 (or 60/120 as in Turrisi).
  • Chest RT dose: 45/30 BID (Turrisi, CONVERT) or 66/33 daily. 70/35 (CALGB 30610) is equivalent. Only about 25% of surveyed radiation oncologists favour BID.
  • Timing: start RT with cycle 1–2, or cycle 2 if bulky, treating post-chemo volumes but including initially involved nodal stations.
  • No elective nodal RT for PET-staged patients (CONVERT, C30610; isolated nodal failure about 2–3%). Consider covering the ipsilateral hilum.
  • Consolidation: durvalumab after chemoRT (ADRIATIC: median OS 37 → 56 months). Atezolizumab added concurrently did not help (NRG-LU005).
  • T1–2 N0: mediastinal staging (PET may suffice), then lobectomy or SABR, followed by adjuvant chemotherapy.
  • Thoracic RT benefit: about 5% OS and about one-third fewer local failures.

Brain: PCI or MRI surveillance

  • LS-SCLC with response: offer PCI 25/10 (Aupérin: about 5% OS benefit, pre-MRI era). MRI surveillance every 3 months is a reasonable alternative (ARS lists it second).
  • Start about 4 weeks after chemotherapy. Repeat brain MRI first.
  • Cognitive decline is clear after 30/10 but not clearly after 25/10. Hippocampal avoidance and memantine remain investigational (NRG-CC003; PREMER positive, Dutch trial negative).
  • ES-SCLC: MRI surveillance rather than PCI for most (Takahashi). PCI reduced brain metastases but median OS was 13.7 vs 11.6 months (p=0.09, favouring observation).

Extensive-stage SCLC

Chemo-immunotherapy is standard. Chest RT is for palliation or for selected responders with residual thoracic disease. Median OS is about 12 months; 5 y OS is <5%.

Decisions

  • Systemic: platinum/etoposide × 4 with durvalumab (CASPIAN) or atezolizumab (IMpower133, carboplatin only), then maintenance immunotherapy.
  • Consolidative chest RT: consider 30/10 for responders with residual chest disease (CREST). Its role with immunotherapy is being tested (RAPTOR / NRG-LU007).
  • Brain: MRI surveillance; treat symptomatic brain metastases.
  • Palliation: RT for symptomatic sites. Low-volume asymptomatic brain metastases can start with chemotherapy.

More key trials

EORTC 08993-22993 (2007)Phase III · extensive-stage SCLC responding to chemo · PCI vs observation (no routine brain imaging)PCI reduced symptomatic brain metastases and improved 1-y OS; later, the Japanese MRI-surveillance trial (Takahashi) found no OS benefit, so MRI surveillance is an alternative. check

SCLC planning

Planning is like NSCLC: 4DCT, IMRT, daily CBCT and adaptive replanning for rapid shrinkage. Do not refuse curative treatment just because V20 is 35–40%.

Volumes and limits

  • Treat the post-chemo primary plus all initially involved nodal stations.
  • Lung V20 <35% (CONVERT, CREST). In CALGB 70 Gy trials, V20 exceeded 40% in 30 of 100 patients, but only 3 had G3 toxicity.
  • Spinal cord: 36 Gy max for 45/30 BID (Turrisi era). 41 Gy in RTOG protocols. CONVERT allowed 42 Gy (BID) and 48 Gy (daily).
  • Cardiac dose independently predicted OS in RTOG 0617; a heart-dose analysis of C30610 is planned. Use IMRT and spare the heart.

This trial confirmed the survival benefit of consolidative RT in extensive SCLC, a condition with poor prognosis and few innovations over years other than immunotherapy. For some the role of RT is controversial - so much so that even the author Slotman published an accompanying editorial doubting his own results! The role of radiotherapy could be in priming an immune response, cytoreduction, or LC reducing future pulmonary complications. The trial reproduced the results of a smaller Yugoslavia trial six years prior. Opponents to consolidative thoracic RT might respond that CR to chemotherapy doesn't benefit, that immunotherapy may negate the benefit of RT or be too toxic together, or that the primary endpoint was not met. Two year OS is obviously an important time point for patients. Some question whether RT is still of benefit with atezolizumab or durvalumab (Goldman 2020), which can have adverse effects of pneumonitis. A clinical trial must be performed to show this. Pneumonitis is low in ADRIATIC. The ADRIATIC, CAPSIAN, and IMpower studies have shown that SCLC is immunogenic.

Slotman speculated that RT only works in residual disease and not CR. This CREST subanalysis was post-hoc and likely underpowered for those with CR, which were only 5%. Now practioners often assess response by PET, which could overestimate the response rate compared to RECIST criteria. Recognize also that a radiologic CR is very different from a pathologic CR, and a CR on imaging is no indication of cure. In IMpower and CASPIAN, immunotherapy improved median OS by only 2 months, and CR rate with immunotherapy was only 3% (measured by RECIST). If CR is what is needed to avoid RT, this is not resolved with immunotherapy, and the improvement in outcomes offered by immunotherpy is not enough. Consider for example the noninferiority trials in CR in favorable early stage Hodgkin lymphoma that show superiority with RT. SCLC has poor survival rates, and is not yet at a status in which practitioners should be omitting therapy or de-escalating. NCCN's 2024 recommendation that chest radiation should only be recommended for those with poor response in the chest seems misguided. Canadian and UK guidelines recommend the opposite, that radiation should be given for those with response to chemotherapy (Melosky 2023 & NICE 2024).

Thoracic RT and PCI were given simultaneously. RT volume was post-chemo volume of involved nodal levels with 1.5 cm margin. Involved levels were treated whether CR occurred there or not. Thoracic failure was still high after RT, raising a hypothesis if control can be further improved with dose escalation.

Management

1. Chemoimmunotherapy: carboplatin and etoposide q3weekly x4+ either durvalumab or atezolizumab until PD or unacceptable toxicity

  • Immunotherapy improves OS (Caspian, Impower133)
  • Beware of TLS for bulky disease

2. Tarlatamab (bispecific T cell engager) in addition to maintenance IO

  • DELPHI-303 (ph 1) showed promising median OS of 25 months, ph 3 data pending from DELPHI-305
  • S/e: cytokine release syndrome, ICANS/neurotoxicity

3. Consolidation thoracic RT if partial response to chemo with residual dx

  • 30Gy/10# or 40Gy/15#
  • Improved 2 yr OS by 10% and reduced thoracic failure by 40% - CREST trial, pre-IO era
  • 30Gy/10# consolidation RT after chemoIO alone did not improve OS and had more toxicities including deaths than IO alone (Treasure trial, ph II)

4. PCI

  • PCI has 14% OS benefit and reduces symptomatic brain mets – Slotman et al
  • Now MRI surveillance is non-inferior to PCI for OS (Takahashi et al)
  • Less neurocognitive decline, higher brain mets rate
  • MRIB q3mo for year 1 then q3-6mo
  • Salvage SRS to brain mets reduces neurocognitive decline vs WBRT without clear OS detriment (ENCEPHALON)

5. Disease progression

  • Primary refractory: progression <6 months from chemo
    • Poor prognosis – months
    • Lurbinectedin, topotecan, clinical trial
  • Platinum sensitive: progression >6months post chemo
  • Prognosis can be several months
  • Rechallenge platinum chemo
  • Second line: topotecan, paclitaxel, tarlatamab
