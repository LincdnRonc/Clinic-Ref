Published cumulative dose limits

By organ, in EQD2 unless stated, each with its source. These come from different patient groups and methods: read the source before applying one outside its setting.

How to use this table

  • Limits are for the sum of all courses unless the row says otherwise.
  • Most are consensus or single-series values, not validated tolerance doses.
  • Recovery: see radiobiology. Only the cord, the brain and the NPC limits build recovery in.
  • A limit met on paper still needs clinical judgement about the first-course toxicity, the interval and what the patient will accept.

Reirradiation evidence is mostly retrospective and consensus-based. Figures here are cited to their source; anything marked Verify was entered from memory. Constraints are starting points for an individual decision, not guarantees of safety. Have a colleague and physics review each case.

Reirradiation calculators

Arithmetic only. They do not replace a physics cumulative dose assessment.

Remaining dose to an organ

Converts the prior organ dose to EQD2, applies any recovery, subtracts it from the cumulative limit you choose, and gives the largest physical dose the organ can receive in the planned number of fractions.

EQD2 = D × (d + α/β) / (2 + α/β). The remaining dose is solved from the same formula for the planned fractions. Enter the dose the organ received, not the prescription, when you know it.

Spinal cord reirradiation check

Checks two published rule sets: Nieder 2006 for conventional reirradiation and Sahgal 2012 / HyTEC for SBRT reirradiation (thecal sac maximum).

Nieder: cumulative BED ≤135.5 Gy2 with each course ≤98 Gy2 and interval ≥6 months (no myelopathy reported at ≤120 Gy2). Sahgal: cumulative EQD22 ≤70 Gy, SBRT course ≤25 Gy, SBRT share ≤0.5, interval ≥5 months; the Sahgal checks always use α/β 2. The Nieder risk score itself is not calculated here (table not yet sourced).
