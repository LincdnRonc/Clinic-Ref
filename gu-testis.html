Testis (seminoma)

RT is for seminoma only. NSGCT gets surgery and chemo.

Seminoma by stage

StagePreferredAlternatives
ISurveillance (5-y relapse 10–20%)Carboplatin AUC7 ×1–2, or para-aortic 20/10 (relapse <5%). Treat if unreliable for follow-up
IIA (≤2 cm)RT 20/10 dog-leg + boost 10/5 = 30 GyEP ×4 or BEP ×3. SAKK 01/10: carbo ×1 → INRT 30 Gy
IIB (2–5 cm)BEP ×3 / EP ×4, or RT if <3 cm: 20/10 + 16/8 = 36 GySAKK 01/10: carbo ×1 → INRT 36 Gy (relapses were systemic)
IIC / IIIBEP ×3–4 or EP ×4RT alone not recommended (5-y RFS 44%)
  • Post-chemo residual <3 cm: surveillance. >3 cm: PET ≥6 wk after chemo; if positive, RPLND or second-line chemo.
  • Prefer chemo with horseshoe kidney, IBD, prior RT or pelvic surgery.
  • SAKK 01/10: 3-y PFS 94% (IIA 95%, IIB 93%); RT volume ~¾ smaller than dog-leg.

Fields and planning

  • Supine, clamshell on the contralateral testis, penis out of field. Sperm banking before treatment (40% azoospermic pre-RT).
  • Para-aortic (stage I): bottom of T11 to bottom of L5 (T11 spares kidney vs T10 without RFS loss); ~10 cm wide. Left primaries: include left renal hilum.
  • Dog-leg (stage II): add ipsilateral pelvic nodes, inferior border at top of acetabulum. Also if prior inguinal surgery.
  • Vascular CTV: aorta + 1.9 cm, IVC + 1.2 cm, iliacs + 1.2 cm; nodes + 0.8 cm; PTV + 0.5 cm; +0.7 cm to block edge (≈2 cm on nodes).
  • INRT (SAKK 01/10): pre-chemo GTV + 2 cm CC / 1 cm axial, PTV + 1 cm; plan CT 2 wk after carbo.
  • Inguinal nodes only with scrotal invasion or prior scrotal/inguinal surgery.
Constraints
  • Each kidney D50% ≤8 Gy; mean both <9 Gy. Single kidney V20 ≤15%. Boost: each kidney D50 ≤2 Gy, mean <3 Gy. Bowel V20 <70%.
  • Antiemetic prophylaxis (ondansetron).

Markers, work-up, follow-up

  • Seminoma: never AFP (raised AFP = non-seminomatous elements); mild β-hCG in ~15%. Markers before and ~1 wk after orchiectomy; don't use them to monitor stage I seminoma.
  • Half-lives: AFP 5–7 d; β-hCG and LDH ~1–1.5 d. False AFP: liver disease; false hCG: marijuana, LH cross-reaction.
  • Radical inguinal orchiectomy, never scrotal biopsy. CT abdo/pelvis; CT chest if stage II+. Repeat imaging at 4–6 wk for borderline IIA.
  • Relapse: most within 3 y for seminoma (2 y NSGCT). On surveillance relapses land in the para-aortic "landing strip"; after para-aortic RT, pelvis or mediastinum/neck; after dog-leg, above the diaphragm.
  • Follow-up: H&P + CT abdo ± pelvis roughly q3m → q6m → q12m over years 1 → 2 → 3+. Second cancer risk keeps rising for decades.

Staging

TNM staging is post surgical and incorporates tumour markers

S0: normal

S1: LDH 1.5x, b-HCG <5000, AFP <1000

S2: LDH 1.5-10x, b-HCG 50 000, AFP 10 000

S3: LDH >10x, b-HCG >50 000, AFP >10 000

½ lives: b-HCG: 7 days, AFP: 3 days, LDH 1 day.

Check tumour markers for serial decline accordingly

No stage IV for any GCT

Stage I: N-

Stage II: N+

Stage III: M+ or N+ & S2/3

Royal Marsden Hospital staging

(similar to Lugano)

Stage 1: testis alone

Stage 2: below diaphgram nodes

Stage 3: above diaphragm nodes

Stage 4: extranodal mets

Outcomes

10 yr OS

Stage I >99%

Stage II 95%

Stage III 90%+

Metastatic NSGCT: 70-90%

Tumour markers

  • Markers before orchidectomy for diagnosis/burden/prognosis.
  • Repeat post-op to assign S stage and detect occult metastatic disease.
  • Persistent elevation = stage IS.
  • Interpret according to half-life: AFP 5–7 d, β-hCG 1–3 d, LDH ~1 d – look for serial decline

Lymphatics

  • Testes arise retroperitoneally hence lymphatics drain to PA nodes
  • Right testicular vein drains to IVC, left to left renal vein
  • lymphatic drainage is to PA.
  • Scrotal/inguinal violation can alter drainage to inguinal nodes - so dog-leg/inguinal coverage may be considered

Ddx for testicular mass

Benign: hydrocele, varicocele, cyst, epididimytis, hernia, hematoma, torsion

Malignant: GCT, NGCT, lymphoma, sarcoma, melanoma

Work-up

  • FDG PET
  • CTCAP
  • Tumour markers

Path

  • anaplastic - outcomes not worse
  • spermatocytic - very slow growing variant seen >65yo
  • can be aggressive if there is a sarcomatous component
  • 15% have ↑b-HCG

Mgmt SGCT

Inguinal orchidectomy with high ligation of spermatic cord

  • Scrotal incisions can disrupt normal lymphatics and ↑ risk of seeding
  • Recheck tumour markers post-op

Stage 1 – node negative

  • Surveillance as per ANZUP (below) preferred if reliable relapse pattern as excellent salvage options exist
  • ~15% recurrence risk in PA nodal strip (at level of renal vessels)
  • If high risk (>4cm or rete testis invasion) then:
  • Adjuvant carboplatin chemotherapy x1
    • preferred (MRC TE19 – chemo reduces out of field recurrence)
  • OR adjuvant PA radiotherapy 20Gy/10# from T12-L5 (MRC TE-18 – 20Gy non inferior to 30Gy PA)
    • If there has been disruption of normal lymphatic drainage (ie orchiopexy or scrotal incision) chemo preferred
  • RFS 95% after adj treatment, equiv for both

Stage IIA <5 nodes, <2cm

  • Adjuvant modified dog leg radiotherapy 30Gy/15# from top of T12 to top of acetabulum
    • improves 5 yr OS to 99% (Glaser et al)
  • OR BEP chemo x3
  • OR ISRT + carboplatin – 10 yr PFS 93% (SAK 01/10 – ph II data)

Stage IIB/C: >5 nodes or >2cm

  • Adjuvant BEP x3 (bleomycin, etoposide, cisplatin) or EP x4 (if concern for lung tox)
    • preferred as less toxicity than RT (Giannatempo MA)
  • OR adjuvant modified dog leg radiotherapy 30Gy/36Gy/18#
  • OR ISRT + carboplatin – 10 yr PFS 93% (SAK 01/10 – phase II data)

Stage III

  • Adjuvant BEP x3-4
  • 4 cycles if S3 or mets outside lung or mediastinal primary

RPLND reserved for salvage Rx in SGCT

Surveillance protocol as per ANZUP

  • Year 1-2: q6monthly PE, tumour markers, CTAP, testosterone
  • Year 3-5: annual PE, tumours, CTAP, testosterone

Mgmt NSGCT

Inguinal orchidectomy with high ligation of spermatic cord

Stage I

  • Surveillance OR
  • Adjuvant BEP x1 or RPLND in high volume centre if:
  • pT3-4 (invading stromal cord or scrotum)
  • LVSI
  • embryonal
  • elevated tumour markers post-op (only BEP chemo)

Stage IIA

  • Tumour marker negative → RPLND preferred at high volume centre
  • Tumour marker positive or multiple nodes → Adjuvant BEP x2 if upstaged

Stage IIB and III

  • Adjuvant BEP x3
  • RPLND if residual nodal mass >1cm

Surveillance protocol as per ANZUP

  • 6 months: monthly PE, tumour markers, 4 monthly CTAP and CXR
  • 6 mo – year 2: 3 monthly PE, tumour markers, 6 monthly CTAP and CXR
  • Year 3 -5: 6 monthly PE, tumour markers, annual CTCAP and CXR

Residual mass

NSGCT

    • >1cm residual mass after chemo: resect/RPLND due viable tumour/teratoma risk
    • <1cm with normalising tumour markers: observe with CT + tumour markers
    • FDG PET not reliable here

Seminoma

  • Bulky seminoma mass can take months to resolve and often replaced by fibrotic tissue
  • if residual mass <3 cm observe with CT and tumour markers
  • if >3 cm, FDG-PET at ≥8 weeks post-chemo;
    • PET+ve: repeat PET in 6 weeks, MDT discussion to consider resection/salvage cisplatin based chemo
    • PET−ve: observe (PET has high -ve predictive value in seminoma – SEMPET trial)
    • Perform serial tumour markers

ASTRO 2026 summary

Figure in the original document (not reproduced here).

EBRT

Dose: 20Gy/10# PA nodes or 30-36Gy/15-18# modified dog leg field

Pre-sim: check tumour markers, fertility preservation for sperm banking, advise contraception use, pre-med antiemetic, baseline renal function

Sim: Supine, arms above head, tape penis to contralateral side, clamshell testis

Volumes

PA field

CTV 2000: 15mm margin around IVC and aorta from T11/12 interpsace to L5/S1 interspace, include LEFT renal hilum (for LEFT testicular tumour), excluding organs, bone, muscle

PTV 2000: +7mm margin

Modified dog leg

CTV 3000: 2cm margin around IVC, aorta, ipsilateral CI until bifurcation

PTV 3000: +7mm margin

GTVp: involved node

CTVp: +5mm

PTV 3000 or 3600: + 7mm

Technique

AP/PA or 3DCRT – block majority of kidneys

Toxicities (essentially like ADT + RT)

  • 2nd malignancy, chronic HTN, atherosclerosis, enteritis, bowel adhesions, strictures, obstruction
  • Hypogonadism: infertility, low testosterone, low libido, weight gain, osteoporosis, low mood, ED
  • Metabolic syndrome due to low testosterone
  • Psychological – body image, anxiety
