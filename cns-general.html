Overview

At a glance

Uveal melanoma
For medium tumours, plaque brachytherapy gives the same survival as enucleation (COMS) and preserves the eye. Survival is driven by liver metastases, so follow up with liver imaging.
Brain tolerances
Necrosis risk rises above 60 Gy and with fractions >2.5 Gy. Lens, lacrimal gland and cornea limits drive orbital planning.

Uveal (choroidal) melanoma

For medium tumours, plaque brachytherapy gives the same survival as enucleation (COMS) and preserves the eye. Survival is driven by liver metastases, so follow up with liver imaging.

By size (COMS)

SizeDefinitionManagement
SmallApical height ≤3 mm, basal diameter 5–16 mmObserve for growth (21% at 2 y, 31% at 5 y); treat on growth
MediumApical height 2.5–10 mm, basal diameter <16 mmI-125 plaque = enucleation for survival (5 y OS about 81%)
LargeBasal diameter >16 mm or apical height >10 mmEnucleation (pre-op EBRT added nothing). 5 y OS about 60%
  • Plaque dose: 85 Gy to the tumour apex (or to 5 mm from the inner sclera if height <5 mm), with a 2 mm margin around the base. Dose rate 0.60–1.05 Gy/h over 4–7 days. Ru-106: 90 Gy.
  • Alternatives: protons 70 GyE (long-term LC about 95%) or SRS 25–40 Gy to the 50% isodose.
  • COMS medium: 5 y local failure about 10%; enucleation for complications or failure about 15%. Vision 20/40 or better fell from 70% at baseline to 34% at 3 y.
  • Distant metastasis: 5 y 14–20% after local therapy; mostly liver (about 90%). Median survival with metastases about 6 months.

Brain and orbit tolerances

Necrosis risk rises above 60 Gy and with fractions >2.5 Gy. Lens, lacrimal gland and cornea limits drive orbital planning.

Tolerances

Brain necrosis (<2.5 Gy/fx)<3% below 60 Gy; about 5% at BED3 120 Gy (72/36); about 10% at BED3 150 Gy (90/45). BID RT is steep above BED3 80 Gy
Optic nerves and chiasm<3% at 55 Gy, <7% at 60 Gy, 7–20% above 60 Gy. SRS: very low <8 Gy; <10% at 12 Gy
Hippocampus (HA-WBRT)100% of hippocampus + 5 mm <9–10 Gy, Dmax <16 Gy (30 Gy/10)
LensCataract in about 33% after 2.5–6.5 Gy (8 y latency) and about 66% after 6.5–11.5 Gy
Lacrimal glandDry eye TD5/5 35 Gy, TD50/5 50 Gy. Dmax 30 Gy recommended
CorneaUlceration >60 Gy; keratitis absent <59 Gy, almost certain >70 Gy

  • TMZ: G3+ thrombocytopenia about 7%; check bloods every 2 weeks. Neutropenia also occurs.

Exam notes

AS's Phase 2 summary, as written and not source-checked. Where it disagrees with this page, go by this page.

Ependymoma

From the exam summary.

Epidemiology

I: <5% in adults, 10% in kids

A: bimodal - <10, 30-50

G: M

R: nil

RF

E: prior cranial RT

I: nil
G: NF-2 → spinal cord ependymoma

I: nil

Arises from ependymal cells lining ventricles and central canal

MRI

  • T1 hypointense, heterogenous contrast enhancement
  • T2 hyperintense

Outcomes

5 yr OS

  • 80% with GTR + adj RT in low grade
  • 60% with STR + adj RT in G2
  • 50% G3
  • 30% with STR alone

Molecular classification

Supratentorial

  • ZFTA (RELA) fusion – unfavourable. 10 yr 60%.
  • YAP1 mutation – favourable. 10 yr 95%

Posterior Fossa – mostly kids

  • Group A- unfavourable, infants. 10 yr OS 60%
  • Group B- intermediate. 10 yr 90%.

These 2 typically occur in kids <10yo

Spinal – mostly adults

  • SP-EPN (NF-2) – favourable, 10 yr 95%
  • SP-MYCN amp – very unfavourable, 10 yr 40%

These typically occur in adults

WHO Grading

  • Grade 1 ~ 15%: subependymal
  • 4th ventricle
  • Adults > kids
  • Grade 2 ~ 70%: classic, myxopapillary
    • PF and spinal cord
    • Kids > adults
  • Grade 3 ~ 15%: anaplastic
    • Intracranial
    • Kids > adults

Extent of resection is more important prognostic factor than grade

Age <3yo is a poor prognostic factor

Histology: perivascular and true ependymal rosettes

IHC: GFAP, EMA dot like pattern

Management

1. Maximal safe resection

Most important prognostic factor

All need LP 2-3 weeks post-op to assess for CSF dissemination

  • If CSF +ve or radiological CSF spread → CSI

Grade 1

GTR → observe

STR → salvage RT

  • 45Gy/25# to cord or 50.4Gy/28# to cauda equina or 54Gy/30# to intracranial

Grade 2

Intracranial → observe or adj RT: STR 59.4Gy/33#, GTR observe

  • Consider delaying RT in children <3yrs with chemotherapy (cyclophosphamide, vincristine, cisplatin, etoposide) though this may compromise survival

Spinal → STR → adj RT: 45Gy/25# to cord or 50.4Gy/28# cauda equina

Grade 3

Intracranial → adj RT for all: 60Gy/30#

Spinal → adj RT for all: 45Gy/25# to cord or 50.4Gy/28# to cauda equina

If CSF dissemination

  • 36Gy CSI if >3yo
  • Boost gross disease to 45Gy
  • CSF dissemination risk is lower than medulloblastoma

EBRT – spinal cord

Dose: 45Gy/25# daily, VMAT, no BD compensation

Pre-sim: Fertility sparing, pituitary panel, neurocognitive testing, audiometry, vision assessment

Sim: supine, arms down, vac bag, fuse pre and post-op MRI

Volumes

GTV: tumour bed including pre-op tumour extent + residual tumour in T1 contrast MRI

CTV: 5-10mm expansion, cropped to anatomical boundaries (bone)

PTV: +5mm

Toxicities

  • Acute: alopecia, fatigue, headache, nausea, erythema
  • Late: cognitive decline, hearing loss, endocrinopathies (most commonly, GH, TSH, ACTH), microcephaly

OARs from Quantec

Spinal cord myelopathy

  • 50Gy = 0.2%
  • 55Gy = up to 1%
  • 60Gy = 6%

Brainstem neuropathy/necrosis

  • Dmax <54Gy < 5%
  • D1-10cc ≤ 59Gy < 5%

Pineal

From the exam summary.

Epidemiology

I: rare, <1% of CNS tumours

A: 20-40yo

G: M

R: nil

RF: Rb, prior cranial RT

Pathology

WHO grade

  • Grade 1: pineocytoma
  • Grade 2-3: pineal parenchymal tumour of intermediate differentiation (PPTiD)
  • Grade 4: pineoblastoma
    • 2nd most common pineal tumour after GCT
    • Most common in children <5yo

Other

  • Germ cell tumours
  • Meningioma, glioma

IHC: synaptophysin, NSE

Management

1. Maximal safe resection

2. If WHO G4 → radiotherapy: 36Gy CSI + 54Gy TD tumour boost in 1.8Gy/# with concurrent lomustine and vincristine

NB children <3yo often treated with intensive chemotherapy to avoid neurocognitive impact of RT (see medulloblastoma protocol)

Same technique as NGGCT
