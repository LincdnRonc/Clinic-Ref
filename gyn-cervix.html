Cervix: treatment by stage

Surgery for small early tumours (open, not minimally invasive), chemoradiation plus MRI-guided brachytherapy for bulky or node-positive disease. Overall treatment time ≤50 days.

CriteriaTreatmentEvidence

IMRT is the standard for adjuvant pelvic RT (PARCER: halves late bowel toxicity).

Definitive chemoradiation

  • EBRT: 45/25 pelvis (± para-aortic) with weekly cisplatin 40 mg/m² ×5–6. Node SIB 55–57.5/25 (EMBRACE II).
  • Brachytherapy: MRI-guided, interstitial needles for bulky, asymmetric or parametrial residual disease (>5 cm, thick vaginal involvement).
  • Total time: keep within 50 days; each extra week costs local control.
  • Para-aortic RT: EMBRACE II extends to the renal vessels for ≥3 pelvic nodes, common iliac or para-aortic nodes ("large pelvis + low pAO" or higher).
  • Intensification: INTERLACE (6 wk induction carbo/paclitaxel) and KEYNOTE-A18 (pembrolizumab with and after chemoRT) both improved OS. OUTBACK (adjuvant carbo/paclitaxel after chemoRT) did not.
  • Response: PET-CT at 3–6 mo; persistent FDG uptake predicts worse CSS (5-y 32% vs 80%). Biopsy suspected persistence before salvage.

  • HR-CTV: residual GTV + whole cervix + presumed extracervical extension at BT (grey zones in parametria, uterus, vagina, rectum, bladder).
  • IR-CTV: HR-CTV + initial extent, with 5–15 mm margins.
  • Cervix superior border ≥1 cm above the uterine vessels. CT-only planning: assume ~3 cm cervical height and treat the whole tandem length.
  • Per-fraction OAR doses after 45/25: 5 × 3.5 Gy ≈ 65 Gy; 5 × 3.9 Gy ≈ 70 Gy; 5 × 4.75 Gy ≈ 80 Gy total.

Cervix EBRT planning

IMRT with an internal target volume for the moving uterus and vagina.

Contour step by step with the Gynae contouring guide (primary targets and ITV, section 3; nodes and SIB, section 4).

Volumes (intact cervix)

  • Sim with full and empty bladder (ITV), IV contrast, vaginal marker; fuse MRI ± PET in treatment position.
  • CTV1: GTV + cervix + uterus → PTV1 +1.5 cm.
  • CTV2: parametria (with ovaries) + vagina (upper half if minimal involvement, upper ⅔ if upper vagina involved, whole vagina if extensive; ≥3 cm below disease) → PTV2 +1 cm. Whole mesorectum if uterosacral involvement.
  • CTV3 nodes: common, external and internal iliac, obturator, presacral; vessels + 7 mm. Add para-aortic to renal vessels when indicated; inguinal if lower third of vagina involved.
  • Postop: vaginal ITV using full/empty bladder scans; vaginal cuff to upper third of symphysis.

EBRT constraints

  • Bone marrow: V40 <37%, V10 <90% (RTOG 0418; higher V40 linked to haematological toxicity).
  • Bowel: V40 <30% (TIME-C). Limit V57 for nodes; EMBRACE: 45 Gy with nodal SIB and bowel D2cc <60 Gy cut late GI toxicity.
  • Duodenum (para-aortic): V55 <1 cc or V50 <4 cc for <10% G2+.
  • Keep elective nodal dose at 45 Gy to reduce long-term diarrhoea.

Commentary
This landmark trial established the role of definitive radiation for cervical cancer. It's often quoted to support definitive RT over surgery in early cervical cancer, but its application is more nuanced. Individualized care is called for. Those that would be expected to require post-op RT, especially chemoRT, may be best suited for definitive chemoRT. In the 2017 update there is trend to benefit with surgery in adeno, and trend to benefit with RT in size >4 cm.

Adverse effects. The adverse effects from post op RT are excess here compared to modern therapy and the study should be referenced limitedly in contemporary context. How often do you encounter hydronephrosis, LE edema, and bowel obstruction after adjuvant RT? Adjuvant RT may have been performed too early in the study, or surgical complications may have been mistakenly attributed to RT. Techniques have also advanced. In the EORTC and Tata Memorial studies the neoadjuvant chemo plus surgery arms with post-op RT as indicated did not lead to worse late adverse effects overall compared to definitive radiation, as was shown in the Milan trial. This could be due to less receiving adjuvant RT (64% post-op RT in Milan study vs. 48% post-op CRT in EORTC, 13% post-op CRT & 10% RT alone in Tata Memorial) and/or improved surgical and post-op radiation techniques such as laparoscopy and IMRT. A modern surgical study reported GU incontinence of 11% after radical hysterectomy (Plante 2024). The Peters trial reported grade 4 effects in 4% vs. 17%. The Sedlis trial reported acute grade 3-4 effects 2% vs. 7%. Adverse effects are also low in adjuvant RT studies for endometrial cancer, though these undergo TAH rather than radical hysterectomy, and now have SLNB.
(Commentary last updated 2024, Estes)
Notes
Cervix summary
-Milan study shows that WPRT+brachy vs surgery have similar OS in early stage cervical cancer. Surgery tends to have more morbidity, so RT becomes standard for many cases
-Trials show OS benefit with chemo in addition to RT for definitive treatment of cervical cancer. This is confirmed in meta-analysis
-Trials have some mixed results regarding the use of coverage of PA fields (EORTC, RTOG 7920). EFRT is generally then restricted for PA N+ only
-The Peters trial shows OS benefit with chemo in post-op cervix with N+, +margin, or +parametria
-The Sedlis trials shows PFS benefit with RT in post-op cervix with intermediate risk factors
-EMBRACE refines the use of image guided HDR brachy for cervical cancer
-Tata Memorial and EORTC 55994 studies showed worse DFS with neoadjuvant chemotherapy prior to surgery vs definitive chemoRT
-OUTBACK trial showed no benefit to additional adjuvant chemo after definitive chemoRT
-KEYNOTE A18 integrates pembrolizumab with chemoRT for survival benefit

Exam notes

AS's Phase 2 summary, as written and not source-checked. Where it disagrees with this page, go by this page.

Cervix

From the exam summary.

Epidemiology

I: most common gynae cancer in developing world

A: 40

G: F

R: developing countries – asia, Africa

RFs

E: smoking, ETOH, in utero DES (clear cell adenoCa at ~20yo), sexual RFs (multiple partners), multiparity, low SES

I: HPV 18 (adenoCa), 16 (SCC), 31,33, 45 (SCC)

G:

I: HIV, solid organ transplant (q3yr screen)

Smoking role

  • causes local cervical immune suppression
  • carcinogen exposure in cervical mucus & DNA damage
  • impaired HPV clearance

FIGO staging

1A1: <3mm DOI

1A2: 3-5mm DOI

1B: >5mm DOI; B1 <2cm, B2 2-4cm, B3 >4cm

2A: upper 2/3 of vagina; A1 <4cm, A2 >4cm

2B: to parametria

3A: to lower 1/3 of vagina

3B: to pelvic side wall or HUN

3C1: pelvic node

3C2: PA node

4A: invades adjacent organs

4B: distant met

Rule of 10 & 15 LN +ve

Stage 1B=15% pelvic, 10% PA

Stage 2=30% pelvic, 20% PA

Stage 3 = 45% pelvic, 30% PA

F/u

  • MRI and PET at 3 months
  • H&P q3-6mo
  • Annual cytology
  • Vaginal dilator use 10min, 3x per week for 2-5 years independent of sexual activity
  • EMBRACE analysis showed combination of both had best outcomes for vaginal stenosis)

Stage III - IVA

  • 5 year OS 70-80%
  • Pelvic control 85-90%

HPV

  • E7 degrades Rb, releasing E2F
  • E6 promotes p53 degradation

Pathology

Pre-invasive

  • LSIL – mild dysplasia and koilocytosis, most spontaneously regress
  • HSIL – mod-severe dysplasia. 5-30% chance of progression over decades

Invasive

  • SCC (80%) – verrucous (very well differentiated, radioresistant), warty (both less likely to spread), basaloid, papillary, keratinising, non-keratinising
  • Adenocarcinoma (15%) – present with barrel cervix. Villoglandular (better prog), serous, clear cell, mucinous (all worse prog)
  • Adenosquamous (<5%)
  • Neuroendocrine – small cell
  • Melanoma
  • Sarcoma
  • Lymphoma

IHC: CK5/6, p40, p63, p16

Adenoca: CK7, CEA, p16, variable ER/PR

Screening

  • CST q5y from 25yo if any sexual contact
  • CST – tests for presence of HPV.
  • If 16 or 18 > LBC + colposcopy
  • If other HPV> LBC

LBC: LSIL or HSIL

  • LSIL > repeat in 1 year
  • HSIL > colposcopy

Colposcopy: visual assessment of TZ as most cancer arises here + LLETZ to excise the TZ/bx. Then annual HPV CST until 2 negative

Gardasil vaccine – year 7 students

Management

Stage 1A – 1B1

  • FS: CKC for 1A OR radical trachelectomy with cerclage for 1B
  • Pelvic node sampling for 1B
  • Non FS: TAH (simple hysterectomy – parametria and vagina left) + BSO
  • radiotherapy alone (EBRT or BT) is a non-surgical option

Stage 1B2 & small 2A

  • Radical hysterectomy (removes parametria and top of vagina) + BSO + SLNBx
    • Minimally invasive surgery has inferior OS to open surgery (LACC trial)
  • No role for NA CT prior to surgery to downstage (EORTC, TATA)
  • OR Radiotherapy +/- concurrent cisplatin – similar OS to surg – Landoni trial
  • Only operate if confident of R0 as trimodality Rx has worst toxicity (GU, GI, lymphoedema) – Landoni trial
  • AdenoCa has better outcomes with surgery – Landoni trial

Adj pelvic RT (GOG 92)

  • Sedlis 2 of 3: LVSI, mid or deep third stromal invasion, size >5cm
  • GOG score > 120
  • GOG 92: RT improved PFS

Adj chemoRT (GOG 109)

  • Peters: Parametria, Positive margin, Pelvic node +
  • GOG 109: cisplatin improved OS by 10% and PFS
  • GOG 120 – concurrent cisplatin based chemotherapy has best survival outcomes and least tox vs hydroxyurea and cis/hydroxy/5FU

Stage 1B3, 2B – 4A

  • Definitive chemoradiation + BT boost
  • Chemo improves 5 yr OS by 8% in stage 3 – TATA memorial, GOG 123
  • Consider neoadjuvant carbo/taxol chemotherapy for 6 weeks in stage 2B – 3 – INTERLACE improves OS by 8%
  • No benefit to adjuvant carbo/taxol – OUTBACK showed no OS benefit and increased toxicity
  • Consider concurrent and adjuvant Pembro with CRT in stage 3-4– KEYNOTE A18 improved OS by 8% (compassionate access)

Stage 4B

  • Doublet chemotherapy > single agent chemo
  • + Pembro improves OS
  • + Bevacizumab improves OS – GOG 240. Use depends on bleeding, fistula and thrombosis risk

Recurrence

  • If no pelvic RT – RT to entire pelvis and boost recurrence
  • If bulky >4cm consider resection then RT
  • If prior RT then pelvic exenteration IF no distant disease and fit
  • Re-irradiation to focal pelvis can be considered for palliation

INTERLACE

P: stage 2B - 3 cervix ca

I: NA carbo/taxol weekly for 6 weeks > definitive chemoRT

O: Improved 5 yr OS from 72% to 80%.

Controversies

2 prv RCT showed no benefit, likely due to failure to control interval between NACT and definitive Rx leading to repopulation.

In interlace 80% of pts started definitive chemoRT within 1 week of completing NA CT

INTERLACE uses old RT technique without MRI planning and had low median RT dose 80Gy EQD2

KEYNOTE A18

P: stage 3-4 cervix ca

I: chemoRT +/- concurrent and adj pembro for 24 months

O: 3 yr PFS improved from 57 to 70%, 3 yr OS 75% vs 83%

Late Tox

  • Skin fibrosis, telangiectasia, lymphoedema
  • Pelvic fracture, osteoporosis
  • Bladder: chronic cystitis (frequency, urgency, nocturia), haematuria
  • Bowel: chronic proctitis, bloody stool, looser stool, frequency, chronic enteritis, bowel adhesions, obstruction
  • Vaginal/sexual: stenosis, shortening, painful intercourse/examinations, bleeding, ulceration, infertility, premature menopause
  • Other: fistula particularly rectovaginal or vesicovaginal
  • Second malignancy within RT field

BT

  • BT improves OS in context of definitive Rx

Dose: 28Gy/4# using Ir-192, 48 hrs apart

Aiming EQD2 90Gy to HR CTV D90, EQD2 60Gy to LR CTV D90

D90% of HR-CTV ≥ 100% TD

Pre-sim

  • MRI in last week of EBRT
  • Bloods: check neuts and plts given recent chemo
  • Transfuse for Hb >100
  • Empty rectum
  • Ensure total treatment time <49 – 56 days

OT

  • GA, dorsolithotomy position
  • IDC inserted and filled with 7mL of contrast
  • PV and speculum exam to assess clinical response
  • Dilate os and sound uterus to assess length
  • Insert tandem + ovoid. If >5mm thick then insert interstitial needles. Needles limited to 20% of total dose delivery (otherwise risk weak central dose, bleeding risk, greater toxicity)
  • Secure with vaginal packing/straps
  • Rectal tube inserted
  • PCA and patient woken
  • IV buscopan then CT + MRI sim performed
  • Prophylactic abx (metronidazole, cefazolin) + mechanical & chemical VTE prophylaxis

Volumes – as per GEC ESTRO

  • GTVbrachy: residual visible and palpable disease
  • CTV HR: GTV + entire cervix
  • CTV LR: CTV HR + 10mm.

Delivery: connect to HDR. Patient admitted overnight, repeat 2nd fraction next day

MRI repeated prior to 2nd week of treatment
