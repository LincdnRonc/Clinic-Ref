Gastric

Epidemiology · Differential diagnosis · Management · EBRT · OARs

Clinic page: Oesophagus and gastric

Epidemiology

I: common

A: 70

G: M

R: Asian

RF

E: high salt or smoked foods, low fruit/veg intake, smoking, ETOH, GORD, obesity, chronic gastritis, NSAIDs

I: H.pylori, EBV

G: GAPPS, Peutz-Jager syndrome, Lynch, hereditary diffuse gastric cancer, FAP, Li Fraumeni, pernicious anemia

I: nil

Staging

T1: into submucosa

T2: into muscularis propria

T3: into serosa

T4: into subserosa (visceral peritoneum) into adj structures

N1: 1-2 nodes

N2: 3-6 nodes

N3: 7+

M1: DM

T2+ should have staging abdo laparoscopy as FDG PET insensitive for this

Tumour marker: Ca19.9

Differential diagnosis

Benign: cyst, gastritis, polyp, ulcer

Malignant: gastric carcinoma, MALT, GIST, mets (luminal breast ca), melanoma, sarcoma

Path

Intestinal type – 60%

  • Adenocarcinoma
  • Correa cascade: stepwise progression from chronic H.pylori gastritis to intestinal metaplasia, dysplasia then adenoCa
  • Intermediate prognosis
  • CK20, CDX2 +ve, HER2 +ve

Diffuse gastric type – 30%

  • Signet ring cells, infiltrative and diffuse growth, linitis plastica
  • Assoc with CDH1 syndrome
  • More advanced and aggressive at diagnosis
  • CK7+ve, CK20 -ve, CDX2 -ve, loss of E-cadherin on IHC

Lymphoepithelial type – 10%

  • Assoc with EBV, lymphocytic infiltrate around tumour
  • Best prognosis
  • EBER ISH, LMP IHC
  • High PDL1 expression

IHC: CK7+, AE1/AE3, HER2, MMR

CK20-

Natural History

  • Early lymphatic spread due to rich lymphatics in submucosa
  • Diffuse type has early haemtogenous spread
  • Transcolemic spread leading to peritoneal carcinomatosis and Krukenburg tumours
  • Nodal skip metastases to Virchows node – indicates advanced disease

Management

Early T1aN0

EMR

  • T1a (through lamina propria but not into submucosa) N0, intestinal type, well diff, ≤2cm, no LVSI

T1b+ Nany M0

Gastrectomy with D2 lymph node dissection

  • Partial gastrectomy for small distal tumours
  • Total gastrectomy for diffuse, large or proximal tumours, commonly with RouX-en-Y reconstructions
  • D2 with ≥ 15 nodes: celiac, splenic, CHA, left gastric artery

Peri-operative systemic therapy – T2+ or N+

  • MAGIC found OS improved with ECF peri-op chemo vs gastrectomy upfront
  • Peri-op FLOT improves 5 yr OS from 36% to 45% compared with peri-op ECF chemotherapy (FLOT 4)
  • No benefit with addition of adjuvant RT in peri-op ECC chemo (CRITICS)

Neoadjuvant RT

  • No OS benefit shown in TOPGEAR with addition of NA 45Gy/25# to peri-operative FLOT
    • Did improve pCR from 8% to 17% and downstaging

Adjuvant chemotherapy and radiotherapy

  • CRT after R0 gastrectomy was historically shown to improve OS, and LRC. 3 yr OS 50%. (INT0116 trial, McDonald et al)
  • No benefit in diffuse type
  • These pts did not receive D2 LND
  • May still be relevant after D0/D1 LND
  • Capecitabine/oxaliplatin after D2 gastrectomy improves OS and DFS. 5 yr OS OS 78% (Classic trial)
  • Adj 45Gy/25# in addition to cape/cis chemotherapy improved DFS in patients who did not have NA chemotherapy in a subgroup analysis - node positive and intestinal subtype (ARTIST 1)
  • RT in addition to SOX chemotherapy better than S1 chemo (ARTIST 2)
  • Overall 45Gy/25# in addition to chemotherapy may improve DFS and can be considered in:
    • R1, N+, D0/1

Definitive chemoradiation

  • No high level evidence
  • Extrapolating from other data – 45Gy/25# or 50.4-54Gy in 28-30# reasonable
  • Concurrent cisplatin, capecitabine, 5FU
  • Definitive chemo: CAPOX, FOLFOX
  • Ph 2 data shows promising outcomes of adjuvant IO

Metastatic

  • FOLFOX
  • CAPOX
  • HER2 directed therapy
  • IO if MMRd or high PDL1

EBRT

Dose: 45Gy/25#, daily, VMAT, with concurrent capecitabine

  • Boost to 50.4Gy if R1

Pre-sim: scars healed, discuss at MDT, aim to commence 4-6 weeks post-op, optimise nutrition

Sim: NBM 4 hours, supine, arms about head, vac bag immobilisation, EEBH, oral contrast

Volumes

CTV: gastrectomy bed, tumour bed, and entire D2 nodal region

  • If proximal – include GOJ
  • If distal – include duodenum

PTV + 8mm margin

OARs

duodenum, small bowel, pancreas, liver, kidneys, lung, heart

Daily CBCT with soft tissue match

GIST

Epidemiology · Pathology · Management

Clinic page: Oesophagus and gastric

Epidemiology

I: Rare

A: 70

G: M

R: Chinese

RF

G: hereditary GIST syndrome, Carneys triad (pulmonary chondroma, extra-adrenal paraganglioma), NF1

Risk stratification

Tumour size >5cm

Location: gastric > small bowel

MR: mitotic rate <5 or >5 per 5mm2

R1/R2 resection

Tumour rupture intra-op

  • Arises from interstitial cells of Cajal in muscularis propria
  • Haematogenous spread can occur, lymphatic spread rare

Pathology

  • Spindle – 80%
    • Fascicles/whorls of uniform spindle cells
    • Better prognosis
    • cKITm common
  • Epitheliod – 10%
    • Round/polygonal epithelial cells in sheets
    • Worse prognosis
    • associated with PDGFRA mutation
  • Mixed – 15%
    • Intermediate prognosis

IHC: CD117, DOG1, CD34

Gastric GIST better than SB GIST

Genetic

  • cKIT mutation (80%) – sensitive to TKI (Imatinib)
  • PDGFRA D842V – often confers resistance to Imatinib, sensitive to avapritinib

Management

Resectable

  • En-bloc resection. No LND routinely.
  • Adjuvant TKI for 3 years if large size >5cm, high mitotic rate or tumour rupture with appropriate mutation
  • No lymph node dissection required in cN0. Lymphatic spread is rare

Unresectable/borderline resectable

  • Neoadjuvant TKI → surgery

Metastatic

  • Imatinib 1st line

GIST is radioresistant, therefore limited role for RT

Oesophagus

Epidemiology · Anatomy · Management · EBRT – NA distal oesophagus · EBRT – cervical oesophagus

Clinic page: Oesophagus and gastric

Epidemiology

I: SCC ↓, more common globally

adenoCa ↑, more common in Western world

A: 65

G: M

R: SCC oesophageal ca Asian belt – Turkey, China, central Asia, Mongolia. Adenoca - Western

RF

SCC

  • A -achalasia (x30 risk)
  • B- betel nut chewing, bad diet
  • C - cigarette smoking
  • D - drinking scalding hot liquids
  • E - ETOH
  • F- Familial:
  • Tylosis (palmoplantar hyperkeratosis), Plummer-vinson, Fanconi anaemia

AdenoCa

  • GORD
  • Obesity
  • Barrett’s oesophagus
  • Smoking (weaker assoc than SCC)
  • Familial Barrett’s syndrome

Staging

T1a: into lamina propria or into muscularis mucosa

T1b: into submucosa

T2: into muscularis propria

T3: into adventitia

T4: into adjacent structures

N1: ≤ 2 locoregional

N2: 3-6 nodes

N3: 7+ nodes

M1: DM

  • SCF included as regional however in a distant oesophageal often treated as M1
  • PA nodes beyond celiac axis are treated as M1

Siewert classification for GOJ tumours

1: -5 to -1cm from cardia

2: -1 to +2cm from cardia

3: +2 to +5cm from cardia

Anatomy

  • 40cm long, divided into thirds
  • 15-20cm: cervical
  • 20-30cm: upper & mid
  • 30-40cm: distal

Path

SCC

  • Exophytic, flat or ulcerated
  • More infiltrative and circumferential
  • Keratin pearls, whorls, intercellular bridges
  • Basaloid – aggressive
  • Spindle, verrucous – less aggressive
  • p40, p63, AE1/AE3, CK5/6

AdenoCa

  • B/g of Barrett’s oesophagus, goblet cells, gland formation, mucin production
  • CK7, AE1/AE3, MUC 1/2 Alcian blue

Barrett’s oesophagus

  • Chronic irritation of oesophageal lining (reflux) → Intestinal metaplasia from squamous cells to columnar cells extending from the Z line → progress to dysplasia → carcinoma
  • 0.5% annual ca risk if metaplasia
  • 5-20% annual ca risk of dysplasia

Response to NA Rx

  • Becker-Mandard grading system – lower score = better response
  • Prescence of fibrosis, necrosis, acellular tissue, granulation inflammatory response
  • Residual invasive or in-situ disease

Pre-treatment considerations

  • Dietitian, SP review
  • Definitive feeding strategy: NGT, JEJ tube, RIG/PEG
  • Gastrostomy avoided if surgery planned
  • Pulmonary function testing

Management

T1aN0

  • Endoscopic mucosal resection, endoscopic submucosal dissection
  • Minimally invasive procedure
  • If upstaged: T1b, poorly differentiated, LVSI then requires adjuvant chemoRT: 50Gy/25# with concurrent carboplatin/paclitaxel

T1b-T2N0

  • Upfront esophagectomy + locoregional nodal dissection
  • Adjuvant chemoRT 50Gy/25# with carboplatin/paclitaxel if: T3, LVSI, R1, N+

Surgical approaches

  • Stomach is used as conduit in all
  • Trans-thoracic approach
  • Ivor Lewis – anastomosis distally in lower thorax
  • Mckeown – anastomosis more proximally in cervical region
  • Trans-hiatal approach
  • High morbidity and mortality ~ 10%
  • Minimally invasive and robotic approaches have been shown to reduce morbidity
  • CI to surgery: T4b (heart, great vessels, surrounding organs), bulky multistation LN, medically unfit

Mid-distal SCC

  • CROSS protocol: 41.4Gy/23# with concurrent carboplatin/paclitaxel followed by oesophagectomy 6-8 weeks post
    • NA CRT Improves median OS from 24 to 48 months, SCC>adenoCa
  • Definitive chemoradiotherapy: 50Gy/25# with cis/5FU in week 1 and week 5 (RTOG 8501)
  • If unfit for chemo 50Gy/25# + 14Gy/7# tumour boost RT alone (RTOG 8501)
  • No role for DE (ART-DECO, Intergroup, <5% LRR from CROSS)
  • No role for ENI (Lyu et al – ENI increases pneumonitis and oesophagitis without PFS or OS benefit in thoracic SCC)

Mid-distal AdenoCa

  • Peri-operative q2weekly x4 FLOT (5FU, leucovorin, oxali, docetaxol) chemotherapy (ESOPEC, improves median OS from 37 to 66 months)
  • If unfit for FLOT → CROSS protocol as above
    • pCR for adenoCa 20% vs 40% for SCC
  • FLOT chemotherapy more toxic than CROSS protocol
  • The addition of Durvalumab to FLOT improves EFS (MATTERHORN)

Cervical oesophageal ca

  • <5cm from cricopharyngeus
  • Often managed as a H&N treatment: 66Gy/33# with carboplatin and paclitaxel or 70Gy/35# with cisplatin
  • If treating as H&N then cover elective nodal regions: level 3/4, SCF to 56Gy
  • Surgical resection involves cervical oesophagectomy + laryngopharyngectomy
  • Significant morbidity and mortality

Immunotherapy

  • Adjuvant Nivolumab for 12mo if no pCR improves DFS for SCC and adenoCa (CHECKMATE 577)
  • Can be considered in all MMRd cases as per NCCN

Organ Preservation

  • Close surveillance with salvage oesphagectomy
  • Consider DE to 54Gy/27# - no high level evidence
  • Emerging management option from SANO ph II trial however:
    • Needs cCR on PET and endoscopy 12 weeks post treatment
    • Short term 2 year OS non-inferior
    • 50% need oesophagectomy at 6 mo
    • Trend towards increase DM with surveillance
  • Ongoing trials – SANO 3, ESOTRATE

Metastatic

  • Carboplatin, paclitaxel
  • Test for HER-2, PDL1 and MMR status to add in targeted and IO options
  • Her2 therapy
  • Immunotherapy, especially in MMRd

Palliative RT

  • ROCS trial shows no benefit in post stent RT (20Gy/5# or 30Gy/10#) for dysphagia in regards to symptom deterioration, stent complication or OS. RT reduced bleeding.

CROSS

P: T1-3N0M0 oesophageal ca, 75% adenoCa

I: oesophagectomy vs NA CRT → oesophagectomy

O: pCR 50% for SCC, 25% for adenoCa

Median OS 24 → 50mo.

10 yrs OS 38% vs 25%

Bigger median OS difference for SCC than adenoCa

Most recurrences were distant, <5% LRR

ART DECO/ Intergroup

  • DE to 64Gy with cis/5FU (Int) or 61Gy with carbo/taxol (ART) in definitive setting demonstrated no benefit in LRR (regardless of histo) or survival and more tox in Int

RTOG 8501

P: inoperable T1-3N0-1

I: 64Gy/32# RT alone vs 50Gy/25# with cisplatin and 5FU

O: 5 yr OS 0% vs 25%. 1/3rd will have LRR

ESOPEC

P: adenoCa, including GOJ

I: peri-operative FLOT chemo vs CROSS protocol

O: median OS 66 mo vs 37mo. G3 toxicity higher in peri-op FLOT, esp haematologic. Arms were not well balanced.

CHECKMATE 577

P: no pCR after CROSS

I: adjuvant nivolumab for 12 mo vs obs

O: DFS improved from 11 to 22.4 months regardless of histology and PDL1. Numerical but no sig OS benefit

SANO (ph II)

P: cCR at 3 months post CROSS

O: 2 yr OS no different ~ 70%, 50% need salvage surgery by 6 months, trend towards increase DM with surveillance

EBRT – NA distal oesophagus

Dose: 41.4Gy/23# with concurrent IV carboplatin 2 AUC weekly and IV paclitaxel 50mg/m2 weekly

95% isodose to cover 95% of PTV volume and 95% of GTV.

Dmax <107% within PTV

Pre-sim: optimise nutrition, establish durable feeding method, lung PFT, if lesion unidentifiable on CT consider oesophageal fiducials

Sim: supine, arms above head, vac bag, 4DCT, fuse FDG PET

Volumes

iGTV: using CT, PET and endoscopy report measurements

ITV: +3cm craniocaudally, 0.5cm radially cropped to VB.

  • If GOJ cancer limit expansion into stomach to 2cm

PTV: +7mm

GTVn

CTV: +1cm, crop to VB

  • If nodal disease defines top of primary volume a 3cm cranial expansion is not required from node however ensure 3cm from top of primary remains and cover intervening oesophagus if feasible

PTV: +7mm

Daily CBCT with soft tissue match

OARs

Lung

  • Important as pneumonitis can increase operative morbidity/mortality
  • unlikely to not meet lung OARS with neoadjuvant doses, however in definitive RT can consider DIBH, 2 dose levels (45Gy/50Gy) or rationalising volumes

Heart

  • V30Gy < 45% assoc with <15% pericarditis risk

Bilateral kidney mean <15Gy

EBRT – cervical oesophagus

Dose: 63Gy/54Gy/30# with concurrent IV carboplatin 2 AUC weekly and IV paclitaxel 50mg/m2 weekly OR IV cisplatin/5FU in week 1 and week 5

Goals: 95% isodose to cover 95% of PTV and 98% of GTV

Same as distal + dental assessment

Sim: supine, arms down, chin extended, TPS head and shoulder mask, fuse FDG PET

Volumes

GTV

CTV: expand 3cm craniocaudally, 0.5cm radially

  • Limit cranial expansion to inferior hypopharynx if uninvolved
  • Crop off uninvolved bone

PTV6300: +5mm

CTV elective nodes (54Gy): adjacent cervical nodes – level 3, 4, SCF

PTV5400: +5mm

HCC

Epidemiology · Differential diagnosis · Management · EBRT - SABR · OARs

Clinic page: Liver and biliary

Epidemiology

I: 6th most common ca globally, more in developing coutries

A: 60

G: M

R: Asian, SEA, Africa

RF

E: smoking, obesity, cirrhosis (hepatitis C, NAFLD, ETOH), PBC

I: Hepatitis B

G: Cirrhosis from alpha1-antitrypsin disease, wilson’s disease, haemachromatosis

I: HIV

A: A1AT

B: biliary – PBC, PSC

C: chronic hepatitis, HBV or HCV

D: Drugs

E: ETOH

F: Fatty liver

G: genetic – wilsons

H: haemachromotosis

*HBV or HCV viral treatment reduces cancer risk by 60%

Child-Pugh score

  • I: INR
  • A: albumin
  • B: bilirubin
  • Sx: ascites, hepatic encephalopathy
  • 5-6: A
  • 7-9: B
  • 10+: C

BCLC

  • 0: <2cm, ECOG 0, CP A
  • A: single >2cm, multifocal – 3 all <3cm, ECOG 0, CP A-B
  • B: multifocal >3 or >3cm, ECOG 0, CP A-B
  • C: ECOG 1-2, CP A-B, macrovascular invasion, LN or DM
  • D: ECOG 3, CP C

0: resection if candidate for transplant, otherwise focal therapy, survival > 5yrs

A: focal therapy, resection, transplant, survival > 5yrs

B: focal therapy, palliative systemic treatment if diffuse, survival >2.5 yrs

C: palliative systemic, survival > 2 yrs

D: BSC, survival 3mo

Differential diagnosis

Benign: haemangioma, cyst, adenoma, lipoma, abscess, focal nodular hyperplasia

Malignant: HCC, CCA, met, sarcoma, lymphoma

Path

  • Loss of normal portal tracts
  • Hypervascular with arterialisation of sinusoids
  • Thickened trabeculae
  • Malignant hepatocytes with nuclear atypia
  • Regenerative nodule > dysplastic nodule > HCC
  • Microvascular invasion is poor prognostic factor

Types

  • Fibrolamellar: young, F, good prognosis
  • Steatohepatic
  • Scirrhous
  • Clear cell
  • Chromophobe
  • IHC: AFP, HepPar1, glypican 3. CK7/20-ve

Triple phase MRI/CT

HCCLiver met
Arterial enhancement
Portovenous washout
Delayed pseudocapsule enhancement		Arterial rim enhancement in most.
Hypervascular mets can show diffuse enhancement (melanoma, RCC)
Portovenous hypointensity persists
Delayed peripheral rim enhancement

Screening

  • High risk HBV or cirrhosis should have q6monthly AFP and liver US – NCCN guidelines

Management

Liver transplant

  • MILAN criteria provides outline:
    • <5cm
    • 3 lesions <3cm
    • No vascular invasion
    • No extrahepatic spread
  • Local statewide liver transplant unit will have specific programs and criteria
  • MELD score used to stratify risk of death (Cr, bilirubin, INR)

Focal therapy

  • Bridging or downstaging to transplant
  • Definitive intent
  • Improve PFS compared to systemic therapy alone or in addition to systemic therapy (RTOG 1112, ph III & multiple ph II trials)
ResectionFit patient, small lesion with good liver function, no vascular invasion
Most established with best LC outcomes 80-90%. Potentially curative
MWA/RFALesion <3cm, away from vessels due to heat sink effect, ideally not capsular lesion or close to GI organs or central biliary tree due to risk of perforation
TACENo portal venous invasion or thrombus (risk of hepatic infarct), effect relies of perfusion of lesion, needs preserved liver function, no portal HTN.
Post-embolization syndrome from inflammation in 80% - pain, transaminitis, n/v, fevers for 3-4 days
SIRTRequires lung shunting and arterial mapping prior
Risk of pulmonary shunting, effect relies of perfusion, risk of arterial reflux into GI organs and ulceration, RILD risk, can be used with vascular invasion
SABRCan treat lesions with vascular invasion, 50Gy/5#. Not for tumour abutting GI organs. Suitability depends on CP B7 or less, cumulative diameter <15cm, single <10cm, uninvolved liver volume
Can be used in addition to TACE for large lesions
Trendy trial, ph II (~30 pts) shows better 2 yr LC than TACE 100% vs 44%. OS no diff.
Xi et al, ph III shows 2 yr LPFS better with SBRT than RFA in recurrent HCC – 93% vs 76%
Improves PFS in addition to Sorafenib (RTOG 1112)
MA shows 3 yr LC >90%, G3 tox 5% with SBRT. LC drops with >5cm/macrovascular invasion to 65%

Systemic

  • Bevacizumab & atezolizumab or durvalumab/tremelimumab
    • Bev s/e: HTN, GI perforation, bleeding and clotting – need to screen for varices and do coagulation studies prior to Bev
  • 2nd line: Sorafenib (TKI for VEGF and EGFR), levatinib (TKI): hand foot syndrome, diarrhoea, fatigue, hypothyroid
  • Sorafenib or bev/atezo have OS benefit in metastatic/advanced HCC but not in the adjuvant setting for localised HCC

Palliative

  • 8Gy/1# to whole liver for pain with antiemetic and dex pre-med (improves pain response compared to BSC alone 70% vs 20% – CCTG HE1, ph III)
  • Alternative whole liver doses: 10Gy/2# or 21Gy/7#

EBRT - SABR

Dose: 50Gy/5#, alternate days, VMAT, FFF MV photons

Goals

GTV D100 >/= 100% of total dose

Dmax 125% - 140% within GTV

PTV D95 >/= 100%

V50 to lie within 2cm from V100, CI 1-1.2

Pre-sim: check liver function (Hb, INR, plt, alb, PT, LFTs), discuss at MDT, cease systemic therapy prior, daily PPI/antiemetic, consider DTPA if near kidney, consider fiducial markers

Sim: NBM 3 hrs, supine, arms up, triple phase contrast CT, EEBH with all CTs, fusion of MRI, scan to include all OARS (heart, kidney)

Volumes

GTV: gross disease on primary dataset using triple phase and MRI for guidance

PTV: +5mm radially, 5-8mm sup-inf

Daily CBCT with soft tissue match

Toxicities

  • Acute: fatigue, n/v, diarrhoea, enteritis
  • Subacute: pneumonitis, RILD (classic or on-classic)
  • Late: GI perforation, ulceration, bleed, pulmonary fibrosis, kidney dysfunction, CW pain, rib fracture, biliary tree stricture, second malignancy

OARs

  • Liver-GTV mean dose: <13Gy
    • Volume: exclude GB, include PV from splenic confluence to first bifurcation to left and right PV
    • Gain ~1Gy of MLD for every 5Gy TD reduction
  • Total liver: V≤15Gy > 700cc
  • Central hepatobiliary tract: Dmean <19Gy
    • 1.5cm expansion of portal vein
  • CBD Dmax 0.1cc<50Gy
  • SB, stomach, oesophagus Dmax 0.03cc <30Gy, avoid V33Gy >0.5cc
  • LB Dmax 0.03cc <35Gy
  • Heart Dmax 0.1cc <38Gy
  • Kidney bilateral Dmean <10Gy
  • Spinal cord Dmax <25Gy

Classic/veno-occlusive RILD

  • DNA damage > ROS release > sinusoidal endothelial cell damage > hepatic venous outflow obstruction and congestion > chronic hypoxia > hepatocyte death
  • Fibrotic reaction from chronic inflammation and TGF-B release
  • ↑ALP, anicteric
  • Resulting liver dysfunction

Non-classic RILD

  • Due to hepatocyte injury in a cirrhotic liver or viral hepatitis
  • ↑ ALT/AST and BR

Cholangiocarcinoma

Epidemiology · Classification · Management

Clinic page: Liver and biliary

Epidemiology

I: Rare

A: 70

G: M

R: SEA (liver fluke)

RF

E: smoking, ETOH, PSC, chronic cholangitis, choledocolithasis

I: liver fluke, HBV/HCV for iCCA

G: Caroli’s disease

I: nil

MRI

Arterial rim enhancement

Delayed phase enhancement

Classification

  • Extrahepatic
    • Perihilar (CHD, left or right hepatic duct) – 60%
    • Distal (CBD) – 30%
    • Presents sooner with obstructive jaundice sx
  • Intrahepatic – 10%
    • Presents later with RUQ pain, wt loss

Bismuth-Corlette classification

  • Relates to resectability of perihilar CCA
  • Involving CHD, one of its main branches (Lt, Rt) or confluence +/- bifurcating branch

Path

  • Usually adenoCa
  • PSN growth patterns:
    • Papillary: intraductal
    • Sclerosing: invades through duct wall, fibrotic, most common
    • Nodular: constricting annular lesion
  • Tubules and cords in desmoplastic stroma with PNI common, early lymphatic spread
  • IHC: CK7, CK20, Ca19.9

Management

Resectable

  • Whipple’s procedure for distal
  • Extended hepatectomy + bile duct resection for peri-hilar
  • Intrahepatic resection for iCCA
  • Adjuvant capecitabine for 6 months std (BILCAP – PP analysis showed OS benefit)
  • If T3-4, N+ or SM+: adjuvant chemo capecitabine → RT with 54-59Gy/45Gy + capecitabine:
    • SWOG phase II single arm trial demonstrated improved OS; this trial only investigated extrahepatic CCA
    • Intrahepatic CCA – no high level evidence
  • Addition of camrelizumab to adj CRT improves OS (ACCORD)

Potentially resectable

  • Neoadjuvant chemotherapy (gem + platinum) → reassess
  • NA CRT 55-57Gy/45/25# + gem → further chemo → resection improves OS in patients with locally adv disease where upfront R0 not achievable (POLCAGB)

Unresectable

  • Intrahepatic
    • SABR: 50Gy/5#
    • Definitive CRT: 54Gy/30# with capecitabine
  • Extrahepatic:
    • RT if feasible to deliver 50+Gy
    • Systemic treatment – gemcitabine + cisplatin + durvalumab improves OS (TOPAZ-1)
    • Targeted therapy: HER2, MMR, IDH1, FGFR2

Symptomatic

  • ERCP with stent insertion
  • PTC / PTBD
  • EUS choledochoduodenostomy
  • Surgical bypass
  • Cholestyramine for pruritis
  • Treat cholangitis
  • Pancreatic enzymes if steatorrhea and fat soluble vitamin replacement

Pancreas

Epidemiology · Path – pre-malignant/benign · Management · NA EBRT · Adj EBRT (from RTOG 0848)

Clinic page: Pancreas

Epidemiology

I: 3rd leading cause of death, 8th most common ca

A: 60

G: M

R: African

RF

E: chronic pancreatitis, IPMN, smoking, obesity, ETOH via pancreatitis, T2DM, cystic fibrosis

I: nil

Unresectable

  • Induction systemic therapy: FOLFIRINOX > gem/Abraxane for 4-6mo → reassess

Palliative

Neuroendocrine

Epidemiology · Pathology · Management

Clinic page: General GI

Epidemiology

I: Rare, increasing incidence

A: 60

G: M

R: Caucasian

RF

G: MEN1, Peutz-jegher, NF-1, VHL

Natural Hx

  • 70% occur in GIT: small bowel > pancreas > large bowel
  • 20% in lung
  • G3 NET or NEC commonly have DM
  • G1-2 NET are indolent but can have late liver mets

Carcinoid syndrome

  • Release of 5-HT, prostaglandins, histamines
  • Often occurs after metastasizing to liver (bypass 1st pass metabolism)
  • Facial flushing, diarrhoea, bronchospasm, palpitations

Pathology

NET

  • Usually well-circumscribed
  • Behaviour depends on Ki-67 and mitotic rate
  • G1: Ki67 <3%, mitotic rate <2
  • G2: Ki67 3-20%, mitotic rate 2-20
  • G3: Ki67 >20%, mitotic rate >20
  • G3 NET is less ‘explosive’ than NEC

NEC

  • Small cell or large cell
  • All are G3, aggressive
  • Invasive, infiltrative borders
  • FDG PET avid

IHC: synaptophysin, chromogranin A, CD56, INSM1

Functional vs non-functional

  • VIPoma, insulinoma, gastrinoma
  • Carcinoid syndrome
  • Tend to present earlier due to sx
  • Needs treatment of symptoms in addition to cancer treatment

Management

Localised

  • Surgical resection + nodal surgery
  • Surveillance with DOTATATE PET

Metastatic/unresectable

G1-2 NET

  • if SSTR positive:
    • somatostatin analogues are antiproliferative but not cytotoxic – octreotide
    • Octreotide s/e: subcut injection, n/v, diarrhoea, steatorrhea cholecystitis/cholelithiasis, labile BSLs
    • 2nd line Lu-177 DOTATATE
  • If SSTR negative – everolimus (mTOR pathway inhibitor) or sunitinib for pancreatic NET

G3 NET

  • SSA, Lu-177 DOTATATE
  • Consider chemo if high Ki67, FDG avid, pancreatic G3 NET, high tumour burden
    • CAPTEM (cape/TMZ) if Ki67 <55%
    • cis/etop if Ki67 >55%

NEC

  • chemotherapy cisplatin/etoposide as per SCLC
  • Palliative RT

Functional NET syndrome management

  • VIPoma: watery diarrhoea, hypokalemia – IVF, electrolytes
  • Insulinoma: glucose support
  • Gastrinoma: high dose PPI
  • Carcinoid syndrome: octreotide/lanreotide
