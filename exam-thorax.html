Early NSCLC

Epidemiology · Differential diagnosis · Mgmt – T1-2N0M0 · EBRT – SABR · Daily 4D CBCT

Clinic page: Early-stage NSCLC (SABR)

Epidemiology

I: Common, 4th most common cancer

Highest mortality

A: 60, increasing in younger pop

G: M>F

R: Caucasian, Asian non-smoker for EGFRm adenoCa

RF

E: tobacco smoking, ETOH, air pollution (fuel fumes), asbestos exposure (synergistic with smoking → more aggressive lung ca), occupational: radon, nickel, chronic LRTI, second hand smoking , previous chest RT, Asian descent, pulmonary fibrosis, COPD, alpha-1 anti-trypsin deficiency

I: TB

G: Fhx of EGRFm adenoCa, Li Fraumeni

I: immunosuppression/HIV

Lifetime risk for smokers is 30% vs 1%

Risk reduces with increasing time from quitting but does not reach non-smoker

Staging (AJCC 9th edition)

  • T1a: 1cm
  • T1b: 1-2cm
  • T1c: 2-3cm
  • T2a: 3-4cm
  • T2b: 4-5cm
  • T3: 5-7cm or nodule in same lobe, phrenic n, CW invasion
  • T4: >7cm or invading other structures (diaphragm, pericardium, great vessels, VB, oesophagus, recurrent laryngeal n), ipsilateral nodule
  • N1: ipsilateral hilar node (st 10-14)
  • N2a: single station mediastinal node (st 1-9)
  • N2b: multiple station mediastinal node
  • N3: contralateral mediastinal or hilar node, SCF node
  • M1a: contralateral pulmonary nodule, pericardial or pleural effusion
  • M1b: DM 1 organ
  • M1c: DM multiple organs
  • NB: T1N2a is not stage IIB lung cancer not stage III

Proximal bronchial tree

  • Distal 2cm of trachea, carina, main bronchi up to 1st division of lobar bronchi

Biopsy

  • CT-guided - best peripheral, highest ptx risk, bleeding, no nodes
  • Bronchoscopy – access st 2, 4, 7
  • radial EBUS - peripheral airway lesions, lower ptx, yield variable
  • EUS- st 2, 4, 7, 8, 9 nodes
  • Surgical - diagnostic/therapeutic
  • Mediastinoscopy, thoracotomy
  • Sputum cytology, bronchopulmonary lavage – higher yield for central lesions

Differential diagnosis

  • Benign: granuloma, TB, abscess, inflammatory nodule, consolidation, sarcoid
  • Malignant: NSCLC, SCLC, metastasis, thymoma, mesothelioma, primary mediastinal B cell lymphoma, hodgkins lymphoma

Pathology

1. Squamous cell carcinoma – 30%

  • Radiologic: large central cavitating mass
  • IHC: p40, p63, AE1/AE3, PDL1

2. Adenocarcinoma – 40%

  • Radiolologic: peripheral tumour
  • Subtypes ‘LAMPS’: lepidic, acinar, micropapillary, papillary, solid predominant
  • ‘STAS’ – spread through air spaces = higher LRR risk, in favour of lobectomy vs sublobar
  • IHC: CK7, napsin A, TTF1

3. Large cell (dx of exclusion)- 10%

  • Radiologic: bulky mass with central necrosis
  • IHC negative for both above

4. Sarcomatoid, adenosquamous

5. Other: neuroendocrine - large cell, carcinoid, small cell covered elsewhere

PDL-1

  • Percentage of viable tumour cells with membranous PDL1 staining
  • Low, moderate, high: <1%, 1-49%, ≥50%

Screening

AU guidelines (fits 2026 NCCN guidelines)

  • 50-70yo
  • pyhx >30 years and current smoker or ex <10 years ago
  • asymptomatic
  • q2 yearly low dose CTC (1-2mSV)
    • Low-mod risk nodule → repeat LD CT in 3-24mo
    • High risk nodule → refer to resp and lung MDT

NLST (US)

  • RCT, >30pyhx current/ex within 15yrs
  • Annual low dose CT screening reduced relative risk of lung cancer death by 20%
  • NNT 300 to prevent 1 lung ca death

NELSON (Dutch)

  • RCT, 15 pyhx current or ex within 10 yrs
  • Lung cancer mortality reduced with low dose CT by 25%, higher in women
  • NNT 130 to prevent 1 lung ca death

PLCO

  • CXR screening did not benefit lung cancer survival

Australian data has proven cost effectiveness

Mgmt – T1-2N0M0

Excision

  • VATS lobectomy
  • sub-lobar resection equivalent to lobectomy for T1 in outer 1/3rd of lung (CALGB)
  • cN0: 1x N1 and 3x N2 station nodal sampling
  • cN1: nodal dissection
  • no head to head comparison between SABR and lobectomy
  • Surgery may be preferred if there is a high risk of occult nodal disease (large, central, aggressive biology)
  • DLCO and FEV1 approx >80% pred for lobectomy fitness

If upstaged intra-op to stage II (T2, N1)

  • Should have adjuvant systemic therapy with platinum doublet followed by IO/TKI (ADAURA, Impower010)
  • If EGFR driver mutation and indications for chemo then should have chemo first followed by Osimertinib for 3 yrs
    • Osi s/e: rash, diarrhea, ILD, pneumonitis, cardiomyopathy
  • No routine role for adjuvant RT (lung-ART, PORT C)
    • Consider for R1/R2 60-66Gy to bronchial stump and tumour bed only

SABR

  • excellent LC outcomes > conventional RT (CHISEL)
  • higher BED>100 associated with better LC, esp with larger T2 tumours (CHISEL)
  • pooled analysis shows at least equivalent outcomes to surgery (STARS & ROSEL pooled analysis)
  • caution with ILD, especially UIP

Central lesion: 50Gy/5#, 60Gy/8#

  • central: PTV ≤ 2cm from proximal bronchial tree or abutting mediastinal/pericardial pleura
  • RTOG 0813: DE above 10Gy/# for central tumours resulted in Rx related deaths. 50Gy/5# safe at 1 yr.

Ultra-central: 55Gy/20# or 60Gy/15# (LUSTRE)

  • PTV overlapping proximal bronchial tree, oesophagus, pulmonary vessels +/- great vessels
  • SUNSET: 60Gy/8# safe for UC lesions though 1 G5 pneumonia. Dmax up to 120%
  • HILUS: 56Gy/8# 15% G5 bronchopulmonary toxicity for UC lesion (hotter coverage – Dmax up to 150%)

Peripheral lesion: 54Gy/3# or 48Gy/4#

  • If GTV <1cm from CW then 48Gy/4#

Systemic therapy

  • Addition of nivolumab improves EFS but associated with G3 toxicity (iSABR, ph II)
  • KEYNOTE – no benefit with addition of pembro, G3+ tox

CALGB/Alliance 140503

P: ≤2cm, outer third, N0

I: sub-lobar resection vs lobectomy

O: 5 yr OS 50%, locoregional recurrence 40% - no diff. Less FEV1 and FVC decline with sublobar

STARS & ROSEL pooled analysis

  • Better 3 yr OS (95% vs 80%) and RFS (86% vs 80%) with SABR vs surgery

CHISEL/TROG

P: ≤ 5cm, inoperable

I: conv RT vs SABR

O: SABR had better 2 yr LC 90% vs 65% and better OS 80% vs 60%. Tox similar.

STARS & ROSEL pooled analysis

P: early NSCLC - individual trials failed to reach recruitment, pooled analysis of ~60 pts

I: surgery vs SABR

O: OS 95% vs 80% for SABR vs surgery, however likely underpowered

Revised STARS analysis

  • OS similar between surgery and SABR, LRR and DM may be slightly higher with SABR

iSABR

P: T1-3N0M0

I: nivolumab x4 + SABR vs SABR alone

O: improved 4 yr EFS from 50% to 80%. G3 toxicity with IO.

ADAURA (after resection)

  • Post-op Osimertinib for 3 yrs in stage I-III with EGFRm exon 19 del or L858R → better OS, DFS and brain mets. 5 yr OS 70% vs 80%

IMpower010

  • post-op cisplatin + atezolizumab in stage II-III improved DFS

Lung-ART

P: R0 resected stage II. Chemo allowed

I: RT 54Gy to lung and nodes vs no RT. 3DCRT.

O: less LRR but no DFS or OS diff. More cardiopulmonary death

PORT-C

P: R0 resected stage with N2 + adj chemo

I: RT 50Gy vs no RT – more modern IMRT

O: RT did not benefit DFS or OS

Lung-ART & PORT-C trials did not include R1/2 resection

Mgmt

Operable stage II-III

  • No N3, non bulky <2cm single station N2, T3, select T4 where R0 feasible, adequate pulmonary reserve FEV1 and DLCO >80%
  • All should have peri-operative chemo-immunotherapy – increase pCR and OS (NADIM II, KEYNOTE 671)
  • Adjuvant TKI for 3 years if EGFRm improves OS by 10% at 5 years - ADAURA [see early NSCLC]
  • If upstaged intra-op and no pre-op chemo-IO given, should still have adjuvant platinum doublet chemo followed by IO for DFS benefit
  • No role for radiotherapy unless R1 – lung-ART, PORT-C [see early NSCLC]

Inoperable stage II

  • Radiotherapy alone – 55Gy/20# or 66Gy/33# for large bulky tumour as per international guidelines.
  • No randomised evidence, small numbers of stage II in CRT trials

Inoperable stage III

  • Concurrent chemoradiation: 60Gy/30# with platinum based chemo (Goustave-Roussy MA)
  • Concurrent chemo options: carbo/taxol or cis/etop (Liang et al). Cis/etop better outcomes but more oesphagitis, carbo/taxol has more pneumonitis risk
  • No role for ENI (PET-PLAN)
  • Equivocal nodes should often be clarified with EBUS if management-changing
  • No role for dose escalation (RTOG 0617 – worse OS by 8mo with 74Gy/37#)
  • Consolidation Durvalumab for 12 months to improve 5 yr OS from 30% to 40% (PACIFIC)
    • Needs restaging demonstrating no progression after CRT
    • Uncertain benefit for PDL1 TPS 0%
  • Adjuvant TKI if EGFRm until progression or unacceptable toxicity (LAURA, 3 yr OS benefit of 10%)
    • Osimertinib or Amivantamab for exon 20 mutation

Oligometastatic

  • EGFRm disease: addition of SABR to all sites including primary to TKI improves OS (SINDAS – however this pre-dated Osimertinib, used 1st gen TKI, NORTHSTAR – showed SABR was well tolerated with Osi)
  • SABR to oligoresistant mets after 1st line systemic therapy with ongoing chemotherapy improves OS and PFS. Mainly pre-IO. (Oligomez, ph II, 50 pts & Iyengar)
  • SABR to oligoresistant mets 1st line systemic therapy including IO is not beneficial though only 31% had primary treated (NRG LU002, ph II/III, 215pts)
  • SABR to oligoprogressive may improve OS (SABR-COMET, CURB showed PFS benefit)

Pancoast tumour (RTOG 0930, INT 0160 – ph II)

  • Pancoast: Horner’s syndrome due to impaired sympathetic innervation, ulnar nerve dysfunction from lower BP invasion causing hand weakness, shoulder to radiating arm pain in ulnar distribution
  • Obtain MRI brachial plexus as part of work-up
  • Unresectable: >50% VB invasion, C spine brachial plexus invasion, N3, medically unfit, subclavian vessel unreconstructable, N2 controversial, trachea/oesophagus
  • Sim: arms down, head turned to contralateral side
  • Resectable: neoadj CRT 45Gy/25# with cis/etop → surgery (lobectomy + chest wall resection) + cis/etop x2c
  • 5 yr OS 50% if pCR
  • Boderline/Unresectable: neoadjuvant CRT → reassess → resect or continue CRT to 60Gy/30#.
  • 5 yr OS 20%

Metastatic

  • AdenoCa: if targetable mutation → first line TKI
  • FLAURA II: Osi + chemo vs chemo alone in EGFRm improves OS vs chemo alone
  • 5 yr OS 30%
  • Others: carbo/pem/pembro or carbo/taxol/pembro or IO alone
  • High PDL1 TPS ≥ 50% → IO monotherapy, low TPS <1% and intermediate TPS 1-49% → chemo-IO
  • Carbo/pem/pem is q3weekly cycles for 4 cycles then maintenance pem/pem for up to 24 months or toxicity limited
  • 5 yr OS 20%

Re-irradiation: 45Gy/30# BD

NADIM II

P: resectable stage III

I: NA platinum chemo and surgery vs NA chemo+nivo, surgery and adj nivo

O: pCR 10% vs 40%, OS 60% vs 83%

KEYNOTE 671

P: resectable stage II-III

I: Same as NADIM II but with pembro

O: pCR 5% vs 20%, improved 2 yr EFS by 20%

Goustave-Roussy MA

  • Concurrent platinum based chemo superior to sequential chemo. Improves 5 yr OS by 5% and reduces LRF by 5%.
  • Sequential still better than no chemo in frail
  • No benefit for induction chemo
  • Increases grade 3-4 oesophagitis. No increase in lung tox.

Liang et al

  • Cis/etop did not meet superiority vs carbo/taxol.
  • Cis/etop had less pneumonitis but more oesophagitis

PET-PLAN

  • 50Gy ENI had higher tox and no OS diff

RTOG 0617

O: OS worse with 74Gy , more G3-5 toxicity with DE

OARs

Lung – iGTV: V20Gy <30 (to keep G2 pneumonitis <20%), V30Gy <20, V5Gy<60, MLD < 20Gy

Oesophageal: mean dose <28Gy, V60Gy <17% (<15% G3 oesophagitis)

Heart: mean dose <20Gy, ALARA

Spinal cord: Dmax <45Gy

BP: Dmax <66Gy

Toxicities: see early stage NSCLC

Pneumonitis: DNA damage → ROS → damage to type 1 & 2 pneumocytes → inflammatory cytokines → leaky capillary → oedematous infiltrate in alveolar spaces → impaired gas exchange and collapse of alveolar space due to surfactant loss

Mgmt

Exclude/treat infection/PE; grade 1 observe; grade ≥2 prednisolone 50mg with slow wean 4- 6 weeks; oxygen/IV methylpred for severe; hold IO/TKI

Pulmonary fibrosis: ROS → chronic inflammation → cytokines including TGF-B → myofibroblast activation & fibrosis, endothelial cell damage and hypoxia, death of type 1 pneumocytes → collapsed and fibrotic lung

Rib fracture: periosteal/osteoblast and vascular injury → impaired remodelling/osteoradionecrosis-like fragility

Fracture risk rises with chest wall V30 (30cc <30Gy)

Chest wall pain: intercostal nerve injury → neuropathic pain

Limited SCLC

Clinic page: Small cell lung cancer

Epidemiology

I: Less common than NSCLC, prevalent in smokers

A: 70

G: M

R: Europe, Asia - high smoking rates

RF

E: smoking, asbestos has synergistic effect with smoking, second hand smoke, occupational: radon, arsenic, nickel, silica

I: nil

G: nil

I: nil

Staging

Veterans affair

  • LS-SCLC: safely encompassable within 1 radiation field /hemithorax – 30%
  • ES-SCLC: not encompassed in 1 radiation field – 70%
  • SCF LN is LS-SCLC
  • Pleural/pericardial effusion is ES-SCLC

TNM

Follows NSCLC

Paraneoplastic syndromes

SIADH – 10-15%

  • muscle weakness, confusion, seziures, hyponatremia,
  • Rx fluid restriction

Lambert-Eaton – <5%

  • proximal muscle weakness which improves with activity
  • Rx pyridostigmine

Cushings syndrome - <5%

  • weight gain, moon face, muscle wasting, high serum cortisol
  • Rx ketaconazole

Pathology

Small round blue cells

Crush artifact

Salt & pepper chromatin

Abundant mitoses & necrosis

IHC: chromogranin A, synaptophysin, CD56, INSM1, high Ki67, often TTF1 +ve

Radiologically

Central mass, frequent N+

All need MRIB to exclude DM

Chemo and radiosensitive

  • Small dose per # effective

Short doubling time, fast repopulation

  • BD # counteracts repopulation
  • Acceleration ↓TTT and allows ↑ dose

Other NEC

Large cell neuroendocrine

  • less aggressive than small cell
  • extensive mitoses & necroses
  • prognosis between SCLC and NSCL
  • peripheral circumscribed mass

Carcinoid tumour

  • less aggressive
  • low mitoses
  • circumscribed polypoid mass

IHC for both same as SCLC, lower ki67

Management

T1-T2N0

  • Uncommon presentation.
  • Lobectomy + nodal dissection followed by chemo can be appropriate if biopsy proven node negative and complete resection possible
  • SABR followed by chemotherapy also an option – retrospective data

1. Chemotherapy: cisplatin and etoposide q3weekly x4 cycles

  • beware of risk of TLS in bulky disease

2. Thoracic RT

  • RT improves 2 yr LC by 25% and 2 yr OS by 5%, survival benefit with commencing RT with cycle 1 or 2 of chemotherapy (MA Warde et al)
  • 45Gy/30# BD – 10% OS benefit at cost of ↑ oesphagitis though used 3DCRT and ENI - Turrisi
  • 60Gy/40# BD – THORA trial improves OS by 10% without sig tox difference
  • 54Gy/45Gy/30# SIB to iGTV BD – Sun et al improves OS by 20%
  • Alternatives: 66Gy/33# (CONVERT), 45Gy/15#
  • No role for ENI
  • Consider sequential chemo → RT for frail patients at likely expense of some survival benefit

3. Consider PCI for responders – 25Gy/10#

  • Rationale: high brain met risk, sanctuary site from chemo, treat occult micromet with RT
  • 3 yr 5% OS benefit with PCI in pre-MRI and IO era (PCI collaborative MA, Auperin et al)
  • Hippocampal avoidance non-inferior with less neurocognitive decline (NRG CC003)
  • Memantine can also reduce cognitive decline: 5mg OD titrated to 10mg BD for 6 months
  • Alternative: MRIB surveillance q3monthly
  • MAVERICK ph III trial: no survival detriment with MRI surveillance, higher CNS progression rates

4. Adjuvant consolidation Durvalumab q4 weekly 24 months

  • Fit patient without PD after CRT
  • improves 3 yr OS by 10% - ADRIATIC
  • The addition of consolidation Tarlatamab (bispecific T cell engager) to IO is being investigated in the DELPHI-306 trial

Turrisi et al

P: LS SCLC

I: 45Gy/30# BD vs 40Gy/25# OD

O: better 5 yr OS 16% vs 26%, median OS 23mo vs 19mo

worse acute G3 oesophagitis 11% vs 27% - NB used 2D planning and ENI

Subsequent trials looking at 45Gy/30# BD vs OD DE CONVERT 66Gy/33#, CALGB 70Gy/35# failed to show survival benefit

THORA

P: LS SCLC

I: 45Gy/30# BD vs 60Gy/40# BD

O: 5 yr OS 28% vs 39%, median OS 44mo vs 22mo

No acute tox diff. 2 oesophageal strictures in 60Gy arm – used mostly 3DCRT, some IMRT

Yu et al

P: LS SCLC

I: 54Gy/30# BD vs 45Gy/30# BD

O: median OS 60mo vs 40mo

CONVERT

P: LS- SCLC

I: 66Gy/33# OD vs 40Gy/30# BD

O: Designed as superiority, failed to demonstrate this however arms had similar outcomes

ADRIATIC

P: LS-SCLC completed definitive CRT without progression

I: Durvalumab for 2 years +/- tremelimumab vs placebo

O: Durvalumab improved 3 yr OS from 48% to 57%, median OS 56mo vs 33mo

NRG-CC003

  • PCI with HA did not improve the primary delayed-recall endpoint but reduced overall neurocognitive failure without compromising intracranial control

Extensive SCLC

Management · CREST

Clinic page: Small cell lung cancer

Management

1. Chemoimmunotherapy: carboplatin and etoposide q3weekly x4+ either durvalumab or atezolizumab until PD or unacceptable toxicity

  • Immunotherapy improves OS (Caspian, Impower133)
  • Beware of TLS for bulky disease

2. Tarlatamab (bispecific T cell engager) in addition to maintenance IO

  • DELPHI-303 (ph 1) showed promising median OS of 25 months, ph 3 data pending from DELPHI-305
  • S/e: cytokine release syndrome, ICANS/neurotoxicity

3. Consolidation thoracic RT if partial response to chemo with residual dx

  • 30Gy/10# or 40Gy/15#
  • Improved 2 yr OS by 10% and reduced thoracic failure by 40% - CREST trial, pre-IO era
  • 30Gy/10# consolidation RT after chemoIO alone did not improve OS and had more toxicities including deaths than IO alone (Treasure trial, ph II)

4. PCI

  • PCI has 14% OS benefit and reduces symptomatic brain mets – Slotman et al
  • Now MRI surveillance is non-inferior to PCI for OS (Takahashi et al)
  • Less neurocognitive decline, higher brain mets rate
  • MRIB q3mo for year 1 then q3-6mo
  • Salvage SRS to brain mets reduces neurocognitive decline vs WBRT without clear OS detriment (ENCEPHALON)

5. Disease progression

  • Primary refractory: progression <6 months from chemo
    • Poor prognosis – months
    • Lurbinectedin, topotecan, clinical trial
  • Platinum sensitive: progression >6months post chemo
  • Prognosis can be several months
  • Rechallenge platinum chemo
  • Second line: topotecan, paclitaxel, tarlatamab

Mesothelioma

Epidemiology · Differential diagnosis · Management · EBRT – hemithorax · Toxicities

Clinic page: Thymic, mesothelioma and other thoracic

Epidemiology

I: Rare, however Au has relatively high incidence given significant asbestos use until ban in 2003

A: 70+

G: M
R: Caucasian

RF

E: occupational asbestos exposure, living near asbestos mines/plants, para-occupational exposure (contaminated clothing), carbon nanotubes, thorotrast, Erionite (rocks in Turkey)

I: Simian virus 40

G: BAP1 gene mutation (also increases melanoma risk)

I: nil

NB – long latency period 20-40 years between asbestos exposure and lung Ca

Asbestos

  • Amphibole fibres (crocidolite)– highest risk
  • Serpentine – lower risk

Pathophysiology

  • Inhaled fibres accumulate in parietal pleura à
  • unable to be cleared by phagocytes à
  • chronic inflammation release of ROS, inflammatory cytokines (TNF-a, IL-1, IL-6) à
  • direct and indirect DNA damage → loss of TSG → malignancy
  • Latency period of 20-50 years

Differential diagnosis

  • Benign: pleural plaques, effusion, empyema, fibrosis
  • Malignant: mesothelioma, pleural mets, malignant effusion

Mesothelioma:
Arises from mesothelium- epithelial lining within body cavities. It is present in:

  • Pleura – 90%
  • Peritoneum – 10%
  • testes tunica vaginalis - <1%
  • pericardium - <1%

Pathology

Pleural malignant mesothelium

1. Epitheliod - 60%

  • Poor prognosis – OS 18-24mo
  • IHC: AE1/AE3, CK5/6, WT-1, D240, calretinin

2. Sarcomatoid – 15%

  • Very poor prognosis – OS 6-12mo
  • IHC: AE1/AE3, CK5/6, vimentin, D2-40/WT1/calretinin weak

3. Mixed – 25%

  • More than 10% of each

Screening

  • No established role for screening, given poor prognosis
  • Guidelines suggest patient education and early investigation of sx

TNM

  • T1: ipsilateral parietal pleura only
  • T2: all ipsilateral pleural surfaces with one of: diaphragm invasion or lung invasion
  • T3: all ipsi surfaces + one of: focal CW invasion, mediastinum or pericardium
  • T4: all ipsi surfaces + one of: diffuse CW invasion, mediastinal organs, spine, contralat pleura
  • N1: ipsilateral nodes
  • N2: contralateral, SCF nodes
  • M1: DM

Management

Discuss all in MDT

Resectable – T1-T3 N1 M0, epitheliod

1. Neoadjuvant chemotherapy: cisplatin/pemetrexed

  • Trovo et al and IMPRINT utilised NACT

2. Cytoreductive Surgery

  • Controversial as the MARS I & II trials showed no benefit of addition of any surgery to chemotherapy however patients were poorly selected
  • Pleurectomy & decortication – removes visceral and parietal pleura and gross tumour, spares lung
  • Extended P/D – above + resection or pericardium or diaphgram
  • Extra-pleural pneumectomy – removes pleura and sacrifices lung, pericardium, diaphgram
  • More morbidity and mortality than P/D (MARS II, ph III)

3. Adjuvant radiotherapy

  • RT after P/D can improve OS with IMRT
  • RT 50-60Gy/30# after lung sparing surgery + chemo improves 2 yr OS from 28% to 58% but high G3-4 tox (Italian trial - Trovo et al, ph III)
  • Modern 50-54Gy IMRT hemi-thorax RT did not have any G4 tox - IMPRINT
  • Mixed data for adj RT after EPP, may improve LC
  • Risk of G5 pneumonitis, lung OARs must be strictly respected
  • No benefit for adj RT for LRC or OS (SAKK 17/04, phase II)
  • 21Gy/3# to surgical tract had a trend to LC benefit but not sig (SMART, ph III)

4. Adjuvant chemotherapy

  • Consider after EP/D or EPP
  • Note surgery + adj chemo vs chemo alone is controversial due to peri-op morbidity and worse OS from the MARS trials

Unresectable – T4, N2, M+, sarcomatoid

1. Systemic therapy

  • First line fit: ipi/nivo
  • Epitheliod: carboplatin/pemetrexed +/- bevacizumab
  • Response rates <50%

2. Palliative RT

  • Doses >4Gy/# for higher local response, response lasts 2-3mo

EBRT – hemithorax

Dose: 60/50Gy/30# seq boost to residual tumour, daily treatment, VMAT

No concurrent chemotherapy

Pre-sim: discuss at MDT, ensure surgical scars healed, baseline PFTs, dust disease board referral if asbestos related

Sim: supine, arms above head, vac bag, 4D CT

Volumes

iGTV: residual tumour on all phases

ITV6000: +7mm

PTV 6000: +5mm

CTV5000: Pleural surfaces, including diaphragm and pericardium and lateral VB and CW

ITV: on all resp phases

CTV: +5mm inner expansion, 10mm outer expansion to create rind, include scar and drain sites + ITV6000

PTV5000: +5mm

Contralateral lung V20Gy <7%, MLD <7Gy

Toxicities

Acute: fatigue, cough, dermatitis, n/v, enteritis, oesophagitis

Subacute: pneumonitis, pericarditis

Late: fibrosis, rib fracture, CW pain, brachial plexopathy, oesphageal stricture, pleural effusion. Negligible - cardiac toxicity, 2nd malignancy

Myasthenia Gravis

  • up to 50% of thymoma
  • proximal muscle weakness, -worsening with activity, ptosis
  • Post-synaptic AChR Ab
  • Managed with pyridostigmine, IVIG, plasmapheresis and immunomodulation
  • Treating thymoma can improve MG

Other assoc paraneoplastic

  • Pure red cell aplasia
  • Good syndrome (B cell ↓ and hypogammaglobulinaemia)

Differential diagnosis

Benign: thymic tissue, thyroid goitre, paraganglioma, neurofibroma, schwannoma, lipoma, myxoma

Malignant: germ cell tumour, thymoma, thyroid ca, mediastinal lymphoma, teratoma, sarcoma, mets, lung ca

Masaoka staging

Surgical staging

  • Stage 1: within capsule
  • Stage 2a: microscopic extension through capsule
  • Stage 2b: macroscopic extension
  • Stage 3: invasion into mediastinum, lung
  • Stage 4: disseminated intrathoracic disease or DM

Pathology

  • Type A: bland spindle/oval epithelial cells, lymphocyte deplete
  • Type AB: mixed
  • Type B1: thymus like, epithelial cells scant, lymphocyte rich
  • Type B2: thymus like, polygonal epithelial cells easily visible, lymphocyte rich
  • Type B3: sheets of polygonal epithelial cells, lymphocyte deplete
  • Thymic carcinoma: very aggressive, can be any type – SCC, adenoCa, sarcomatoid

IHC

  • Epithelial cells: AE1/AE3, CK5/6, vimentin
  • T lymphocytes: TdT (immature T cells), CD4, CD8, CD1, CD3
  • Thymic carcinoma: CD5, CD117, often p40/p63

Management

Resectable

1. Optimise myasthenia gravis pre-operatively

  • Pyridostigmine → acetylcholinesterase inhibition at NMJ

2. Total thymectomy

3. Adjuvant radiotherapy

  • Stage II B3 – consider case by case
  • Stage III
  • R2: 60Gy
  • R1 resection: 54Gy
  • R0: 50Gy

4. Adjuvant concurrent chemotherapy – cis/etop

  • Thymic carcinoma – all except stage I with R0

Unresectable

1. Induction chemotherapy – cis/etop or cis, doxorubicin, cyclophosphamide for 2-4 cycles

2. Reassess → local therapy surgery or definitive CRT 60Gy + cis/etop if thymic carcinoma

Metastatic

  • Cisplatin/etoposide
  • Doxorubicin, cyclophosphamide, cisplatin

Surveillance

  • B3 and thymic carcinoma are PET avid and thus surveillance and work-up PET can be considered

Dose: 54Gy (R1), 60Gy (gross disease) in 2Gy/#, VMAT, daily, with concurrent cisplatin 50mg/m2 and etoposide 50mg/m2 chemotherapy

Pre-sim: PFT, check sternotomy scar healed

Sim: supine, arms up, vac bag

4DCT but consider motion management if >10mm movement: BH, gating
