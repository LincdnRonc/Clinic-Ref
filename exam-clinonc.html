Chemotherapy

Toxicities · Common regimes

Toxicities

  • Lomustine/CCNU (PO): delayed myelosuppression
  • Procarbazine (PO): myelosuppression, GI upset
  • TMZ (PO): myelosuppression
  • Capecitabine (PO): more hand foot syndrome, diarrhoea, less mucositis & myelosuppression
  • 5FU (IV): myelosuppression, mucositis, diarrhoea, alopecia, hand foot syndrome, coronary artery spasm
    • DPYD testing suggested. Intermediate 5% or poor metaboliser 0.5% which can cause fatal tox to 5FU/cape if poor metaboliser.
  • Irinotecan (IV): neutropenia, diarrhoea & delayed diarrhoea, cholinergic syndrome
  • Oxaliplatin (IV): peripheral neuropathy, myelosuppression pharyngolaryngeal spasm with cold air, jaw tightness
  • Leucovorin (IV): reduces folate to increase 5FU efficacy
  • MMC (IV): myelosuppression, GI upset
  • Gemcitabine (IV): myelosuppression, myalgia, fevers, GI upset
  • Carboplatin (IV): Plt low/myelosuppression, electrolyte derangement, nausea/vomiting [PEN].
    • Less renal and ototoxicity
  • Paclitaxel (IV): peripheral neuropathy, alopecia, myalgia/arthralgia [PAL] + myelosuppression
  • Etoposide (IV): myelosuppression, alopecia, n/v [MAN]
  • Docetaxel (IV): myelosuppression, alopecia, n/v [MAN]

Targeted

VEGF

VEGF

  • Bevacizumab: VTE, bleeding, HTN, GI perforation, increased rates of chemo tox if concurrent

EGFR

  • TKI (Osimertinib): blocks intracellular ATP binding site of tyrosine kinase enzyme after dimerization and prevents downstream pathways
  • EGFR monoclonal Ab (Cetuximab/panitumumab): extracellular receptor blockade preventing ligand activation/enzyme dimerization
  • S/e: acneiform rash (generally improves after 1st 3 months), skin fissure, pruritis, dry skin, mucositis, diarrhoea, pneumonitis (TKI)

Radionuclide

Lu-177

Lu-177

  • DOTATATE: n/v, diarrhoea, hepatotoxicity
  • PSMA: xerostomia, dysgeusia, dry eye
  • Both: infertility risk, second malignancy (AML), myelosuppression, AKI
  • Primarily B emission <1mm range (therapeutic component), low abundance gamma (for imaging). T ½ 7 days.

I-131

  • B-emission is therapeutic, high gamma emission used for imaging /more radiation safety implications. T1/2 8 days. Range of 0.6mm
  • S/e: sialadenitis + thyroiditis + second malignancy + fertility concern, second malignancy, myelosuppression

Ix

Hypercalcaemia

Mechanism · Management

Mechanism

  • PTHrP release (SCC) → ↑ osteoclast activity and bone resorption + renal reabsorption of calcium (and ↓ renal phosphate reabsorption)
    • High cCa, low PTH, low phosphate
  • Lytic bony mets (multiple myeloma) → ↑ osteoclastic activity and bone turnover → release of calcium into blood stream
  • Calcitriol mediated (lymphoma) → extra-renal calcitriol production → ↑ GI Ca absorption (and ↑ phosphate absorption +/- bone resorption)
    • High cCa, high phosphate
  • Dehydration → relative increase of calcium concentration in blood

Symptoms

  • Confusion, renal stone, constipation, abdo pain
  • ‘stones, bones, groans, psychiatric overtones’

Management

  • Bloods: assess Vit D, Mg and phosphate
  • ECG – short QT interval
  • Cease contributory agents: thiazides, lithium
  • IV hydration with 0.9% NS
  • Bisphosphonates (Zoledronic acid) – correct Mg and phosphate prior, can take 3 days for effect
  • RANKL ligand inhibitor (Denosumab) - if refractory to above or severe renal impairment, can take 1 week for effect
  • Calcitonin for rapid effect if severe/symptomatic
  • Dialysis
  • Goals of care discussion
  • Poor prognostic marker

Cord compression

Symptoms · Management

Clinic page: Bone metastases, cord compression and spine SBRT

Symptoms

  • Progressive back pain, radicular band-like
  • Limb weakness, gait disturbance
  • Loss of sensation, saddle anaesthesia
  • Bladder or bowel change

Examination findings

  • UMN (below level of compression): weakness, rigid tone, hyperreflexia, upgoing Babinski
  • LMN (at level of compression, peripheral nerve cause): weakness, flacid, hyporeflexia, absent Babinski
  • Sensory level: loss of sensation below dermatome level, indicates compression at/above that level
  • Dermatomal sensory loss: exiting peripheral nerve root compression (usually unilateral, unless there is extensive bilateral nerve exit root impingement)
  • Cauda equina: Reduced anal tone, saddle anaesthesia, faecal incontinence, bladder retention

Management

Medical

  • Steroids + PPI, BSL monitoring
  • MRI whole spine. Alternatives – CT myelogram

Decompressive/separation surgery indications (laminectomy, debulking):

  • Bilsky grade 3 (no CSF)
  • Neurological symptoms
  • Not multi-level disease
  • Young, fit patient with reasonable prognosis

Stabilisation surgery

  • SINS score >6
  • Unstable fracture, mechanical pain, spinal deformity, bony retropulsion

Upfront radiotherapy: 20Gy/5# to 30Gy/10#. Reserve 8Gy/1# for very palliative cases with prognosis of weeks- few months

  • Better neurological recovery (ability to walk) with surgery followed by RT if pre-existing neurology (Patchell et al)
  • early cord compression with preserved ambulation
  • radiosensitive disease (lymphoma, SCLC)
  • stable spine
  • multilevel compression

Paraplegia >48hrs

  • chance of neurological recovery is poor → surgery/RT still used for pain, stability, local control

Patchell

P: 100 pts with MSCC, paraplegia <48hrs, ph III RCT

I: decompressive surgery + post-op RT with RT upfront with 30Gy/10#

O: Regain ambulation: 60% vs 20%.

Ambulatory after treatment: 85% vs 60%.

Median duration of ambulation: 122 days vs 13 days

Other emergencies

Figure

  • Febrile neutropenia/sepsis:
    • cultures, empiric IV antibiotics within 1 hour (tazocin 4.5g q6hrly), G-CSF as preventative measure to shorten neutropenia duration
  • Tumour lysis syndrome (see Haem):
    • aggressive bulky lymphoma, hyperK/hyperPO₄/hypoCa/hyperuricaemia, prophylactic fluids, allopurinol vs rasburicase
  • Malignant airway obstruction/stridor:
    • steroids, nebulised adrenaline, ENT/anaesthetics, stent/bronchoscopy/RT
  • UGIB:
    • resus, lateral decubitus bleeding side down, gastroscopy, haemospray, IR angioembolization, palliative RT
  • Pericardial effusion/tamponade:
    • echo, pericardiocentesis, surgical window, systemic therapy to treat cause
  • Pleural effusion:
    • pleural drain, pleurX, talc or mechanical pleurodesis, trapped lung requires in dwelling pleural catheter
  • Bowel obstruction:
    • CTAP, surgical vs conservative NGT, dexamethasone, octreotide (reduces proximal secretion accumulation from cancer), antiemetics (haloperidol/levomepromazine do not act on gut motility), opioid, hyoscine, venting PEG/stoma, large bowel stent
  • Cancer pain:
    • WHO ladder, renal failure opioid choice – avoid morphine, endone if mild, hydromorphone, fentanyl, buprenorphine, methadone, adjuncts (neuropathic pain, antispasmodic, anxiolytic, steroids), neurolysis, nerve block, vertebroplasty, sacroplasty, palliative RT
  • Anaemia/thrombocytopenia:
    • due to marrow infiltration from cancer, DIC in advanced malignancy (systemic coagulation activation causing clotting followed by overconsumption of clotting factors/plt and bleeding), systemic treatment related cytopenia, additional causes such as haemolysis. Transfuse to keep Hb >80
  • SIADH (SCLC, brain met):
    • euvolemic hyponatremia with high urine Na osmolality, fluid restriction, controlled sodium correction <8mmol/L in first 24 hrs due to risk of osmotic demyelination
  • Cachexia:
    • systemic inflammatory metabolic syndrome causing weight and muscle loss not fully reversible by nutrition alone
  • IV contrast anaphylaxis:
    • 0.5mg 1:1000 (1mg/mL) adrenaline IM into lateral thigh PRN q5min, IVF bolus, neb salbutamol for bronchospasm, antihistamines after urgent period

Radiosensitivity

Syndromes with radiation hypersensitivity · Genetic conditions associated with

Syndromes with radiation hypersensitivity

Syndromes with radiation hypersensitivity – DNA repair deficiency syndromes

  • ATM (more in homozygous than heterozygous)
  • Nijmegen breakage syndrome (NBS1)
  • Fanconi anaemia
  • Xeroderma pigmentosum – nucleotide excision repair
  • Werner syndrome

Mgmt

  • Use techniques which increase conformality and reduce dose to nearby OARS

Genetic conditions associated with

Genetic conditions associated with increased risk of second malignancy

  • Li fraumeni (TP53) = 30% develop second malignancy within 10 years
  • Hereditary retinoblastoma (RB1 gene)
  • Neurofibromatosis Type 1 (NF1) – optic pathway gliomas
  • Gorlin Syndrome (Nevoid basal carcinoma syndrome (PTCH1 gene)
  • Homologous recombination pathway defects (including BRCA2)

Mgmt

  • Use techniques which reduce low dose wash (3DCRT)

Trials & PROMs

PROM

PROM

  • improves toxicity detection, captures patient-centred outcomes, supports shared decision-making, detects under-reported symptoms, compares treatment tolerability.
  • Prostate: EPIC-26, EORTC QLQ-PR25, FACT-P. Other: FACT-G, MDADI, EORTC-QLQ-C30

QOL

  • Quality of life (QOL): Broad overall wellbeing. Includes physical, psychological, social, spiritual, financial and functional domains.
  • Health-related quality of life (HRQOL): The component of QOL specifically affected by health status, disease and treatment.
  • Quality-adjusted life year (QALY): A health economics measure combining quantity and quality of survival

Surrogate endpoints

  • Substitution outcome expected to predict a clinically meaningful endpoint
  • A: less cost, earlier access to Rx, shorter time to readout, smaller sample size, avoid crossover effect condounding OS, for PFS pseudoprogression can complicate interpretation
  • D: inaccurate, ineffective Rx, complicates data interpretation, reporting bias (PFS), misses longer term QoL/toxicity data, overestimate benefit

Bias (PRADS)

  • Performance bias: differences in care apart from the intervention, intervention heterogeneity (no central rv).
  • Reporting bias: selective publication/reporting of outcomes, incomplete records
  • Attrition bias: differential loss to follow-up.
  • Detection bias: outcome assessors influenced by treatment knowledge.
  • Selection bias: baseline differences due to enrolment/allocation.

RT planning

Adaptive replan uncertainties

Adaptive replan uncertainties

  • contour propagation and deformable registration uncertainty
  • anatomy change, total dose uncertainty
  • increased QA requirements
  • resource-intensive staffing and longer treatment times
  • complex online/offline workflow
  • limited image quality, especially with CBCT
  • intrafraction motion during adaptation

Plan QA

  • Ensure TPS uses validated algorithm for type of plan being delivered
  • Phantom with dosimeter (ion chamber), film dosimetry, diode array,
  • Anthromorphic phantom
  • dosimeter in vivo – TLD, OSLD, diode, ion chamber
  • day 0
  • intrafraction target monitoring
  • RO to attend day 1

SRS QA

  • Check isocentre – winston luttz
  • Check beam fluence (EPID) and MLC QA
  • PSQA
  • Diode array in spherical phantom → assess gamma analysis within tolerance
  • anatomical phantom
  • film based

Re-RT radiobiology

  • late responding tissue and late effect has low a/b (more fractionation sensitivity), tumour has high a/b (less fractionation size sensitive) → hyperfrac increases therapeutic ratio by reducing effect on late tissue without huge incr on late effect
  • Accel minimises repop whilst allowing repair of sublethal damage if >6hr
  • Accel hyperfrac can increase acute tox. Need to be cautious to not cause consequential late effect
  • Hyperfrac can permit total dose escalation

Re-irradiation factors

  • Patient: Change in anatomy
  • Tumour
  • Treatment: consider alternative – surg, focal therapy, brachytherapy, proton
  • RT specific: likelihood of control, proximity to critical OARS, estimated dose to OARS/remaining dose available, recovery of OARs (cord), technique for treatment (SBRT), anatomy change affecting dose recon, risk of severe toxicity

Extremity RT challenges

  • Clinical issues: Pain/skin reaction of weight bearing surface, lymphoedema with circumferential treatment, meatal/urethra stenosis for penis + need to circumcise before
  • Dosimetric issues: no specific setup equipment, shadow area underdosing, oblique, difficult to immobilise, less reproducible, hard to treat opposing surfaces, air gaps with bolus with irregular surface
  • RT sim: 3D printed custom bolus, wax block, water bath, direct e-1 field with couch kick if just treating dorsum/plantar, fryburg flap brachy
  • If organ swells then a custom wax mould is less forgiving / may need to be adjusted
  • Prone on platform with water bath secured underneath board
  • Take photos, wire intended field edge, mark water level on foot/ankle/arm, immobilise limb
