  • Build-up region: skin sparing increases with energy; it is lost with bolus, oblique incidence and immobilisation masks.
  • Lung: low density widens the penumbra and reduces build-up at the tumour edge; prefer 6 MV for small lung targets and use a type B (or Monte Carlo) algorithm.
  • Inverse square law: dose ∝ 1/distance². Moving from 100 to 110 cm reduces dose by about 17%.

Hand monitor unit check

  • Isocentric (SAD): MU = Dose ÷ (output × Sc × Sp × TMR × inverse-square factor × other factors, e.g. wedge or tray).
  • Fixed SSD: MU = Dose ÷ (output × Sc × Sp × PDD/100 × other factors). If SSD ≠ 100 cm, correct PDD with the Mayneord F factor.
  • Equivalent square of a rectangle ≈ 4 × area ÷ perimeter.
  • Independent MU checks for IMRT/VMAT use secondary dose-calculation software; a hand calculation is still useful for simple palliative fields.

Treatment techniques

  • 3D conformal: shaped static fields. Still fine for many palliative and whole-breast cases.
  • IMRT / VMAT: inverse-planned modulated fluence; more conformal, more low-dose spread and more MU. VMAT is faster.
  • SRS / SABR: very high dose per fraction, steep fall-off, small margins; requires high-precision immobilisation and image guidance. Prescription and reporting: ICRU 91.
  • Field-in-field: forward-planned segments to improve homogeneity (e.g. breast tangents).
  • Large-field: TBI (extended distance, lung blocks or compensators, low dose rate) and total skin electron therapy.

Electron beams

Rules of thumb (E in MeV)

  • Depth of 80% dose ≈ E/3 cm; 90% dose ≈ E/3.2 cm; practical range ≈ E/2 cm.
  • Surface dose rises with energy (≈75–80% at 6 MeV, ≈90% or more at higher energies): add bolus if the skin surface must receive full dose at low energy.
  • Lead thickness to stop the beam (mm) ≈ E/2 (add about 20% for Cerrobend). Internal shields (lip, eyelid) need a low-Z coating to absorb backscatter.
  • Isodose lines bulge out at depth for low isodoses and constrict for high isodoses: margins at depth are larger than they look on the surface. Allow ≈1 cm beyond the target at the surface for the 90% line, more for small fields.
  • Heterogeneities change range: air cavities and lung extend it, bone shortens it.
  • Avoid gaps and overlaps between abutting electron fields at the surface; feather the junction.

Common uses

  • Skin cancer, scar and chest-wall boosts, tumour-bed boost in breast, superficial nodes (posterior neck in older techniques), total skin electron therapy for mycosis fungoides.
  • Orthovoltage (kV) photons are an alternative for superficial skin lesions: no build-up (maximum at surface), but higher dose to bone (photoelectric).

Target volumes and dose reporting

VolumeMeaning
GTVVisible or palpable disease
CTVGTV plus subclinical spread, or an elective region at risk
ITVCTV plus internal motion and shape change (4DCT, multiple scans)
PTVGeometric margin for set-up and residual motion uncertainty; dose is reported to it
PRVOAR plus a margin, for serial organs near steep gradients (cord, optic chiasm)
TV / IVTreated volume (enclosed by the prescription isodose) and irradiated volume (significant dose)

PTV margin recipe

  • van Herk: PTV margin ≈ 2.5Σ + 0.7σ, where Σ is the systematic and σ the random error (SD). This gives at least 95% of the prescription to the CTV in 90% of patients.
  • Systematic errors matter about three times more than random ones: fix them first (simulation, delineation, set-up procedures).
  • Delineation variation is a systematic error that daily imaging cannot correct.

Dose reporting (ICRU 83 and 91)

  • IMRT: report D50% (median), D98% (near-minimum) and D2% (near-maximum) to the PTV, not a point dose.
  • Conventional aims: D95% ≥95% and D2% ≤107% of prescription.
  • SABR (ICRU 91): prescribe to an isodose covering the PTV (often 60–90% of the maximum); report Dnear-max as D2% or, for small volumes, D0.035cc; report conformity (e.g. Paddick CI) and gradient indices.
  • Homogeneity index = (D2% − D98%) / D50%.

Safety and implanted devices

Pacemakers and defibrillators (CIEDs) Verify

  • Follow the local CIED policy (based on AAPM TG-203). Risk is graded by cumulative device dose, beam energy and whether the patient is pacing-dependent.
  • Typical thresholds: device dose <2 Gy low risk; 2–5 Gy medium; >5 Gy or pacing-dependent high risk, needing cardiology input and monitoring.
  • Avoid beams >10 MV (neutrons cause resets). Never place the device in the primary beam if it can be avoided.
  • Check the device before the course and after it (and weekly for high risk), and have a defibrillator and staff trained available.

Other implants and hardware

  • Metal (hip prostheses, spinal hardware, dental work) causes CT artefact and dose perturbation: avoid beams through prostheses, override artefact densities, consider MVCT or dual-energy CT.
  • Breast expanders with a metallic port: check the port position and dose effect with physics.
  • Insulin pumps, neurostimulators and cochlear implants: remove or keep out of field where possible; follow manufacturer advice.

Bunker shielding (concepts)

  • Primary barriers face the beam; secondary barriers handle scatter and leakage.
  • Workload (W), use factor (U) and occupancy factor (T) set the barrier thickness, expressed in tenth-value layers.
  • Above about 10 MV the maze and door must also deal with neutrons (polyethylene, borated materials).
