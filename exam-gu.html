Bladder

Clinic page: Bladder

Epidemiology

I: Common, 4th most common in M, 8th in F

A: 60yo

G: M

R: Western countries

RF

E: smoking, ETOH, chronic UTI, stones, neurogenic bladder, previous alkylating chemotherapy (cyclophosphamide), working – aromatic amines, paints, hair dyes, prv pelvic RT, prv upper renal tract ca (40% risk)

I: Schistosoma parasite (endemic in Egypt) – causes SCC

G: nil

I: nil

Staging

Tis: carcinoma in situ (flat)

Ta: pre-invasive papillary neoplasm

T1: no muscle invasion

T2: muscle invasive

T3: perivesical soft tissue

T4: adjacent organ invasion

N1: single pelvic node

N2: multiple pelvic node

M1: DM

Outcomes

5 yr OS

  • Localised node negative: 55%
  • Node positive: 30%

Distant relapse: 20-30%

Node relapse 10-15%

  • Worse for variant histology

5 yr bladder preservation: 80%

F/u

  • q3monthly surveillance cystoscopy for 2 yrs, then q6mo for year 3-5 then annual
  • q6monthly CTCAP

Pre-invasive path

Tis: flat, carcinoma in situ

  • low, intermediate, high grade

Ta: papillary neoplasm

  • papillary urothelial neoplasm of low malignant potential
  • low grade papillary neoplasm
  • high grade papillary neoplasm

Invasive

Urothelial cell carcinoma: classified by flat or papillary.

  • Flat is always high grade
  • Subtypes: micropapillary, sarcomatoid, nested, plasmacytoid

Variant

  • SCC 3-5%
  • AdenoCa
  • Sarcoma: RMS
  • Small cell NEC – RT + cis/etop
  • Melanoma
  • Lymphoma

IHC: Uroplakin, GATA3, CK7, CK20, AE1/AE3

Note: core biopsy must sample detrusor muscle

  • Exception is cancer in diverticulum which lacks muscle wall

Multiple GU cancer theories

Monocloncal seeding

  • malignant clone travels through GU tract and implants into another site resulting in a secondary primary cancer

Field cancerization

  • Epigenetic changes due to exposure to a common carcinogen lead to accumulation to mutations, dysplasia and malignancy

Mgmt – pre-invasive & T1N0M0

1. Cystoscopy + maximal TURBT

  • Repeat if R1 as BCG only effective for eliminating microscopic disease

2. Intravesical MMC or BCG

  • commence by 2 mo post op to avoid macro regrowth
  • Low risk: Ta, low grade, unifocal, <3cm
  • Intermediate: not low or high risk
  • High risk: T1, G3, CIS, multifocal/diffuse, recurrent, large >3cm

Induction

  • ALL: weekly BCG for 6 weeks
  • Low risk can have MMC

Maintenance

  • Check cystoscopy
  • Low risk: TURBT +/-MMC
  • Intermediate risk: intravesical BCG at month 3, 6, 12
  • High risk: if non response → cystectomy. If good response then maintenance BCG q6mo for 3 yrs

50% or more LR by 10 years for high risk

  • Rechallenge BCG if >6mo
  • cystectomy if <6mo
  • or adj RT

BCG toxicity: systemic TB (if bladder injury to ongoing haematuria due to systemic seeding), irritative bladder sx, bladder stricture

Mgmt – T2NanyM0

1.Cystoscopy + maximal TURBT +/- HUN management for all

Cystectomy

NA chemotherapy if for cystectomy OR N+

  • ddMVAC: methotrexate, vinblastine, Adriamycin, cyclophosphamide
    • 5% OS benefit, may be up to 15% for higher risk
  • Cis/gem/Durvalumab + adj durva for 1 yr vs cis/gem had 8% 5yr OS benefit and 20% EFS – NIAGARA (on PBS now)
  • Enfortumab vedotin + pembro showing promising pCR rates ~60%

Cystectomy + PLND with at least 10 LN

  • Indications: young, fit, N-ve, non TCC histology
  • males: removal of prostate, SV, distal vas deferens, proximal urethra
  • females: removal of uterus, anterior vaginal wall, all urethra
  • all: need urinary diversion through neo-bladder using ileum or cutaneous stoma via ileal conduit
    • Neo-bladder is continent, ileal conduit/stoma can be continent or non-continent
  • G3 toxicity rate of 20%, G5 2% in one series (Hirobi et al), QoL is poor

Adjuvant nivolumab (CHECKMATE 274 – improved DFS)

  • if ypT2+ or pN+ or pT3+

Adjuvant radiotherapy: 50.4Gy/28# (BART trial – improved LRC and maybe OS without increased tox)

  • if SM+ve, pT3-4, N+, ≤10 nodes dissected
  • Pre-IO era, no data integrating IO and RT
  • CTV: cystectomy bed + pelvic nodes, spare stoma. Boost SM+ve

TMT

  • Equivalent survival outcomes - Zlotta et al. Better QoL.
  • Selective bladder preservation:
  • functional bladder
  • willing to attend surveillance cystoscopies
  • unifocal tumour without extensive CIS
  • non-variant histology
  • maximal TURBT completed
  • treated HUN
  • 55Gy/20# preferred (pooled analysis of BC2001 & BCON)
  • 64Gy/32# with concurrent MMC/5FU (BC2001 trial)
  • Concurrent weekly cisplatin is alternative (TROG 97.01 & 99.06 analysis)
  • Dose escalation to 70Gy and adaptive RT not shown to be superior (RAIDER)
  • Carbogen + nicotinamide if unfit for chemo (BCON)
  • if N-ve not role for ENI (Tunio et al)
  • if N+ then boost involved nodes to 60Gy and ENI 50Gy ENI EQD2
  • 20% need salvage cystectomy, more morbid than upfront surg

Palliative RT

  • 21Gy/3# 2nd daily non-inferior to 35Gy/10# for sx relief (MRC trial)
  • 70% haematuria relief at 3 mo

Zlotta et al

  • Retrospective review with matched cohort analysis showed TMT is comparable to cystectomy for 5 year CSS, DFS and beneficial for OS 66% vs 73%

BC2001

P: MIBC, unresectable

I: 64Gy/32# or 55Gy/20# +/- MMC/5FU

whole bladder RT vs modified bladder RT (80% dose to whole bladder, 100% to GTV)

No ENI, 30% had NA CT

O: CRT vs RT:

  • 5 yr OS 48% vs 35%
  • Invasive bladder recurrence 11% vs 19%
  • Nodal relapse 5% vs 7%
  • 5 yr LRC 60% vs 50%
  • Cystectomy 14% vs 22%
  • G3/4 tox: 8% vs 16%
  • Benefit persists at 10 yrs
  • Modified bladder RT non-inferior

BCON and BC2001 pooled analysis

  • 55Gy/20# had better LRC and isotoxic, hypothesised due to less repopulation time

Tunio et al

  • in N-ve ENI did not improve DFS, bladder preservation or OS

RAIDER

  • adaptive plan of the day RT is feasible but did not reduce toxicity
  • 74Gy vs 60Gy DE has numerical but non significant benefit in LRC

BCON

P: MIBC receiving radical RT

I: radical RT +/- carbogen and nicotinamide

O: 3 yrs OS 45% vs 50% however no sig difference at 10 yrs ~ 30%. Tumour necrosis predictive for greater benefit

BART (ph III RCT)

P: High risk MIBC – any of: T3-4, N+, SM+, ≤10 nodes dissected

I: Post-op pelvic RT 50.4Gy/28# vs no RT.

Chemo allowed in neoadj or adj setting. No IO.

O: 10% reduction in LRR at 2 yrs. 2 yr OS 70% vs 57%. No G3 tox.

CHECKMATE 274

  • Addition of adj nivo to cis/gem chemo improved mOS from 50 to 70 months
  • 10% DFS benefit, 6-8% OS benefit in interim analysis

NIAGRA

Neoadjuvant gem/cis/durva compared to gem/cis had 8% 5 yr OS benefit.

NB the VESPER/GETUG trial has previously compared ddMVAC to cis/gem and found ddMVAC to have superior OS but more tox

Figure in the original document (not reproduced here).

Kidney

Clinic page: Kidney

Epidemiology

I: 3% of all cancers, mortality reducing with targeted and immunotherapy

A: 60yo

G: M>F 2:1

R: Caucasian

RF

E: complex renal cyst, CKD, smoking, ETOH, obesity, dialysis, low fruit/veg diet

I:nil

G: VHL, polycystic kidney disease, hereditary papillary carcinoma (MET proto-oncogene), hereditary clear cell renal carcinoma (VHL gene), birt-hogg-dube

I: nil

Staging

T1: a <4cm, b 4-7cm

T2: a 7-10cm, b >10cm

T3: through perinephric fat or into renal vessels

T4: through gerota’s fascia, including adrenal

N1: N+

M1: DM

Role of bx

Pros

  • 10% of <4cm masses are benign, high positive predictive value

Cons

  • Majority are RCC, false negative, tumour seeding, can be non diagnostic, risk of pain, haematuria, pneumothorax

Pathogenesis

VHL loss → HIF accumulation → VEGF upregulation

Figure in the original document (not reproduced here).

Differential diagnosis

  • Benign: Prescence of fat is almost always an angiomyolipoma

Path

  • Clear cell carcinoma – 90%
  • chicken wire capillary
  • clear yellow cells
  • IHC: PAX8, PAX2, RCCma
  • Chromophobe carcinoma – 10%
    • CK7+, CD117+ve
  • Papillary carcinoma – 10%
    • Assoc with dialysis and hereditary syndrome
    • CK7+, AMACR+, PAX8+
  • Collecting duct
    • Aggressive
    • CK7, PAX8
  • Oncocytic carcinoma <5%
  • Sarcomatoid tumour <10%
    • Aggressive

WHO grade

I-IV: based on increasing nucleoli prominence appearance

  • Anaplasia, rhabdoid and sarcomatoid are grade IV

Risk of malignancy

  • F has 2-3x likelihood of benign renal mass

Solid mass

  • Malignant risk increases with size (<1cm = 40% benign), growth rate >5mm/year, no presence of fat

Cystic mass

  • Bosniak criteria

Radiological classification of a cystic renal cyst

  • Bosniak classification from I-IV
  • Internal septae, soft tissue component, wall thickness, calcifications

Natural history

  • 25% will have LN+ and 25% with have DM+ on dx
  • Lytic bone mets
  • Haemorrhagic brain mets

RCC can show avidity on PSMA PET. Some show FDG avidity

Follow-up

  • q3-6mo CTCAP and renal function – can have increase in size followed by gradual shrinkage over many months

IMDC / Heng criteria for metastatic RCC prognosis — 1 point each:

  • Karnofsky PS <80%
  • Time from diagnosis to systemic therapy <1 year
  • Hb < LLN
  • Corrected calcium > ULN
  • Neutrophils > ULN
  • Platelets > ULN

Risk groups

  • Favourable: 0 factors
  • Intermediate: 1–2 factors
  • Poor: ≥3 factors

Management

SOC

  • Total nephrectomy + LN sampling:
    • >7cm
    • if T3-4, node positive, WHO grade 4, then PA LND
  • Nephron sparing - partial nephrectomy
    • <7cm, within renal capsule, no vessel invasion
    • DTPA scan to assess differential function
    • small tumour, patient at risk of CKD or single kidney or bilateral disease
    • 10 yr CSS no diff with partial vs total nephrectomy
    • eGFR drop of 10% overall

Adjuvant pembrolizumab for 1 year

  • T2 WHO grade IV, T3-4, N+
  • 5% OS benefit at 4 yrs (KEYNOTE 564)
  • Addition of Belzutifan improves DFS (Litespark 022, abstract only)

No benefit to adjuvant RT

SABR (Fastrack, iROCK)

  • Not first line as no RCT data, operable candidates should still have surgery
  • Biopsy is preferred to confirm pathology for non-surgical options
    • If solid component available to bx
  • <4cm 26Gy/1# (T1a)
  • 4-10cm 42Gy/3#
  • Exclusions: ≥T2b, eGFR ≤ 30mL/min, abutting bowel,
  • Relative exclusions: single kidney, poor contralateral function, surgical or surveillance candidate

Active surveillance

  • Elderly pt, fit for treatment
  • Lesions <1cm have up to 40% chance of being benign, can pursue AS for up to 4cm
  • Growth rate <5mm/year
  • LE <5years
  • CT, MRI or US q3-6mo for 2 years, then q6-12mo

Alternatives

  • RFA, MWA, cryoablation: <3cm, non-central, away from vessels
  • >95% LC at 5 yrs, though low level evidence

Oligometastatic

  • If long progression free survival consider ablative local therapy (SABR) as per ESTRO/EAU consensus guidelines
  • Can prevent local progression, improve PFS, delay systemic therapy

Metastatic

  • TKI works faster, IO slower but possibly more durable
  • First line: doublet IO (ipi/nivo) or IO/TKI (pembro/axitinib or nivo/cabozatinib)
  • Less fit favourable IMDC - TKI alone – sunitinib, pazopanib
  • TKI tox: HTN, diarrhoea, fatigue, hand-foot syndrome, mucositis, hypothyroid, hepatotoxicity, proteinuria, bleeding/thrombosis.
  • Role for nephrectomy:
    • Avoid upfront cytoreductive nephrectomy unless symptomatic (CARMENA)
    • May be a role for delayed CN in responders to systemic therapy though trial was underpowered (SURTIME)

Fastrack II (Shankar, 2024)

P: single arm, ph II RCC, inoperable

I: SABR

O: LC 100% at 2yrs, LC 99% at 5 yrs, 3 yr OS 80%, 2 & 5 yr eGFR reduction 15 mL/min. 8% G3 toxicity. No G4.

iROCK pooled analysis (2022)

P: multi-institution pooled data

I: SABR with 1-5#

O: 5 yr LRC 95%, 7yr CSS 92%, larger size assoc with worse LRC and OS

CARMENA

upfront sunitinib alone was non-inferior and trended towards OS benefit vs upfront sunitinib + CN

SURTIME

Upfront CN vs delayed CN → delayed CN trended to better OS though trial underpowered

Fuse PET/MRI

  • FASTRACK used 4DCT and ITV as gating has higher risk of geographical miss

Staging

TNM staging is post surgical and incorporates tumour markers

S0: normal

S1: LDH 1.5x, b-HCG <5000, AFP <1000

S2: LDH 1.5-10x, b-HCG 50 000, AFP 10 000

S3: LDH >10x, b-HCG >50 000, AFP >10 000

½ lives: b-HCG: 7 days, AFP: 3 days, LDH 1 day.

Check tumour markers for serial decline accordingly

No stage IV for any GCT

Stage I: N-

Stage II: N+

Stage III: M+ or N+ & S2/3

Royal Marsden Hospital staging

(similar to Lugano)

Stage 1: testis alone

Stage 2: below diaphgram nodes

Stage 3: above diaphragm nodes

Stage 4: extranodal mets

Outcomes

10 yr OS

Stage I >99%

Stage II 95%

Stage III 90%+

Metastatic NSGCT: 70-90%

Tumour markers

  • Markers before orchidectomy for diagnosis/burden/prognosis.
  • Repeat post-op to assign S stage and detect occult metastatic disease.
  • Persistent elevation = stage IS.
  • Interpret according to half-life: AFP 5–7 d, β-hCG 1–3 d, LDH ~1 d – look for serial decline

Lymphatics

  • Testes arise retroperitoneally hence lymphatics drain to PA nodes
  • Right testicular vein drains to IVC, left to left renal vein
  • lymphatic drainage is to PA.
  • Scrotal/inguinal violation can alter drainage to inguinal nodes - so dog-leg/inguinal coverage may be considered

Ddx for testicular mass

Benign: hydrocele, varicocele, cyst, epididimytis, hernia, hematoma, torsion

Malignant: GCT, NGCT, lymphoma, sarcoma, melanoma

Work-up

  • FDG PET
  • CTCAP
  • Tumour markers

Mgmt SGCT

Inguinal orchidectomy with high ligation of spermatic cord

  • Scrotal incisions can disrupt normal lymphatics and ↑ risk of seeding
  • Recheck tumour markers post-op

Stage 1 – node negative

  • Surveillance as per ANZUP (below) preferred if reliable relapse pattern as excellent salvage options exist
  • ~15% recurrence risk in PA nodal strip (at level of renal vessels)
  • If high risk (>4cm or rete testis invasion) then:
  • Adjuvant carboplatin chemotherapy x1
    • preferred (MRC TE19 – chemo reduces out of field recurrence)
  • OR adjuvant PA radiotherapy 20Gy/10# from T12-L5 (MRC TE-18 – 20Gy non inferior to 30Gy PA)
    • If there has been disruption of normal lymphatic drainage (ie orchiopexy or scrotal incision) chemo preferred
  • RFS 95% after adj treatment, equiv for both

Stage IIA <5 nodes, <2cm

  • Adjuvant modified dog leg radiotherapy 30Gy/15# from top of T12 to top of acetabulum
    • improves 5 yr OS to 99% (Glaser et al)
  • OR BEP chemo x3
  • OR ISRT + carboplatin – 10 yr PFS 93% (SAK 01/10 – ph II data)

Stage IIB/C: >5 nodes or >2cm

  • Adjuvant BEP x3 (bleomycin, etoposide, cisplatin) or EP x4 (if concern for lung tox)
    • preferred as less toxicity than RT (Giannatempo MA)
  • OR adjuvant modified dog leg radiotherapy 30Gy/36Gy/18#
  • OR ISRT + carboplatin – 10 yr PFS 93% (SAK 01/10 – phase II data)

Stage III

  • Adjuvant BEP x3-4
  • 4 cycles if S3 or mets outside lung or mediastinal primary

RPLND reserved for salvage Rx in SGCT

Surveillance protocol as per ANZUP

  • Year 1-2: q6monthly PE, tumour markers, CTAP, testosterone
  • Year 3-5: annual PE, tumours, CTAP, testosterone

Mgmt NSGCT

Inguinal orchidectomy with high ligation of spermatic cord

Stage I

  • Surveillance OR
  • Adjuvant BEP x1 or RPLND in high volume centre if:
  • pT3-4 (invading stromal cord or scrotum)
  • LVSI
  • embryonal
  • elevated tumour markers post-op (only BEP chemo)

Stage IIA

  • Tumour marker negative → RPLND preferred at high volume centre
  • Tumour marker positive or multiple nodes → Adjuvant BEP x2 if upstaged

Stage IIB and III

  • Adjuvant BEP x3
  • RPLND if residual nodal mass >1cm

Surveillance protocol as per ANZUP

  • 6 months: monthly PE, tumour markers, 4 monthly CTAP and CXR
  • 6 mo – year 2: 3 monthly PE, tumour markers, 6 monthly CTAP and CXR
  • Year 3 -5: 6 monthly PE, tumour markers, annual CTCAP and CXR

Residual mass

NSGCT

    • >1cm residual mass after chemo: resect/RPLND due viable tumour/teratoma risk
    • <1cm with normalising tumour markers: observe with CT + tumour markers
    • FDG PET not reliable here

Seminoma

  • Bulky seminoma mass can take months to resolve and often replaced by fibrotic tissue
  • if residual mass <3 cm observe with CT and tumour markers
  • if >3 cm, FDG-PET at ≥8 weeks post-chemo;
    • PET+ve: repeat PET in 6 weeks, MDT discussion to consider resection/salvage cisplatin based chemo
    • PET−ve: observe (PET has high -ve predictive value in seminoma – SEMPET trial)
    • Perform serial tumour markers
