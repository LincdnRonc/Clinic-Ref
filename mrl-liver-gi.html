Liver lesions on MR-linac images

Most malignant liver lesions are mildly to moderately bright on T2 and dark on T1 relative to liver. A lesion as bright as the gallbladder or CSF is a cyst or haemangioma until proven otherwise.

T2 brightness ladder (darkest → brightest)

Fibrotic/treated scar < normal liver < solid met or HCC (mildly bright) < NET, mucinous or necrotic met < haemangioma ("light bulb", near fluid) < simple cyst = gallbladder = CSF.

When the lesion is hard to see on the day

  • Start from the reference. Register rigidly on the liver (not bone) and use the propagated GTV as your start, then check it against visible landmarks.
  • Vascular landmarks. Note on the reference which hepatic and portal branches touch the lesion; find the same branches daily.
  • Try the other sequence. A lesion invisible on T2 nav may be a dark hole on 3D Vane, and vice versa.
  • Fiducials show as small voids, blooming more on T1 GRE than T2.
  • Contrast is an option: some centres give half-dose gadolinium on the Unity for poorly visible upper abdominal tumours.

Liver SABR: finding your way around

Orient with the vessels, not the outline. On T2 nav the hepatic and portal veins are black and the bile ducts white, and together they define every Couinaud segment.

A five-stop scroll for every liver case

  1. Hepatic vein confluence (just below the diaphragm): right, middle and left hepatic veins drain into the IVC. Right HV splits anterior (5/8) from posterior (6/7) right sectors; middle HV splits right lobe from segment 4; left HV splits 4 from 2.
  2. Portal bifurcation: its horizontal plane splits superior segments (2, 4a, 7, 8) from inferior (3, 4b, 5, 6). Bright ducts run alongside the black portal branches.
  3. Fissures and fat: falciform ligament and ligamentum teres (bright fat) separate 4 from 2/3. The caudate (1) lies between the fissure for the ligamentum venosum and the IVC.
  4. Hilum: CBD (bright) anterolateral, portal vein (black) posterior, hepatic artery (small) anteromedial. The gallbladder fossa marks Cantlie's line.
  5. Down the CBD: follow it behind D1, through the pancreatic head, to the ampulla in D2. Use coronal.

Central lesions

  • Many groups contour a central hepatobiliary tract (cHBT) as a 15 mm expansion of the portal vein from the splenic confluence to the first bifurcation (Osmundson, IJROBP 2015; Toesca, Radiother Oncol 2017). Constraints are on the Liver and biliary page.
  • On MR you can see the ducts themselves: check the central ducts sit where you expect.

Other liver tips

  • Liver − GTV: edit only where the edge has moved near the PTV.
  • Biliary stents and pneumobilia are both black; non-dependent duct gas after stenting is pneumobilia, not tumour.
  • Cirrhosis: nodular, heterogeneous liver; regenerative nodules can be T1-bright. Lean on the reference GTV and fused diagnostic MRI.
  • Iron overload makes liver and spleen very dark on both sequences, so lesions look relatively bright.

Upper GI OARs: telling bowel from bowel

Trace every hollow organ as a continuous tube from a fixed landmark. Never contour a loop on one slice without knowing which segment it joins on the next.

Duodenum D1–D4

  1. D1 (bulb): from the pylorus (thick dark muscular ring), right and posterior towards the gallbladder neck; often gas-filled.
  2. D2: vertical, lateral to the pancreatic head, medial to right kidney/liver; ampulla on the medial wall. Best coronal.
  3. D3: crosses left between SMA/SMV (anterior) and aorta/IVC (posterior). The most reliable landmark.
  4. D4: ascends left of the aorta to the DJ flexure (about L2), then jejunum.

The duodenum is retroperitoneal and fairly fixed; jejunum is not. A loop between the SMA and aorta is D3.

Stomach

  • Follow GOJ → fundus → body → antrum → pylorus → bulb.
  • Rugal folds and a non-dependent fundal gas bubble identify it. Contents vary daily: fluid bright, food mottled, gas black.
  • Contour the outer wall including contents.
  • A thin bright fat plane (lesser sac) separates posterior stomach from pancreas; loss of it hints at tumour.

Pancreas

  • Body and tail lie directly anterior to the splenic vein (black on T2), ending at the splenic hilum.
  • Head sits in the duodenal C-loop right of the SMV; uncinate tucks behind the SMV.
  • On 3D Vane the pancreas is relatively bright and the fluid-filled duodenum dark, so the head–duodenum boundary is often clearer there.
  • Tumour is usually darker than normal pancreas on T1 and subtle on T2. Look for abrupt cut-off of the bright duct with upstream dilatation, or a double duct sign.

Kidneys, spleen, spinal canal

  • Kidneys: easy on both sequences; include the collecting system if your protocol does.
  • Spleen: brighter than liver on T2, darker on T1.
  • Spinal canal: bright CSF on T2 makes it easy; contour canal or cord per protocol.
