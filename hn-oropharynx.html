Oropharynx: who needs what (ASTRO 2024, HPV+)

Most HPV+ OPC is cured with either TORS ± adjuvant RT or definitive (chemo)RT. Choose so the patient gets one modality where possible. Cisplatin remains standard; cetuximab is inferior. De-escalation outside trials is not yet standard.

Volumes, level selection and PATHOS post-TORS groups: Head and neck contouring guide, sections 3–5

Definitive RT

  • RT alone: T1–2 N0, or T1 with a single node ≤3 cm. T2 with a single node ≤3 cm: RT alone or chemoRT.
  • ChemoRT: T3–4, ≥2 nodes, or a single node >3 cm. Cisplatin 100 mg/m² q3w or 40 mg/m² weekly. If not fit for cisplatin: cetuximab, carboplatin/5-FU or a taxane.
  • Dose: 70 Gy in 33–35 fractions with chemo. RT alone: 66–70 Gy accelerated or hypofractionated, or 68–70 Gy conventional. Elective nodes EQD2 ≥46 Gy.
  • No induction chemotherapy and no immunotherapy outside trials.

After TORS

  • ChemoRT: pT3–4 or pN+ with a positive margin or ENE.
  • PORT: pN+ with pT3–4, ≥2 nodes or a single node >3 cm. pT3–4 N0. Conditionally for PNI/LVSI or close margins.
  • Observe: pT1–2 with a single node ≤3 cm and no other risk factors.
  • Dose: 60–66 Gy to positive margin or ENE; 56–60 Gy to the bed and involved nodal levels; EQD2 ≥50 Gy to uninvolved levels in a node-positive neck.

Volumes

  • GTV to CTV_high ≤5 mm. CTV to PTV 3–5 mm.
  • Unilateral RT: T1–2 tonsil confined to the fossa with N0 or a single node ≤3 cm (ASTRO). MDACC series: 6 y contralateral neck failure 2%.
  • May omit: contralateral high level II, retrostyloid and retropharyngeal nodes if the contralateral neck is node-negative and there is no extensive soft palate, posterior pharyngeal wall or nasopharyngeal involvement. Level IB if neck-negative and no oral cavity involvement. Level V if neck-negative and no nasopharynx or hypopharynx involvement.

Key oropharynx trials

Cetuximab failed as de-escalation. Dose reduction, TORS-based and adaptive approaches are promising but not yet standard of care.

Cisplatin vs cetuximab

RTOG 1016 (2019)HPV+ · 70 Gy over 6 weeks · cetuximab vs cisplatinCisplatin better: 5 y OS 78 → 85%, PFS 67 → 78%, LRF 17 → 10%. Acute G3–4 about 80% and late G3–4 about 20% in both arms; cisplatin had more G3–4 events per patient (2.35 → 3.19).
De-ESCALaTE (2019)low-risk HPV+ · cetuximab vs cisplatin2 y OS 89 → 98% and recurrence 16 → 6% favouring cisplatin. No toxicity advantage for cetuximab.
TROG 12.01 ANZ (2021)low-risk HPV+ · 70/35 with weekly cisplatin vs cetuximabNo significant difference in G3+ events (p=0.11). 3 y failure-free survival 80% (cetuximab) vs 93% (cisplatin).

De-escalation and TORS

NRG-HN002 (2021)Phase II · p16+ ≤10 pack-years · 60 Gy + weekly cisplatin vs 60 Gy accelerated aloneOnly the chemoRT arm met the target (2 y PFS 91%). 2 y LRF 10 → 3% with cisplatin.
ECOG 3311 (2022)495 · TORS · intermediate risk randomised to 50 vs 60 Gy PORT4.5 y PFS 95% (50 Gy) vs 90% (60 Gy); 4.5 y OS 98 vs 95%. Supports 50 Gy for intermediate risk.
ORATOR (2019; long-term 2022)Phase II · T1–2 N0–2 · TORS ± adjuvant vs definitive (chemo)RT1 y MDADI swallowing score 80 (TORS) vs 87 (RT); by 5 y the scores converged (84.6 vs 85.3).
MC1273 (2019)Phase II · after TORS · 30–36 Gy BID with docetaxel2 y LRC 96%, PFS 91%, OS 99%. Very low G3+ toxicity.

Commentary
2-yr PFS of 98% in the control arm is outstanding. HN-005 is a cautionary tale of adopting phase II data too quickly. Phase II and prospective data may be reasonable to apply in disease states with poor prognosis in which one is willing to take risks, but one should be more cautious in applying experimental therapy in favorable prognosis. In HPV+ H&N, where LRC is 85-90%, such an approach is probably not appropriate. Some had a goal of reducing adverse effects. HN-005 finally resolved the question.

An interesting feature of RTOG 1016 and HN-005 is that the control used the DAHANCA regimen with concurrent chemotherapy. The original DAHANCA 6&7 and IAEA-ACC trials did not use chemotherapy concurrent with the 6 days per week regimen.

Commentary
ENTs have concerns regarding the surgical quality, and rad oncs should have concerns that the RT dose is too low. The trial closed early due to excess rates of G5 post-op hemorrhage. There are concerns regarding surgical quality: 4% mortality in this study (n=2/35). Compare to other studies: In ECOG 3311, there were 1/495 deaths (0.2%) with TORS, and in MC1675, no grade 5 toxicites with n=194. In ECOG there were no further deaths once the protocol was amended to strongly recommend vessel ligation, which was not specified in ORATOR2. Palma comments on Twitter (2022) however that ECOG 3311 outcomes are not representative of US outcomes. In an editorial Teckie notes that if there was just 1 fewer surgical death, ORATOR2 would have still been ongoing.

The trial should have selected 70 Gy for the radiation arm. 60 Gy was chosen prematurely based on phase II findings. After the trial's design, 60 Gy was compared to 70 Gy for HPV+ in NRG-005 and was determined to be not noninferior.

This study has higher rates of positive margins compared to E3311, 8% vs. 3.8%. Mandated prophylactic tracheostomy in the trial is also rarely used in contemporary transoral surgery. Given the variability in surgical quality it may be difficult how to best interpret results. For transoral surgery for HPV OPSCC, one can say that, if surgery is to be performed, careful patient selection and experienced surgeons at high volume centers are strongly preferred; at least while awaiting the results of EORTC 1420 and EORTC 1701-HNCG (PATHOS).

Commentary
Surgery. ENTs have concerns regarding the surgical quality. There were no longer any deaths after the protocol was amended to strongly recommend vessel ligation. Palma comments on Twitter (2022) however that ECOG 3311 outcomes are not representative of US outcomes. ENE was the most common reason for allocation to high-risk arm (44%), which typically only can be determined following pathologic assessment, requiring staged surgeries (i.e. upfront neck dissection) to eventually indicate chemoradiotherapy.

Radiation. There is concern regarding high-quality and protocol-compliant radiotherapy delivery. Many patients received less than protocol-intended radiotherapy dose to the primary site (int 50 Gy in only 28%, 60 Gy in only 47%), with some patients not receiving RT to the primary site at all [int 50 Gy (26%), 60 Gy (19%)], which may complicate interpretation of study outcomes. Granted, the AVOID Phase II trial has suggested that RT is not of benefit to a post-operative bed without risk factors (Swisher-McClure 2019).

There was also significant dropoff rate in completion of MDADI and FACT-HN beyond the early post-treatment period which may limit drawing firm conclusions in late QOL.

Eligibility. Why were those with ≥5 resected nodes included in the high risk arm? Data to support this practice is sparse. Some data shows a high risk of DM in this population (Lu 2022, Zhan 2017). However the best evidence, the pooled analysis of EORTC 22931, RTOG 9501, and RTOG 0234 shows no DM benefit with chemotherapy in increasing nodal count, p=0.8 (Lu 2022). The ECOG authors do not share how many had ≥5 nodes as a single risk factor without ECE or positive margin. 27% had ≥5 nodes, 77% had ENE, and 11% had +M. A randomized trial could be warranted.

Notes
Oropharynx summary
-HPV was discovered as a prognostic factor for response to treatment and OS, validated in RTOG 0129
-Studies sought to de-escalate therapy in HPV+ tumors by various means, but most of these failed under multi institutional randomized trials
-RTOG 1016 and De-Escalate found that chemo cannot be replaced by cetuximab
-A surgical technique, TORS, is developed that leads to less morbidity than standard surgery
-In HN005, 60 Gy was found inferior and the arm was closed
-ECOG 3311 validates TORS and post-op approaches as options in low, int, and high risk HPV
-ORATOR2 compared chemoRT vs surgery in HPV+ and was closed due to deaths in the surgery arm, but some question the surgical quality in the trial
-EORTC "Best of" to evaluate TORS vs. chemoRT in HPV+

Epidemiology

A: 50-60

G: M>F

Staging

  • T1: ≤2cm
  • T2: 2-4cm
  • T3: ≥ 4cm
  • T4: invading oesophagus, bone, larynx

HPV +ve

  • cN1: ipsilateral nodes <6cm
  • cN2: contralateral nodes
  • cN3: any >6cm
  • pN1: <4 nodes
  • pN2: >4 nodes
  • M1 = stage IV

HPV -ve

  • cN2a: single, ipsilateral, 3-6cm
  • cN2b: multiple, ipsilateral, <6cm

  • cN3: ENE or >6cm

  • M1: distant nodes/mets

T4 or N2 or M1 = stage IV

F/u

Outcomes

  • HPV +ve: 5 yr OS 80-90%
  • HPV -ve 5 yr OS 50%
  • HPV+ve generally does better even in metastatic disease and has late distant relapse

Subsites

Path

Malignant: SCC, salivary gland carcinoma, EM plasmacytoma, melanoma, lymphoma

Pre-invasive

  • Erythroplakia: red plaque. 30-50% invasive risk, more likely to be CIS
  • Leukoplakia: white plaque. 5-10% invasive risk can be hyperplasia, dysplasia, less likely CIS

Invasive

  • SCC – non-keratinising, basaloid (both are HPV+), keratinising (HPV-)
  • Grade 1-3 only applies to HPV-ve SCC
  • IHC: p16+ve (>70% block nuclear/cytoplasmic stain), CK5/6, p40, p63
  • Other: HPV ISH/PCR +ve, EBER ISH -ve

HPV carcinogenesis

  • HPV 16 & 18 infection of basal epithelial cells (via microabrasions)
  • Viral genome integration into host DNA → loss of E2 regulation
  • E6 and E7 oncoprotein overexpression:
    • E6 → p53 degradation → impaired apoptosis, genomic instability
    • E7 → Rb inactivation → release of E2F → G1/S progression → uncontrolled cell cycle progression
  • Result: cell cycle dysregulation + accumulation of mutations → malignant transformation
  • Latency of 20+ years

EGFR overexpression

  • Common in H&N SCC – 85%, more common in HPV-
  • Upregulation of downstream pathways (MAPK, PI3K, JAK/STAT) increases cell growth, survival, angiogenesis
  • Cetuximab is EGFR targeted therapy but assoc with worse OS compared to cisplatin
  • Does not predict response to IO
  • Worse prognosis

Management

  • Note: Avoid trimodality Rx where possible to minimise toxicity

Well lateralised tonsil with N0-1

  • <1/3 of lateral hemi-oropharynx, <1cm into BoT or soft palate, no deep or muscle invasion
  • Definitive RT +/- cisplatin with unilateral neck RT: 70Gy/35# or 55Gy/20#
  • TORS with unilateral LND

Early stage operable (T1-2N0-1)

  • Should have lateralised tumour with good exposure
  • TORS + bilateral LND – ORATOR
  • Adj RT indicated if: T3/T4, LVI, PNI, SM <2mm, ENE, large node ≥3cm, ≥2 nodes
  • Adj cisplatin if: ENE, SM+ - improves OS/PFS (pooled analysis of EORTC 22931 and RTOG 9501)

Inoperable (T2Nany)

  • Definitive chemoradiotherapy with cisplatin (MACH-NC MA)
  • If unfit for cisplatin consider cetuximab (Bonner trial) though OS inferior (RTOG 1016), or carbo/5FU (French GORTEC trial)
  • Alternate fractionation: 6# per week (DAHANCA), accelerated hyperfractionation (MARCH MA, RTOG 9003)
    • Hyperfractionation improves OS by 8% at the expense of acute mucositis, acceleration improves LRC
    • Hypofractionation is non-inferior for LRC and similar for tox: 55Gy/20# (HYPNO)

Metastatic

  • Chemoimmunotherapy: cisplatin/5FU/pembro (10% OS benefit vs chemo + cetuximab)
  • IO monotherapy – ORR 20% alone vs 40% with chemo, however still beneficial. Ideally CPS >20%
  • Palliative RT

Re-irradiation

  • 60Gy/30-40# OD-BD

De-escalation of HPV+

  • No ph III evidence supports this
  • Ph III RTOG1016/De-escalate trial & TROG 12.01 – worse OS with 70Gy RT + cetuximab vs cisplatin - 5 yr OS 78% vs 85%
  • Ph II/III NRG HN005 – pts with HPV <10pyhx 60Gy + cisplatin or nivo was inferior to 70Gy with cisplatin for 2 year PFS
  • Ph II ECOG E3311 with risk adapted adjuvant RT (obs, 50-60Gy, 66Gy) with cisplatin showed acceptable 2 yr PFS and OS for 50Gy vs 60Gy in intermediate risk group (T1-2, N1-2, SM-) though not powered for efficacy comparison between arms
  • Other pending studies: PATHOS (ph III of ECOG E3311) and F-MISO PET to de-escalate hypoxia-negative disease

ORATOR, ph II

P: T1-2, N0-2, OP SCC

I: TORS + neck dissection +/- adj Rx vs 70Gy +/- cisplatin

O: Used MDADI scores which was worse for tonsil. TORS: worse social eating, swallow and trismus. RT: worse tinnitus, hearing loss, xerostomia. PFS & OS no diff. Trimodality toxicity worst. Bimodality toxicity (sug + RT or CRT) are equivalent

ORATOR II: TORS vs de-escalated CRT 60Gy – 2 deaths in surgical arm, closed early

Pooled analysis of EORTC 22931 and RTOG 9501

  • in adj setting if SM+ or ENE then cisplatin adds 6% 5 yr PFS benefit

Bernier et al

  • Post-operative concurrent 3 weekly cisplatin chemotherapy + RT improved OS by ~7% at 5 yrs as well as PFS. 20% more G3 toxicity.

MACH-NC MA

  • Concurrent cisplatin adds 6.5% 5 yr OS benefit
  • Platinum based chemo has best OS benefit
  • Concurrent > induction chemo
  • Benefit reduces with age, no benefit >70yo

MARCH-MA

  • altered fractionation vs conv RT alone improves 5 yr OS by 3%
  • hyperfractionation specifically adds 8% 5 yr OS benefit vs conv RT alone
  • accelerated RT has smaller benefit, mainly LRC benefit
  • chemoRT has better 6-5% 5-10 yr OS benefit vs hyperfrac RT alone
  • Altered fractionation increases acute tox – mucositis and feeding tube need during RT

DAHANCA 6&7

  • 6# per week improved 5 yr LRC by 10% and DFS. Worse acute mucositis

Bonner trial

  • Improved median OS with RT + cetuximab vs RT alone – 50 vs 30 months

HYPNO

P: locally adv H&N SCC

I: 55Gy/20# 5fx/week vs 66Gy/33# 6fx/week. Cisplatin allowed.

  • NB control arm deemed biologically equiv to 70Gy/35#

O: 55Gy/20# non-inferior at 3 yrs for LRC and G3 tox

Curative RT

Dose: 70Gy/[63Gy]/56Gy/35# 10# per fortnight with BD compensation with:

  • concurrent cisplatin 100mg/m2 3 weekly OR 40mg/m2 weekly if less toxicity desired (ototoxicity, renal impairment, neuropathy)
  • cetuximab OR carbo/taxol (NCCN category 2b)

Pre-sim: dietician, speech pathologist, dental review, prophylactic PEG/RIG insertion if treating bilateral neck, bloods, audiometry, smoking cessation

Sim

  • Supine, arms down, neck extended, TPS over head and neck
  • 1cm bolus for involved nodes
  • CT with IV contrast
  • Fuse PET and MRI

Xerostomia reduction/mgmt

  • Amifostine use has low level evidence and not supported by guidelines
  • Submandibular or parotid sparing on contralateral side
  • Saliva substitutes/sialogoges
  • Dental care
  • Smoking cessation

Dysphagia reduction/mgmt

  • Pharyngeal constrictor sparing
  • SP led swallow exercises
  • Use it or lose it during RT
