Kidney (AJCC 8)

Kidney (RCC)

SABR is a curative non-surgical option for primary RCC in inoperable patients, and a way to delay or protect systemic therapy in oligometastatic disease.

SABR for primary RCC FASTRACK II

  • Dose: 26/1 for ≤4 cm; 42/3 for >4 cm (FASTRACK II, TROG 15.03/ANZUP). Other series: 36/3, 40/5; range 26/1 to 30–45 Gy in 3–5.
  • Eligibility (FASTRACK II): biopsy-proven, ≤10 cm, ECOG ≤2; excluded eGFR <30, bowel contact, horseshoe kidney, prior systemic therapy.
  • Results: LC 100% at 36, 60 and 84 mo; CSS 100%; G3 10%, no G4–5.
  • Renal function: mean eGFR fall ~11 at 1 y, ~15 at 2 y, then plateau. One patient needed dialysis (baseline eGFR 34, central 59 mm tumour). Counsel on this for large/central tumours.
  • Single fraction is safe in a solitary kidney (IROCK). Cost-effective vs RFA/cryo in Australian analysis.
  • Coverage goal D99% ≥Rx (D95% acceptable for OARs); peak 125–143%.

Oligometastatic and oligoprogressive RCC

  • SABR in lieu of systemic therapy (MDACC Tang, ≤5 mets, serial SBRT): most stayed off systemic therapy; G3+ 0–10% vs ~50% ipi/nivo, 64% sunitinib.
  • Oligoprogression: SABR to 1–2 sites lets patients stay on current systemic therapy (mostly favourable/intermediate IMDC, clear cell).
  • Dose: aim BED10 ≥59.5 Gy; single fraction ≥20/1, preferably ≥24/1 (local PFS 88% vs 21% below 24 Gy). 25/5 had 18% 1-y local failure.
  • Brain mets: SRS LC ≥90%.
  • CYTOSHRINK: SBRT to primary during ipi/nivo did not improve PFS. NRG-GU012/SAMURAI ongoing.

Holding systemic therapy around RT

  • Bevacizumab (t½ ~20 d): suspend ~4 wk before and after.
  • Sunitinib / sorafenib: pause 5–10 d before and after.
  • Ipilimumab: pause 2 d either side. PD-1/PD-L1 agents: limited data, generally safe with conventional RT (watch pneumonitis).

Systemic therapy and surgery

  • Stratify by IMDC: favourable 0, intermediate 1–2, poor ≥3 factors.
  • First line: IO combinations (nivo/ipi for intermediate/poor; IO + TKI e.g. pembro/axitinib, cabo/nivo across risk groups).
  • After PD-1: belzutifan + lenvatinib beat cabozantinib for PFS (LITESPARK-011).
  • Adjuvant pembrolizumab after nephrectomy (KEYNOTE-564, OS benefit); LITESPARK-022 adds belzutifan for modest DFS gain.
  • Cytoreductive nephrectomy: not upfront for intermediate/poor risk (CARMENA). Consider with only 1 IMDC factor, oligometastatic disease, or after response. Small renal masses: biopsy if it changes management; partial nephrectomy standard; AS if comorbid.
  • Recurrence after surgery for ≥pT3a: lung 53%, liver 20%, bone 18%; >10 cm and G4 favour bone and brain.

Kidney

From the exam summary.

Epidemiology

I: 3% of all cancers, mortality reducing with targeted and immunotherapy

A: 60yo

G: M>F 2:1

R: Caucasian

RF

E: complex renal cyst, CKD, smoking, ETOH, obesity, dialysis, low fruit/veg diet

I:nil

G: VHL, polycystic kidney disease, hereditary papillary carcinoma (MET proto-oncogene), hereditary clear cell renal carcinoma (VHL gene), birt-hogg-dube

I: nil

Staging

T1: a <4cm, b 4-7cm

T2: a 7-10cm, b >10cm

T3: through perinephric fat or into renal vessels

T4: through gerota’s fascia, including adrenal

N1: N+

M1: DM

Role of bx

Pros

  • 10% of <4cm masses are benign, high positive predictive value

Cons

  • Majority are RCC, false negative, tumour seeding, can be non diagnostic, risk of pain, haematuria, pneumothorax

Pathogenesis

VHL loss → HIF accumulation → VEGF upregulation

Figure in the original document (not reproduced here).

Differential diagnosis

  • Benign: Prescence of fat is almost always an angiomyolipoma

Path

  • Clear cell carcinoma – 90%
  • chicken wire capillary
  • clear yellow cells
  • IHC: PAX8, PAX2, RCCma
  • Chromophobe carcinoma – 10%
    • CK7+, CD117+ve
  • Papillary carcinoma – 10%
    • Assoc with dialysis and hereditary syndrome
    • CK7+, AMACR+, PAX8+
  • Collecting duct
    • Aggressive
    • CK7, PAX8
  • Oncocytic carcinoma <5%
  • Sarcomatoid tumour <10%
    • Aggressive

WHO grade

I-IV: based on increasing nucleoli prominence appearance

  • Anaplasia, rhabdoid and sarcomatoid are grade IV

Risk of malignancy

  • F has 2-3x likelihood of benign renal mass

Solid mass

  • Malignant risk increases with size (<1cm = 40% benign), growth rate >5mm/year, no presence of fat

Cystic mass

  • Bosniak criteria

Radiological classification of a cystic renal cyst

  • Bosniak classification from I-IV
  • Internal septae, soft tissue component, wall thickness, calcifications

Natural history

  • 25% will have LN+ and 25% with have DM+ on dx
  • Lytic bone mets
  • Haemorrhagic brain mets

RCC can show avidity on PSMA PET. Some show FDG avidity

Follow-up

  • q3-6mo CTCAP and renal function – can have increase in size followed by gradual shrinkage over many months

IMDC / Heng criteria for metastatic RCC prognosis — 1 point each:

  • Karnofsky PS <80%
  • Time from diagnosis to systemic therapy <1 year
  • Hb < LLN
  • Corrected calcium > ULN
  • Neutrophils > ULN
  • Platelets > ULN

Risk groups

  • Favourable: 0 factors
  • Intermediate: 1–2 factors
  • Poor: ≥3 factors

Management

SOC

  • Total nephrectomy + LN sampling:
    • >7cm
    • if T3-4, node positive, WHO grade 4, then PA LND
  • Nephron sparing - partial nephrectomy
    • <7cm, within renal capsule, no vessel invasion
    • DTPA scan to assess differential function
    • small tumour, patient at risk of CKD or single kidney or bilateral disease
    • 10 yr CSS no diff with partial vs total nephrectomy
    • eGFR drop of 10% overall

Adjuvant pembrolizumab for 1 year

  • T2 WHO grade IV, T3-4, N+
  • 5% OS benefit at 4 yrs (KEYNOTE 564)
  • Addition of Belzutifan improves DFS (Litespark 022, abstract only)

No benefit to adjuvant RT

SABR (Fastrack, iROCK)

  • Not first line as no RCT data, operable candidates should still have surgery
  • Biopsy is preferred to confirm pathology for non-surgical options
    • If solid component available to bx
  • <4cm 26Gy/1# (T1a)
  • 4-10cm 42Gy/3#
  • Exclusions: ≥T2b, eGFR ≤ 30mL/min, abutting bowel,
  • Relative exclusions: single kidney, poor contralateral function, surgical or surveillance candidate

Active surveillance

  • Elderly pt, fit for treatment
  • Lesions <1cm have up to 40% chance of being benign, can pursue AS for up to 4cm
  • Growth rate <5mm/year
  • LE <5years
  • CT, MRI or US q3-6mo for 2 years, then q6-12mo

Alternatives

  • RFA, MWA, cryoablation: <3cm, non-central, away from vessels
  • >95% LC at 5 yrs, though low level evidence

Oligometastatic

  • If long progression free survival consider ablative local therapy (SABR) as per ESTRO/EAU consensus guidelines
  • Can prevent local progression, improve PFS, delay systemic therapy

Metastatic

  • TKI works faster, IO slower but possibly more durable
  • First line: doublet IO (ipi/nivo) or IO/TKI (pembro/axitinib or nivo/cabozatinib)
  • Less fit favourable IMDC - TKI alone – sunitinib, pazopanib
  • TKI tox: HTN, diarrhoea, fatigue, hand-foot syndrome, mucositis, hypothyroid, hepatotoxicity, proteinuria, bleeding/thrombosis.
  • Role for nephrectomy:
    • Avoid upfront cytoreductive nephrectomy unless symptomatic (CARMENA)
    • May be a role for delayed CN in responders to systemic therapy though trial was underpowered (SURTIME)

Fastrack II (Shankar, 2024)

P: single arm, ph II RCC, inoperable

I: SABR

O: LC 100% at 2yrs, LC 99% at 5 yrs, 3 yr OS 80%, 2 & 5 yr eGFR reduction 15 mL/min. 8% G3 toxicity. No G4.

iROCK pooled analysis (2022)

P: multi-institution pooled data

I: SABR with 1-5#

O: 5 yr LRC 95%, 7yr CSS 92%, larger size assoc with worse LRC and OS

CARMENA

upfront sunitinib alone was non-inferior and trended towards OS benefit vs upfront sunitinib + CN

SURTIME

Upfront CN vs delayed CN → delayed CN trended to better OS though trial underpowered
