Anal canal (AGITG atlas, Ng 2012; RTOG, Myerson 2009)

The AGITG atlas is the Australasian standard and the most practical: it splits the elective volume into three regions (A, B, C) so each can be checked separately. For rectal cancer use the rectal long-course atlas.

Gross and high-dose volumes

VolumeDefinition
GTVAPrimary: examination (draw the clinical diagram), MRI T2 and PET. Note the anal verge, dentate line and anorectal junction on MRI.
CTV primaryGTVA + a margin (AGITG uses about 2 cm), always including the whole anal canal and internal sphincter, edited off bone and air.
GTVN / CTV nodesInvolved nodes (PET, MRI) + about 1 cm, edited.

Elective regions

RegionContentsPoints
CTVAMesorectum, presacral space, internal iliac nodes, ischiorectal fossaeSuperior: rectosigmoid junction or about 2 cm above the most cranial disease. Vessel-based margin (about 7 mm) for internal iliac; whole mesorectum to its fascia.
CTVBExternal iliac nodesVessels + about 7 mm, more anteriorly; superior at the iliac bifurcation (or higher with involved pelvic nodes).
CTVCInguinal nodesAround the femoral vessels with a wider margin (about 2 cm); lateral at sartorius / iliopsoas; inferior about 2 cm below the saphenofemoral junction, or lower for involved nodes.

Margins here are from memory of the AGITG paper: check Ng 2012 Tables 1–3 before using them clinically. The Nordic NOAC volumes (Nilsson 2023) are smaller.

Common pitfalls

Most anal recurrences are in-field, but marginal misses happen at the inguinal and presacral edges.

Check before you finish

  • Inguinal CTV cut too tightly to the vessels laterally: the nodes lie along the femoral sheath and in the fat lateral to it.
  • Ischiorectal fossa omitted or incomplete for low or T3–4 tumours.
  • Bladder and bowel filling at simulation not reproduced at treatment (bladder protocol).
  • Female patients: vagina and vulva as OARs (sparing), but include them when the tumour invades.
  • Genitalia, femoral heads, bladder, bowel bag (Gay 2012 atlas) contoured for every case.
