Pancreas: overview and resectability

Systemic therapy drives survival. RT's role is local control, margin clearance in borderline disease, and pain. Only about half of "resectable" cases end up R0.

Resectability (NCCN)

CategoryArterialVenous
ResectableFat plane around coeliac axis, SMA and CHANo SMV/PV contact, or ≤180° without vein irregularity
BorderlineSMA ≤180° (head) or CA ≤180° (body/tail); CA >180° with uninvolved GDA (modified Appleby); CHA contact reconstructableSMV/PV >180°, distortion or occlusion but reconstructable; IVC contact
UnresectableSMA or CA >180° (unless Appleby); aortic invasionUnreconstructable SMV/PV; contact with first jejunal branch

Distant metastases, including non-regional nodes, = unresectable.

Work-up

  • Pancreas-protocol multiphase CT (arterial 25–35 s, portal venous 65–70 s) or MRI. EUS ± FNA. CA 19-9 after biliary decompression.
  • Staging laparoscopy is institutional; higher yield in body/tail tumours (more occult mets).
  • Germline testing for all (BRCA, PALB2: platinum and PARP inhibitor relevance); MMR/MSI (pembrolizumab if dMMR).
  • Place biliary stents before simulation where possible.
  • Imaging underestimates size by ~0.5 cm; occult extrapancreatic extension in 60–90% of "resectable" tumours.

SBRT or chemoRT (stable locally advanced)

No randomised head-to-head. Choose on anatomy, logistics and aim.

FactorFavours SBRTFavours chemoRT
Bowel and stomachClear gap, or MR guidance with daily adaptationTumour abuts or invades mucosa (SBRT under-doses the interface)
TargetCompact primaryLarge tumour, elective nodes or long perivascular tracks
Systemic therapyShortest break from full-dose chemo; chemo fatigueCapecitabine wanted as sensitiser with ongoing systemic effect
PatientAge, frailty, travel; poor fluoropyrimidine toleranceFit for 5–6 weeks of daily treatment
AimAblative dose (BED10 around 100 Gy)Preoperative margin sterilisation (CONKO-007)

Comparative data
PAULA-1 (2020)Matched case-control · n=80 · SBRT vs chemoRT2-y LC 49.8 vs 40.5% favouring SBRT; OS and toxicity similar. (check)
Korean propensity-matched series (2022)Retrospective · 95 SBRT, 66 chemoRTNo significant difference in 1-y OS, PFS or local control after matching. (check)
Brussels isotoxic high-dose SBRT series (2022)Retrospective, single centre · n=82 · dose-escalated SBRT vs chemoRTMS 22.5 vs 15.9 mo and 1-y LC 75.8 vs 39.3% favouring SBRT. (check)

A021501 warns against low-dose SBRT (33–40/5 or 25/5 without escalation). 33/5 is only about 45 Gy conventional.

Before offering RT

  • Restaging within about 4 weeks: CT chest, abdomen and pelvis, CA 19-9 trend; no new metastases.
  • Surgical opinion on resectability if there has been a response.
  • Distance from tumour to duodenum, stomach and jejunum decides SBRT dose or the switch to chemoRT.
  • Biliary stent in place and working; fiducials if SBRT without MR guidance.
  • Chemo given so far, tolerance, and the plan for chemo after RT.

RT dosing, SBRT and planning

Conventional 45–54 Gy, 15-fraction hypofractionation, or SBRT. Aim for BED10 >60 Gy (e.g. 40/5) to gross disease when ablating.

Planning

  • NPO 2 h; neutral oral contrast (water) and the same volume daily. Triple-phase contrast CT. Fiducials if not on MR-linac.
  • Motion: ~2 cm SI, 6–8 mm AP. 4DCT minimum; DIBH, gating or compression. Compression can push bowel into the field.
  • Conventional PTV 1.5–2 cm AP/lat, 2–3 cm CC (non-gated); SBRT PTV ~3 mm, goal PTV V40 >95% where OARs allow.
  • Nodes: head — proximal 1–1.5 cm CA, proximal 2.5 cm SMA, porta hepatis, suprapancreatic, pancreaticoduodenal, para-aortic T11–L3. Tail — coeliac, SMA, splenic, lateral suprapancreatic.
  • Elective volume (NRG atlas 2024): cover perineural/perivascular spread — CA from origin to trifurcation (to CHA for body tumours abutting CA), SMA from aorta to the lowest pancreatic head, ≥1 cm anatomically edited margin on GTV. >90% of failures lie within 1 cm of CA, SMA or retroperitoneum.
  • Postop (RTOG 0848): PV, PJ, CA (1–1.5 cm), SMA (2.5–3 cm), aorta + bed; expand 1–1.5 cm on vessels, aorta 2.5–3 cm right/1 cm left/2–2.5 cm anterior/0.2 cm posterior; PTV 0.5–1 cm. Pancreaticogastrostomy not included.

Landmark trials

Adjuvant

CONKO-001 (2007; 5 y 2013)Phase III · n=354 resected (80% R0) · observation vs gemcitabine ×6MS 20.2 → 22.8 mo; 5-y OS 10 → 21%; DFS 6.7 → 13.4 mo.
ESPAC-4 (2017)Phase III · resected (60% R1) · gemcitabine ± capecitabineGem-cap better OS. Over half develop local failure after upfront resection.

Neoadjuvant and locally advanced

PREOPANC-1 (2020; long-term 2022)Phase III · resectable/borderline · upfront surgery vs 36/15 + gemcitabine (neoadjuvant + adjuvant gem)R0 43 → 72%, node positivity 82 → 35%; improved DFS and long-term OS. Control arm gemcitabine is no longer standard.
PREOPANC-2 (2025)Phase III · n=375 resectable/borderline · mFOLFIRINOX ×8 vs gem-based chemoRT → surgeryNo OS difference (MS ~21.5 mo, 3-y OS ~34%); resection ~76% both.

MASTERPLAN (ESMO 2026)Phase II (AGITG/TROG) · n=75 randomised 2:1; high-risk operable/borderline (48) or inoperable (26) · chemo (mFOLFIRINOX or gem–nab-paclitaxel) ± SBRT 40/512-mo locoregional recurrence 52 → 20% (p=0.022); median PFS 6.3 → 11.9 mo; MS 14.0 → 22.0 mo (p=0.006); R0 40 → 62% (NS); G3+ 64 vs 70%. Small mixed cohort; 80% of the SBRT arm started SBRT. (check)

  • N/v, steatorrhea

Radiology

  • Hypoattenuating on delayed arterial and PV phases of a triple phase CT compared to surrounding contrast enhancing pancreas tissue

Path – pre-malignant/benign

  • IPMN: indeterminate papillary mucinous neoplasm
    • Main duct IPMN has 40-60% chance of malignant transformation
    • Branch duct IPMN has 15-20% risk
  • MCN: mucinous cystic neoplasm – middle aged, F
    • high risk of transformation adenoCa
  • PanIN: intraepithelial neoplasm (grade 1-3)

Path – malignant

Exocrine - 95%

  • Ductal adenocarcinoma – 90%
    • 60% in head, 15% in body or tail, 15% diffuse
  • Acinar adenocarcinoma 1-2%
  • Pseudopapillary neoplasm – 20-30F, low grade, indolent
  • Adenosquamous
  • Signet ring cell
  • Mixed

IHC ductal adenoCa: CK7+, MUC1+, Ca19.9

IHC acinar adenoCa: trypsin, chymotrypsin, lipase

Endocrine - 5%

  • neuroendocrine carcinoma
  • neuroendocrine tumour
  • carcinoid tumour
  • IHC: synaptophysin, chromogranin A

Molecular

  • CDKN2A/2B
  • TP53
  • SMAD4
  • KRAS

Tumour markers

  • Ca19.9, raised lipase from obstruction
  • Post-op Ca19.9 >90 assoc with worse OS (RTOG 97-04)

Natural Hx

  • Prone to widespread dissemination however many pts die from local progression
  • 50% have DM at presentation
  • Radioresistant pathology

Management

Surgery is the only established cure

  • Has OS benefit compared to CRT: 3 yr OS 20% vs 0% (Doi et al)

Resectable

  • Whipple’s procedure for head/uncinate process
    • Posterior “uncinate” margin is most commonly positive
  • Distal pancreatectomy for tail
  • Neoadjuvant chemotherapy if high risk: FOLFIRINOX to increase R0 and control micrometastatic disease
    • high Ca19.9 >100, large primary or nodes, wt loss ++
  • Adjuvant chemotherapy improves 5 yr OS by 10% (ESPAC-1)
    • FOLFIRINOX or gem/abraxane in less fit pts
  • Adjuvant CRT: adj gemcitabine followed by 50.4Gy/28# + 5FU capecitabine if stable has shown to improve DFS in N- (RTOG RTOG 0848), however clinically offered in R1 and N+ also if stable after chemo

Boderline

  • Neoadjuvant mFOLFIRINOX x 8 cycles (Alliance) -> surgery -> mFOLFIRINOX to complete 6mo in total
  • If unfit for FOLFIRINOX: NA 36Gy/15# + gemcitabine (PREOPANC-1 & 2)
  • If grey zone between borderline and unresectable: induction CHT with FOLFIRINOX or gemcitabine for ~3mo →
    • CRT: 50.4Gy/28# with IV weekly gemcitabine if stable disease (CONKO-007)
    • If progresses on induction chemo then palliative systemic
  • Role of RT:
    • Given if no progression during chemo and:
    • To reduce tumour at tumour vessel interface, sterilise a likely positive margin, reduce pain

Unresectable

  • Induction systemic therapy: FOLFIRINOX > gem/Abraxane for 4-6mo → reassess
    • SABR: 30-40Gy/5# (SPAN-C shows safety/feasibility) – minimise time off chemo
    • ‘Definitive’ CRT: 54Gy/30# with capecitabine – (LAP-07)
    • Continue CHT
    • MSI high: IO, BRCA: PARPi

Oligomet

  • <5 mets
  • Metastasis directed therapy (SABR) improves PFS – 3 vs 10mo (EXTEND, ph II)

Palliative

  • Pain: palliative RT (25Gy/5#, 30Gy/10#, 45Gy/5#), celiac plexus nerve block or neurolysis
  • Obstruction: ERCP + stent
  • Bleeding: palliative RT, endoscopy + hemospray, CTMA + angioembolization
  • Systemic therapy: mFOLFIRINOX, gem/Abraxane, gem/capecitabine
  • Daraxonrasib (RAS inhibitor) in the 2nd line systemic treatment setting improves OS from 7 to 13months (RASolute 302)

Alliance (ph II)

P: mFOLFIRINOX x 8 vs mFOLFIRINOX x 7 + ultrahypofrac 40Gy/5# RT → surgery

O: RT worsened R0 and OS – 18mo OS 70% vs 50%

PREOPANC-1

P: Resectable or borderline pts

I: Surgery vs NA CRT (3 cycles gemcitabine + 36Gy/15# in cycle 2) → surgery

O: OS improved in borderline pts 13 mo vs 18mo

PREOPANC-2 (ph III)

P: Resectable or borderline pts

I: FOLFIRINOX → surgery vs 36Gy/15# + gem → surgery → gemcitabine

O: FOLFIRINOX not superior to NA CRT, mOS 21 months for both arms, PFS 12 months for both arms

CONKO-007

P: Unresectable or borderline [more locally advanced than above trials]

I: induction chemo FOLFIRINOX or gem for 3 mo→ stable disease → further chemo or gem + 50.4Gy/28#

O: RT improves LC and R0, trend to improved OS

SPAN-C (ph II, single centre)

P: Boderline or unresectable

I: induction chemo → SBRT 40-45Gy/5#

O: 1 yr LC 100% in resected group, 77% in unresected

LAP-07

P: Unresectable

I: chemo → further chemo vs 54Gy/30# with capecitabine

O: LC improved from 54% to 68%. Also improves pain. No OS benefit.

NA EBRT

Dose: 50.4Gy/28#, VMAT, daily treatment + concurrent IV weekly gemcitabine

Sim: NBM 3 hrs, supine, arms above head, vac bag, 4DCT or EEBH if >10mm movement, IV contrast, arterial and portovenous phase imagine, fuse PET/MRI, pre-med antiemetic

Toxicities

Acute: enteritis, n/v, dysphagia, dyspepsia, fatigue

Subacute: RILD

Late: bile duct stricture, GI luminal organ ulcer, bleed, perforation, obstruction, renal impairment, chronic enteritis, pancreatic dysfunction, second malignancy

Troubleshooting if OARs overlapping/not met

  • Create PRV by adding 3mm to OAR
  • Subtract PRV from PTV to create SPTV and assess coverage to this structure instead
  • Compromise GTV coverage next
  • Consider dose reduction, 2 dose levels, switching from SABR to conventional fractionation if unsafe
  • Consider adaptive MR guided RT – recontour each day with greater matching accuracy, also allows for motion gated treatment on machine
