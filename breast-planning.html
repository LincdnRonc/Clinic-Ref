Volumes and technique

Contour to an atlas (ESTRO or RTOG), use DIBH for left-sided cases (and consider it for right-sided ones), and aim for homogeneity. Use IMRT or VMAT when cardiac anatomy is difficult, not routinely.

Breast and chest wall (NSABP B-39 and B-51 style)

PTVCTV + 7 mm. Excludes heart; should not cross midline
PTV_evalPTV cropped 5 mm from skin (3 mm for chest wall) and no deeper than the anterior ribs
  • Tangent field edges (2D/3D): superior 1 cm above palpable tissue; inferior 1–2 cm below the inframammary fold; lateral at the mid-axillary line; medial at midline; 2 cm anterior flash. Lung in field <3 cm (typically 1–2 cm).
  • Nodal fields: match at the inferior clavicular head. Use a mono-isocentric technique with half-beam block (field length up to 20 cm), or two isocentres with couch kick.
  • Chest wall boost: electrons with a 2–5 cm margin on the scar. Use only for close or positive margins or inflammatory cancer.

Toxicity and consent

These are the numbers to quote. Hypofractionation and partial-breast RT reduce acute toxicity. Smoking status matters most for long-term lung and heart risk.

With reconstruction

  • Major reconstruction complications are common with PMRT (about 40% in one series). Reconstruction failure is about 10%.
  • Irradiating a tissue expander carries more risk than irradiating the permanent implant.
  • Infection 6 → 13% and skin necrosis 3 → 8% with PMRT in one implant series.
