Vestibular schwannoma: who to treat

For Koos I–III, surveillance and SRS are both evidence-based first choices. Documented growth is the clearest trigger to treat, and SRS is the usual treatment. Koos IV with brainstem compression or hydrocephalus needs surgery.

Decision by scenario (EANO 2020, CNS 2018, ISRS)

ScenarioUsual plan
Koos I–II, new, growth not shownSurveillance or upfront SRS. About two-thirds never grow. V-REX: SRS shrinks the tumour more but gives no hearing or QoL gain at 4 y.
Koos I–II, growing on serial MRISRS 12 Gy. Hearing-preservation surgery for selected young patients with class A hearing.
Koos III (≤3 cm, touching brainstem)SRS or surgery. Weigh age, symptoms and preference.
Koos IV, compression, hydrocephalus, large cysticMicrosurgery, often planned near-/subtotal resection ± SRS to the remnant. Selected Koos IV without symptomatic compression: SRS 11–13 Gy or 25/5.
Elderly or comorbid, no mass effectSurveillance; SRS if growing. Consider stopping imaging after 5–10 stable years.
Large, unfit for surgery50.4/28–54/30 or 25/5. Watch for swelling and hydrocephalus.
Residual or recurrent after surgerySRS.
Sudden hearing loss, tumour stableTreat as SSNHL (steroids) and keep watching. Hearing loss alone is a weak reason to treat.

What tips the balance

Favours treatment
  • Growth ≥2 mm linear or ≥20% volume on comparable imaging.
  • Extracanalicular tumour near the brainstem, with little room to grow before options narrow.
  • Younger age, anxiety about living with an untreated tumour, unreliable MRI follow-up.
  • Progressive disabling symptoms from the tumour (imbalance, trigeminal).
Favours surveillance
  • Koos I–II without documented growth, especially intracanalicular with 100% WRS.
  • Older age, comorbidity, limited life expectancy.
  • Wants to avoid treatment toxicity now. V-REX showed no hearing advantage either way.

Natural history

  • Growth: 17% intrameatal, 29% extrameatal (Stangerup 2006, n=552, mean 3.6 y). Growth occurs within 5 y. Age and sex don't predict it.
  • Intracanalicular, 9.5 y: 37% grew, 23% grew into the CPA, 15% failed conservative management. Good hearing (SDS >70%) fell 52 → 22% (Kirchmann 2017).
  • 100% WRS at diagnosis: 87% still had good hearing at last review (Stangerup 2010).
  • V-REX wait-and-scan arm: 42% needed SRS by 4 y (14 of 21 in year 1); 56% never treated.
  • Growth on the first interval scan is the strongest predictor of further growth.

  • Define growth before you start. Use the same sequences and reader; measurement variability is about 1–2 mm.
  • High-resolution non-contrast T2 (CISS/FIESTA) is acceptable once the diagnosis is secure.
  • Re-scan early for sudden hearing change, facial numbness or weakness, worse imbalance, headache.

Work-up

Answer four questions: how big, and is it growing; is there serviceable hearing; are symptoms forcing treatment now; could this be NF2 or another diagnosis. MRI is enough for diagnosis.

Clinic checklist

  • History: hearing (side, sudden vs gradual, other ear, aids, whether they rely on it), tinnitus, imbalance or vertigo (17–75%, under-reported), facial numbness or pain, facial weakness or twitch, headache or gait change (hydrocephalus), dysphagia.
  • Priorities: tumour control vs hearing, face and balance. Anxiety with an untreated tumour. Can they attend MRI long term?
  • Background: comorbidity, anticoagulants, life expectancy, prior head and neck RT.
  • Exam: CN V (corneal reflex), CN VII House–Brackmann, Weber/Rinne, lower cranial nerves, cerebellar signs, Romberg, tandem gait, nystagmus, head impulse, fundi.
  • NF2 red flags: age <30, bilateral VS, family history, other schwannomas or meningiomas, juvenile cataract, skin schwannomas → genetics.
  • MDT: skull base MDT (neurosurgery, neurotology, RO, neuroradiology, audiology).

Imaging and Koos grade

  • Thin-slice (≤1 mm) post-gadolinium 3D T1 plus heavily T2-weighted 3D CISS/FIESTA/SPACE through the IAC. The T2 shows the fundal CSF cap, cochlear signal and nerves.
  • Collect every prior scan. Measure maximal extrameatal diameter and, ideally, volume.
  • Differentials: meningioma (dural tail, broad base, off-centre to the IAC), epidermoid (DWI), facial schwannoma, metastasis, lipoma.
  • Look for cystic change, brainstem compression, fourth-ventricle effacement, hydrocephalus, contralateral VS.

Hearing classification

Pure-tone average (0.5, 1, 2, 3 kHz) and word recognition score for both ears. Serviceable = AAO-HNS A–B or GR I–II.

SRS: doses and planning

Single-fraction 12 Gy (≤13 Gy) is standard. Fractionation gave no hearing advantage in the ACOUNEU RCT. Keep the cochlear mean ≤4 Gy.

Target delineation and ISRS doses: CNS contouring protocol, section 5.

Regimens

  • Gamma Knife usually at the 50% isodose; linac or CyberKnife at 70–90%. No platform is preferred (CNS 2018: no comparative data).
  • PTV margin 0–1 mm with frame or high-precision mask.

Planning and organs at risk

  • Planning MRI: ≤1 mm T1+C and T2 3D, fused to CT. Target the enhancing tumour including the intracanalicular part (CTV = GTV).
  • Cochlea: mean ≤4 Gy (CNS 2018). Best hearing with mean <3 Gy (NYU).
  • Modiolus: as low as possible.
  • Brainstem: keep the high-dose region off it; limit the surface point dose (commonly <12–14 Gy single fraction).
  • Trigeminal root and facial nerve sit within the target; protect them by keeping the margin dose ≤12–13 Gy.
  • Contour cochlea, modiolus, brainstem, trigeminal root, vestibule and semicircular canals.

Pseudoprogression and follow-up

  • Transient enlargement from about 6 months, usually settling by 2 y. Rates 14% (linear), 23% (volumetric, Hayhurst 2012), up to 45% with a late peak at 31 months (Tufts).
  • Central loss of enhancement is common and is not failure.
  • Treat growth after 24 months as true failure. No salvage before 36 months unless forced by hydrocephalus or compression.
  • Follow-up: MRI at 6 and 12 months, yearly to 5 y, then every 2 y. Audiogram with each scan if hearing is serviceable.
  • Salvage for true failure (2–6%): repeat SRS or surgery. Surgery after SRS is harder, with worse facial outcomes.

Toxicity and consent

Hearing decline is the main thing to discuss: about 50% keep serviceable hearing at 5 y, falling to 25–50% at 10 y. Permanent facial weakness is about 1%.

Consent checklist for SRS

  • Aim is to stop growth; the tumour stays visible on scans.
  • Early swelling at 6–24 months is expected and may briefly worsen imbalance, tinnitus or facial sensation.
  • Hearing may keep falling over years, with or without SRS.
  • Rare: facial weakness, trigeminal numbness or pain, hydrocephalus (report headache, gait change, confusion, incontinence), malignant change.
  • Single session; frame or mask; short steroid course if symptomatic.
  • Lifelong MRI follow-up.
  • Offer hearing rehabilitation, vestibular physiotherapy and tinnitus support whatever is chosen. Dizziness and headache drive long-term QoL more than the modality.

Microsurgery

Surgery is for mass effect and large tumours. After gross total resection control is durable, but facial and hearing outcomes are worse than SRS for small and medium tumours.

Outcomes

  • Recurrence-free survival at 5, 10, 15 y: GTR 96%, 82%, 73% vs STR 47%, 17%, 8% (Nakatomi/Mayo).
  • NTR vs STR: regrowth 0% vs 36% (n=60, J Neurol Surg B 2020).
  • Facial HB I–II: about 90–95% for small tumours. Large VS: 63% translab, 65% retrosigmoid. Danish translab series about 70% overall.
  • Hearing: class A kept in 14.3% after surgery vs 42.9% after SRS (Mayo matched cohort).
  • Complications: CSF leak 5–10%, meningitis 1–2%, chronic headache (retrosigmoid), stroke or haemorrhage, mortality <1%, dry eye if the face is weak.
  • Anxiety improved more after surgery (10.8 points PANQOL) than SRS (5.3) or observation (1.5).

Large VS: planned NTR/STR plus SRS

  • Debulk to relieve compression and protect the facial nerve, then SRS to the remnant.
  • ISRS 2025 review: high control with good facial and cochlear outcomes. Classic GTR of large tumours causes facial deficits in 30–50%.
  • EANO endorses debulking followed by SRS or observation.
Surgery preferred when
  • Symptomatic brainstem compression, hydrocephalus from mass effect, large or rapidly growing cystic tumour.
  • Diagnostic doubt; young patient with a large tumour; strong wish for removal.
  • True SRS failure beyond the pseudoprogression window.

Key trials and evidence

Two RCTs inform SRS (V-REX for timing, ACOUNEU for fractionation). No trial compares SRS with surgery; prospective cohorts favour SRS for facial and hearing outcomes under about 2.5–3 cm.
