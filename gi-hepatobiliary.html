Hepatocellular carcinoma (AJCC 8)

Hepatocellular carcinoma

SBRT gives better local control than TACE or RFA in many settings, and is the only local therapy with randomised data in macrovascular invasion (RTOG 1112). Liver function decides the dose.

Liver function and staging

  • Child-Pugh: A (5–6) / B (7–9) / C (10–15); 2-y OS ~85% / 57% / 35%. SBRT for CP-A or B7; caution B8–9; not CP-C outside trials.
  • ALBI / mALBI refines risk within CP-A; most constraints come from CP-A5 cohorts.
  • BCLC: 0 single <2 cm → resection/ablation · A ≤3 nodules <3 cm (Milan) → ablation/resection/transplant · B multinodular → TACE/TARE (or SBRT) · C macrovascular invasion/extrahepatic → systemic (SBRT increasingly used) · D CP-C or ECOG >2 → supportive care.
  • Milan: single ≤5 cm or ≤3 nodules ≤3 cm, no vascular invasion (5-y OS ~70% after transplant). UCSF: single ≤6.5 cm, ≤3 nodules ≤4.5 cm, total ≤8 cm.
  • LI-RADS: 3 repeat 3–6 mo · 4 MDT ± biopsy (⅓ become HCC in 6 mo) · 5 definite HCC. Imaging diagnosis needs cirrhosis.
  • Surveillance in cirrhosis: ultrasound ± AFP q6 mo.

Where SBRT fits

  • Over TACE: high TACE-toxicity risk (large tumour, poor liver function), poor response or progression after TACE, macrovascular invasion. LC 2–4× better than TACE (TRENDY: 2-y LC 44% vs 100% per protocol).
  • Over RFA: >3 cm, perivascular (heat-sink), after TACE, dome or inaccessible lesions. Small HCC ≤2 cm: SBRT 50/5 non-inferior to RFA for 2-y LC (interim RCT).
  • PVTT / macrovascular invasion: SBRT appropriate first-line or consolidative; can shrink thrombus and restore patency.
  • Bridge to transplant: dropout similar to TACE/RFA (17 / 20 / 17%); favour ≤40/5.
  • With systemic therapy: sequence it. Avoid concurrent bevacizumab; hold agents for about a half-life (bevacizumab 20 d, atezolizumab 27 d).
  • ISRS meta (1889 pts): 3/5-y LC 84/82%, OS 57/40%; classic RILD 0%, non-classic 8%, late G3+ hepatic 0–9%.

Use 5 fx when abutting luminal GI organs or the central biliary tree (avoid 3 fx there). Be more conservative with low platelets, anticoagulants or prior GI bleeding. RTOG 1112: stomach/duodenum/bowel goal <25 Gy, max <30 Gy (5 fx).

Systemic and IR options

  • First line: atezolizumab + bevacizumab (treat varices first); TKIs (sorafenib, lenvatinib) if contraindicated. Later: cabozantinib, regorafenib, ramucirumab (AFP ≥400), nivo/ipi.
  • TACE + IO combinations (EMERALD-1, EMERALD-3) improve PFS in embolisation-eligible disease.
  • TARE (Y-90): higher tumour-absorbed dose (≥400 Gy) linked to longer OS with PVT.
  • Resection over RFA for resectable CP-A T1–2; transplant for portal hypertension or decompensation.

Liver metastases

SBRT for 1–3 liver mets gives high control when the dose is high enough and the lesion is small.

SBRT for liver metastases

  • Dose: 60/3 where safe (Rusthoven: 2-y LC 92%, 100% if ≤3 cm at 60 Gy, 77% if >3 cm). Single fraction up to 40/1 reported as safe and ablative in selected patients.
  • Liver sparing: ≥700 cc of normal liver <15 Gy (3 fx) / <15–21 Gy (5 fx); QUANTEC mean liver <15 Gy (3 fx) for healthy liver.
  • Volume matters: median 15 cc lesions → 1-y LC 95% vs 75 cc lesions → 71% (Rusthoven vs Lee).
  • Colorectal mets are more radioresistant; KRAS-mutant does worse. Protons promising for >6 cm.
  • Toxicity peaks differ at 3 and 6 mo; check LFTs and CP score at both.

Liver SBRT technique

  • Ideal: lesion >8 mm from luminal organs. Fiducials (3 per lesion) if tracking; empty stomach; ≤3 mm slices; multiphase IV contrast.
  • 4DCT. Motion >5 mm → compression (15–40 mmHg; CC ≤5 mm), DIBH (preferred) or gating. ITV if motion <1 cm.
  • Primary liver tumours: consider 4–5 mm CTV. PTV ~5 mm axial, 7 mm CC (MR-guided).
  • Prescribe to 60–90% isodose (usually 80–90%); hot spots inside target; PTV coverage may drop to ~70% where it overlaps OARs (last resort). SIB: run GTV hot where ≥1 cm from luminal structures.
  • Palliative whole liver: 21/7 or 7–8/1 for symptomatic diffuse disease.

Gallbladder and cholangiocarcinoma

Adjuvant capecitabine is standard; chemoRT for R1 or N+ extrahepatic/gallbladder. Unresectable intrahepatic disease responds to high BED.

  • About half of gallbladder cancers have occult peritoneal or liver disease at laparoscopy.
  • Portal venous phase shows mets and IHCC best; late arterial phase shows HCC.
  • Biomarkers: IDH1, FGFR2, HER2 (12–18% of gallbladder), KRAS.

Dose and BED

  • MDACC (Tao): BED10 >80.5 Gy → 3-y OS 38 → 72%, LC 45 → 78% for inoperable IHCC (median 7.9 cm). 80.5 Gy ≈ 43.5/5.
  • Brunner SBRT: Dmean BED10 >76 Gy (~41.5/5) → 2-y LC 39 → 80%; Dmax BED10 >91 Gy (~47/5) → MS 13 → 24 mo.
  • Biliary stricture after RT is hard to tell from progression.
  • Adjuvant volumes (SWOG 0809): retropancreaticoduodenal, coeliac and portal vein nodes + bed.

Commentary
Large tumor volume was permitted in this study: total tumor volume up to 20 cm, single size 15 cm, and up to 5 mets! SBRT easily can be delivered first, followed by immunotherapy, or the two could potentially be given confurrently. RTOG 1112 was terminated early, possibly due to enthusiasm for immunotherapy at the time. The statistical power of 1112 was modified.

How could SBRT improve OS? If radiation is clearing tumor thrombus, this would improve overall hepatic function. Since TACE is reliant on circulatory pathways, it likely is less effective in clearing thrombotic tumor.

In a study evaluating pre-op immuntherapy in HCC, pCR was only 18% (D'Alessio 2024). The IMBrave trial, randomizing atezolumab + bev vs. sorafenib showed OS & PFS benefit with immunotherapy, median OS 19.2 vs. 13.4 mos and median PFS 6.9 vs. 4.3 mos. Although certainly an achievement in cancer therapy, a 6 month improvement in OS falls short of cure and does not justify omission of five days of radiation. The authors also point out that immunotherapy was not as effective in those with macrovascular invasion.

Other immunotherapy trials showed a similar benefit in OS to SBRT in 1112 but unlike SBRT did not show a PFS benefit (KEYNOTE 240, pembro; HIMALAYA, tremelimumab + durva; Checkmate 459, nivolumab)

Commentary
The ORR was only 44%, which seems worse than SBRT (2-yr LPFS 93% in Xi 2024, disease control 75% in RTOG 1112, and 2-yr LC 100% in TRENDY). There is concern that bevacizumab masks true progression. In the HIMALAYA trial tremelimumab + durvalumab, without TACE, shows an OS benefit compared to sorafinib.

Refer to the Discussion section of the study for commentary on adverse effects. According to the authors, the investigator determined whether an adverse event was due to treatment or not. For example bevacizumab has risk of hemorrhage, and many liver patients already have esophageal varices, and others for example have comorbidity of cirrhosis. Thus the authors reported treatment related adverse effects and "possible" treatment related adverse effects. The reader will notice that the "possible" adverse effects and deaths do increase with durvalumab and D+B combination therapy. This is why randomized trials must be performed. OS was not reported, but based on the number of deaths, seems that OS is worse with D and D+B.

In the HIMALAYA trial, tremelimumab + durvalumab, without any TACE, did show OS benefit. In that study however, durvalumab alone was noninferior to sorafinib.

The authors write that adverse effects doesn't seem in excess when comparing to prior TACE, Bev, and durvalumab studies. They also note that there were no deaths from hemorrhage in the study.

Systemic

  • Bevacizumab & atezolizumab or durvalumab/tremelimumab
    • Bev s/e: HTN, GI perforation, bleeding and clotting – need to screen for varices and do coagulation studies prior to Bev
  • 2nd line: Sorafenib (TKI for VEGF and EGFR), levatinib (TKI): hand foot syndrome, diarrhoea, fatigue, hypothyroid
  • Sorafenib or bev/atezo have OS benefit in metastatic/advanced HCC but not in the adjuvant setting for localised HCC

Palliative

Daily CBCT with soft tissue match

Toxicities

  • Acute: fatigue, n/v, diarrhoea, enteritis
  • Subacute: pneumonitis, RILD (classic or on-classic)
  • Late: GI perforation, ulceration, bleed, pulmonary fibrosis, kidney dysfunction, CW pain, rib fracture, biliary tree stricture, second malignancy

OARs

  • Liver-GTV mean dose: <13Gy
    • Volume: exclude GB, include PV from splenic confluence to first bifurcation to left and right PV
    • Gain ~1Gy of MLD for every 5Gy TD reduction
  • Total liver: V≤15Gy > 700cc
  • Central hepatobiliary tract: Dmean <19Gy
    • 1.5cm expansion of portal vein
  • CBD Dmax 0.1cc<50Gy
  • SB, stomach, oesophagus Dmax 0.03cc <30Gy, avoid V33Gy >0.5cc
  • LB Dmax 0.03cc <35Gy
  • Heart Dmax 0.1cc <38Gy
  • Kidney bilateral Dmean <10Gy
  • Spinal cord Dmax <25Gy

Classic/veno-occlusive RILD

  • DNA damage > ROS release > sinusoidal endothelial cell damage > hepatic venous outflow obstruction and congestion > chronic hypoxia > hepatocyte death
  • Fibrotic reaction from chronic inflammation and TGF-B release
  • ↑ALP, anicteric
  • Resulting liver dysfunction

Non-classic RILD

  • Due to hepatocyte injury in a cirrhotic liver or viral hepatitis
  • ↑ ALT/AST and BR

Liver mets

From the exam summary.

Pathophysiology

  • PV drains majority of blood supply from GIT, thus seeding to liver is common
  • Favourable microenvironment rich in nutrients for cellular growth and pro-inflammatory cells (Kpuffer cells)
  • Liver is constantly exposed to GIT antigens resulting in immune tolerance and easier immune evasion

See HCC for imaging

Fractionated palliative RT

  • Diffuse multifocal disease, unsuitable for focal therapies
  • whole liver or partial liver can be treated for pain, bloating, jaundice, deranged LFTs secondary to liver mets
  • 10Gy/2# consecutive days, 8Gy/1#, 21Gy/7#
  • eVIQ suggest generous margins – 1.5-2cm for CTV & PTV if no 4DCT
  • Up to 70% pain response with whole liver single fraction RT

SABR

  • Ideally 1-3 mets, combined diameter ≤10cm with adequate liver function (CP B7 or less if cirrhotic)
  • ≥5mm margin from GI organs
  • Consider other focal therapies with more established outcomes in fit patients: resection, MWA/RFA
  • 3# regime preferable (42Gy/3#, 45Gy/3# - 3rd daily)
  • Alternatives: 50Gy/5# as per HCC
  • HyTEC analysis showed promising outcomes with SBRT: 1yr LC 90%, 3yr LC 75%, BED >100Gy assoc with better LC
  • There is established dose response with higher dose per fraction increasing LC (e.g. 60Gy/5# at 100% 2yr LC vs 30Gy/5# had 50% 2 yr LC – Rule et al)
