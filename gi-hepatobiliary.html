Exam summary: HCC · Cholangiocarcinoma · Liver mets

Hepatocellular carcinoma

SBRT gives better local control than TACE or RFA in many settings, and is the only local therapy with randomised data in macrovascular invasion (RTOG 1112). Liver function decides the dose.

Liver function and staging

  • Child-Pugh: A (5–6) / B (7–9) / C (10–15); 2-y OS ~85% / 57% / 35%. SBRT for CP-A or B7; caution B8–9; not CP-C outside trials.
  • ALBI / mALBI refines risk within CP-A; most constraints come from CP-A5 cohorts.
  • BCLC: 0 single <2 cm → resection/ablation · A ≤3 nodules <3 cm (Milan) → ablation/resection/transplant · B multinodular → TACE/TARE (or SBRT) · C macrovascular invasion/extrahepatic → systemic (SBRT increasingly used) · D CP-C or ECOG >2 → supportive care.
  • Milan: single ≤5 cm or ≤3 nodules ≤3 cm, no vascular invasion (5-y OS ~70% after transplant). UCSF: single ≤6.5 cm, ≤3 nodules ≤4.5 cm, total ≤8 cm.
  • LI-RADS: 3 repeat 3–6 mo · 4 MDT ± biopsy (⅓ become HCC in 6 mo) · 5 definite HCC. Imaging diagnosis needs cirrhosis.
  • Surveillance in cirrhosis: ultrasound ± AFP q6 mo.

Where SBRT fits

  • Over TACE: high TACE-toxicity risk (large tumour, poor liver function), poor response or progression after TACE, macrovascular invasion. LC 2–4× better than TACE (TRENDY: 2-y LC 44% vs 100% per protocol).
  • Over RFA: >3 cm, perivascular (heat-sink), after TACE, dome or inaccessible lesions. Small HCC ≤2 cm: SBRT 50/5 non-inferior to RFA for 2-y LC (interim RCT).
  • PVTT / macrovascular invasion: SBRT appropriate first-line or consolidative; can shrink thrombus and restore patency.
  • Bridge to transplant: dropout similar to TACE/RFA (17 / 20 / 17%); favour ≤40/5.
  • With systemic therapy: sequence it. Avoid concurrent bevacizumab; hold agents for about a half-life (bevacizumab 20 d, atezolizumab 27 d).
  • ISRS meta (1889 pts): 3/5-y LC 84/82%, OS 57/40%; classic RILD 0%, non-classic 8%, late G3+ hepatic 0–9%.

Use 5 fx when abutting luminal GI organs or the central biliary tree (avoid 3 fx there). Be more conservative with low platelets, anticoagulants or prior GI bleeding. RTOG 1112: stomach/duodenum/bowel goal <25 Gy, max <30 Gy (5 fx).

Systemic and IR options

  • First line: atezolizumab + bevacizumab (treat varices first); TKIs (sorafenib, lenvatinib) if contraindicated. Later: cabozantinib, regorafenib, ramucirumab (AFP ≥400), nivo/ipi.
  • TACE + IO combinations (EMERALD-1, EMERALD-3) improve PFS in embolisation-eligible disease.
  • TARE (Y-90): higher tumour-absorbed dose (≥400 Gy) linked to longer OS with PVT.
  • Resection over RFA for resectable CP-A T1–2; transplant for portal hypertension or decompensation.

Liver metastases

SBRT for 1–3 liver mets gives high control when the dose is high enough and the lesion is small.

SBRT for liver metastases

  • Dose: 60/3 where safe (Rusthoven: 2-y LC 92%, 100% if ≤3 cm at 60 Gy, 77% if >3 cm). Single fraction up to 40/1 reported as safe and ablative in selected patients.
  • Liver sparing: ≥700 cc of normal liver <15 Gy (3 fx) / <15–21 Gy (5 fx); QUANTEC mean liver <15 Gy (3 fx) for healthy liver.
  • Volume matters: median 15 cc lesions → 1-y LC 95% vs 75 cc lesions → 71% (Rusthoven vs Lee).
  • Colorectal mets are more radioresistant; KRAS-mutant does worse. Protons promising for >6 cm.
  • Toxicity peaks differ at 3 and 6 mo; check LFTs and CP score at both.

Liver SBRT technique

  • Ideal: lesion >8 mm from luminal organs. Fiducials (3 per lesion) if tracking; empty stomach; ≤3 mm slices; multiphase IV contrast.
  • 4DCT. Motion >5 mm → compression (15–40 mmHg; CC ≤5 mm), DIBH (preferred) or gating. ITV if motion <1 cm.
  • Primary liver tumours: consider 4–5 mm CTV. PTV ~5 mm axial, 7 mm CC (MR-guided).
  • Prescribe to 60–90% isodose (usually 80–90%); hot spots inside target; PTV coverage may drop to ~70% where it overlaps OARs (last resort). SIB: run GTV hot where ≥1 cm from luminal structures.
  • Palliative whole liver: 21/7 or 7–8/1 for symptomatic diffuse disease.

Gallbladder and cholangiocarcinoma

Adjuvant capecitabine is standard; chemoRT for R1 or N+ extrahepatic/gallbladder. Unresectable intrahepatic disease responds to high BED.

  • About half of gallbladder cancers have occult peritoneal or liver disease at laparoscopy.
  • Portal venous phase shows mets and IHCC best; late arterial phase shows HCC.
  • Biomarkers: IDH1, FGFR2, HER2 (12–18% of gallbladder), KRAS.

Dose and BED

  • MDACC (Tao): BED10 >80.5 Gy → 3-y OS 38 → 72%, LC 45 → 78% for inoperable IHCC (median 7.9 cm). 80.5 Gy ≈ 43.5/5.
  • Brunner SBRT: Dmean BED10 >76 Gy (~41.5/5) → 2-y LC 39 → 80%; Dmax BED10 >91 Gy (~47/5) → MS 13 → 24 mo.
  • Biliary stricture after RT is hard to tell from progression.
  • Adjuvant volumes (SWOG 0809): retropancreaticoduodenal, coeliac and portal vein nodes + bed.
